    In Summary

This week, you learned about the fundamental principles of electrical circuits, encompassing Ohm’s Law,
resistors in series and parallel configurations, and Kirchhoff’s laws. These concepts provide the foundation for
analyzing and designing circuits to meet specific performance criteria. Ohm’s Law elucidates the relationship
between voltage, current, and resistance, while Kirchhoff’s laws govern the conservation of charge and
energy in circuits. Understanding these principles empowers us to calculate the total resistance of complex
circuits and solve circuit problems, such as determining voltage and current distribution.




    Bullet point summary

•    Ohm’s Law (V = IR) relates voltage, current, and resistance.
•    Current (I) is measured in amperes (A), voltage (V) in volts (V), and resistance (R) in ohms (Ω).
•    In series circuits, total resistance is the sum of individual resistances.
•    In parallel circuits, the inverse of total resistance is the sum of inverses of individual resistances.
•    Kirchhoff’s laws describe the conservation of charge and energy in electrical circuits.
•    Kirchhoff’s current law states that the total current entering a junction must equal the total current
     leaving the junction.
•    Kirchhoff’s voltage law states that the sum of the voltages around any closed loop in a circuit must
     equal zero.
