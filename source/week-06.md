    In Summary

This week, you learned about series RLC circuits, which combine resistance, capacitance, and inductance
in series under an alternating supply. Each passive component exhibits unique phase relationships with a
sinusoidal alternating voltage. In a pure resistor, voltage and current waveforms are in phase. Combining
these elements allows us to analyse their reactances and determine the overall circuit reactance. Series
RLC circuits are second-order circuits with energy storage elements such as inductance and capacitance.
While current is common across all components, voltage drops can be out-of-phase due to reactances.
Kirchhoff’s voltage law is employed to find the supply voltage by vectorially adding component voltages.
Impedance depends on angular frequency, leading to leading or lagging phase angles based on the balance
between capacitive and inductive reactances.

•    Series RLC circuits combine resistance, inductance, and capacitance in series.

•    Series RLC circuits are second-order circuits with inductance (L) and capacitance (C).
•    The impedance of the circuit depends on the angular frequency and reactances.
