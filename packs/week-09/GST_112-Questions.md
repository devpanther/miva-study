## Section B - Short answer (3)

**13.** Define reorientation strategies as the lesson does, name the six reorientation efforts the lesson lists, and state for each of the four that have their own treatment the year (or years), the government that launched it, and its stated aim.

**14.** Account for austerity measures in Nigeria: define them, give the three waves the lesson identifies with the leaders or periods attached, list the six Nigerian examples, and state why the lesson says austerity has continually failed.

## Answers

**1. b** — *the lesson's definition*: **government authorities, institutions, or programmes aimed at reshaping or redirecting people's attitudes, beliefs, or behaviours towards a particular purpose or set of ideals**, working through **public awareness campaigns, educational programmes, skill development, counselling, and community outreach**. Option (a) is **last week's** material (civic responsibility, s.24). Option (c) is the definition of **austerity measures** — one strategy inside the topic, not the topic. Option (d) puts the initiative outside government, whereas the definition is explicitly of **government** authorities and institutions.

**2. a** — *NOA's founding instrument*: **Decree 100 of 1993**. Option (b) borrows **MAMSER's** year, 1987. Option (c) invents a decree out of **WAI's** end date, September 1985. Option (d) is the **naturalisation** provision from Week 8 — a constitutional section, not a founding decree.

**3. c** — *NOA is the successor of the **Directorate for Social Mobilisation – MAMSER***. Option (a) is the body that **recommended** MAMSER's creation, not the body NOA replaced. Option (b), the **National Rehabilitation Commission**, belongs to **Gowon's 3 R's** (Summary PDF) and handled **reconstruction and rehabilitation in the Eastern States**. Option (d), **DFRRI (1986)**, is one of the **programmes under OFN**.

**4. a** — *OFN*: an **agricultural initiative launched in Nigeria in 1976-80**, introduced by the **military government of General Olusegun Obasanjo**, to **address food security challenges and reduce dependence on food imports**. Option (b) attaches it to **Babangida and SAP**, which belong to the **austerity** strand. Option (c) is **WAI**, Buhari's programme. Option (d) is **MAMSER**.

**5. d** — *the four failings the lesson lists*: **resources not provided as promised**; **activities not effectively performed**; **farmers sceptical of new methods and administrative officials not doing the job expected of them**; **the manpower needed to expand farming operations did not materialise**. Urban gardening of undeveloped building plots is stated as **what the programme encouraged**, not as a failure — (d) inverts a feature into a fault. And note the balance the lesson strikes: OFN **did not completely achieve its objectives** but **raised awareness about agriculture and food production** and **laid the foundation for subsequent agricultural development programmes and policies**.

**6. b** — *the years as listed*: **ADP (1976), ACGS (1977), RBDA (1978), RBS (1978), GR (1980), DFRRI (1986)**. Option (a) swaps ADP and ACGS. Option (c) moves the **Green Revolution** back to 1978 and pushes **RBS** to 1986. Option (d) does the same trick in reverse with **DFRRI** and **RBS** — the two entries dated **1978** are **RBDA** and **RBS**, and **DFRRI** is the outlier at **1986**.

**7. b** — *the definition, verbatim*: **strict, frugal economic policies used by the government to manage public debt**, **typically undertaken during periods of economic hardship or budget deficits**. Option (a) describes the opposite, a stimulus. Option (c) describes **OFN's** field of action. Option (d) is **WAI's** method — **enforcement of discipline through penalties and public works projects**.

**8. a** — *the three types*: **higher taxes (revenue generation) to fund government spending**; **revenue generation plus lower government spending**; **lower taxes plus lower government spending**. Option (b) lists three of the **six Nigerian examples** — the examples answer a different question from the types. Option (c) invents SAP-era instruments the deck does not give in this list. Option (d) pairs cuts with **higher** spending, which is not austerity at all.

**9. b** — *the deck's own answer*: **Babangida**. The deck concedes that **Buhari's military regime in the 1980s also implemented austerity measures**, but says it was **specifically under Babangida's Structural Adjustment Programme (SAP)** that they were **further developed and implemented**. This is the one place the deck's narrative slide ("introduced after the oil boom of the 1970s, during Buhari's military regime") and its question disagree — sit the exam with **Babangida**. Option (a), **Obasanjo**, belongs to **OFN**; (c) **Goodluck Jonathan** appears only as a distractor; (d) **Gowon** belongs to the **3 R's**.

**10. a** — *WAI*: a **mass mobilisation programme organised by the military dictatorship of Buhari** to **correct social maladjustments**, instilling **discipline, moral values and a sense of national pride**, promoting **punctuality, respect for queues, environmental cleanliness and adherence to traffic regulations**, and it **ended in September 1985**. Option (b) is **MAMSER's** 1987 remit. Option (c) makes it civilian and pairs it with **SAP**, which is Babangida's. Option (d) reverses the chronology: **NOA (1993)** comes after WAI, and succeeds **MAMSER**, not WAI.

**11. c** — *the criticism is the high handedness of the **WAI Brigade***. The lesson's point is that WAI **achieved remarkable success** because the Brigade **dealt decisively with anyone who failed to act in accordance with the dictates of the state** — and that **the same fact was its utmost criticism**. Option (b) is the criticism of **austerity** (**the bulk of the burden shifted to the masses**). Option (d) is what happened to **MAMSER**. Option (a) is invented.

**12. a** — *the **Public Bureau headed by Samuel Cookey***. Learn the name: it is the most droppable proper noun in the week. Option (b) belongs to **Gowon's 3 R's**; option (c), **DFRRI**, is an **OFN** sub-programme; option (d), the **WAI Brigade**, was the enforcement arm of **WAI**, not an advisory body.

**13.** *Definition, list, and the four with their own treatment.* **Reorientation strategies refer to government authorities, institutions, or programmes aimed at reshaping or redirecting people's attitudes, beliefs, or behaviours towards a particular purpose or set of ideals**; to promote good change these organisations often participate in **public awareness campaigns, educational programmes, skill development, counselling, and community outreach**. The lesson lists **six**: **National Orientation Agency (NOA)**; **Operation Feed the Nation**; **Austerity Measures**; **War Against Indiscipline**; **War Against Indiscipline and Corruption**; and **Mass Mobilisation for Self-reliance, Social Justice and Economic Recovery (MAMSER)** — the fifth, **WAIC**, is named in the list but given no separate treatment. **NOA** — established by **Decree 100 of 1993**, the **successor of the Directorate for Social Mobilisation – MAMSER**; it exists to **create and sustain public awareness, understanding, and support for government policies, programmes, and initiatives**, and was **founded with the mandate to reorient Nigerians towards embracing positive values, patriotism, national unity, and development**; its programmes include the **Nationwide Attitudinal Reorientation Programme for Students in Tertiary Institutions**, the **National Ethics and Integrity Policy (Secondary Schools)**, the **Campaign Against Open Defecation**, **Building a People of Peace (Inter-Faith Peaceful Coexistence)**, **Nationwide Sensitisation on the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA)**, **Sensitisation on COVID-19**, **Sensitisation on Routine Immunization**, and **Change Begins With Me: Patronise Made in Nigeria Products and Services**. **Operation Feed the Nation** — an **agricultural initiative launched in 1976-80** by the **military government of General Olusegun Obasanjo**, to **address the country's food security challenges and reduce dependence on food imports**; **urban dwellers were encouraged to garden undeveloped building plots**, and the programme worked through **awareness campaigns, training programmes, and the provision of inputs such as improved seeds, fertilizers and farm implements**, stressing **sustainable agricultural practices and the conservation of natural resources**. **Austerity measures** — **strict, frugal economic policies used to manage public debt**, introduced **after the oil boom of the 1970s during Buhari's military regime**, developed under **Babangida's Structural Adjustment Programme**, and resumed **from 2015** to **plug budget deficit arising from low oil prices and low oil output**. **WAI** — a **mass mobilisation programme of the military dictatorship of Buhari** aimed at **correcting social maladjustments**, ended **September 1985**. **MAMSER** — launched **in 1987** on the **recommendation of the Public Bureau headed by Samuel Cookey**, to **mobilise and empower Nigerians to actively participate in nation-building, promote self-reliance, achieve social justice, and drive economic recovery**.

**14.** *Austerity in Nigeria.* **Austerity measures are strict, frugal economic policies used by the government to manage public debt**, and they are **typically undertaken during periods of economic hardship or budget deficits**. The lesson identifies **three waves**: first, austerity **introduced in Nigeria after the oil boom of the 1970s, during Buhari's military regime**; second, the **Structural Adjustment Programme of Babangida**, which **also introduced austerity** and under which, on the deck's own mid-lesson answer, the measures were **further developed and implemented**; third, **from 2015**, when the government **began austerity measures to plug budget deficit arising from low oil prices and low oil output**. The **six Nigerian examples** are **fiscal consolidation; reduction of recurrent expenditure; subsidy reforms; revenue diversification; debt management; and rationalisation of subsidies and social programs**. Distinguish these from the **three main types** of austerity in general — **higher taxes (revenue generation) to fund government spending; revenue generation plus lower government spending; and lower taxes plus lower government spending**. The lesson's verdict: **austerity measures have continually failed because the government has not addressed the issue of waste in government and lower government spending**, and because **the bulk of the burden of austerity is often shifted to the masses to carry** — the failure is one of **incidence** (who pays) and of **not cutting the government's own waste**, not of the instruments themselves.

**15.** *MAMSER against NOA, and the conclusion.* **MAMSER** is **Mass Mobilisation for Self-reliance, Social Justice and Economic Recovery**, **an initiative launched by the Nigerian government in 1987 following the recommendation of the Public Bureau headed by Samuel Cookey**. Its **primary goal** was to **mobilise and empower Nigerians to actively participate in nation-building, promote self-reliance, achieve social justice, and drive economic recovery**, and it **played a key role in the emergence of cooperative societies playing a key role in the Nigerian workplace**. It was **eventually phased out, and its functions and activities were absorbed into other government agencies and initiatives**; while it **had its strengths in promoting self-reliance, social justice and economic recovery**, its **overall effectiveness and long-term impact have been a subject of analysis and discussion**. The **National Orientation Agency** is the body that took up that work: **established by Decree 100 of 1993**, it is expressly **the successor of the Directorate for Social Mobilisation – MAMSER**, and is **a government agency established to create and sustain public awareness, understanding and support for government policies, programmes and initiatives**, **founded with the mandate to reorient Nigerians towards embracing positive values, patriotism, national unity, and development**. The relationship, then, is **succession**: **MAMSER (1987) → Directorate for Social Mobilisation → NOA (Decree 100 of 1993)**, a mobilisation drive of the late 1980s converted into a **permanent standing agency**. The lesson concludes that **reorientation strategies have often failed in Nigeria because of corruption, lack of political will, poor leadership, exemplary leadership** (read: the lack of it) **and improper planning**, and that **there is a need for a declaration of a state of emergency on reorientating Nigerians on the values of trust, legitimate work and moral conscience** — the weekly summary renders these values as **trust, hard work and moral conscience**. The pattern across all six efforts is the same one: the programmes were **not short of aims**, they were **short of delivery** — OFN's **inputs never arrived** and its **officials did not do the job expected of them**; austerity's **burden fell on the masses** while **government waste went unaddressed**; WAI's **enforcement succeeded but by high handedness**; and MAMSER was **absorbed away** before its impact could be settled.
