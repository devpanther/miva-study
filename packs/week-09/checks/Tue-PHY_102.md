# Tuesday — PHY_102 nightly check

*The Lorentz force, as concepts.*
*Sit cold, notes closed, 15 minutes. 8 multiple choice, 4 written. Score out of 12.*

**1.** A charged particle moving through a uniform magnetic field feels a force F_max when its velocity is perpendicular to B. At what angle between v and B is the force exactly half of F_max?
A. 30°
B. 60°
C. 45°
D. 28.6°

**2.** A charge of −3.0 μC is at rest in a uniform electric field of 4.0 × 10⁴ N/C. Calculate the force on it and give its direction relative to E.
A. 0.12 N, perpendicular to E
B. 0 N, because the charge is not moving
C. 0.12 N, opposite to E
D. 0.12 N, along E

**3.** A positive charge moves due east through a uniform magnetic field that points vertically upward. In which direction is the magnetic force on it?
A. Due north
B. Due south
C. Vertically upward
D. Due west

**4.** A proton (q = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) is at rest in a region where E = 500 N/C and B = 2.0 T. Calculate the net force on it.
A. 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ N
B. 0 N
C. 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁶ N
D. 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁷ N

**5.** A proton (q = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) moves at 1.0 × 10⁶ m/s in a circle in a uniform field of 0.50 T and covers 2.0 m of its path. Calculate the work done on it by the magnetic force.
A. 1.6 × 10⁻¹³ J
B. 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ J
C. −1.6 × 10⁻¹³ J
D. 0 J

**6.** A proton circles in a uniform magnetic field with a period of 2.6 × 10⁻⁷ s. Its speed is then doubled, with the field unchanged. Calculate the new period.
A. 5.2 × 10⁻⁷ s
B. 2.6 × 10⁻⁷ s
C. 1.3 × 10⁻⁷ s
D. 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ s

**7.** In a velocity selector a positive charge moves along +x, B points along +y and E points along −z. Particles at speed v₀ = E/B pass straight through. In which direction is a slower positive particle deflected?
A. −z
B. +y
C. +z
D. −x

**8.** A plane electromagnetic wave travels along +x and its electric field oscillates along ±y. Along which direction does its magnetic field oscillate?
A. Along ±x, the direction of travel
B. Along ±y, parallel to E
C. Along ±z
D. In no fixed direction

**9. (show your working)** A wire carries free charge of linear density λ drifting at speed v, so that the current is I = λv. Starting from the force F = qv × B on a single charge, derive the force dF = I dl × B on an element dl of the wire. Show your working.

**10. (show your working)** A charge q of mass m enters a uniform field B with speed v perpendicular to B. By equating the magnetic force to the centripetal force, derive r = mv/(qB) and T = 2πm/(qB), and explain why T does not depend on v. Show your working.

**11. (show your working)** An electron (q = −1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) moves along +x at 2.0 × 10⁶ m/s through a uniform field B = 0.30 T pointing along +z. Calculate the magnitude of the magnetic force on it and give its direction. Show your working.

**12. (show your working)** A charged particle in a uniform magnetic field moves in a circle at constant speed. Explain why the magnetic force can never change its kinetic energy, and why this does not mean the particle has no acceleration.

---

## Answers

**1. A** — *Angle dependence of F = qvB sin θ.* F = qvB sin θ and F_max = qvB, so F/F_max = sin θ = 0.5, giving θ = sin⁻¹(0.5) = 30°.

60° is cos⁻¹(0.5), using cos in place of sin; 45° assumes the force is half at half the right angle, but sin 45° = 0.707; 28.6° reads 0.5 as an angle in radians and converts it.

**2. C** — *Electric force qE and the sign of the charge.* F = qE = (3.0 × 10⁻⁶)(4.0 × 10⁴) = 0.12 N. The charge is negative, so the force points opposite to E. The electric term of the Lorentz force needs no motion.

Along E is the direction for a positive charge; perpendicular to E is the geometry of the magnetic force, not the electric one; 0 N confuses the electric term with the magnetic term, which is the one that vanishes for a charge at rest.

**3. B** — *Direction of the magnetic force by right-hand rule 1.* F = qv × B. Take east = x, north = y, up = z: v × B = x̂ × ẑ = −ŷ, which is due south. Right-hand rule: thumb east (v), fingers up (B), the palm faces south, and the charge is positive so that is the force.

Due north is the answer with the cross product taken in the wrong order, or for a negative charge; vertically upward puts F along B, which a cross product can never do; due west puts F along the line of v, which is equally impossible since F is always perpendicular to v.

**4. D** — *Magnetic term vanishes for a charge at rest.* With v = 0 the magnetic term qv × B is zero, however large B is. Only the electric term acts: F = qE = (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹)(500) = 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁷ N, along E.

0 N assumes a stationary charge feels nothing, which is true of the magnetic force only; 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ N is qB, treating B like an electric field; 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁶ N multiplies qE by B as if the two fields combined.

**5. D** — *The magnetic force does no work.* The force has magnitude qvB = (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹)(1.0 × 10⁶)(0.50) = 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ N, but it is always perpendicular to the velocity, so F·v = 0 at every instant and the work over any path length is 0 J. The speed and kinetic energy are unchanged.

1.6 × 10⁻¹³ J is F × d, treating the force as if it acted along the path; 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ J is F × 1 m, the same error with the distance lost; −1.6 × 10⁻¹³ J imagines the force acting against the motion, but it never has a component along v in either sense.

**6. B** — *Period of the orbit is independent of speed.* T = 2πm/(qB) contains no v. Doubling the speed doubles the radius r = mv/qB, so the proton goes twice as far round at twice the speed and the lap time is unchanged: 2.6 × 10⁻⁷ s. This is what lets a cyclotron run at one fixed frequency.

5.2 × 10⁻⁷ s assumes T grows with v as if the radius were fixed; 1.3 × 10⁻⁷ s assumes a faster particle completes a fixed circle sooner, forgetting the circle grows; 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ s squares a dependence that does not exist.

**7. A** — *Which force wins in a velocity selector.* The magnetic force is qv(x̂ × ŷ) = qvB along +z and the electric force is qE along −z (positive charge, E along −z). At v₀ they cancel. A slower particle has a smaller magnetic force while qE is unchanged, so the electric force wins and the net force is toward −z.

+z is where a faster particle goes, because then the magnetic force dominates; +y is along B, a direction the magnetic force can never have; −x would be a braking force, but neither force has a component along v.

**8. C** — *Transverse geometry of an electromagnetic wave.* An electromagnetic wave is transverse: E and B are perpendicular to each other and both are perpendicular to the direction of travel, which is the direction of E × B. With travel along x and E along y, B must be along ±z.

Along x would make the wave longitudinal, like sound, which an EM wave never is; parallel to E would leave no third direction for the wave to travel along; the two fields are rigidly locked in orientation and phase, so B has a definite direction.

**9.** *Deriving the force on a current element from the single-charge law.* A length dl of the wire contains charge dq = λ dl, all moving with velocity v along the wire. The magnetic force on that packet is the single-charge law applied to dq: dF = (dq) v × B = (λ dl) v × B. Since λ and dl are scalars, this is dF = (λv) dl × B with the direction now carried by dl, and λv = I, so dF = I dl × B. For a straight wire of length L in a uniform field this integrates to F = I L × B, magnitude BIL sin θ.

A correct answer must write dq = λ dl, substitute into qv × B, move the direction from v to dl, and use I = λv. Introducing a factor 1/c is the cgs form and is wrong in SI.

**10.** *Deriving the radius and period of the circular orbit.* The magnetic force qvB is perpendicular to v and of constant size, so it acts as a centripetal force: qvB = mv²/r. Cancel one v and rearrange: r = mv/(qB). The period is the circumference over the speed: T = 2πr/v = 2π(mv/qB)/v = 2πm/(qB). The v cancels because a faster particle sweeps a proportionally larger circle: the distance round grows exactly as fast as the speed, so the lap time is fixed by m, q and B alone.

A correct answer must show qvB = mv²/r, obtain r = mv/qB, form T = 2πr/v and cancel v, and state the proportional-growth reason. Writing T = 2πr/v and stopping, without eliminating r, does not show independence from v.

**11.** *Magnetic force on a negative charge with direction.* v is perpendicular to B, so F = |q|vB = (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹)(2.0 × 10⁶)(0.30) = 9.6 × 10⁻¹⁴ N. Direction: v × B = x̂ × ẑ = −ŷ, which is the force on a positive charge; the electron is negative, so its force is reversed, along +y.

Final answer: 9.6 × 10⁻¹⁴ N along +y (accept 9.5 to 9.7 × 10⁻¹⁴ N). The working must show the cross product x̂ × ẑ = −ŷ and then the reversal for the negative charge. Giving −y forgets the sign of the electron; giving a direction along z or x is impossible because F is perpendicular to both B and v.

**12.** *Zero work versus non-zero acceleration in a magnetic field.* The magnetic force F = qv × B is perpendicular to v at every instant, so its power F·v is zero and it does no work along any path; kinetic energy ½mv² therefore stays constant and the speed cannot change. But a force still produces an acceleration a = F/m = qvB/m; because it is perpendicular to v it changes the direction of the velocity, not its magnitude, which is exactly what keeps the particle on a circle. Momentum, a vector, changes continuously even though its magnitude does not.

A correct answer states F ⊥ v so W = 0 and KE is constant, then distinguishes speed from velocity: there is a real centripetal acceleration qvB/m that only turns the velocity. Saying the force is too weak to do work, or that induced fields cancel the work, is wrong.
