# Wednesday — GST_122 fast-hour check

*Speaking prompts: the definition, the guidelines, the importance list and stress.*
*Five questions, straight after the fast hour. Recall only. Score out of 5.*

**1.** A speaking prompt is designed to do what?
A. Elicit a spoken response
B. Assess written grammar
C. Set an essay topic
D. Score pronunciation

**2.** Which guideline is first in the deck's list of thirteen?
A. Organise Your Thoughts
B. Understand the Prompt/Question
C. Introduction
D. Address the Key Points

**3.** Which guideline asks you to consider counter arguments?
A. Stay Relevant
B. Elaborate and Expand
C. Balance
D. Adapt to the Context

**4.** Which stated importance covers attending to others' contributions?
A. Building Confidence
B. Critical Thinking
C. Promoting Active Participation
D. Enhancing Listening Skills

**5.** Emphasis on a syllable inside a single word is called:
A. Word stress
B. Sentence stress
C. Vocal variety
D. Enunciation

## Answers

**1. A** — The deck defines a prompt as a question or statement designed to elicit a spoken response, serving as a stimulus that initiates and guides talk. A topic alone carries no such instruction.

**2. B** — Understanding comes first, then organising, then the introduction. Anything that puts drafting or speaking ahead of comprehension has the order wrong.

**3. C** — Balance means a balanced perspective, considering different angles or counter arguments, and it shows nuanced understanding. Stay Relevant forbids tangents; Elaborate asks for depth.

**4. D** — The deck lists Enhancing Listening Skills because interactive settings require you to listen to others. Promoting Active Participation is about joining in, not about listening.

**5. A** — Word stress falls on a syllable within a word, as in demonstrate. Sentence stress falls on a whole word within a sentence, as in the coffee example, and changes the sentence's meaning.
