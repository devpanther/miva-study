# Wednesday — GST_122 fast-hour check

*Speaking prompts: the definition, the guidelines, the importance list and stress.*
*5 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 5.*

**1.** In a panel discussion Dele answers the opening question well, but when the moderator adds a follow-up comment he ignores it and carries on with his prepared points. Which guideline for responding to speaking prompts has he broken?
A. Adapt to the Context
B. Practice Active Listening
C. Stay Relevant
D. Manage Time Effectively

**2.** Asked about a new policy, Amina fills her answer with technical jargon and speaks so fast that the interviewer cannot follow her. Which guideline has she broken?
A. Elaborate and Expand
B. Manage Time Effectively
C. Speak Clearly and Concisely
D. Provide Examples and Evidence

**3.** Put these guidelines for responding to a speaking prompt in the order they should be applied: (1) Introduction; (2) Organise your thoughts; (3) Understand the prompt; (4) Address the key points.
A. 3, 2, 1, 4
B. 2, 3, 1, 4
C. 3, 1, 2, 4
D. 3, 2, 4, 1

**4.** Which of the following is NOT one of the nine stated importances of speaking prompts?
A. Critical Thinking
B. Building Confidence
C. Language Proficiency
D. Grammar Correction

**5.** Which term is defined as the emphasis on a particular syllable or segment within a word, phrase or sentence during speech?
A. Stress
B. Intonation
C. Enunciation
D. Pace

---

## Answers

**1. B** — *Matching a scenario to a speaking-prompt guideline.* Practice Active Listening says that when the prompt is part of a conversation or discussion you actively listen to follow-up questions or comments so you can adapt and give relevant information based on the ongoing exchange.

Adapt to the Context concerns tone and setting; Stay Relevant forbids unrelated tangents, and his points were on topic; Manage Time concerns duration.

**2. C** — *Speak Clearly and Concisely versus Elaborate and Expand.* Speak Clearly and Concisely warns against unnecessary jargon or overly complex language and tells you to be mindful of pace and enunciation.

Elaborate and Expand asks for sufficient detail, which is not her fault; Manage Time is about staying within an allotted duration; Provide Examples is about supporting points with concrete evidence.

**3. A** — *Ordering the speaking-prompt guidelines.* The guidelines begin Understand the Prompt/Question, then Organise Your Thoughts, then Introduction, then Address the Key Points. You cannot organise what you have not understood, and the introduction is the first thing you say.

'2, 3, 1, 4' organises before understanding; '3, 1, 2, 4' speaks the introduction before organising; '3, 2, 4, 1' puts the introduction after the body.

**4. D** — *The importance list for speaking prompts.* The nine importances are Communication Skill Development, Critical Thinking, Expressing Ideas Clearly, Language Proficiency, Interviews and Assessments, Promoting Active Participation, Building Confidence, Cultural and Social Awareness, and Enhancing Listening Skills.

Grammar is mentioned only as something practised under Language Proficiency; 'Grammar Correction' is not a heading on the list.

**5. A** — *Definition of stress in linguistics.* Stress in linguistics is the emphasis on a particular syllable or segment within a word, phrase or sentence during speech; word stress works on syllables and sentence stress on words.

Intonation is the rise and fall of the voice; enunciation and pace are aspects of clear delivery named under Speak Clearly and Concisely, not the definition of stress.
