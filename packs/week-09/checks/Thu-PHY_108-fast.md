# Thursday — PHY_108 fast-hour check

*Specific heat of a solid by mixtures: unit, energy balance, calorimeter term and apparatus.*
*Five questions, straight after the fast hour. Recall only. Score out of 5.*

**1.** What is the SI unit of specific heat capacity?
A. J/°C
B. J/kg·°C
C. J/kg
D. kg·°C/J

**2.** In the method of mixtures, heat lost by the solid equals heat gained by:
A. The water alone
B. The calorimeter alone
C. The water and the calorimeter
D. The room and the water

**3.** Omitting the calorimeter term m_c c_c makes the answer for c_s:
A. Unchanged
B. Too high
C. Negative
D. Too low

**4.** Which piece of apparatus closes the heat-lost-equals-heat-gained equation?
A. The insulating jacket with cover
B. The crucible tongs
C. The digital balance
D. The hot plate

**5.** Which difference is the temperature rise of the water?
A. T_s - T_f
B. T_s - T_r
C. T_f - T_r
D. T_r - T_f

## Answers

**1. B** — Specific heat is energy per kilogram per degree, so J/kg·°C (same number as J/kg·K). J/°C on its own is heat capacity, which belongs to an object, not a material.

**2. C** — The vessel warms with the water, so the gain is (m_w c_w + m_c c_c)(T_f - T_r). The insulating jacket is what keeps the room out of the equation.

**3. D** — Dropping it understates the heat gained, so c_s comes out too low: 391 instead of 410 J/kg·°C on the deck's copper data. A negative value would mean a temperature subtracted backwards.

**4. A** — Without the jacket and cover, heat leaks to the room and the equation needs a third term. The tongs address transfer losses and the balance gives the masses.

**5. C** — The water rises from T_r to T_f, while the solid falls from T_s to T_f. T_s - T_r appears nowhere, and T_r - T_f is that rise written backwards.
