## Section B - Short answer (3)

**13.** List the thirteen guidelines for responding to speaking prompts in the order the lesson gives them, and explain in two sentences why *Understand the Prompt/Question* and *Organise Your Thoughts* must precede the *Introduction*.

**14.** Name six of the nine importances of speaking prompts the lesson lists, with one line on the stated reason for each. Then state which two of them are specifically about *contexts of assessment and participation* rather than about the individual's own skill.

**15.** Define a speaking prompt as the lesson defines it. Then take the prompt "Examine the socio-economic implications of the gig economy on labour markets" and outline the four-part shape all three model responses followed, naming for each part the guideline it satisfies.

## Answers

**1. b** — *definition of speaking prompts*: "questions or statements designed to **elicit a spoken response** from you", serving as a **stimulus to initiate and guide verbal communication**. (a) inverts it — prompts elicit responses, they are not the responses; (c) is pronunciation, a different lesson; (d) is the *Organise Your Thoughts* stage, which happens after the prompt is given, and the deck's outline is mental or jotted, not the prompt itself.

**2. c** — *order of the guidelines*: the list opens with **Understand the Prompt/Question** — carefully read or listen, identify key components and any specific instructions. (a) is second and cannot happen until you know what is being asked; (d) is third; (b) is fifth, well inside the body of the response.

**3. b** — *Stay Relevant*: keep the response focused on the topic and **avoid veering off into unrelated tangents, as this can dilute the impact of your answer**. (a) balance is about counter arguments **on the topic**, not about other topics; (c) elaboration is depth on the key points, and he has depth on the wrong thing; (d) time may also suffer, but the named fault is irrelevance.

**4. a** — *Balance*: maintain a balanced perspective, **considering different angles or counter arguments if applicable**, which **demonstrates a nuanced understanding of the topic**. (b) addressing key points is about covering the prompt's core elements; (c) critical thinking is one of the nine **importances**, not one of the thirteen guidelines — the classic mix-up between the two lists; (d) adapting concerns tone and setting.

**5. c** — *Adapt to the Context*: consider the context and adjust accordingly, formal for academic, looser for casual. (a) concerns jargon, pace and enunciation, not register by setting; (b) concerns follow-up questions in an ongoing exchange; (d) concerns opening with context or a preview of main points.

**6. c** — *the nine importances*: Communication Skill Development, Critical Thinking, Expressing Ideas Clearly, Language Proficiency, Interviews and Assessments, Promoting Active Participation, Building Confidence, Cultural and Social Awareness, Enhancing Listening Skills. Handwriting appears nowhere — the whole lesson concerns **spoken** response. (a), (b) and (d) are all genuine headings on the list.

**7. b** — *Speak Clearly and Concisely*: "avoid **unnecessary jargon or overly complex language** that may hinder understanding", and **be mindful of your pace and enunciation**. (a) contradicts *Provide Examples and Evidence*, which demands concrete examples; (c) contradicts *Balance*; (d) contradicts *Manage Time Effectively*, which asks for succinct answers **within** the allotted time.

**8. b** — *AI sample response*: robust ethical frameworks built on **transparency, accountability, and inclusivity**, striking a balance between innovation and ethical responsibility. (a) names commercial values the response never proposes; (c) is the opposite of transparency and inverts the privacy concern, which was named as a **risk** to be addressed, not a principle; (d) names policy instruments from the **gig-economy** sample, not the AI one.

**9. c** — *Conclude Effectively*: wrap up with a **brief conclusion that summarises your main points** and **reinforces the key message**. (a) adds detail rather than closing; (b) is body coverage; (d) is the opening, which previews rather than restates — a conclusion is judged by what it consolidates, not what it announces.

**10. d** — *gig-economy sample*: the response named **updating labour laws**, **portable benefits systems**, and **a culture of lifelong learning**. An outright ban is the opposite of its stance — it explicitly frames the policy task as **balancing the benefits of gig work with protecting workers' rights and social safety nets**, which is *Balance* in action. (a), (b) and (c) are all verbatim from the response.

**11. b** — *Enhancing Listening Skills*: in interactive settings you must **actively listen to others' contributions**, which improves listening and the ability to engage in meaningful conversations. (a) is about comfort in verbal expression; (c) is about practising vocabulary, grammar and pronunciation; (d) is about clarity and concision in your own output. The question tests the one heading on the list that is not about speaking.

**12. c** — *sentence stress*: stressing the verb **say** contrasts the act of saying with other channels — implying, writing, hinting — so the denial narrows to the speech act itself. (a) is stress on **I**; (b) is stress on **didn't**; (d) is stress on **you**. All four are genuine readings of the same seven-word sentence, which is exactly the point the case study makes.

**13.** *The thirteen guidelines, in order*: **Understand the Prompt/Question** (read or listen carefully; identify key components and specific instructions) → **Organise Your Thoughts** (a mental outline or jotted key points) → **Introduction** (concise, giving context or previewing main points, setting the tone) → **Address the Key Points** (respond directly to the main components) → **Provide Examples and Evidence** (specific, concrete, convincing) → **Speak Clearly and Concisely** (no unnecessary jargon; mind pace and enunciation) → **Elaborate and Expand** (sufficient detail for a thorough, insightful answer) → **Stay Relevant** (no unrelated tangents) → **Balance** (different angles or counter arguments where applicable) → **Conclude Effectively** (brief summary reinforcing the key message) → **Adapt to the Context** (formal for academic, looser for casual) → **Practice Active Listening** (attend to follow-up questions and comments) → **Manage Time Effectively** (succinct yet comprehensive within the allotted time). *Why the order*: the introduction has to state the context and preview the main points you intend to discuss, so those points must already exist — which requires having organised your thoughts; and you cannot organise thoughts about a prompt whose key components and specific instructions you have not yet identified. Speaking before either step produces an opening that commits you to a structure you have not chosen and may not match what was asked.

**14.** *Six of the nine importances*: **Communication Skill Development** — practice in articulating thoughts coherently and expressing ideas effectively; **Critical Thinking** — thoughtful prompts require analysing information, considering different perspectives and giving well-reasoned responses; **Expressing Ideas Clearly** — clarity and concision matter in professional settings, academic discussions and everyday conversation; **Language Proficiency** — for language learners, practice in vocabulary, grammar and pronunciation in a contextually relevant manner; **Interviews and Assessments** — in job interviews, academic assessments and standardised tests, prompts evaluate the ability to communicate effectively, think on one's feet and present oneself coherently; **Promoting Active Participation** — prompts draw people into discussions and group settings, fostering engagement and collaboration; (also acceptable: **Building Confidence**, **Cultural and Social Awareness**, **Enhancing Listening Skills**). *The two about contexts rather than personal skill*: **Interviews and Assessments** (the evaluative setting) and **Promoting Active Participation** (the group setting).

**15.** *Definition*: speaking prompts are **questions or statements designed to elicit a spoken response**; they serve as a **stimulus to initiate and guide verbal communication**, are used in **interviews, discussions, language learning and public speaking**, and range from **open-ended questions to more complex scenarios** requiring you to articulate thoughts, share opinions or provide information verbally. *The four-part shape*: **(1) a concise introduction** naming the tension the topic contains — the gig economy has reshaped labour markets, bringing both opportunities and challenges — which satisfies **Introduction**; **(2) both sides with concrete named examples** — freelance and on-demand platforms offer flexibility but bring income volatility and job insecurity, with consequences for income distribution and access to healthcare and retirement benefits — satisfying **Address the Key Points**, **Provide Examples and Evidence** and **Balance**; **(3) proposed solutions or recommendations** — updating labour laws, portable benefits systems, a culture of lifelong learning — satisfying **Elaborate and Expand** and demonstrating the critical thinking the analyses praised; **(4) a brief conclusion** consolidating the key message, satisfying **Conclude Effectively**. Throughout, **Stay Relevant**, **Speak Clearly and Concisely**, **Adapt to the Context** and **Manage Time Effectively** govern delivery rather than structure.
