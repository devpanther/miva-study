# GST_112 — Week 9 Summary

*Nigerian Peoples and Culture · Week 9 (2 – 8 Nov 2026) · Topic: Reorientation Strategies*

## The 8 most examinable things this week

1. **Definition.** **Reorientation strategies refer to government authorities, institutions, or programmes aimed at reshaping or redirecting people's attitudes, beliefs, or behaviours towards a particular purpose or set of ideals.** To promote good change these organisations often participate in **public awareness campaigns, educational programmes, skill development, counselling, and community outreach** — learn those five.
2. **The deck's own list of the strategies, in its order:** **National Orientation Agency (NOA)** · **Operation Feed the Nation** · **Austerity Measures** · **War Against Indiscipline** · **War Against Indiscipline and Corruption** · **Mass Mobilisation for Self-reliance, Social Justice and Economic Recovery (MAMSER)**. Six items. (**WAIC** is listed but given no slide of its own.)
3. **NOA — the two facts that carry the marks:** it is the **successor of the Directorate for Social Mobilisation – MAMSER**, and it was **established by Decree 100 of 1993**. Its **mandate**: to **reorient Nigerians towards embracing positive values, patriotism, national unity, and development**, and to **create and sustain public awareness, understanding, and support for government policies, programmes, and initiatives**.
4. **Operation Feed the Nation (OFN)** — an **agricultural initiative launched in Nigeria in 1976-80**, introduced by the **military government of General Olusegun Obasanjo**, aimed at **addressing the country's food security challenges and reducing dependence on food imports**. Its signature instruction: **urban dwellers were encouraged to garden undeveloped building plots.**
5. **The six programmes under OFN, with their years** — **ADP (1976), ACGS (1977), RBDA (1978), RBS (1978), GR (1980), DFRRI (1986)**. Expect a year-matching question; see the table below.
6. **Austerity measures** = **strict, frugal economic policies used by the government to manage public debt**, typically undertaken **during periods of economic hardship or budget deficits**. **Three waves in Nigeria**: after the **oil boom of the 1970s, during Buhari's military regime**; **Babangida's Structural Adjustment Programme (SAP)**; and **from 2015**, to **plug budget deficit arising from low oil prices and low oil output**.
7. **War Against Indiscipline (WAI)** — a **mass mobilisation programme organised by the military dictatorship of Buhari** with the aim of **correcting social maladjustments**. It promoted **punctuality, respect for queues, environmental cleanliness, and adherence to traffic regulations**. It **ended in September 1985**. Its success and its **utmost criticism** were the same thing: the **high handedness of the WAI Brigade**.
8. **MAMSER** — **Mass Mobilisation for Self-reliance, Social Justice and Economic Recovery**, **launched in 1987 following the recommendation of the Public Bureau headed by Samuel Cookey**. Primary goal: to **mobilise and empower Nigerians to actively participate in nation-building, promote self-reliance, achieve social justice, and drive economic recovery.** It was **eventually phased out** and its functions **absorbed into other government agencies and initiatives** — the body that succeeded it is the **NOA**.

## Austerity: the detail the deck asks back

**Six examples implemented in Nigeria**, in the deck's order: **1. Fiscal consolidation · 2. Reduction of recurrent expenditure · 3. Subsidy reforms · 4. Revenue diversification · 5. Debt management · 6. Rationalisation of subsidies and social programs.**

**Three main types of austerity:** **(i) higher taxes (revenue generation) to fund government spending**; **(ii) revenue generation plus lower government spending** (raising taxes **and** cutting spending); **(iii) lower taxes plus lower government spending.**

**The deck's mid-lesson question**, verbatim: *"Which Nigerian leader introduced austerity measures in response to economic challenges during their respective terms?"* — the answer the deck gives is **B) Babangida**, with the explanation that **while Buhari's military regime in the 1980s also implemented austerity measures, it was specifically under Babangida's Structural Adjustment Programme (SAP) that these measures were further developed and implemented.** The deck's own narrative slide says austerity was introduced **after the oil boom of the 1970s during Buhari's military regime** — so the two statements sit awkwardly together. **In an exam, answer as the deck answers: Babangida / SAP.**

## The conclusion — likely essay prompt

**Reorientation strategies have often failed in Nigeria because of corruption, lack of political will, poor leadership, exemplary leadership, improper planning, etc.** (the deck genuinely lists "exemplary leadership" among the causes of failure — read it as **lack of** exemplary leadership, but reproduce the list as given). **There is a need for a declaration of a state of emergence on reorientating Nigerians on the values of trust, sycophancy, legitimate work and moral conscience.** (Likewise "sycophancy" sits oddly in a list of values; the weekly Summary PDF renders the same conclusion as the values of **trust, hard work, and moral conscience**.)

## Commonly confused

- **MAMSER vs NOA.** **MAMSER (1987)** came **first**; the **NOA (Decree 100 of 1993)** is the **successor of the Directorate for Social Mobilisation – MAMSER**. If asked which body absorbed the other, the **NOA absorbed MAMSER**, never the reverse.
- **MAMSER the programme vs the Directorate for Social Mobilisation.** The deck treats **MAMSER** and the **Directorate for Social Mobilisation** as the **same body under two names** — that is why NOA is called the successor of "the Directorate for Social Mobilisation – MAMSER". Do not count them as two separate agencies.
- **WAI vs WAIC.** **War Against Indiscipline** is **Buhari's** 1984-85 programme that **ended in September 1985**. **War Against Indiscipline and Corruption** is a **separate item on the deck's list** with **no content slide** — if a question names WAIC, it is asking whether you noticed it is a distinct entry, not for a date or a leader.
- **Buhari vs Babangida on austerity.** **Buhari's military regime** introduced austerity **after the oil boom of the 1970s**; **Babangida's Structural Adjustment Programme (SAP)** is where the deck says the measures were **further developed and implemented**, and **Babangida is the answer to the mid-lesson question**. Both names belong to the topic; only Babangida is the deck's answer.
- **Buhari the military ruler vs Buhari the 2015 president.** The **1980s** austerity and **WAI** are the **military regime**. The **from-2015** austerity is described only as "the government" plugging a deficit from **low oil prices and low oil output** — do not attach WAI or the WAI Brigade to it.
- **Decree 100 of 1993 vs 1987 vs 1976-80.** **1993** = **NOA's founding decree**. **1987** = **MAMSER's launch**. **1976-80** = **OFN**. Three numbers, three different bodies; the most common slip is dating NOA to MAMSER's year.
- **OFN's aim vs OFN's sub-programmes.** **OFN itself** is the **umbrella agricultural initiative (1976-80)**. **ADP, ACGS, RBDA, RBS, GR and DFRRI** are the **programmes under it**, and two of them (**GR 1980**, **DFRRI 1986**) are dated at or after OFN's own end — learn the years as the deck lists them rather than assuming they all fall inside 1976-80.
- **ACGS vs ADP vs RBDA vs RBS.** **ADP (1976)** = **Agricultural Development Project**; **ACGS (1977)** = **Agricultural Credit Guarantee Scheme**; **RBDA (1978)** = **River Basin Development Programme**; **RBS (1978)** = **Rural Banking System**; **GR (1980)** = **Green Revolution**; **DFRRI (1986)** = **Directorate for Food, Roads, and Rural Infrastructure**. The two 1978 entries are the trap.
- **The three types of austerity vs the six examples.** The **three types** are about **taxes and spending** (higher taxes; higher taxes plus lower spending; lower taxes plus lower spending). The **six examples** are Nigeria-specific policies (**fiscal consolidation, reduction of recurrent expenditure, subsidy reforms, revenue diversification, debt management, rationalisation of subsidies and social programs**). Do not answer one list when asked for the other.
- **Green Revolution vs Operation Feed the Nation.** In this deck the **Green Revolution (1980)** is a **programme under OFN**, not a rival scheme of a later government. Answer it as the deck frames it.
- **WAI's success vs WAI's criticism.** The deck's point is that they are **the same fact**: the **WAI Brigade** dealt **decisively** with offenders, and that **high handedness** was **the criticism**. A question asking for the criticism wants **high handedness of the WAI Brigade**, not "it failed".
