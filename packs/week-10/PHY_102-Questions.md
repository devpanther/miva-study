## Section B — Short answer (3)

**13.** Define **magnetic flux** and **flux linkage**, giving the formula, the meaning of the angle, and the unit of each. State **Faraday's law** in full, defining every symbol. List the **three** independent ways in which the flux through a circuit can be made to change, giving a physical example of each. Then explain (i) why an emf appears even when the circuit is **open** and no current flows at all, and (ii) exactly what the **minus sign** in the law does and does not mean.

**14.** State **Lenz's law**. Using a bar magnet pushed north-pole-first towards a coil, explain step by step how you determine the direction of the induced current, and state what pole appears on the near face of the coil while the magnet approaches and while it recedes. Then give the **energy argument** in full: where the electrical energy dissipated in the coil comes from, and precisely what would go wrong — as a matter of conservation of energy — if the induced current aided the change instead of opposing it. Finally, explain why a generator becomes **harder to turn** when its external load is switched on.

**15.** A solenoid of **900 turns** is wound over a length of **40 cm**; its cross-sectional area is **8.0 cm²** and its core is air. Find (i) its self-inductance, (ii) the magnitude of the back-emf when the current is reduced uniformly from 4.0 A to zero in 20 ms, and (iii) the energy stored at 4.0 A. A second coil of **300 turns** is then wound tightly over the middle of the first. Find (iv) the mutual inductance of the pair and (v) the emf induced in the second coil during the same current change. Explain why the self-inductance depends on **N²** while the mutual inductance depends on **N₁N₂**, and state what both would become if an iron core of relative permeability 500 were inserted.

## Answers

**1. c** — *Φ = BA cos θ with θ measured from the normal.* If the **plane** of the coil is parallel to B, the **normal** is perpendicular to B, so θ = 90°, cos θ = 0, and **Φ = 0**: no field line threads the loop, they all skim past it edge-on. (a) is BA with the angle ignored altogether, which is also what you get by reading θ as the angle from the plane — the central error of the topic. (b) is BA cos 60°, a guessed angle from a half-remembered rule. (d) quotes the field itself as the flux; 0.50 is a number of **teslas**, and a flux must carry an area factor and be in webers.

**2. d** — *An emf needs a **changing** flux, not motion.* A loop drifting about inside a large uniform field encloses the same B and the same A at every instant, so Φ is constant and **ε = 0** despite the motion. (a) is the standard transformer situation: nothing moves, but the wire's field grows, so Φ grows and an emf appears — this is the counter-example to the belief that motion is required. (b) changes θ continuously; it is an a.c. generator. (c) changes the **area** still inside the field; this is motional emf, ε = BLv. Only in (d) is dΦ/dt zero.

**3. a** — *ε = N ΔΦ/Δt.* ΔΦ = 0.060 − 0.020 = 0.040 Wb, so ε = (50)(0.040)/(0.10) = **20 V**. (b) drops N and gives the emf of a **single** turn, 0.40 V. (c) uses the **final** flux 0.060 Wb instead of the change — the error of forgetting that only differences induce anything. (d) **adds** the two flux values (0.080 Wb) instead of subtracting them, which also happens to be what a student gets who reads a flux *reversal* into a question that has none.

**4. c** — *Lenz's law in pole form: oppose the approach.* The flux through the coil is increasing, so the induced current must produce flux **against** the magnet's, making the near face a **north** pole that repels it. Seen from the magnet, a current with its magnetic moment pointing back at you circulates **anticlockwise** (right-hand rule). (a) makes the coil attract the magnet, which would accelerate it, generating more current, generating more attraction — energy from nothing. (b) invents a "sympathetic" rule with no basis; the induced current's sense is fixed by the **change** in flux, and it reverses when the magnet is withdrawn. (d) is right about the sense but wrong about persistence: when the magnet stops, dΦ/dt = 0 and the current stops instantly, which is precisely the deck's galvanometer returning to zero.

**5. d** — *ε = BLv, with all three mutually perpendicular.* ε = (0.25)(0.40)(3.0) = **0.30 V**. (a) is Lv = 1.2, the field dropped. (b) is BL = 0.10, the speed dropped. (c) is Bv = 0.75, the length dropped. Each wrong option is one missing factor, and the way to catch all three is a unit check: T × m × m/s = V.

**6. d** — *q = N ΔΦ/R, independent of the time taken.* Integrating I = (N/R)|dΦ/dt| over the change gives q = N ΔΦ/R; the rate cancels out. (a) is the intuitive answer and is false: a faster flip gives a larger current for a proportionally shorter time, and the product is unchanged. This is exactly the principle of the ballistic galvanometer. (b) is wrong twice: the flux change does not cancel — flipping through 180° **doubles** it, from +NΦ to −NΦ — and q cannot depend on R alone by dimensions. (c) misuses q = It with ε in place of I and no division by R that survives.

**7. b** — *The emf follows the **slope** of the flux, not its size.* With Φ = BA cos ωt, ε = NBAω sin ωt: the emf peaks where the flux passes through **zero**, i.e. edge-on, plane containing B, where the coil sweeps through field lines fastest. (a) is the face-on position, where the flux is greatest and momentarily **stationary**, so ε = **0** — the single commonest generator error. (c) inverts the ω dependence: ε₀ = NBAω is largest when the coil spins **fastest**. (d) picks the halfway orientation because it feels like a compromise; the emf there is ε₀ sin 45° = 0.71 ε₀, neither maximum nor zero.

**8. b** — *ε = L dI/dt.* ε = (0.25)(8.0) = **2.0 V**. (a) is L/(dI/dt) = 0.031, the division inverted. (c) is ½L dI/dt, a spurious ½ imported from the energy formula U = ½LI², where it genuinely belongs and here does not. (d) is (dI/dt)/L = 32, the same inversion the other way; both (a) and (d) fail a unit check, since H × A/s = V.

**9. a** — *U = ½LI².* U = ½(0.40)(3.0)² = ½(0.40)(9.0) = **1.8 J**. (b) omits the ½ (LI² = 3.6 J). (c) is LI = 1.2, the flux linkage in webers, not an energy. (d) is ½LI = 0.60, the square forgotten. Only (a) has the dimensions of joules; note also that no time appears — energy is stored by the *presence* of the current, whereas the emf needs its *rate of change*.

**10. c** — *V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p, and power is conserved.* V_s = 240 × (100/2000) = **12 V**, a step-**down** of 20. Since V_pI_p = V_sI_s, stepping the voltage down by 20 steps the current **up** by 20. (a) inverts the turns ratio, giving 4800 V from 100 turns — a step-up transformer built with fewer secondary turns, which is impossible. (b) is the free-energy answer: 12 V at the same current would be a twentieth of the power, and if the currents were truly forced equal there would be nothing to stop one building a transformer that stepped voltage up at constant current. (d) gets the voltage right and then lets both quantities fall, which would mean the secondary delivers a twentieth of the primary's power for no stated reason.

**11. c** — *The reciprocity theorem: M₁₂ = M₂₁.* Mutual inductance is a single property of the **pair** of coils and their geometry; it does not matter which coil is driven. For two coaxial windings M = μ₀μᵣN₁N₂A/ℓ, a formula symmetric in the two turn counts, so swapping the roles changes nothing. (a) and (b) each read the turns ratio into M, which is the rule for the **voltage** ratio of a transformer, not for M. (d) invents a condition: M is defined as a ratio, ε₂/(dI₁/dt), and is independent of the size of the current entirely.

**12. b** — *No changing flux, no emf.* A steady primary current gives a steady core flux, so dΦ/dt = 0 in the secondary and V_s = 0 once the switch-on transient has died away. This is why transformers are an a.c.-only device and why d.c. transmission needs entirely different hardware. (a) is false and is the commonest wrong reason offered for a right conclusion: a d.c. current produces a perfectly good magnetic field — it simply produces a **constant** one. (c) confuses a design limit with the principle; a lightly loaded core need not saturate, and the output would still be zero. (d) muddles resistance with reactance: it is true that d.c. draws a dangerously large primary current for exactly this reason, but that is a consequence of the missing back-emf, not the reason the secondary is dead.

**13.** *Concept: flux, flux linkage, Faraday's law, and what the minus sign is.* **Magnetic flux** through a flat surface of area A in a uniform field is **Φ = B·A = BA cos θ**, where **θ is the angle between B and the *normal* to the surface** — not between B and the plane. Its unit is the **weber**, 1 Wb = 1 T·m² = 1 V·s. It is a **scalar**, and its sign depends on which way round you take the normal. **Flux linkage** for a coil of N turns is **NΦ**, also in webers, because each turn is threaded separately and the turns' emfs add in series. **Faraday's law**: **ε = −N dΦ/dt**, where ε is the induced electromotive force in volts, N the number of turns, and dΦ/dt the rate of change of the flux through **one** turn in Wb/s; for a uniform rate, ε = −N ΔΦ/Δt. **The three ways to change Φ = BA cos θ.** (1) **Change B** — move a magnet nearer, or raise the current in a nearby coil; this is how a transformer works. (2) **Change A** — slide a conducting rod along rails so the enclosed area grows, or pull a loop out through the boundary of a field region; this gives ε = BLv. (3) **Change θ** — rotate the coil in a fixed field; this gives ε = NBAω sin ωt and is the a.c. generator. Any combination will do; what is required is only that **Φ changes**, which is why a stationary coil in a rising field has an emf while a coil drifting inside a uniform field has none. **(i) Emf without current.** An emf is **energy given to unit charge by a non-electrostatic agency**, in volts. The changing flux acts on the charges in the wire whether or not they can complete a circuit; in an open coil they simply pile up at the two ends until the electrostatic field they create balances the induced one, and the potential difference between the ends is exactly ε. Current is a *consequence*, given by I = ε/R, and requires a closed path; the emf does not. **(ii) The minus sign** expresses **Lenz's law**: the induced emf drives a current whose own magnetic field **opposes the change** in flux that produced it. It does **not** mean that ε is a negative number, it does **not** mean the induced field always opposes B (when the flux is *falling*, the induced field points the **same** way as B, to maintain it), and it is not a mere bookkeeping artefact — although which way you write the sign does depend on the normal and circulation sense you chose, the physical content, that induction always fights the change, is fixed by conservation of energy.

**14.** *Concept: Lenz's law as conservation of energy.* **Statement.** *The induced current flows in whatever direction its own magnetic field opposes the change in flux that is producing it.* **Applying it to the approaching magnet.** (1) The magnet's north pole faces the coil, so the field through the coil points **away from the magnet**, and as the magnet approaches the flux is **increasing**. (2) The induced current must therefore set up a field **opposing** that increase, i.e. pointing **back towards the magnet** inside the coil. (3) By the right-hand grip rule, that current circulates **anticlockwise as seen from the magnet**. (4) Mechanical check: the near face of the coil is now a **north** pole facing the magnet's north pole, so the coil **repels** the magnet — the force opposes the motion, as it must. On **withdrawal** the flux is now **decreasing**, the induced current reverses, the near face becomes a **south** pole, and the coil **attracts** the magnet, again opposing the motion. This is precisely the deck's galvanometer swinging one way on entry and the other on exit, and reading zero whenever the magnet is held still. **The energy argument.** The coil dissipates I²R as heat. That energy is not created by the magnetic field, which does no net work on the charges; it is supplied by **the person pushing the magnet**, who must work against the repulsion, and by the kinetic energy of the magnet if nobody is pushing (which is why a magnet dropped down a copper tube falls slowly and arrives warm). **If the induced current aided the change**, the coil would attract the approaching magnet: the magnet would speed up, the flux would change faster, the current and the attraction would grow, and the system would deliver unlimited kinetic energy *and* unlimited heat with no source at all. That is a perpetual-motion machine of the first kind, so Lenz's law is not an independent empirical rule but **the first law of thermodynamics written for induction**. **The loaded generator.** With the external circuit open, no current flows in the armature, so there is no force on it and the shaft turns easily against friction alone. Close the switch and a current I flows in the coils, which sit in the machine's own field and therefore feel a torque τ = NBAI opposing the rotation. The operator must now supply mechanical power equal to the electrical power delivered plus the losses — turn on a heavy load and the engine driving the generator audibly labours. Electrical energy is always paid for in mechanical work; Lenz's law is the mechanism that collects the payment.

**15.** *Concept: L = μ₀N²A/ℓ, ε = L dI/dt, U = ½LI², M = μ₀N₁N₂A/ℓ, and the role of the core.* **(i) Self-inductance.** A = 8.0 cm² = 8.0×10⁻⁴ m², ℓ = 0.40 m, N = 900. L = μ₀N²A/ℓ = (4π×10⁻⁷)(900²)(8.0×10⁻⁴)/(0.40) = (1.2566×10⁻⁶)(8.10×10⁵)(8.0×10⁻⁴)/0.40 = **2.04×10⁻³ H (2.04 mH)**. **(ii) Back-emf.** dI/dt = 4.0/0.020 = **200 A/s**, so |ε| = L dI/dt = (2.04×10⁻³)(200) = **0.41 V**. Its sense opposes the *decrease*, i.e. it acts to keep the current flowing — this is the emf that arcs across an opening switch. **(iii) Energy stored.** U = ½LI² = ½(2.04×10⁻³)(4.0)² = ½(2.04×10⁻³)(16) = **1.6×10⁻² J (16 mJ)**, released into the circuit as the current dies. **(iv) Mutual inductance.** M = μ₀N₁N₂A/ℓ = (1.2566×10⁻⁶)(900)(300)(8.0×10⁻⁴)/(0.40) = **6.79×10⁻⁴ H (0.68 mH)**. Shortcut worth knowing: for perfect coupling M/L₁ = N₂/N₁ = 300/900 = 1/3, and 2.04×10⁻³/3 = 6.79×10⁻⁴ H ✔. **(v) Emf in the second coil.** ε₂ = M dI₁/dt = (6.79×10⁻⁴)(200) = **0.136 V**, which is likewise one third of the 0.41 V in the primary, as the turns ratio demands. **Why N² and why N₁N₂.** The field inside the solenoid is B = μ₀(N₁/ℓ)I, so the flux through one turn is already proportional to **N₁**; the flux *linkage* is that flux multiplied by the number of turns it threads. For **self**-inductance the flux it threads is the same winding, so a second factor of N₁ appears and **L ∝ N₁²**. For **mutual** inductance the flux is produced by winding 1 (one factor of N₁) and linked by winding 2 (one factor of N₂), so **M ∝ N₁N₂**. The reciprocity M₁₂ = M₂₁ is visible immediately in the symmetry of that product. **With an iron core of μᵣ = 500**, every flux is multiplied by 500 with the geometry untouched, so **L = 500 × 2.04 mH = 1.02 H**, **M = 500 × 0.68 mH = 0.34 H**, and the back-emf in (ii) becomes **204 V** from a 4 A current being switched off in 20 ms — a vivid demonstration of why inductive circuits need snubbers, and of why a transformer is built on iron rather than air.
