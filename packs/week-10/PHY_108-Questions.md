## Short answer

**13.** State the tangent law and explain, with a diagram in words, why a compass needle placed in two perpendicular horizontal fields settles so that B = B_H tan θ. Then list the apparatus for this experiment and say what the metre stick and the compass needle are each there for. Why does no current flow in the coil?

**14.** Starting from the fields of the two poles, derive the axial (end-on) field of a bar magnet of pole strength m and half-length L at a distance d from its centre, and combine it with the tangent law to obtain the equation of the straight line that is plotted. State what is plotted against what, what the gradient equals, and what the intercept should be.

**15.** At d = 30 cm the eight recorded angles are 44, 44, 43, 43, 45, 45, 44 and 44 degrees; at d = 50 cm they are 12, 12, 13, 13, 12, 12, 11 and 11 degrees. The magnet is 10 cm long. Taking B_H = 40 × 10⁻⁶ T and μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A, find the gradient of the graph and hence the magnetic moment and pole strength of the magnet. Then give three precautions and three sources of error, saying in each case what the precaution protects or what the error does to the result.

## Answers

**1. B** — *the tangent law*: the needle sits in the Earth's horizontal component B_H and in the magnet's field B at right angles to it, and turns until the two torques balance, mB_H sin θ = mB cos θ, i.e. **B = B_H tan θ**. Option A is the assumption the law replaces — the deflection is proportional to the *tangent*, not to the angle, and the two agree only for very small θ. Option C inverts the galvanometer's current relation, which is tan θ **∝** I, not inversely. Option D uses the **total** field instead of its **horizontal component**; the vertical component is taken up by the pivot and plays no part, which is why B_H and not B is what appears.

**2. C** — *units*: magnetic moment is **A·m²** (the deck's summary sheet states this explicitly, "the ampere-metre squared, reflecting the strength and spatial extent of the moment"); pole strength, being M divided by a length, is **A·m**. Option A swaps them — the commonest single mark lost on this experiment. Option B gives units of field and of current, neither of which is a property of the magnet. Option D makes pole strength a field.

**3. A** — *M = pole strength × length*: with the poles separated by the full length 2L, **M = m × 2L = 2mL**, exactly as the deck's theory slide states. Option B uses the half-length, i.e. takes L for the whole magnet — the error that follows from misreading which of L and 2L the deck means. Option C inverts the relation and would give a moment that *grows* as the magnet is shortened. Option D imports the 4π from μ₀/4π, which belongs to the field expression, not to the definition of M.

**4. D** — *why east–west*: the tangent law only applies when the deflecting field is at **right angles** to B_H. B_H points north, so the magnet's axis — and therefore its axial field at the needle — must point east–west. This is the **end-on (Tan A) position**. Option A is a real precaution about storing magnets, but has nothing to do with the geometry of the measurement. Option B confuses this experiment with the galvanometer's use as an ammeter, where the *coil* is set in the magnetic meridian; here no current flows. Option C is true of any orientation of the stick and so explains nothing. ⚠ *Note for marking*: the deck never states an orientation for the metre stick at all. The east–west setting is required by the physics and is stated in the Week 10 summary's source note, but it is not on any slide, so a marker working strictly from the deck may not recognise it.

**5. C** — *linearising the working equation*: tan θ = (μ₀/4π)(2M/B_H) · d/(d² − L²)² is of the form y = sx with no constant term, so plotting tan θ_AV against **d/(d² − L²)²** gives a straight line through the origin. Option A is a **curve** — tan θ falls roughly as 1/d³, and no gradient drawn on it means anything. Option B plots the raw angle, not its tangent, and against the wrong variable. Option D is the **reciprocal** of most of the correct abscissa, so it curves the other way. ⚠ *Note for marking*: the deck's Data Analysis slide instructs "Plot the graph of tan θAV against [d2 / (d2 − L2)2]", i.e. with **d² on top**. The deck's own five rows disprove that — tan θ_AV ÷ [d/(d² − L²)²] is constant to 3 % while tan θ_AV ÷ [d²/(d² − L²)²] drifts by 58 % — so this set follows the physics, as the Week 10 summary does, and flags the disagreement.

**6. B** — *evaluating the abscissa*: the magnet is 10 cm long, so **L = 0.05 m** (half-length) and d = 0.40 m. d² − L² = 0.1600 − 0.0025 = **0.1575 m²**, so (d² − L²)² = 0.02481 m⁴ and d/(d² − L²)² = 0.40/0.02481 = **16.1 m⁻³**. Option A, 6.45, is d²/(d² − L²)² — the deck's own misprinted abscissa, with d² on top instead of d. Option C, 17.8, takes **L = 0.10 m**, i.e. uses the full length where the half-length is wanted. Option D, 15.6, is the point-dipole abscissa **1/d³**, which drops the (d² − L²) correction; it is only 3 % adrift here but 6 % at d = 30 cm, and the whole point of keeping L in the formula is that the magnet is not far away.

**7. A** — *average the angles, then take the tangent*: θ_AV = (32+32+30+30+32+32+31+31)/8 = 250/8 = **31.25°**, and tan 31.25° = **0.607**. Option B is tan of 32°, i.e. just the most frequent reading rather than the mean of all eight. Option C is tan 31° — the mean rounded down to a whole degree, which throws away the quarter-degree the averaging bought. Option D is tan 30°, the smallest reading in the set.

**8. D** — *what each repetition removes*: reversing the polarity swaps the direction of the deflection, so a constant zero error of the scale, and any small residual field, add to one reading and subtract from the other and cancel in the mean. Reading the **two ends of the needle** removes eccentricity of the pivot, and repeating on the **other side** of the galvanometer removes the effect of the magnet not being exactly centred. Option A is wrong — reversal changes the *sense* of the deflection, not its size. Option B is a misconception: reversing an external magnet does not demagnetise the needle. Option C confuses a check on the apparatus with a source of measurement error.

**9. C** — *from gradient to moment*: s = (μ₀/4π)(2M/B_H), so **M = 2πB_H s/μ₀ = s · B_H × 5 × 10⁶** = 0.0243 × 40 × 10⁻⁶ × 5 × 10⁶ = **4.9 A·m²**. Option B, 2.4, drops the factor 2 that comes from the *2*Md in the numerator of the axial field. Option A, 6.1 × 10⁻⁶, is what you get by taking the deck's "H₀ = 40 × 10⁻⁶ **A/m**" literally and writing M = 2πH₀s = 2π × 40 × 10⁻⁶ × 0.0243; the number 40 × 10⁻⁶ is the Earth's horizontal **flux density in tesla**, and the giveaway is that no bar magnet has a moment of a microampere-metre-squared. Option D, 49, is the **pole strength** in A·m, the answer to the next question.

**10. D** — *pole strength*: **m = M/2L**, and 2L is the **full length** of the magnet, 0.10 m, so m = 4.9/0.10 = **49 A·m**. Option A divides by the **half**-length 0.05 m, doubling the answer — the same L-versus-2L trap as question 3. Option B multiplies by 0.10 instead of dividing. Option C divides by 0.01, a slip of one power of ten from working in centimetres.

**11. A** — *what an intercept means*: the working equation has **no constant term**, so a genuine intercept can only come from a field or a zero that is present when the magnet's contribution is not — a misaligned stick (so B is no longer perpendicular to B_H), a needle not zeroed, or stray iron. Option B produces **scatter about** the line, not a shift of it. Option C rescales the **gradient**'s interpretation only: an error in B_H changes the M you calculate but cannot move the plotted points at all, since B_H appears nowhere in either axis. Option D changes L, which distorts the **spacing** of the x-values and bends the line slightly; it does not lift it bodily off the origin.

**12. B** — *protecting perpendicularity*: the tangent law needs the magnet's field at right angles to B_H, and the practical check is that the needle rests along north–south reading zero while the stick runs east–west. Options A, C and D are all genuine precautions but defend different things: **A** removes eccentricity, zero error and off-centre placement; **C** protects the **distance** d that goes into the abscissa; **D** protects the **reading** of the angle. As in every practical question of this shape, all four are real — the question is which condition each one defends. ⚠ As at question 4, the east–west orientation is required by the physics and is flagged in the Week 10 summary, but the deck itself never states it.

**13.** *The tangent law, the apparatus, and why the coil is idle.* **Statement**: when a freely pivoted magnetic needle is acted on by two mutually perpendicular horizontal fields — the Earth's horizontal component **B_H** and a deflecting field **B** — the needle settles along their resultant, and **B = B_H tan θ**, where θ is measured from the needle's undeflected position. **Why**: the needle is itself a small dipole of pole strength m′. B_H exerts a restoring torque m′B_H sin θ tending to pull it back to north; B exerts a deflecting torque m′B cos θ. At rest the two are equal, m′B_H sin θ = m′B cos θ, so B/B_H = sin θ/cos θ = tan θ. The needle's own strength m′ cancels, which is why the instrument needs no calibration of the needle. Geometrically: the needle lies along the diagonal of the rectangle whose sides are B_H (north) and B (east), and the tangent of the angle that diagonal makes with north is the ratio of the sides. **Apparatus**: metre stick, tangent galvanometer (coil, pivoted compass needle, circular degree scale) and bar magnet, in the Praxilabs virtual laboratory. The **metre stick** defines the straight line along which the magnet slides and along which d is measured, and it must lie east–west so that the magnet's axial field reaches the needle at right angles to B_H (an orientation the physics requires but the deck does not state). The **compass needle** is the actual instrument: it is what responds to the two fields and reads out the ratio B/B_H as an angle. **No current flows** because in this experiment the deflecting field is supplied by the **bar magnet**, not by the coil — the galvanometer is being used simply as a mounted, calibrated compass. Its usual role, in which the coil carries a current I and tan θ ∝ I, is the same tangent law with a different source of B.

**14.** *Derivation and the graph.* Put the point on the axis of the magnet, a distance **d from its centre**. The north pole (strength +m) is then a distance (d + L) away and the south pole (−m) a distance (d − L) away, where **2L is the length of the magnet**. Each pole gives an inverse-square field along the axis, so the resultant, directed along the axis, is

**B = (μ₀/4π) m [1/(d − L)² − 1/(d + L)²]**.

Putting the bracket over a common denominator, (d + L)² − (d − L)² = 4Ld and (d − L)²(d + L)² = (d² − L²)², so

**B = (μ₀/4π) · m·4Ld/(d² − L²)² = (μ₀/4π) · 2Md/(d² − L²)²**, using **M = 2mL**.

(For d ≫ L this reduces to the point-dipole result B ≈ (μ₀/4π)·2M/d³, but the experiment works at d as small as six times L and keeps the exact form.) Now apply the tangent law, **B = B_H tan θ**:

**tan θ = (μ₀/4π)·(2M/B_H)·d/(d² − L²)²**.

**What is plotted**: **tan θ_AV on the y-axis against d/(d² − L²)² on the x-axis**, where θ_AV is the mean of the eight angles recorded at that distance. (The deck's slide prints d² in the numerator of the abscissa; its own data show that to be an error, and this derivation gives d.) **Gradient**: **s = (μ₀/4π)(2M/B_H) = μ₀M/(2πB_H)**, with units m³, so that **M = 2πB_H s/μ₀ = s·B_H × 5 × 10⁶** and then **m = M/2L**. **Intercept**: the equation has no constant term, so the line must pass **through the origin**; a positive intercept signals a misaligned stick, an unzeroed needle or stray magnetic material rather than a fault in the magnet.

**15. s = 0.0243 m³, M = 4.9 A·m², m = 49 A·m** — *the calculation, with precautions and errors.* **Angles**: at d = 30 cm, θ_AV = (44+44+43+43+45+45+44+44)/8 = 352/8 = **44.00°**, tan θ_AV = **0.9657**. At d = 50 cm, θ_AV = (12+12+13+13+12+12+11+11)/8 = 96/8 = **12.00°**, tan θ_AV = **0.2126**. **Abscissae**, with **L = 0.05 m** (half of the 10 cm magnet): at d = 0.30 m, d² − L² = 0.0900 − 0.0025 = 0.0875 m², squared 0.007656 m⁴, so x = 0.30/0.007656 = **39.18 m⁻³**; at d = 0.50 m, d² − L² = 0.2500 − 0.0025 = 0.2475 m², squared 0.06126 m⁴, so x = 0.50/0.06126 = **8.16 m⁻³**. **Gradient**: s = (0.9657 − 0.2126)/(39.18 − 8.16) = 0.7531/31.02 = **0.0243 m³**. **Moment**: M = 2πB_H s/μ₀ = 0.0243 × 40 × 10⁻⁶ × 5 × 10⁶ = **4.9 A·m²**. **Pole strength**: m = M/2L = 4.9/0.10 = **49 A·m**. **Precautions** (any three): lay the metre stick **exactly east–west** and check the needle reads zero before the magnet is brought up — this protects the perpendicularity that the tangent law requires and keeps the line through the origin; measure **d from the centre of the magnet to the needle pivot**, not from the nearest pole — d in the formula is a centre-to-centre distance; **remove all iron, other magnets and current-carrying wires** from the bench and use a non-magnetic rule — stray fields add a constant term the theory does not contain; take **all eight readings** at every distance, since each of the three repetitions cancels a different error (needle eccentricity, scale zero, off-centre magnet); keep the magnet **horizontal and level with the needle** with its axis through the pivot; work where possible in the **30°–60°** band, where tan θ is least sensitive to a half-degree misreading; let the needle **settle** and read it with the eye vertically above the scale. **Sources of error** (any three, with effect): **B_H assumed to be the nominal 40 × 10⁻⁶ T** rather than the local value — M is directly proportional to B_H, so this transfers straight into the answer and is usually the largest uncertainty; **the effective magnetic length is about 0.84 of the geometric length**, because the poles lie inside the ends, so L is systematically overstated and both M and m are wrong; **angles read only to the nearest degree** give a ±0.5° quantisation worth about 4 % in tan θ at 12° and 1 % at 44°, which is exactly the scatter seen when the five ratios tan θ_AV/x are compared; **the stick not truly east–west, or stray iron nearby**, breaks the tangent law and puts an intercept on the graph; **using the point-dipole form 2M/d³** in place of 2Md/(d² − L²)² is about 6 % out at d = 30 cm; **parallax and an eccentric pivot** scatter the points, though averaging the two ends largely removes them; and **induced magnetism** — the Earth's field and the needle slightly re-magnetising the bar and vice versa — means the pole strength is not perfectly constant through the run. Note also that the deck **never states the length of the bar magnet**; 2L = 10 cm is the assumption the pack works with, and it is flagged as such in the summary.
