# Monday — GST_112 fast-hour check

*Popular culture, the four waves of Nigerian music, and the #EndSARS crisis.*
*5 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 5.*

**1.** A Nigerian artist blends reggae and R&B into his sound, is signed by an international label, and is criticised for sounding more American than Nigerian; the labels later leave. Which wave of Nigerian popular music is this?
A. First Wave, 1950-67
B. Second Wave, 1967-80
C. Third Wave, 1980-90
D. Fourth Wave, 1992 to present

**2.** Which of the following statements about the genres of the Second Wave (1967-80) is correct?
A. Fuji emerged as a fusion of Apala and Ajisari and was mostly for the lower class
B. Juju, played by Ebenezer Obey and King Sunny Ade, was a song for the lower class
C. In Ghana, Highlife had been feel-good upper-class music before Nigeria borrowed it
D. Afrobeat became mainstream through Salawa Abeni

**3.** Put these #EndSARS events in the order they happened: (A) the Nigerian Army opens fire on protesters at the Lekki toll gate; (B) #EndSARS begins as a hashtag on Twitter; (C) a video of a police officer shooting an unarmed civilian sparks the crisis; (D) a record 10 million new voters register for the 2023 elections.
A. C, B, A, D
B. B, C, A, D
C. B, A, C, D
D. B, C, D, A

**4.** Which of the following statements about the 2023 elections is correct?
A. Voter turnout was 76%, the highest since independence
B. Only 40% of registered voters obtained their Permanent Voter Cards
C. Young people made up 29% of newly registered voters
D. Voter turnout was 29%, the lowest in Nigeria's six decades of independence

**5.** The term popular culture was coined in the mid-19th century. What does it refer to?
A. The official culture of the state and the governing classes
B. The cultural traditions of the people, in contrast to the official culture of the state or governing classes
C. Music that requires guided listening by a trained audience
D. Cultural products created only by the middle class

---

## Answers

**1. C** — *Identifying a wave of Nigerian popular music.* The Third Wave (1980-90) is when international labels arrived (Island Records signed Sunny Ade, Virgin signed Ebenezer Obey), artists infused reggae, calypso, rap and R&B, and the music dwindled as the labels left and it sounded more American than Nigerian. The Fourth Wave continued the American-sounding quest but is the rebuilding period with groups like Plantashun Boiz and Psquare; the First Wave was deeply traditional with Ghanaian Highlife and calypso influences; the Second Wave was Highlife's revival, Fela's Afrobeat, Juju and Fuji.

**2. A** — *Second Wave genres and their class audiences.* Fuji emerged as a fusion of Apala and Ajisari, drew major influences from Islam and was mostly for the lower class, with Sikiru Ayinde Barrister and Ayinla Kollington. Juju was a song for the upper class, not the lower. The Highlife point is reversed: in Ghana it addressed social problems, and it was in Nigeria that it became feel-good upper-class music. Afrobeat became mainstream through Fela; Salawa Abeni is the Waka star of the Third Wave.

**3. B** — *Chronology of the #EndSARS crisis.* The hashtag began on Twitter in 2017 (B); the immediate cause of the October 2020 crisis was the video of a police officer targeting an unarmed civilian (C); the three-week protest ended with the army opening fire at the Lekki toll gate on 20 October 2020 (A); the movement then produced a record 10 million new registered voters for the 2023 elections (D). The first option puts the video before the hashtag, the third puts Lekki before the shooting that triggered the protests, and the fourth puts voter registration before Lekki, when it was the Lekki incident that sent the youth to the polls.

**4. D** — *Youth participation and turnout in the 2023 elections.* Despite huge youth attendance, countrywide turnout was 29%, and no election in Nigeria's six decades of independence has had a lower participation rate. The 76% figure is the share of newly registered voters who were young people, and 40% of those registered as students. Of the 93.4 million registered voters, 87.2 million obtained their Permanent Voter Cards, so the PVC figure is far above 40%.

**5. B** — *Definition of popular culture.* Popular culture refers to the cultural traditions of the people, in contrast to the official culture of the state or governing classes; more broadly it is the traditions and material culture of a society, and in the modern West the music, art, film, television and other products consumed by most of the population. The first option is the opposite of the definition. Popular music is receptive precisely because it does not require guided listening. The middle class created by the Industrial Revolution is where scholars trace the origins of popular culture, but the culture is that of the people at large, not of one class.
