# Wednesday — GST_122 fast-hour check

*Inductive and deductive reasoning: definitions, named forms, and valid versus sound.*
*5 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 5.*

**1.** For ten years in a row, a shop's sales have risen every December. The manager concludes that sales will probably rise this December too. Which type of reasoning is this?
A. Deductive reasoning
B. Inductive reasoning
C. Modus ponens
D. Categorical syllogism

**2.** 'If the fuse is blown, the light is off. The fuse is blown. Therefore, the light is off.' Which named form of deductive argument is this?
A. Modus tollens
B. Categorical syllogism
C. Modus ponens
D. Statistical reasoning

**3.** 'All birds can fly. Penguins are birds. Therefore, penguins can fly.' The conclusion follows logically from the premises, but the first premise is false. How should the argument be described?
A. Valid but not sound
B. Sound but not valid
C. Both valid and sound
D. Neither valid nor sound

**4.** After watching many students carry umbrellas on rainy days, Ada states that 'most students carry umbrellas when it rains.' Which type of inductive reasoning is this?
A. Analogical reasoning
B. Statistical reasoning
C. Modus tollens
D. Generalisation

**5.** Which form of reasoning is defined as one in which the conclusion is necessarily true if the premises are true?
A. Inductive reasoning
B. Deductive reasoning
C. Analogical reasoning
D. Abductive reasoning

---

## Answers

**1. B** — *Classifying an argument as inductive or deductive.* Inductive reasoning moves from specific observations or past experiences to a conclusion that is likely or probable but not certain; a prediction from a repeated pattern is exactly this.

Deductive reasoning gives a conclusion that is necessarily true if the premises are true; modus ponens and the categorical syllogism are particular deductive forms, and nothing here is certain.

**2. C** — *Naming a deductive argument form.* Modus ponens affirms the antecedent, the 'if' part of a conditional, and concludes the consequent: if A then B; A; therefore B.

Modus tollens denies the consequent (not B, therefore not A); a categorical syllogism links classes with 'all' statements rather than a conditional; statistical reasoning is a type of inductive reasoning.

**3. A** — *Valid versus sound deductive arguments.* A deductive argument is valid if the conclusion follows logically from the premises, and sound if it is valid and its premises are true. The form here is correct, so it is valid, but a false premise makes it unsound.

'Sound but not valid' is impossible because soundness includes validity; 'both' ignores the false premise; 'neither' ignores that the logic is correct.

**4. D** — *Types of inductive reasoning.* Generalisation makes a broad statement about a group or category based on specific observations, as in observing people wearing sunglasses on sunny days and concluding that many people do.

Analogical reasoning concludes about one thing from its similarity to another; statistical reasoning draws on statistical data or probability figures; modus tollens is a deductive form.

**5. B** — *Definition of deductive reasoning.* Deductive reasoning is a form of reasoning in which the conclusion is necessarily true if the premises are true; it is often used in mathematics and formal logic.

Inductive reasoning gives a conclusion that is likely or probable but not certain; analogical and abductive reasoning are types of inductive reasoning.
