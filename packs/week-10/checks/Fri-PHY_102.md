# Friday — PHY_102 nightly check

*Numerical induction work.*
*Sit cold, notes closed, 15 minutes. 8 multiple choice, 4 written. Score out of 12.*

**1.** A circular coil of 150 turns has a diameter of 8.0 cm. Its plane is perpendicular to a uniform field of 0.50 T. What is the flux linkage NΦ of the coil?
A. 1.5 Wb
B. 0.38 Wb
C. 2.5 × 10⁻³ Wb
D. 0 Wb

**2.** A 200-turn coil of area 3.0 × 10⁻³ m² and total resistance 4.0 Ω has its plane perpendicular to a uniform field. The field falls from 0.80 T to 0.20 T in 0.50 s. How much charge flows round the circuit during the change?
A. 0.18 C
B. 4.5 × 10⁻⁴ C
C. 0.030 C
D. 0.090 C

**3.** A rod of length 0.40 m slides at 3.0 m/s on rails in a field of 0.50 T perpendicular to the plane of the circuit. The total resistance is 0.20 Ω. What is the magnitude of the magnetic force on the rod?
A. 0.60 N
B. 0.12 N
C. 1.2 N
D. 1.5 N

**4.** A coil of 100 turns and area 80 cm² rotates at 25 revolutions per second in a uniform field of 0.12 T, about an axis perpendicular to the field. What is the peak emf?
A. 2.4 V
B. 11 V
C. 15 V
D. 1.5 × 10³ V

**5.** An air-cored solenoid has a self-inductance of 2.0 mH. Its number of turns is doubled with the length and area unchanged, and an iron core of relative permeability 200 is inserted. What is the new inductance?
A. 0.80 H
B. 8.0 mH
C. 0.40 H
D. 1.6 H

**6.** A solenoid of 600 turns, length 0.25 m and cross-sectional area 4.0 cm² has a 200-turn coil wound tightly over its middle. The core is air, μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A. What is the mutual inductance of the pair?
A. 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ H
B. 7.2 × 10⁻⁴ H
C. 8.0 × 10⁻⁵ H
D. 2.4 × 10⁻² H

**7.** An ideal transformer steps 240 V a.c. down to 12 V. The secondary drives a lamp of resistance 4.0 Ω. What current does the primary draw?
A. 3.0 A
B. 0.15 A
C. 60 A
D. 0.60 A

**8.** The rotation rate of a generator coil in a fixed field is doubled. What happens to the peak emf and to the frequency of the output?
A. Peak emf doubles; frequency unchanged
B. Both quadruple
C. Both double
D. Peak emf quadruples; frequency doubles

**9. (show your working)** A 60-turn circular coil, radius 5.0 cm, resistance 3.0 Ω, lies face-on to a uniform field into the page that rises uniformly from zero to 0.90 T in 0.30 s. Calculate the emf and the current, and state whether the current is clockwise or anticlockwise as seen by the reader. Show your working.

**10. (show your working)** A 0.50 m rod slides at a steady 8.0 m/s on frictionless rails in a 0.30 T field perpendicular to the circuit; total resistance 0.60 Ω. Calculate the emf, the current and the mechanical power needed to keep the rod moving, and show that it equals the electrical power dissipated. Show your working.

**11. (show your working)** An air-cored solenoid has 800 turns, length 0.20 m and area 3.0 cm²; μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A. Calculate (a) its self-inductance, (b) the back-emf when the current rises uniformly from zero to 3.0 A in 15 ms, (c) the energy stored at 3.0 A. Show your working.

**12. (show your working)** A transformer is connected by mistake to a 12 V d.c. supply instead of 12 V a.c. Explain why the secondary gives no steady output, and why the primary winding is likely to overheat.

## Answers

**1. B** — *Flux linkage of a circular coil.* Radius r = 4.0 cm = 0.040 m, so A = πr² = π(0.040)² = 5.03 × 10⁻³ m². Plane perpendicular to B means θ = 0, so Φ = BA = (0.50)(5.03 × 10⁻³) = 2.51 × 10⁻³ Wb per turn, and NΦ = 150 × 2.51 × 10⁻³ = 0.377 Wb, about 0.38 Wb.

1.5 Wb uses the diameter 0.080 m as the radius, four times too much area; 2.5 × 10⁻³ Wb is the flux through one turn with N forgotten; 0 Wb misreads 'plane perpendicular to B' as θ = 90°, when the normal is in fact along B.

**2. D** — *Induced charge through a coil.* q = N ΔΦ/R, and the time does not enter. ΔΦ = ΔB × A = (0.80 − 0.20)(3.0 × 10⁻³) = 1.8 × 10⁻³ Wb; N ΔΦ = 0.36 Wb; q = 0.36/4.0 = 0.090 C.

0.18 C is the current (ε = 0.72 V, I = 0.18 A) reported as a charge, the 0.50 s divided in and never multiplied back out; 4.5 × 10⁻⁴ C forgets N; 0.030 C uses the final field 0.20 T instead of the change 0.60 T.

**3. A** — *Retarding force on a rod moving in a field.* Chain three formulas: ε = BLv = (0.50)(0.40)(3.0) = 0.60 V; I = ε/R = 0.60/0.20 = 3.0 A; F = BIL = (0.50)(3.0)(0.40) = 0.60 N, directed against the motion. Equivalently F = B²L²v/R = (0.25)(0.16)(3.0)/0.20 = 0.60 N.

0.12 N uses the emf 0.60 V as if it were the current, never dividing by R; 1.2 N is BL²v/R, with B not squared; 1.5 N is B²Lv/R, with L not squared.

**4. C** — *Peak emf of a rotating coil.* ε₀ = NBAω. A = 80 cm² = 80 × 10⁻⁴ = 8.0 × 10⁻³ m²; ω = 2πf = 2π(25) = 157 rad/s. ε₀ = (100)(0.12)(8.0 × 10⁻³)(157) = 15.1 V, about 15 V.

2.4 V uses f = 25 in place of ω, missing the factor 2π; 11 V is the rms value ε₀/√2, not the peak; 1.5 × 10³ V converts cm² with 10⁻² instead of 10⁻⁴.

**5. D** — *Scaling of solenoid inductance with turns and core.* L = μ₀μᵣN²A/ℓ. Doubling N multiplies L by 2² = 4; the core multiplies it by μᵣ = 200. New L = 2.0 mH × 4 × 200 = 1600 mH = 1.6 H.

0.80 H scales with N instead of N², a factor 2 for the turns; 8.0 mH applies the N² factor but forgets the core; 0.40 H applies the core but forgets the extra turns.

**6. A** — *Mutual inductance of coaxial coils.* M = μ₀N₁N₂A/ℓ with A = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ m²: M = (4π × 10⁻⁷)(600)(200)(4.0 × 10⁻⁴)/(0.25) = (1.257 × 10⁻⁶)(1.2 × 10⁵)(1.6 × 10⁻³) = 2.41 × 10⁻⁴ H, about 0.24 mH.

7.2 × 10⁻⁴ H uses 600² in place of 600 × 200 and is the self-inductance of the solenoid; 8.0 × 10⁻⁵ H uses 200²; 2.4 × 10⁻² H converts cm² with 10⁻² instead of 10⁻⁴.

**7. B** — *Primary current of a loaded transformer.* Secondary current I_s = V_s/R = 12/4.0 = 3.0 A; secondary power = V_sI_s = 36 W. Ideal, so primary power is the same: I_p = 36/240 = 0.15 A. Check: the voltage is stepped down by 20, so the current is stepped up by 20, and 3.0/20 = 0.15 A.

3.0 A assumes the same current on both sides, which would mean the primary takes 720 W to deliver 36 W; 60 A inverts the ratio and multiplies 3.0 A by 20; 0.60 A divides 12 V by the turns ratio 20, a voltage treated as a current.

**8. C** — *Dependence of generator output on rotation rate.* ε₀ = NBAω and f = ω/2π. Doubling the rotation rate doubles ω, so the peak emf doubles and the output frequency doubles, one cycle per revolution.

'Frequency unchanged' forgets that each revolution is one cycle; 'both quadruple' squares a linear dependence; 'peak emf quadruples' confuses the emf with the power into a fixed resistor, which does go up four-fold since P ∝ ε₀².

**9.** *Faraday's law chained with Ohm's law and Lenz's law.* A = πr² = π(0.050)² = 7.85 × 10⁻³ m². ΔΦ = ΔB × A = (0.90)(7.85 × 10⁻³) = 7.07 × 10⁻³ Wb per turn; N ΔΦ = 60 × 7.07 × 10⁻³ = 0.424 Wb. ε = N ΔΦ/Δt = 0.424/0.30 = 1.41 V. I = ε/R = 1.41/3.0 = 0.47 A. Direction: the into-page flux is increasing, so the induced current must make an out-of-page field inside the loop; by the right-hand grip rule that is an anticlockwise current as seen by the reader.

Final answer: ε = 1.4 V (accept 1.3 to 1.5), I = 0.47 A (accept 0.45 to 0.49), anticlockwise. A correct answer converts the radius to metres, multiplies by N, divides by the time, applies Ohm's law, and gives anticlockwise with the reason that the induced field opposes the growth of into-page flux. Clockwise is the answer for a decreasing field and is wrong here; 0.024 V forgot N.

**10.** *Power audit of a moving rod.* ε = BLv = (0.30)(0.50)(8.0) = 1.2 V. I = ε/R = 1.2/0.60 = 2.0 A. The retarding force is F = BIL = (0.30)(2.0)(0.50) = 0.30 N, so the mechanical power needed is P = Fv = (0.30)(8.0) = 2.4 W. Electrical power dissipated: I²R = (2.0)²(0.60) = 2.4 W, and εI = (1.2)(2.0) = 2.4 W. The two agree: the hand's work is what heats the resistor.

Final answer: 1.2 V, 2.0 A, 2.4 W mechanical = 2.4 W electrical (accept 2.3 to 2.5 W). A correct answer must compute the force (or use P = εI) and show the two powers equal. 0.30 W is the force quoted as a power; 4.8 W or 9.6 W has an extra factor of v or of I.

**11.** *Solenoid inductance, back-emf and stored energy.* (a) L = μ₀N²A/ℓ = (4π × 10⁻⁷)(800)²(3.0 × 10⁻⁴)/(0.20) = (1.257 × 10⁻⁶)(6.4 × 10⁵)(1.5 × 10⁻³) = 1.21 × 10⁻³ H, about 1.2 mH. (b) ΔI/Δt = 3.0/0.015 = 200 A/s, so |ε| = L ΔI/Δt = (1.21 × 10⁻³)(200) = 0.24 V. (c) U = ½LI² = ½(1.21 × 10⁻³)(3.0)² = 5.4 × 10⁻³ J, about 5.4 mJ.

Final answer: L = 1.2 mH (accept 1.15 to 1.25 mH), ε = 0.24 V (accept 0.23 to 0.25), U = 5.4 mJ (accept 5.2 to 5.6). A correct answer converts cm² to 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ m², squares N, and uses the rate 200 A/s rather than the current 3.0 A in (b). 1.5 × 10⁻⁶ H used N instead of N²; 0.12 H converted the area with 10⁻²; 3.6 mJ is LI, with the ½ and the square both dropped.

**12.** *Why a transformer needs alternating current.* The secondary emf is ε = −N_s dΦ/dt: it needs a changing flux in the core. A steady direct current gives a steady core flux, so after the brief switch-on transient dΦ/dt = 0 and the secondary voltage is zero. A d.c. current still makes a perfectly good field; it simply makes a constant one, and constant flux induces nothing.

The primary overheats because on a.c. the current is limited mainly by the back-emf L dI/dt of the winding; on d.c. there is no changing current and hence no back-emf, so the current is limited only by the winding's small resistance, I = V/R, which is far larger than the design current and dissipates I²R as heat. A correct answer says (1) no changing flux so no induced emf, and (2) no back-emf so a large resistive current in the primary.
