## Answers

**1. c** — *Φ = BA cos θ, with the area in m² and the angle from the normal.* A = πr² = π(0.060)² = 1.131×10⁻² m²; the plane is perpendicular to B, so the **normal** is along B and cos θ = 1. Φ = (0.20)(1.131×10⁻²) = **2.3×10⁻³ Wb**. (a) is 0.18 Wb = NΦ, the **flux linkage** of all 80 turns — the question asked for one turn. (b) is 22.6 Wb, from leaving the radius in centimetres, so that A = π(6.0)² = 113 m² — a factor of 10⁴ that a glance at the magnitude should catch. (d) is 7.5×10⁻² Wb, from using the **circumference** 2πr = 0.377 m in place of the area.

**2. b** — *ε = N A ΔB/Δt, driven by the **change** in B.* ΔB = 0.50 − 0.20 = 0.30 T, so ε = (80)(1.131×10⁻²)(0.30/0.15) = (0.905)(2.0) = **1.8 V**. (a) is 3.0 V, from using the **final** field 0.50 T instead of the change: only differences of flux induce anything. (c) is 4.2 V, from **adding** the two field values (0.70 T) instead of subtracting them; it is the answer of a student who has assumed the field reversed when it did not. (d) is 2.3×10⁻² V, with N omitted — the emf of a single turn.

**3. d** — *q = N ΔΦ/R, the time cancelling out.* N ΔΦ = (80)(1.131×10⁻²)(0.30) = 0.271 Wb, so q = 0.271/12 = **2.3×10⁻² C**. Equivalently I × t = (1.81/12)(0.15) = 0.0226 C, the same number — which is the point: the charge does not depend on how fast the field is changed. (a) is 3.3 C, from **multiplying** by R instead of dividing — a bigger resistance can only move *less* charge. (b) is 2.8×10⁻⁴ C with N omitted. (c) is 0.15, which is the **current** in amperes quoted as a charge in coulombs; the units alone condemn it.

**4. c** — *ε = BLv, all three mutually perpendicular.* ε = (0.40)(0.50)(6.0) = **1.2 V**. Unit check: T × m × m/s = V. (a) is 0.12, which is in fact the **retarding force** B²L²v/R in newtons — the right formula from the next stage of the problem, applied to the wrong question, and carrying the wrong unit. (b) is 3.0 V = Lv, the field dropped. (d) is 0.60 V, a spurious factor ½ imported from ε = ½BL²ω (which is the emf of a rod **rotating** about one end, a different geometry).

**5. b** — *P_mech = Fv, and it must equal the electrical power dissipated.* I = ε/R = 1.2/2.0 = 0.60 A; F = BIL = (0.40)(0.60)(0.50) = 0.12 N; P = Fv = (0.12)(6.0) = **0.72 W**. Check: I²R = (0.60)²(2.0) = 0.72 W ✔, and εI = (1.2)(0.60) = 0.72 W ✔. (a) is 2.9 W = ε²R, the resistance multiplied instead of divided; ε²/R is the correct form, and it gives 0.72 W. (c) is the trap the question is built on: "frictionless" removes the *mechanical* resistance but not the *magnetic* one, and the 0.72 W appearing as heat in R has to come from somewhere. (d) halves the answer with an invented ½.

**6. d** — *ε₀ = NBAω with ω = 2πf and A in m².* A = 200 cm² = 2.00×10⁻² m²; ω = 2π(25) = 157 rad/s; ε₀ = (150)(0.12)(2.00×10⁻²)(157) = (0.36)(157) = **57 V**. (a) is 9.0 V, from using **f = 25** in place of ω = 2πf, an error of 2π ≈ 6.28. (b) is 0.36 V = NBA, the rotation rate left out altogether, which would mean a stationary coil generates a voltage. (c) is 40 V = 56.5/√2, the **r.m.s.** value quoted where the peak was asked for — the most respectable of the wrong answers, and a marks-loser rather than a physics error.

**7. a** — *The emf is the **slope** of the flux curve, so it vanishes where the flux is at its extreme.* With Φ = BA cos ωt the flux is momentarily stationary at its maximum, face-on to the field, and there ε = NBAω sin ωt = 0. (b) is the standard inversion: edge-on is where the flux passes through **zero**, and that is where the emf is **greatest**, because the flux is changing fastest. (c) confuses constant *speed* with constant *emf*: the emf is a sinusoid, which is the whole point of an a.c. generator. (d) is the 45° position, where sin 45° = 0.71 and the emf is 0.71ε₀ — neither zero nor maximum.

**8. c** — *|ε| = L|dI/dt|, and the energy released is ½L(I₁² − I₂²).* dI/dt = 4.0/(8.0×10⁻³) = 500 A/s, so |ε| = (0.060)(500) = **30 V**; energy = ½(0.060)(5.0² − 1.0²) = (0.030)(24) = **0.72 J**. (a) leaves 8.0 ms as 8.0 s, giving dI/dt = 0.5 A/s and an emf a thousand times too small — the single most expensive conversion in the topic. (b) is 0.48 J = ½L(ΔI)², **squaring the difference** instead of differencing the squares: 24 has been replaced by 16, and the two are equal only when I₂ = 0. (d) gets the emf right but computes ½LI₁² = 0.75 J, ignoring the 1.0 A still flowing and the ½L(1.0)² = 0.03 J still stored.

**9.** *Concept: Φ = BA cos θ, average emf from the change in flux linkage, and why induced charge is independent of the rate.* **(i) Flux and linkage.** A = (0.12)² = 1.44×10⁻² m². With the **normal at 60° to B**, Φ = BA cos 60° = (0.75)(1.44×10⁻²)(0.500) = **5.40×10⁻³ Wb** per turn, and the flux linkage is NΦ = (60)(5.40×10⁻³) = **0.324 Wb**. (Note the angle is already given from the normal; had the question said "the *plane* at 60° to B", the angle from the normal would be 30° and the cosine 0.866.) **(ii) Emf and current.** In the final orientation the normal lies along B, so Φ_f = BA = 1.08×10⁻² Wb and NΦ_f = **0.648 Wb**. The change in flux linkage is Δ(NΦ) = 0.648 − 0.324 = **0.324 Wb**, so the average emf is ε = Δ(NΦ)/Δt = 0.324/0.050 = **6.48 V**, and the average current is I = ε/R = 6.48/15 = **0.432 A**. **(iii) Charge.** q = N ΔΦ/R = 0.324/15 = **2.16×10⁻² C (21.6 mC)**; equivalently I × t = (0.432)(0.050) = 0.0216 C ✔. **Ten times more slowly (0.50 s):** the emf becomes **0.648 V**, the current becomes **4.32×10⁻² A** — each divided by ten — but the charge is **still 2.16×10⁻² C**. **Why the charge does not change:** integrating Faraday's law with Ohm's law gives q = ∫I dt = ∫(N/R)(dΦ/dt) dt = N ΔΦ/R, in which **the time has cancelled**. Slowing the rotation reduces the current by exactly the factor by which it lengthens the time, and the product — the charge — is untouched. Only the **total flux change** and the resistance matter, which is why a ballistic galvanometer can measure a flux change without anyone controlling how fast it is made.

**10.** *Concept: motional emf, the retarding force, and the closed energy audit.* **Steady state.** ε = BLv = (0.25)(0.80)(5.0) = **1.0 V**. Current I = ε/R = 1.0/0.40 = **2.5 A**. The rod now carries current in the field, so it feels **F = BIL = (0.25)(2.5)(0.80) = 0.50 N**, directed **against** its motion (Lenz). Equivalently F = B²L²v/R = (0.0625)(0.64)(5.0)/0.40 = 0.50 N ✔. To hold the speed constant the applied force must exactly balance this, so the mechanical power supplied is **P = Fv = (0.50)(5.0) = 2.5 W**. **Two independent checks of the electrical power:** I²R = (2.5)²(0.40) = **2.5 W** ✔, and εI = (1.0)(2.5) = **2.5 W** ✔. Every joule pushed in appears as heat in the resistance; the magnetic field itself neither stores nor supplies any of it, since the magnetic force on a charge is always perpendicular to its velocity and does no work. **Release the rod.** The only horizontal force left is the retarding one, F = B²L²v/R = (0.0625)(0.64)v/0.40 = 0.10v newtons, proportional to the speed. Newton's second law gives m dv/dt = −(B²L²/R)v, whose solution is an **exponential decay**, v = v₀e^(−t/τ), with **τ = mR/(B²L²) = (0.20)(0.40)/[(0.0625)(0.64)] = 0.080/0.040 = 2.0 s**. The rod slows to 37 % of 5.0 m/s after 2.0 s and asymptotically to rest; the emf, current and force decay with the same time constant. **Total heat produced** = all of the initial kinetic energy = ½mv₀² = ½(0.20)(5.0)² = **2.5 J**, deposited in R. **Why the force must oppose.** If it assisted the motion, the rod would accelerate, raising ε, raising I, raising the assisting force — the rod would run away to arbitrarily high speed while simultaneously dissipating ever more heat in the resistor, with **no energy source anywhere in the system**. That violates conservation of energy, so the induced current can only take the sense that resists the motion. Lenz's law is exactly the rule that makes the rod a **generator** rather than a perpetual-motion machine, and the retarding force is the mechanism by which the mechanical work you do is converted into the electrical energy you take out.

**11.** *Concept: L = μ₀μᵣN²A/ℓ, ε = L dI/dt, U = ½LI², and what a ferromagnetic core multiplies.* **(i) Air core.** A = 12 cm² = 1.2×10⁻³ m², ℓ = 0.25 m, N = 500. L = μ₀N²A/ℓ = (4π×10⁻⁷)(2.5×10⁵)(1.2×10⁻³)/(0.25) = **1.51×10⁻³ H (1.51 mH)**. **(ii) With μᵣ = 400.** The geometry is unchanged and the permeability multiplies straight through: L = 400 × 1.51×10⁻³ = **0.603 H** — four hundred times larger from the same copper. **(iii) Switch-off emf.** dI/dt = 3.0/(5.0×10⁻³) = **600 A/s**. Air core: |ε| = (1.51×10⁻³)(600) = **0.90 V**. Iron core: |ε| = (0.603)(600) = **362 V**. **(iv) Stored energy at 3.0 A.** Air: U = ½(1.51×10⁻³)(9.0) = **6.8×10⁻³ J**. Iron: U = ½(0.603)(9.0) = **2.71 J**, again a factor of 400. **Why N² and not N.** The field inside the solenoid is B = μ₀(N/ℓ)I, so the flux through **one** turn is already proportional to N; that flux is then linked by **all N turns**, and the flux linkage NΦ therefore carries a second factor of N. Since L = NΦ/I, the two factors multiply and **L ∝ N²**. Doubling the turns at fixed length quadruples the inductance. **Why L does not depend on I.** L = μ₀μᵣN²A/ℓ contains no current at all: it is a property of the **geometry and the core material** only, exactly as capacitance is a property of plate area and separation. Raising the current raises the flux and the flux linkage in exactly the same proportion, so their ratio — the inductance — is unchanged. (What does change with current is the stored energy, and it changes as I².) **What (iii) says about switching.** A modest 3.0 A being interrupted in a few milliseconds produces **362 V** across an iron-cored coil fed from what may be a 12 V supply — far more than the supply voltage, because the emf is set by dI/dt and a mechanical switch makes dI/dt enormous. This is why opening an inductive circuit **arcs at the contacts**, why relay and motor coils are fitted with a flywheel diode or an RC snubber to give the current a path while the field collapses, and why the stored ½LI² (2.71 J here) must be deliberately dumped somewhere safe rather than into the switch.

**12.** *Concept: the transformer's turns and current ratios, and why transmission is done at high voltage.* **The numbers.** Turns ratio: N_s = N_p(V_s/V_p) = 3200 × (9.0/240) = **120 turns**. Secondary current: I_s = V_s/R = 9.0/4.5 = **2.0 A**. Power delivered: P = V_sI_s = (9.0)(2.0) = **18 W**. Primary current, from ideal power conservation V_pI_p = V_sI_s: I_p = 18/240 = **0.075 A**. **Check against the turns ratio:** I_p/I_s = N_s/N_p = 120/3200 = 1/26.7, and 2.0/26.7 = 0.075 A ✔. The voltage is stepped **down** by 26.7 and the current **up** by 26.7; the power is identical on both sides, as it must be, since a transformer redistributes V and I and manufactures no watts. **(i) On d.c.** After the switch-on transient the primary current is steady, so the core flux is steady, dΦ/dt = 0 and the **secondary emf is zero** — the heater does nothing. Worse, the primary no longer has a back-emf to limit its current: it is now just a coil of wire across 240 V, its current set only by its small ohmic resistance, and it will overheat and burn out. Transformers are an **a.c.-only** device, which is the whole reason mains distribution is a.c. **(ii) Why 24 kV and not 240 V.** Line loss is **I²R**, and it depends on the current, not on the transmitted voltage. At **240 V**: I = P/V = 18 000/240 = **75 A**, so the loss is I²R = (75)²(2.0) = **11 250 W = 11.25 kW** — 63 % of everything sent, and the line would also drop 150 V. At **24 kV**: I = 18 000/24 000 = **0.75 A**, so the loss is (0.75)²(2.0) = **1.1 W** — about 0.006 %. Raising the voltage a hundredfold cuts the current a hundredfold and the loss **ten thousandfold**, because the loss goes as I². This is the entire justification for the step-up transformer at the power station and the step-down transformer on the distribution pole. **(iii) The deck's phrasing.** *"…induces an alternating current in the secondary coil"* is **not strictly correct**. Faraday's law induces an **emf**; a current flows only if the secondary circuit is **closed**. An unloaded transformer with its secondary open has the full 9.0 V across its terminals and essentially no secondary current at all — which is exactly the state of every phone charger left plugged in with nothing attached. The correct statement is that the changing flux induces an alternating **emf** in the secondary, which drives an alternating current through whatever load is connected.
