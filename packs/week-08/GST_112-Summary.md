# GST_112 — Week 8 Summary

*Nigerian Peoples and Culture · Week 8 (26 Oct – 1 Nov 2026) · Topic: Citizenship and civic responsibilities, and the patterns of acquiring Nigerian citizenship*

## The 8 most examinable things this week

1. **Definition.** **Citizenship is the state of being a citizen, as well as the rights, obligations and advantages that come with it.** With citizenship of a nation you have the right to **live, work, vote and pay taxes** there. It is **usually granted in the nation where you were born**; an **immigrant from another nation must apply**.
2. **Citizenship is not nationality.** The lesson is explicit that the two are confused, and that **nationality is of wider scope than citizenship**.
3. **Where it sits in law: Chapter Three of the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 (as amended)** provides extensively for citizenship, on the principle that **every country has the right to decide who will be its citizen**.
4. **The three routes, and only three: by birth, by registration, by naturalisation.**
5. **Citizenship by birth** (1999 Constitution): a person is a Nigerian by birth if **both parents are Nigerians**, **or either parent is a Nigerian**, **or any one of the grandparents is a Nigerian**. Note the disjunction — **one** qualifying relative is enough.
6. **Registration is open to exactly two classes**: **(1) a non-Nigerian woman married to a Nigerian**, and **(2) every person of full age and capacity born outside Nigeria whose grandparent is a citizen of Nigeria.** The applicant must prove three things **to the President**: **good character**; a **clear intention to be domiciled in / reside in Nigeria**; and that he or she **has taken the prescribed Oath of Allegiance contained in the Seventh Schedule to the 1999 Constitution**.
7. **Naturalisation is governed by Section 27 of the 1999 Constitution**, and a **certificate of grant of citizenship by naturalisation** is issued. It is the route **by which a foreigner who has lived for a long time in Nigeria** becomes a citizen.
8. **Civic responsibilities are contained in Section 24 of the 1999 Constitution (as amended)**, six lettered duties (A–F), and the lesson separately lists six responsibilities: **obey the law, protection of public property, payment of taxes, rendering national service, loyalty, respect for others' rights.**

## Historical and legal background

- **Before the date of independence there were no Nigerian citizens.** The Nigerian Constitution **from the time of independence** provides for some types of citizenship.
- **Since 1979 there have been significant changes in the law relating to citizenship**, the lesson naming: the **Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1979**, the **Nigeria Citizenship Act 1960**, and the **Nigerian Citizenship Act 1961**. (The deck lists them in that order even though the Acts are the earlier instruments — learn the three names as the deck gives them.)

## The three routes, side by side

| Route | Who qualifies | The exact conditions the lesson states |
|---|---|---|
| **By birth** | A person with the stated Nigerian ancestry | **Both parents Nigerian**, **or either parent Nigerian**, **or any one of the grandparents Nigerian** |
| **By registration** | **A non-Nigerian woman married to a Nigerian**; **every person of full age and capacity born outside Nigeria whose grandparent is a citizen of Nigeria** | Must prove **to the President**: **(i) person of good character**; **(ii) clear intention of his desire to be domiciled or to live or reside in Nigeria**; **(iii) has taken the prescribed Oath of Allegiance to Nigeria in the Seventh Schedule to the 1999 Constitution** |
| **By naturalisation** | A **foreign national** who meets the **predetermined eligibility criteria established by the country's laws and regulations** — in practice **any foreigner who has lived for a long time in Nigeria** | Requirements as **stipulated in Section 27 of the 1999 Constitution**; on fulfilment a **certificate of grant of citizenship by naturalisation** is issued |

**Definition of naturalisation, verbatim:** the **process by which an individual becomes a legal citizen of a specific country** — the **formal act of granting citizenship to a foreign national who meets the predetermined eligibility criteria** established by that country's laws.

## Dual citizenship, renunciation, deprivation

- **Dual citizenship is allowed.** A **foreigner who is a citizen of another country by birth** may **also be a citizen of Nigeria by registration or by naturalisation**. And a **Nigerian citizen by birth may acquire the citizenship of another country without renouncing his Nigerian citizenship.**
- **Renunciation.** A **citizen of Nigeria who is of full age** has the **right to renounce and abandon** his Nigerian citizenship and take up the citizenship of **any other country of his choice**, by **making the prescribed declaration to that effect**. The **President may refuse to register the declaration** where it was made **(1) when Nigeria is involved in a war**, or **(2) contrary to public policy.**
- **Deprivation (loss).** The **President may deprive a person who is not a Nigerian by birth** of citizenship if that person is **sentenced to a term of imprisonment of not less than 3 years within a period of 7 years after becoming a citizen of Nigeria by naturalisation.** Additionally, persons who acquired citizenship **by naturalisation or by registration** may lose it if they: **(A) are disloyal to Nigeria by act or speech**; **(B) traded with, engaged in or associated in any business with an enemy country at war with Nigeria**; **(C) assisted such an enemy country in any manner**; **(D) unlawfully communicated with such enemy country to the detriment of, or with intent to cause damage to, Nigeria.**
- The structural point the exam turns on: **renunciation is the citizen's own act**; **deprivation is the President's act**, and it reaches only **acquired** citizenship, never citizenship **by birth**.

## Civic responsibility

**Definition:** the **duties and obligations that citizens are expected to fulfil for the betterment of their society and the functioning of their country**, outlined in **various laws, policies and cultural expectations**.

**The lesson's six:** 1. **Obey the law** · 2. **Protection of public property** · 3. **Payment of taxes** · 4. **Rendering national service** · 5. **Loyalty** · 6. **Respect for others' rights**.

**Section 24, CFRN 1999 (as amended) — "It shall be the duty of every citizen to":**

| | Duty |
|---|---|
| **A** | **Abide by this Constitution**, respect its **ideals and its institutions**, the **National Flag**, the **National Anthem**, the **National Pledge**, and **legitimate authorities** |
| **B** | Help to **enhance the power, prestige and good name of Nigeria**, **defend Nigeria** and **render such national service as may be required** |
| **C** | **Respect the dignity of other citizens** and the rights and legitimate interests of others and **live in unity and harmony and in the spirit of common brotherhood** |
| **D** | Make **positive and useful contribution to the advancement, progress and well-being of the community where he resides** |
| **E** | **Render assistance to appropriate and lawful agencies in the maintenance of law and order** |
| **F** | **Declare his income honestly** to appropriate and lawful agencies and **pay his tax promptly** |

**The conclusion, and a likely essay prompt:** civic responsibility is **not only a legal obligation but also a moral duty** that **promotes active citizenship** and **contributes to the overall development of Nigeria**; by fulfilling these duties citizens **actively contribute to a thriving and inclusive society**.

## Commonly confused

- **Citizenship vs nationality.** **Nationality is of wider scope.** If a question asks which term the other is "often confused with, though broader", the answer is **nationality**.
- **Chapter Three vs Section 24 vs Section 27 vs the Seventh Schedule.** **Chapter Three** = citizenship generally. **Section 24** = the **duties of every citizen**. **Section 27** = **naturalisation**. **Seventh Schedule** = where the **Oath of Allegiance** is set out. Four different references, one week.
- **Registration vs naturalisation.** **Registration** is for the **two named classes** — the **non-Nigerian wife of a Nigerian** and the **foreign-born adult with a Nigerian grandparent**. **Naturalisation** is the **general route for a foreigner who has lived long in Nigeria**, under **s.27**, ending in a **certificate**. A foreign **husband** of a Nigerian woman is **not** in the registration class as the lesson states it.
- **Parents vs grandparents.** For **birth**: **either parent** or **any one grandparent** being Nigerian suffices. For **registration** of the foreign-born adult, the qualifying relative is specifically a **grandparent** who is a citizen — not a parent.
- **3 years and 7 years.** The deprivation trigger is a sentence of **not less than 3 years' imprisonment**, imposed **within 7 years of becoming a citizen by naturalisation**. Do not swap the two numbers.
- **Renunciation vs deprivation.** **Renunciation** — the **citizen of full age** declares; the **President may refuse registration** only in **war** or where **contrary to public policy**. **Deprivation** — the **President** initiates, on **criminal sentence** or on the **disloyalty/enemy-country** grounds, and only against **acquired** citizenship.
- **Refusing renunciation: the two grounds only.** Wanting to move abroad, to study abroad, or reaching a given age are **not** grounds (this is the deck's own mid-lesson question, answer **A**).
- **The six civic responsibilities vs the six Section 24 duties.** They are **two separate lists** that overlap. If asked for the **constitutional** duties, give **A–F of Section 24** in the Constitution's own words; if asked for the **lesson's list**, give **obey the law, protect public property, pay taxes, render national service, loyalty, respect others' rights**.
