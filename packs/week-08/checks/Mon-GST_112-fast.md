# Monday — GST_112 fast-hour check

*The three routes to Nigerian citizenship, loss of citizenship, and the Section 24 duties.*
*Five questions, straight after the fast hour. Recall only. Score out of 5.*

**1.** Nigerian citizenship may be acquired by exactly three routes. They are:
A. birth, marriage and naturalisation
B. birth, registration and naturalisation
C. birth, registration and residence
D. registration, naturalisation and descent

**2.** Which section of the 1999 Constitution sets out the duties of every citizen?
A. Section 27
B. Chapter Three
C. Section 24
D. The Seventh Schedule

**3.** Which person qualifies for Nigerian citizenship by registration?
A. A foreign man married to a Nigerian woman
B. A non-Nigerian woman married to a Nigerian man
C. Any foreigner resident in Nigeria for ten years
D. A refugee granted asylum in Nigeria

**4.** Deprivation of citizenship may follow a prison sentence of:
A. not less than 7 years, imposed within 3 years
B. not less than 5 years, imposed at any time
C. any length, imposed within 7 years
D. not less than 3 years, imposed within 7 years

**5.** The President may refuse to register a renunciation of citizenship only when:
A. Nigeria is at war, or it is contrary to public policy
B. the citizen has not yet reached full age
C. the citizen intends to study abroad
D. the citizen was naturalised less than seven years ago

## Answers

**1. B** — Chapter Three of the 1999 Constitution provides for citizenship by birth, by registration and by naturalisation, and nothing else. Marriage and descent are routes into the registration and birth categories, not separate routes, and long residence is what naturalisation is for.

**2. C** — Section 24, as amended, gives the six lettered duties from abiding by the Constitution to declaring income honestly. Section 27 governs naturalisation, Chapter Three covers citizenship generally, and the Seventh Schedule holds the Oath of Allegiance.

**3. B** — The lesson names only two registration classes: a non-Nigerian woman married to a Nigerian, and an adult born outside Nigeria with a Nigerian grandparent. A foreign husband is not in the class as the lesson states it, and a long-resident foreigner goes by naturalisation under Section 27.

**4. D** — The President may deprive a person who is not a Nigerian by birth if that person is sentenced to not less than three years' imprisonment within seven years of becoming a citizen by naturalisation. The two numbers are easily swapped, and the power never reaches a citizen by birth.

**5. A** — The two grounds are that the declaration was made while Nigeria is involved in a war, or that it is contrary to public policy. Full age is a condition of making the declaration at all, not a ground of refusal, and wanting to study or live abroad is exactly the motive the right protects.
