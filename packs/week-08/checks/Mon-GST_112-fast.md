# Monday — GST_112 fast-hour check

*The three routes to Nigerian citizenship, loss of citizenship, and the Section 24 duties.*
*5 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 5.*

**1.** A Togolese woman marries a Nigerian man and wishes to become a Nigerian citizen. Which route to citizenship is open to her?
A. By birth
B. By registration
C. By naturalisation
D. By dual citizenship

**2.** A woman became a Nigerian citizen by naturalisation in 2016. In 2021 she is sentenced to five years' imprisonment. May the President deprive her of her Nigerian citizenship?
A. Yes, because the sentence is not less than 3 years and falls within 7 years of her naturalisation
B. No, because only citizens by registration can be deprived of citizenship
C. No, because the 7-year period after naturalisation had already passed
D. No, because a sentence of at least 10 years is required

**3.** Which of the following is NOT one of the duties of every citizen under Section 24 of the 1999 Constitution?
A. Abide by the Constitution and respect the National Flag, Anthem and Pledge
B. Declare his income honestly and pay his tax promptly
C. Render assistance to lawful agencies in the maintenance of law and order
D. Vote in every national election

**4.** Which of the following statements about dual citizenship in Nigeria is correct?
A. Dual citizenship is not permitted under the Nigerian Constitution
B. A Nigerian by birth must renounce Nigerian citizenship before taking the citizenship of another country
C. A Nigerian citizen by birth may acquire the citizenship of another country without renouncing his Nigerian citizenship
D. Only citizens by naturalisation may hold the citizenship of two countries

**5.** What is naturalisation?
A. The formal act of granting citizenship to a foreign national who meets the eligibility criteria set by a country's laws
B. The registration of a non-Nigerian woman who is married to a Nigerian
C. The acquisition of citizenship through a Nigerian parent or grandparent
D. The declaration by which a citizen gives up one citizenship to take up another

---

## Answers

**1. B** — *Three routes to Nigerian citizenship.* Citizenship by registration is open to a non-Nigerian woman married to a Nigerian, and to a person of full age and capacity born outside Nigeria whose grandparent is a Nigerian citizen. She has no Nigerian parent or grandparent, so birth does not apply. Naturalisation is the route for a foreigner who has lived in Nigeria for a long time under Section 27. Dual citizenship is a status a person may hold, not a route to acquiring citizenship.

**2. A** — *Loss of citizenship after a prison sentence.* The President may deprive a person who is not a Nigerian by birth of citizenship if the person is sentenced to imprisonment of not less than 3 years within a period of 7 years after becoming a citizen by naturalisation. Five years exceeds the 3-year threshold and 2021 is within 7 years of 2016, so deprivation is possible. Deprivation on this ground applies to naturalised citizens, not only registered ones; the 7-year window has not expired; and there is no 10-year requirement.

**3. D** — *Section 24 duties of a citizen.* Section 24 lists six duties: abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals, institutions, the Flag, Anthem, Pledge and legitimate authorities; enhance the good name of Nigeria, defend it and render national service; respect the dignity and rights of others; contribute to the community where he resides; assist lawful agencies in maintaining law and order; and declare income honestly and pay tax promptly. Voting is one of the rights that come with citizenship, not a Section 24 duty.

**4. C** — *Dual citizenship and renunciation.* The Constitution allows dual citizenship: a foreigner who is a citizen of another country by birth may also become a Nigerian by registration or naturalisation, and a Nigerian citizen by birth may acquire another country's citizenship without renouncing his Nigerian citizenship. Renunciation is a separate voluntary act by a citizen of full age, which the President may refuse to register during a war or where it is contrary to public policy. It is not restricted to naturalised citizens.

**5. A** — *Definition of naturalisation.* Naturalisation is the process by which an individual becomes a legal citizen of a specific country: the formal act of granting citizenship to a foreign national who meets the predetermined eligibility criteria, in Nigeria under Section 27 of the 1999 Constitution, ending in a certificate of grant of citizenship by naturalisation. Registration of a wife is the registration route, descent from a parent or grandparent is citizenship by birth, and giving up one citizenship for another is renunciation.
