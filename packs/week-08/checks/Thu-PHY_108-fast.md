# Thursday — PHY_108 fast-hour check

*Verifying Boyle's law with a J-tube: the conditions, the pressure formula, and the straight-line graph.*
*Five questions, straight after the fast hour. Recall only. Score out of 5.*

**1.** Boyle's law holds only when which two quantities are held fixed?
A. Pressure and volume
B. Volume and temperature
C. Temperature and the mass of gas
D. Pressure and the mass of gas

**2.** In the J-tube, with mercury at the same level in both limbs, the trapped air is at:
A. atmospheric pressure
B. zero pressure
C. twice atmospheric pressure
D. a pressure equal to rho g h

**3.** The pressure of the trapped gas at height h is calculated as:
A. rho g h
B. P_a minus rho g h
C. P_a times rho g h
D. P_a plus rho g h

**4.** Which plot actually verifies Boyle's law?
A. P against V
B. 1/V against P
C. V against h
D. ln V against P

**5.** A non-zero intercept on that straight line most often means:
A. the gas leaked past the seal
B. the temperature rose during compression
C. atmospheric pressure was left out of P
D. the tube's bore was uniform

## Answers

**1. C** — The law says that at constant temperature the pressure of a definite quantity of gas is inversely proportional to its volume, and a statement missing either condition earns nothing. Pressure and volume are the two quantities that vary, and holding volume fixed instead gives Gay-Lussac's law.

**2. A** — With the levels equal there is no excess mercury column, so the trapped air sits at atmospheric pressure and this is the reading recorded as the starting volume. The rho g h term is only the increase added once the open limb is raised.

**3. D** — The weight of the excess mercury column adds to the atmospheric pressure the gas already had, so the two terms are added. Using rho g h alone omits atmospheric pressure and is the classic source of a non-zero intercept.

**4. B** — Plotting 1 over V against P gives a straight line through the origin, and linearity plus the predicted origin is what can be tested. P against V is a hyperbola, which no eye can distinguish from any other falling curve, so it proves nothing.

**5. C** — Leaving atmospheric pressure out shifts every pressure sideways by the same amount, so the line no longer passes through the origin. A leak makes the product PV fall steadily down the table, heating makes it drift upward and curve, and a uniform bore is the assumption the method needs, not a fault.
