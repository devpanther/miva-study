## Marking scheme

*Award 1 mark per correct answer in Section A. In Section B, award the marks shown in brackets; a mark is earned once the stated step appears, even if a later step is wrong.*

**1. C** — Week 1 — *Range ⊆ codomain.* The codomain is the declared target set; the range is what is actually attained, so the containment can be strict (x² + 1 on ℝ → ℝ has range [1, ∞)). B reverses the containment; A and D are the two standard over-claims.

**2. B** — Week 3 — *Definition of a limit.* The limit describes the approach, with x ≠ a excluded by the 0 < |x − a| in the formal definition. A is the limit-equals-value slip; C smuggles in continuity, which is a conclusion and not part of the definition; D confuses a limit with a bound.

**3. C** — Week 2 — *Degree comparison for asymptotes.* deg N = deg D + 1 is exactly the oblique case; divide to get y = qx + r. A is the equal-degree rule misapplied to unequal degrees; B is the deg N < deg D rule.

**4. D** — Week 4 — *The three conditions for continuity.* A, B and C are each only part of the definition; C in particular does not even guarantee the limit exists, since the two one-sided limits may differ.

**5. A** — Week 1 — *Domain/range swap on inversion.* dom(f⁻¹) = ran(f) and ran(f⁻¹) = dom(f). B swaps the swap; C confuses codomain with range; D confuses f⁻¹ with 1/f.

**6. B** — Week 2 — *The odd test, done algebraically.* Odd means f(−x) = −f(x). D is the "judge by the powers" slip that the summary warns against — x³ + 1 has an odd leading power and is neither even nor odd.

**7. C** — Week 3 — *Hypotheses of L'Hôpital's Rule.* The form must be checked before every application. B is the determinate form k/0, on which the rule gives a confidently wrong number; D drops the form condition entirely.

**8. D** — Week 4 — *An asymptote is a limit statement.* A and B are the "never touches" folklore — 2x/(x² + 1) crosses y = 0 at the origin. C is the equal-degree rule quoted as though it were the definition.

**9. A** — Week 1 — *Log domain rule plus inequality handling.* 6 − 3x > 0 ⟹ 6 > 3x ⟹ x < 2. B treats the strict > as ≥; C is the sign slip from failing to flip when moving the −3x; D treats the log argument like a denominator.

**10. C** — Week 1 — *Composite order.* g(−1) = 1/2, then f(1/2) = 1/4 − 1 = **−3/4**. A is (g∘f)(−1) = g(0) = 1/3, the order reversal; B drops the square; D multiplies f(−1)·g(−1) instead of composing.

**11. B** — Week 2 — *Oblique asymptote by long division.* deg N = 3 = deg D + 1, so divide: 6x³ − x = (3x² + 5)(2x) − 11x, giving f(x) = 2x − 11x/(3x² + 5) and asymptote **y = 2x**. A is the ratio of leading coefficients used when the degrees are unequal.

**12. D** — Week 2 — *Sign chart for a rational inequality.* Critical values −1 (undefined) and 4 (zero). Testing: x = −2 gives (−6)/(−1) > 0; x = 0 gives (−4)/(1) < 0 ✓; x = 5 gives (1)/(6) > 0. Solution **(−1, 4)**. A is the outside-the-roots answer; B includes an undefined point and a zero under a strict inequality.

**13. A** — Week 3 — *0/0 in a rational function ⟹ (x − a) is a common factor.* (x + 2)(x + 3)/[(x + 2)(x − 2)] = (x + 3)/(x − 2) for x ≠ −2, → 1/(−4) = **−1/4**. B is a sign slip on the denominator; C reads 0/0 as 0; D reads "indeterminate" as "no limit".

**14. B** — Week 3 — *Conjugate multiplication.* [(9 + x) − 9]/[2x(√(9 + x) + 3)] = 1/[2(√(9 + x) + 3)] → 1/(2·6) = **1/12**. A is the same work with the factor 2 in the denominator ignored; C is the 0/0-reads-as-0 slip.

**15. A** — Weeks 2 and 4 — *Cancelled factor gives a hole; the surviving zero gives the asymptote.* (x − 3)/[(x − 3)(x + 3)] = 1/(x + 3) for x ≠ 3, so the hole sits at (3, 1/6) and x = −3 is a vertical asymptote. B is the slip of reading every denominator zero as an asymptote; D swaps the two roles.

**16. D** — Week 4 — *Existence test read off the branches; the filled dot is ignored.* Left branch → 5, right branch → 3; two different finite heights ⟹ a **jump**, so the limit does not exist. C reads f(1) = 2 and calls it the limit — the commonest graph error.

**17. B** — Week 1 — *Range by completing the square.* −x² + 8x − 11 = −[(x − 4)² − 16] − 11 = −(x − 4)² + 5 ≤ 5, so the range is **(−∞, 5]**. A ignores the downward opening; C mistakes the y-intercept for the extreme value.

**18. A** — Weeks 1 and 2 — *dom(f⁻¹) = ran(f), and the excluded value is where f⁻¹ blows up.* Inverting: y(x + 4) = 2x − 1 ⟹ x(y − 2) = −(4y + 1) ⟹ f⁻¹(x) = (4x + 1)/(2 − x), so the vertical asymptote is **x = 2**. B is the asymptote of f itself, not of f⁻¹; C is the zero of f.

**19. C** — Weeks 2 and 3 — *Limits at infinity with a root: √(x²) = |x| = −x as x → −∞.* Dividing through by x: √(4x² + 3x)/x = −√(4 + 3/x) → −2, and (1 − 2x)/x → −2, so the limit is (−2)/(−2) = **1**. A is the answer obtained by writing √(x²) = x; B takes the ratio 4/(−2) of the coefficients without taking the square root; D is false — 4x² dominates 3x, so the radicand is positive for large |x|.

**20. B** — Weeks 3 and 4 — *Check the form before cancelling: only one factor cancels here.* (x − 2)(x − 3)/(x − 2)² = (x − 3)/(x − 2) for x ≠ 2, which is k/0 with k = −1. On the left the denominator is 0⁻ so the quotient → +∞; on the right it is 0⁺ so the quotient → −∞: opposite infinities ⟹ DNE, and x = 2 is a vertical asymptote. A cancels without noticing that a factor survives; C assumes the squared denominator keeps its sign after cancellation; D applies the leading-coefficient ratio at a finite point.

**21. A** — Weeks 2 and 4 — *Hole height at the cancelled factor; equal degrees for the far-out behaviour.* 3(x − 2)(x + 2)/[(x − 2)(x + 1)] = 3(x + 2)/(x + 1), so lim_{x→2} f = 3(4)/3 = **4** (a hole, not an asymptote) and lim_{x→∞} f = 3/1 = **3**. B misreads the cancelled factor as an asymptote; C applies the deg N < deg D rule to equal degrees; D reads 0/0 as 0.

**22. B** — Weeks 1 and 3 — *Build the composite first, then classify the form.* (f∘g)(x) = 1/(x² − 1), and x² − 1 → 0⁻ from the left of 1 and 0⁺ from the right, so the one-sided limits are −∞ and +∞ and the limit **does not exist**. A cancels a factor that is not common to both parts of a genuine 0/0 — a fabrication, since the form here is 1/0; C is the answer for a squared denominator; D confuses k/0 with 0/k.

**23. D** — Week 2 — *Sign chart including undefined points.* Critical values −3, −2, 3. Tests: x = −4 gives (−2)/(7) < 0; x = −2.5 gives (−0.5)/(−2.75) > 0 ✓; x = 0 gives (2)/(−9) < 0; x = 4 gives (6)/(7) > 0 ✓. Include the zero x = −2 (the sign is ≥), exclude x = ±3 (undefined). Solution **(−3, −2] ∪ (3, ∞)**. A includes undefined points; B drops the zero although equality is allowed; C is the complement.

**24. C** — Weeks 3 and 4 — *Continuity at a removable point means defining the value to equal the limit.* (x − 3)(x + 2)/(x − 3) = x + 2 for x ≠ 3, so the limit is **5** and c = 5. A reads 0/0 as 0; B sets c equal to the point rather than the limit; D repeats the myth that a 0/0 formula must break the graph.

**25. D** — Weeks 1, 2 and 4 — *Domain, hole, asymptote and classification in one function.* (x − 3)(x + 3)/[(x + 3)(x − 2)] = (x − 3)/(x − 2) for x ≠ −3, so dom f = ℝ \ {−3, 2}; hole at x = −3 of height (−6)/(−5) = 6/5; vertical asymptote x = 2 (infinite discontinuity); equal degrees give HA y = 1. A forgets that the cancelled point is still outside the domain and still a discontinuity; B treats both denominator zeros as asymptotes; C reads the hole height as 0 and the HA as y = 0.

**26.** — Week 1 — *Domain rules, range by inversion, the inverse, and the domain of a composite.*
(a) **dom f = ℝ \ {2}** [1, for excluding the denominator zero]. Range: y(x − 2) = 4x + 3 ⟹ yx − 2y = 4x + 3 ⟹ x(y − 4) = 2y + 3 ⟹ x = (2y + 3)/(y − 4), defined for all y except 4, so **ran f = ℝ \ {4}** [1, for the rearrangement leading to the excluded value].
(b) **f⁻¹(x) = (2x + 3)/(x − 4)**, with **dom(f⁻¹) = ℝ \ {4} = ran f** and **ran(f⁻¹) = ℝ \ {2} = dom f** [1 for the formula together with both stated sets].
(c) g(8) = √9 = 3, so **(f∘g)(8) = (12 + 3)/(3 − 2) = 15** [1]. Domain: need x + 1 ≥ 0 ⟹ x ≥ −1 (even-root rule), and g(x) ∈ dom f ⟹ √(x + 1) ≠ 2 ⟹ x ≠ 3. **dom(f∘g) = [−1, 3) ∪ (3, ∞)** [1 — award only if both restrictions appear; quoting dom g alone scores 0 for this mark].

**27.** — Week 2 — *Full analysis of a rational function: hole, asymptotes, intercepts, one-sided behaviour.*
(a) 2(x − 1)(x + 1)/[(x − 1)(x − 2)] [1 for both factorisations]. The cancelled factor gives a **hole at (1, −4)** (reduced form 2(x + 1)/(x − 2) at x = 1 is 4/(−1) = −4) and the surviving zero gives the **vertical asymptote x = 2** [1 for hole coordinates and asymptote together].
(b) deg N = deg D = 2, so the horizontal asymptote is the **ratio of leading coefficients, y = 2/1 = 2** [1 — the rule must be stated, not just the answer]. **x-intercept (−1, 0)** from the numerator of the reduced form; **y-intercept (0, −1)** since f(0) = (−2)/(2) = −1 [1 for both].
(c) As x → 2⁻: numerator 2(x + 1) → 6 > 0, denominator (x − 2) → 0⁻, so **f(x) → −∞**; as x → 2⁺ the denominator is 0⁺, so **f(x) → +∞** [1 for both, with the sign reasoning].

**28.** — Week 3 — *Three algebraic limit techniques.*
(a) Substitution gives 0/0. Either factor x − 4 = (√x − 2)(√x + 2) or multiply by the conjugate (√x + 2): the quotient becomes √x + 2 for x ≠ 4 [1 for the conjugate/factorisation step], → 2 + 2 = **4** [1].
(b) Divide every term by x³, the highest power in the denominator: (5/x³ − 2)/(3 + 1/x²) → (0 − 2)/(3 + 0) = **−2/3** [1 — the sign must survive; the limit is the same at −∞ as at +∞ because the degrees are equal and odd powers cancel in the ratio].
(c) Combine the top over a common denominator: (2 − x)/(2x) [1]. Dividing by (x − 2) gives −(x − 2)/[2x(x − 2)] = −1/(2x) for x ≠ 2, → **−1/4** [1].

**29.** — Week 4 — *Reading limits, values and continuity off an explicitly defined piecewise graph.*
(a) lim_{x→−2⁻} f = −2 + 3 = **1**; lim_{x→−2⁺} f = (−2)² = **4**; the two finite heights differ so **lim_{x→−2} f does not exist**; **f(−2) = 4** [1 for all four]. f is **not continuous at −2**; the break is a **jump** (of size 3); since f(−2) = 4 equals the right-hand limit, f is **continuous from the right** at −2 and not from the left [1].
(b) lim_{x→1⁻} f = 1² = **1**; lim_{x→1⁺} f = 2/(x − 1) with 2 > 0 over 0⁺, so **+∞**; hence **lim_{x→1} f does not exist**; **f(1) is undefined** (no rule covers x = 1) [1 for all four]. One one-sided limit is infinite, so this is an **infinite discontinuity** [1].
(c) **Vertical asymptote x = 1**, encoding lim_{x→1⁺} f(x) = +∞; **horizontal asymptote y = 0**, encoding lim_{x→+∞} f(x) = 0 [1 for both; note there is no horizontal asymptote on the left, since f(x) = x + 3 → −∞ as x → −∞].

**30.** — Weeks 4 and 3 — *The continuity definition stated precisely and then applied.*
(a) f is continuous at c if and only if all three hold: (i) **f(c) is defined**, i.e. c is in the domain; (ii) **lim_{x→c} f(x) exists** — both one-sided limits exist, are finite and are equal; (iii) **lim_{x→c} f(x) = f(c)** [2 — 1 mark for conditions (i) and (ii), 1 mark for the equality (iii); a version that omits (iii) scores at most 1]. *Week 4.*
(b) Substitution at x = 1 gives (1 + a − 4)/0, which is a finite limit only if the numerator also vanishes there, i.e. only if x = 1 is a root: 1 + a − 4 = 0 ⟹ **a = 3** [1 for recognising that the numerator must vanish, and solving]. Check: x² + 3x − 4 = (x − 1)(x + 4), so f(x) = x + 4 for x ≠ 1 and lim_{x→1} f(x) = 5, which equals f(1) = 5, so all three conditions hold [1]. *Week 3 — the limit must be produced before continuity can be tested.*
(c) With f(1) = 2 the limit still exists and equals 5, so conditions (i) and (ii) hold and only (iii) fails: a **removable discontinuity**, repaired by redefining **f(1) := 5** [1]. *Week 4.*

## How to read your score

Add your Section A and Section B marks, then also add up your marks week by week using the labels in the marking scheme — the second number is the useful one.

- **40–50 (80%+).** Solid. Re-read only the specific commonly-confused bullets behind the questions you dropped; do not re-read whole weeks.
- **30–39 (60–79%).** Passing but leaky. Find the week where you lost the most marks and re-read that summary's worked examples, then re-attempt questions 18–25 and Section B cold.
- **20–29 (40–59%).** The definitions are probably being quoted correctly and the multi-step work is failing. Re-do Section B on paper without notes, then check line by line against the mark breakdown to see which line you never wrote.
- **Below 20 (under 40%).** Restart from Week 1 and work forward; every later week rests on it.

Then read the week split:

- **Week 1 low (under 7/12).** Domain rules, range by inversion or completing the square, inverses and the domain/range swap, and composites. Re-read the Week 1 summary sections "Definitions" and worked examples (1)–(4). Symptom: you can differentiate nothing wrong, but you keep losing marks on what a function is even allowed to eat.
- **Week 2 low (under 7/12).** Holes versus vertical asymptotes, the three degree cases, and sign charts. Re-read Week 2 worked examples (1), (2) and (4). Symptom: you quoted the ratio of leading coefficients when the degrees were unequal, or you multiplied an inequality by an expression whose sign you did not know.
- **Week 3 low (under 8/13).** Algebraic limits: check the form, then factor, conjugate or combine. Re-read Week 3 worked examples (2), (3), (5) and (7). Symptom: you treated 0/0 as 0, or as "does not exist", or you applied L'Hôpital's Rule to k/0.
