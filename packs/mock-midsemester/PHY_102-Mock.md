## Marking scheme

*Each entry gives the answer, the week it tests, and the concept. Total 50 marks.*

**1. C** — Week 1. *Charging is transfer of electrons only.* Protons are bound in the nuclei and never migrate in a solid; a positive body is one with an electron deficit. A is the "positive charges move" misreading of the slides; B breaks conservation; D would leave every body neutral.

**2. D** — Week 2. *Properties of field lines.* Direction = the push on a positive test charge; line density = field strength. A reverses start and end; B is impossible, since crossing lines would give two field directions at one point; C confuses "uniform" with "radial".

**3. A** — Week 3. *The lost volts, Ir.* Energy per coulomb burnt inside the cell by its own internal resistance, so V = ε − Ir. B is a power (watts, not volts); C is IR, the terminal p.d.; D wrongly treats ε as something that shrinks with use during a calculation.

**4. B** — Week 4. *Dipole in a non-uniform field.* The two charges now sit in fields of different magnitude, so +qE and −qE no longer cancel: a net force appears, and it survives even at the orientations where the torque is zero. A is the uniform-field result; C and D deny the torque that plainly exists.

**5. C** — Week 1. *Induction without earthing gives only polarisation.* No charge entered or left the sphere, so removing the rod lets the free electrons redistribute and the sphere returns to neutral. A and B are the earthed outcomes; D correctly describes the near face *while the rod is there* — for a negative rod that face is positive — but not the state afterwards.

**6. B** — Week 3. *Motional emf and the flux Φ = Blx.* B is constant but x grows, so the flux through the circuit changes and Faraday's law gives ε = Blv. A is the "constant B ⇒ no emf" error inverted; C forgets that relative motion (or a changing field) is required; D confuses resistance with flux.

**7. A** — Week 4. *Currents and voltages superpose; powers do not.* P = I²R is quadratic, so the separate contributions must be added as currents first and squared afterwards. B, C and D all superpose power at some point.

**8. B** — Week 2. *Validity conditions of Coulomb's law.* Point charges or uniform spheres (centre-to-centre r), at rest, in vacuum or air. A drops every condition; C inverts the electrostatic requirement; D forgets that k is the vacuum value — in a medium of permittivity ε the force is smaller by ε/ε₀.

**9. B** — Week 1. *Quantisation with an algebraic sum (q = ±ne, then additivity).* Added charge = −(1.0×10¹¹)(1.602×10⁻¹⁹) = −16.0 nC; net = +8.0 − 16.0 = **−8.0 nC**. A drops the initial +8.0 nC; C treats the electrons as adding positive charge; D takes the initial charge as negative too.

**10. C** — Week 2. *Inverse-square scaling of E.* Tripling r divides E by 3² = 9: 900/9 = **100 N/C**. A divides by 3 (a 1/r law); B multiplies by 3; D multiplies by 9.

**11. D** — Week 3. *Energy stored in a capacitor, U = ½CV².* ½(220×10⁻⁶)(50)² = ½(220×10⁻⁶)(2500) = **0.275 J**. A drops the ½; B forgets to square V; C is a power-of-ten slip.

**12. A** — Week 4. *Torque on a dipole, τ = pE sin θ.* (6.0×10⁻¹²)(2.0×10⁵)(sin 30°) = (1.2×10⁻⁶)(0.500) = **6.0×10⁻⁷ N·m**. B uses cos 30°; C omits the angular factor altogether; D confuses "net force is zero" with "torque is zero".

**13. C** — Week 1. *Coulomb's law, and the sign rule.* F = (8.99×10⁹)(4.0×10⁻⁶)(6.0×10⁻⁶)/(0.15)² = 0.2158/0.0225 = **9.6 N, repulsive** — both charges are negative, so they are *like* charges. A is the sign flip; B forgets to square r; D leaves r in centimetres.

**14. B** — Week 3. *I = ε/(R + r), then V = ε − Ir.* I = 9.0/(7.5 + 1.5) = 1.0 A; V = 9.0 − (1.0)(1.5) = **7.5 V** (= IR ✓). A assumes V = ε; C uses I = ε/R = 1.2 A, leaving r out of the series total; D quotes the lost volts as the terminal p.d.

**15. D** — Week 4. *Real current source: current divider through the internal resistance.* I_load = I_s·r/(r + R) = 20 × 40/(40 + 10) = **16 mA**. A inverts the divider ratio; B computes a voltage of 800 V and divides by the load, ignoring the divider; C assumes the source is ideal.

**16. A** — Week 2. *Definition E = F/q₀.* E = 0.30/(6.0×10⁻⁶) = **5.0×10⁴ N/C due east**; the test charge is *positive*, so E is along F. B applies the negative-charge rule to a positive charge; C inverts the ratio; D multiplies instead of dividing.

**17. B** — Week 1. *Charging by induction with earthing.* The negative rod repels the sphere's free electrons; earthing lets them escape to earth; removing the wire *before* the rod traps the deficit, so the sphere ends **positive** — always the sign opposite the rod. A is the sequence for a *positive* rod; C gets the flow right but the resulting sign backwards; D forgets that induction needs no contact.

**18. C** — Week 3. *Two readings, one emf: ε = I(R + r).* 0.60(2.0 + r) = 0.40(3.5 + r) → 0.2r = 0.2 → r = 1.0 Ω; ε = 0.60(3.0) = **1.8 V** (check 0.40 × 4.5 = 1.8 ✓). A uses ε = IR on the first reading; B is an algebra slip that fails the check; D averages the two IR products.

**19. C** — Week 4. *Two-source network by superposition or nodal analysis.* Nodal at A: (V−10)/5 + (V−5)/5 + V/10 = 0 → 5V = 30 → V = 6.0 V, so I = 6.0/10 = **0.60 A**. (Superposition check: 0.40 A from the 10 V source alone plus 0.20 A from the 5 V source alone.) A adds the emfs and ignores the series resistors; B counts only the larger source; D treats the whole thing as one series loop, 15/(5+10+5).

**20. D** — Week 1. *Additivity, conservation and quantisation in one.* Identical spheres share the total equally: (+9.0 − 3.0)/2 = **+3.0 nC each**. The first sphere went from +9.0 to +3.0 nC, so 6.0 nC of negative charge arrived: n = 6.0×10⁻⁹/1.602×10⁻¹⁹ = **3.7×10¹⁰ electrons**. A forgets to halve; B uses the final 3.0 nC instead of the 6.0 nC actually transferred; C uses the 12 nC difference between the original charges.

**21. B** — Week 4. *Thévenin equivalent: open-circuit voltage, then sources zeroed.* With the load off, (V−24)/6 + (V−12)/3 = 0 → 3V = 48 → **V_Th = 16 V**. Short both sources: **R_Th = 6 ∥ 3 = 2.0 Ω**. A adds the emfs; C averages them; D adds the resistances instead of paralleling them.

**22. C** — Weeks 1 & 2. *Quantisation to get the charges (Wk 1), then Coulomb's law with the sign rule (Wk 2).* Each sphere carries q = (2.0×10¹²)(1.602×10⁻¹⁹) = 3.204×10⁻⁷ C, one positive (electrons removed) and one negative (electrons added). F = (8.99×10⁹)(3.204×10⁻⁷)²/(0.50)² = 9.23×10⁻⁴/0.25 = **3.7×10⁻³ N, attractive**. A forgets to square r; B gets the magnitude but calls unlike charges repulsive; D uses ε₀ in place of k.

**23. D** — Weeks 2 & 4. *Field of a point charge (Wk 2) feeding the dipole torque (Wk 4).* E = kq/r² = (8.99×10⁹)(2.0×10⁻⁶)/(0.30)² = 2.0×10⁵ N/C. τ = pE sin θ = (4.0×10⁻³⁰)(2.0×10⁵)(sin 60°) = **6.9×10⁻²⁵ N·m**. A omits sin θ; B uses cos 60°; C forgets to square r when finding E.

**24. A** — Weeks 1 & 3. *Q = CV and U = ½CV² (Wk 3), then quantisation n = Q/e (Wk 1).* Q = (50×10⁻⁶)(200) = 1.0×10⁻² C, so n = 1.0×10⁻²/1.602×10⁻¹⁹ = **6.2×10¹⁶ electrons**; U = ½(50×10⁻⁶)(200)² = **1.0 J**. B drops the ½; C uses ½CV as the charge; D forgets to square V in the energy.

**25. B** — Weeks 1 & 2. *Charge sharing on contact (Wk 1), then the null point between like charges (Wk 2).* Identical spheres share: (8.0 + 2.0)/2 = +5.0 μC each. Equal like charges → the forces cancel only **at the midpoint, 0.30 m from each**. **26.** — Week 1. *Conservation and quantisation of charge, stated and applied to charging by friction.* **[5]**
(a) **[1]** *Conservation:* the total charge of an isolated system is constant — charging never creates or destroys charge, it only transfers electrons. **[1]** *Quantisation:* charge exists only in whole-number multiples of the elementary charge, q = ±ne with e = 1.602×10⁻¹⁹ C; a net charge of, say, 0.5e is impossible.
(b) **[1]** Mechanism: **charging by friction**; the cloth carries **+7.2 nC** — equal in magnitude and opposite in sign, because the system rod + cloth started neutral and conservation requires the total to stay zero. **[1]** n = q/e = 7.2×10⁻⁹/1.602×10⁻¹⁹ = **4.5×10¹⁰ electrons**. **[1]** They moved **from the wool to the polythene** (the rod gained electrons, hence its negative charge; the cloth, the loser, is left positive).

**27.** — Week 2. *Coulomb's law with two source charges and 2-D vector superposition.* **[5]**
(a) **[1]** F₁ = k q₀q₁/r² = (8.99×10⁹)(3.0×10⁻⁶)(6.0×10⁻⁶)/(0.30)² = 0.1618/0.090 = **1.80 N**. **[1]** Both charges positive → repulsion → q₀ is pushed *away* from q₁, i.e. along **−x (due west)**.
(b) **[1]** F₂ = (8.99×10⁹)(3.0×10⁻⁶)(4.0×10⁻⁶)/(0.40)² = 0.1079/0.160 = **0.674 N**, directed along **+y (due north)**, since q₀ is attracted towards the negative q₂.
(c) **[1]** Components: ΣFₓ = −1.80 N, ΣF_y = +0.674 N; F = √(1.80² + 0.674²) = √3.687 = **1.92 N**. **[1]** θ = tan⁻¹(0.674/1.80) = 20.6°, so the resultant is **1.92 N directed 20.6° north of west** (159° anticlockwise from +x). *(No mark for 2.47 N — that is the arithmetic sum, not the vector sum.)*

**28.** — Week 3. *EMF defined and applied; motional emf and Faraday's law.* **[5]**
(a) **[1]** The emf of a source is the **work done by the source on each coulomb of charge** driven round the circuit — the energy per unit charge it supplies. **[1]** ε = W/Q, where W is the work (energy) in joules given to charge Q in coulombs; the SI unit is the **volt (V) = J/C**. (It is not a force: its dimensions are [M¹L²T⁻³I⁻¹].)
(b) **[1]** ε = Blv = (0.80)(0.25)(3.0) = **0.60 V**. **[1]** I = ε/R_total = 0.60/0.50 = **1.2 A**.
(c) **[1]** ε = −N dΦ_B/dt, with Φ_B = BA = Blx. B is constant, but the rod's motion increases x, so the enclosed **area** — and hence the flux — changes with time; it is the rate of change of flux, not the flux itself, that Faraday's law responds to.

**29.** — Week 4. *Dipole moment, dipole potential, and torque in a uniform field.* **[5]**
(a) **[1]** p = qd = (4.0×10⁻⁹)(5.0×10⁻³) = **2.0×10⁻¹¹ C·m** (coulomb-metre), directed **from the −4.0 nC charge towards the +4.0 nC charge** (physics convention).
(b) **[1]** V = (1/4πε₀)(p cos θ)/r² = (8.99×10⁹)(2.0×10⁻¹¹)(cos 60°)/(0.25)². **[1]** = (0.1798)(0.500)/0.0625 = **1.44 V**. *(Half a mark's worth of care: it falls as 1/r², not 1/r.)*
(c) **[1]** In a uniform field the forces +qE and −qE are equal and opposite, so the **net force is zero**. **[1]** τ = pE sin θ = (2.0×10⁻¹¹)(3.0×10⁵)(sin 60°) = (6.0×10⁻⁶)(0.866) = **5.2×10⁻⁶ N·m**; the torque turns **p into alignment with E** (θ = 0°, where τ = 0 and the dipole is stable).

**30.** — Weeks 2, 3 and 4. *Internal resistance and capacitor energy (Wk 3, 2 marks); uniform field and F = qE (Wk 2, 2 marks); dipole torque (Wk 4, 1 mark).* **[5]**
(a) **[1]** Week 3. I = ε/(R + r) = 12.0/(10.0 + 2.0) = **1.00 A**; the capacitor is across the 10.0 Ω resistor, so V_C = IR = **10.0 V** (= the terminal p.d., 12.0 − 2.0 ✓).
(b) **[1]** Week 3. C = ε₀A/d = (8.85×10⁻¹²)(4.0×10⁻³)/(2.0×10⁻³) = **1.77×10⁻¹¹ F**; U = ½CV² = ½(1.77×10⁻¹¹)(10.0)² = **8.85×10⁻¹⁰ J**.
(c) **[1]** Week 2. E = V/d = 10.0/(2.0×10⁻³) = **5.0×10³ V/m** (uniform between the plates). **[1]** F = |q|E = (1.602×10⁻¹⁹)(5.0×10³) = **8.0×10⁻¹⁶ N**, directed **opposite to E — from the negative plate towards the positive plate** — because the electron's charge is negative.
(d) **[1]** Week 4. τ = pE sin 90° = (3.0×10⁻³⁰)(5.0×10³)(1) = **1.5×10⁻²⁶ N·m** (the maximum-torque orientation; the net force on it is zero).

## How to read your score

**Overall, out of 50.** 40+ — solid; you are examinable on Weeks 1–4 as they stand. 30–39 — competent, but one week is leaking marks; find it below. 20–29 — the recall is there and the multi-step work is not; re-do every Section B question with the summaries open, then re-sit this paper in a week. Under 20 — treat Weeks 1–4 as unlearned rather than half-learned, and re-read all four summaries before touching Week 6 material.

**Week by week (each week is worth 12.5 marks of this paper).** Add up the marks you earned on the questions labelled with each week in the marking scheme; cross-week questions count half to each of their two weeks.

- **Below 8 out of 12.5 on Week 1** — the fault is almost always the charging methods, not the arithmetic. Re-read the Week 1 summary's induction paragraph and the conduction-vs-induction contrast, and redo Q17, Q20 and Q25 before Week 6.
- **Below 8 out of 12.5 on Week 2** — check whether you lost the marks to *directions* rather than to magnitudes. If Q27(c) or Q20 went wrong on the vector sum, re-read the Week 2 summary's worked examples 3 and 4 and the "adding magnitudes instead of vectors" note.
- **Below 8 out of 12.5 on Week 3** — re-read the Week 3 summary before Week 6, paying attention to two things only: I = ε/(R + r) rather than ε/R, and the difference between the energy U (joules) and the energy density u_E (J/m³). Those two account for most of what this paper tests from that week.
- **Below 8 out of 12.5 on Week 4** — separate the two halves. If the circuit questions (Q7, Q15, Q19, Q21) went wrong, re-read the zeroing rules and the ideal-source duality (r = 0 in series against r = ∞ in parallel). If the dipole questions (Q4, Q12, Q23, Q29) went wrong, re-read p = qd, the potential's cos θ, and the torque's sin θ.
- **If you lost most of your marks on Q22–Q25 and Q30 only**, no single week is at fault — the gap is in combining weeks. Re-do those five questions, writing down which week each step came from, rather than re-reading anything.
