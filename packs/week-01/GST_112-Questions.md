## Short answer

**13.** Name the three fundamental components of culture and give the course's own gloss for each in one phrase.

**14.** State the two categories of society and explain, in two or three sentences, how they differ on the three bases given in the course.

**15.** Define nation-building, then explain in three or four sentences why national integration is essential for Nigeria and what the case study identifies as the main hurdle.

## Answers

**1. a** — The course names them technological (material culture), sociological, ideological.
**2. c** — Sociological = institutions and norms; technological is material culture, ideological is beliefs and values.
**3. b** — Culture is stated as traditional behaviours, beliefs, practices and values.
**4. d** — Week 1 categorises society into communal and associational forms only.
**5. c** — The recap gives division of labour, social interactions, and traditions as the points of difference.
**6. a** — Marx's communist society: collective ownership of the means of production plus wealth distribution.
**7. d** — Functionalism is the organism analogy; Marx focuses on ownership and class instead.
**8. b** — Colonialism's stated impact on African societies was on governance, culture and family structures.
**9. a** — Comparative analysis completes the case study's six listed methods.
**10. c** — Both halves are required: mobilising resources *and* constructing a national identity.
**11. b** — Cosmas Odinaka, 2020, listed as a key reference.
**12. d** — University of Lagos, Centre for General Studies, Lagos, 2013.
**13.** Technological (material culture — tools and artefacts); sociological (institutions and norms); ideological (beliefs and values).
**14.** Communal and associational. Communal society has a simple, largely undifferentiated division of labour, close face-to-face social interaction, and strong binding traditions; associational society has a specialised division of labour, more impersonal or purpose-based interaction, and a weaker hold of tradition. The course fixes these three bases — labour, interaction, tradition — as the test.
**15.** Nation-building is mobilising resources for socio-economic and political development and constructing a national identity. Nigeria is a diverse nation, so without integration that diversity fragments loyalty and blocks shared development; integration binds many ethnic groups into one national community with a common identity. The case study identifies ethnic diversity as the central hurdle in Nigeria's nation-building journey, and evaluates government interventions and policies aimed at promoting unity.
