# GST_122 — Week 1 Summary

*Communication in English II · Week 1 (7–13 Sep 2026) · Topic: Language Skills*

## The 8 most examinable things this week

1. There are four key language skills: **listening, speaking, reading, writing**. Listening and reading are **receptive**; speaking and writing are **productive**.
2. **Listening** = the ability to understand, interpret, and respond to spoken language — distinct from **hearing**, which is merely the physical perception of sound.
3. **Active listening** involves full engagement (following a lecture); **passive listening** is hearing without deep comprehension (background music).
4. **Speaking** = the ability to articulate thoughts, ideas and information coherently and effectively.
5. The named components of effective oral communication: **clear pronunciation, appropriate tone, audience engagement, voice control, body language, delivery**.
6. **Reading** = the ability to comprehend, interpret and analyse written text — main ideas, context and subtext.
7. **Writing** = the capacity to convey thoughts, information and emotions through written language.
8. The **four writing styles**: persuasive, descriptive, narrative, expository — highly examinable as a set.

## The four writing styles (memorise exactly)

- **Persuasive** — writing intended to convince the reader to accept a viewpoint or take an action.
- **Descriptive** — writing that paints a vivid picture of a person, place or thing using sensory detail.
- **Narrative** — writing that tells a story, with a sequence of events.
- **Expository** — writing that explains or informs, presenting facts or a process in a clear, structured way.

## Interconnectedness (the standard essay question)

The lesson stresses that listening, speaking, reading and writing are not isolated skills but pieces that fit together: each influences and reinforces the others. Good listening improves what you can meaningfully speak or write about; strong reading builds the vocabulary and ideas that feed speaking and writing. Combining the four is presented as the route to sharper critical thinking, the ability to engage complex discourse, and a broader perspective on issues. The skills are framed as a lifelong, evolving journey rather than a fixed competency, central to academic and professional success and to personal growth.

A good essay answer names all four skills, states the receptive/productive split, and argues explicitly that mastery of one skill supports mastery of the others, ultimately sharpening critical thinking.

## Commonly confused

- **Listening vs hearing** — hearing is the passive physical reception of sound; listening is active understanding, interpretation and response.
- **Active vs passive listening** — active is full engagement and comprehension; passive is sound registering without deep processing.
- **Persuasive vs expository writing** — persuasive argues a position to change opinion or prompt action; expository explains or informs without arguing a side.
- **Narrative vs descriptive writing** — narrative tells a story, a sequence of events with a plot; descriptive paints a picture of a subject through sensory detail, not necessarily in sequence.
