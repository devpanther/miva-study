## Definitions and key terms

- **Language skills** — the four aspects of language that underpin communication: listening, speaking, reading, writing. They are interconnected and influence each other; they are a lifelong journey, not fixed tools.
- **Listening** — the ability to understand, interpret, and respond to spoken language. *Example:* listening to a TED Talk and summarising the main ideas. Forms/purposes named: listening tasks, audio books or textbooks, instructions, descriptions.
- **Hearing** — contrasted with listening (the lesson opens by asking the difference). Hearing is the bare reception of sound; listening adds understanding, interpretation and response.
- **Active listening** — listening with full engagement. *Example:* listening during a lecture.
- **Passive listening** — merely hearing without deep comprehension. *Example:* background music.
- **Speaking** — the ability to articulate thoughts, ideas, and information coherently and effectively. *Example:* a persuasive speech on a controversial topic ("The Legalisation of Assisted Suicide: A Case for Personal Autonomy").
- **Oral communication / effective speaking** — includes clear pronunciation, appropriate tone, the ability to engage the audience, voice control, body language, and delivery. *Example:* answering relevant questions during a job interview (Job Seeker A gives a structured, relevant, work-focused answer; Job Seeker B rambles about friends, TV and a cat — the negative model).
- **Structure of a persuasive speech (as demonstrated)** — *Introduction:* state your position ("I believe the legalisation of assisted suicide is a matter of personal autonomy…"). *Body:* historical context, arguments in favour (personal autonomy, compassion, reducing suffering), plus ethical considerations on the other side (sanctity of life, potential for abuse) — so that both sides are covered.
- **Reading** — the ability to comprehend, interpret, and analyse written text. *Example:* reading a complex research paper and extracting key findings.
- **Reading comprehension** — effective reading involves understanding main ideas, context, and subtext. *Example:* analysing a novel's symbolism and its impact on the story.
- **Writing** — the capacity to convey thoughts, information, and emotions through written language. *Example:* composing a well-structured persuasive essay (the model essay is "The Benefits of Regular Exercise").
- **Writing styles** — persuasive, descriptive, narrative, expository.
- **Skills/competencies credited for this lesson** — Active Listening, Effective Speaking, Reading Comprehension, Persuasive Writing, Critical Thinking.
- **Case study** — "Transforming Marketing Team Collaboration through Enhanced Communication Skills": skill-building sessions emphasising **emotive language** (message resonance) and **precision** in communication, then practical application on real-world marketing scenarios; result was improved team collaboration and project outcomes.
- **References** — Pinker (1995) *The Language Instinct*; Kermode (2015) *Speak So Your Audience Will Listen*; Dehaene (2009) *Reading in the Brain*.

## Commonly confused

- **Hearing vs. Listening** — Did meaning get processed and a response formed? No = hearing; yes = listening.
- **Active vs. Passive listening** — Is the listener fully engaged and comprehending, or is the sound just present in the background? Engaged = active; background = passive.
- **Reading vs. Reading comprehension** — Reading is decoding and analysing the text; comprehension is specifically grasping main ideas, context and subtext (the *meaning behind* the words).
- **Speaking vs. Oral communication (delivery)** — Speaking is producing coherent content; oral communication adds the delivery package: pronunciation, tone, voice control, body language, audience engagement.
- **Persuasive vs. Expository writing** — Is the writer taking a side and pushing you to agree (persuasive) or just explaining the facts neutrally (expository)?
- **Narrative vs. Descriptive writing** — Is there a sequence of events over time (narrative) or a static picture of detail (descriptive)?
- **Job Seeker A vs. Job Seeker B** — A is relevant, structured and work-focused (effective speaking); B is unfocused and irrelevant (the failure model).
- **Communication vs. big words/perfect grammar** — the course's own point: communication is about expressing ideas effectively, not about fancy vocabulary; grammar matters because it aids credibility and critical thinking, not for its own sake.
