## Most likely to be examined

1. **The three fundamental components of culture** — technological (material culture), sociological (institutions and norms), and ideological (beliefs and values). Learn all three names plus their bracketed glosses; this is the single most examinable list in the week.
2. **The two categories of society** — communal and associational — and the three bases on which they differ: division of labour, social interactions, and traditions.
3. **Karl Marx's vision of society** — a communist society characterised by collective ownership of the means of production and distribution of wealth.
4. **Functionalism** — the view of society as an organism in which each component plays a crucial role (i.e. every part contributes to the working of the whole).
5. **Definition of nation-building** — mobilising resources for socio-economic and political development *and* constructing a national identity. Both halves are required for full marks.
6. **The impact of colonialism on African societies** — it altered governance, culture, and family structures (the recap's exact three domains; the pre-colonial Yoruba community is the worked example).
7. **National integration in Nigeria** — why it is essential for a diverse country, Nigeria's obstacles to it (ethnic diversity above all), and government initiatives intended to foster unity.
8. **The definition of culture itself** — a complex system encompassing traditional behaviours, beliefs, practices, and values.

## Definitions and key terms

- **Culture** — a complex concept/system encompassing traditional behaviours, beliefs, practices, and values (source-stated).
- **Technological component of culture** — the material culture: tools, artefacts, technology (source-stated gloss: "material culture").
- **Sociological component of culture** — the institutions and norms of a people (source-stated).
- **Ideological component of culture** — the beliefs and values of a people (source-stated).
- **Society** — a grouping of people, categorised in this course into two forms: communal and associational (source-stated).
- **Communal society** — the form of society marked by simple/undifferentiated division of labour, face-to-face social interaction, and strong binding traditions (expanded from the recap's three bases of difference).
- **Associational society** — the form marked by complex/specialised division of labour, impersonal or contractual social interaction, and weaker hold of tradition (expanded, same basis).
- **Emergence of society** — the historical development of human society, treated in the week alongside Marx and functionalism (source-stated as a topic).
- **Communist society (Karl Marx)** — a society with collective ownership of the means of production and distribution of wealth (source-stated).
- **Functionalism** — the perspective that views society as an organism in which each component plays a crucial role (source-stated).
- **Colonialism** — foreign rule whose impact on African societies significantly altered governance, culture, and family structures (source-stated).
- **Nation-building** — mobilising resources for socio-economic and political development and constructing a national identity (source-stated).
- **National integration** — the binding of a diverse population into a single national community; presented as Nigeria's central challenge and the object of government initiatives (source-stated).
- **Case-study methods listed** — historical analysis, document review, case studies, government policy evaluation, surveys/interviews, and comparative analysis (source-stated; examinable as a list).
- **Skills claimed for the lesson** — cultural awareness, historical awareness, sociological understanding, interdisciplinary thinking, communication skills (source-stated).

## Commonly confused

- **Technological vs sociological component of culture** — if you can touch it or it is a made object, it is technological (material culture); if it is a rule, role or institution governing people, it is sociological.
- **Sociological vs ideological component** — sociological = the *structures* people operate inside (institutions, norms); ideological = what people *believe is true or good* (beliefs, values).
- **Communal vs associational society** — ask "is membership by birth and kinship with shared tradition (communal), or by joining for a shared purpose with specialised roles (associational)?"
- **Nation-building vs national integration** — nation-building is the *process of building* (mobilising resources + constructing identity); national integration is the *outcome/state* of a diverse population cohering as one nation.
- **Karl Marx's view vs functionalism** — Marx frames society around ownership of the means of production and class/wealth distribution; functionalism frames it as an organism whose every part serves a role for the whole.
- **Nation vs state (expanded)** — a nation is a people sharing identity; a state is the political-legal apparatus of governance. Nigeria is one state containing many nations, which is exactly why integration is a challenge.
- **Culture vs society** — culture is *what a people share* (behaviours, beliefs, practices, values); society is *the people themselves* organised as a group.
