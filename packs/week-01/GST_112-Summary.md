## Culture

**Culture** is the complex system of traditional behaviours, beliefs, practices and values shared by a people. It has three components:

- **Technological (material culture)** — tools, technology, and the physical/material objects a people produce and use.
- **Sociological** — the institutions and norms of a society (marriage, family, government, kinship rules).
- **Ideological** — the beliefs, values and worldview (religion, philosophy, moral values).

## Society

Society exists in two forms, differing along three dimensions — **division of labour, social interactions, and traditions**:

- **Communal society**: low, undifferentiated division of labour; informal and personal (kinship-based) social interactions; strong attachment to tradition.
- **Associational society**: high, specialised division of labour; formal and impersonal (interest- or contract-based) interactions; weaker attachment to tradition.

**Karl Marx's communist vision**: a society built on the **collective ownership of the means of production** and the **distribution of wealth**, eliminating private ownership as the basis of class division.

**Functionalism** views society as **an organism** in which each component (institution) performs a role that contributes to the survival and stability of the whole.

**Colonialism's impacts on African societies**: it altered indigenous **governance** structures, disrupted **culture**, and reshaped **family structures**.

## Nation-building and national integration

**Nation-building** = (1) mobilising resources for socio-economic and political development, and (2) constructing a national identity.

**National integration** = the process of unifying diverse groups into a cohesive nation. It is a major challenge in a country as ethnically diverse as Nigeria, and requires deliberate government initiatives to foster unity.

**Applied to Nigeria**: Nigeria's ethnic diversity (Hausa-Fulani, Yoruba, Igbo and hundreds of other groups) makes national integration especially difficult, a difficulty compounded by colonialism's disruption of pre-colonial governance and family structures and by the arbitrary grouping of diverse peoples into one colonial territory. This is why nation-building in Nigeria has required deliberate government initiatives aimed at forging a shared national identity alongside socio-economic development.

## Commonly confused

- **Culture vs society** — culture is the shared system of behaviours, beliefs, values and practices; society is the organised group of people who hold and live out that culture.
- **Communal vs associational society** — communal is kinship-based, informal, tradition-bound, low division of labour; associational is interest/contract-based, formal, weaker on tradition, high specialised division of labour.
- **Nation-building vs national integration** — nation-building is the active process of mobilising resources and forging a national identity; national integration is the resulting unification of diverse groups. Nation-building is the effort, integration is the outcome.
- **The three components of culture** — technological is material and tools; sociological is institutions and norms; ideological is beliefs and values. Students routinely swap the last two: institutions are the *structures*, beliefs are the *ideas behind them*.
