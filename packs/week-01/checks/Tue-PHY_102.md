# Tuesday — PHY_102 nightly check

*This session takes week 1's material - electric charge and the three methods of charging - and works it conceptually rather than numerically.*
*12 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 12.*

**1.** An object carries a net charge of −6.4 × 10⁻¹⁷ C. Take e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. How many excess electrons does it hold?
A. 2.5 × 10⁻³ electrons
B. 4.0 × 10² electrons
C. 1.0 × 10⁻³⁵ electrons
D. 4.0 × 10³ electrons

**2.** Rubbing an ebonite rod with fur moves 5.0 × 10¹¹ electrons from the fur to the rod. Take e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. What are the charges on the rod and on the fur afterwards?
A. Rod +8.0 × 10⁻⁸ C, fur −8.0 × 10⁻⁸ C
B. Rod −3.1 × 10³⁰ C, fur +3.1 × 10³⁰ C
C. Rod −8.0 × 10⁻⁸ C, fur 0
D. Rod −8.0 × 10⁻⁸ C, fur +8.0 × 10⁻⁸ C

**3.** Two identical metal spheres carry −8.0 nC and +2.0 nC. They are touched together and then separated. What charge does each sphere carry afterwards?
A. −3.0 nC
B. −5.0 nC
C. −6.0 nC
D. +3.0 nC

**4.** Point charges of +2.0 μC and +3.0 μC are held 3.0 cm apart in vacuum. Take k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C². What is the force between them?
A. 1.8 N, repulsive
B. 6.0 × 10⁻³ N, repulsive
C. 60 N, repulsive
D. 6.0 × 10⁻⁵ N, attractive

**5.** Two fixed point charges repel with a force F. The separation between them is then halved, with the charges unchanged. What is the new force?
A. 4F
B. 2F
C. F/4
D. F/2

**6.** A point charge Q = +4.0 nC is fixed in vacuum. Take k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C². Find the electric field at a point 2.0 cm from Q.
A. 9.0 N/C, directed away from Q
B. 1.8 × 10³ N/C, directed away from Q
C. 9.0 × 10⁴ N/C, directed toward Q
D. 9.0 × 10⁴ N/C, directed away from Q

**7.** At a point P the electric field is 5.0 × 10³ N/C pointing north. A charge of −2.0 μC is placed at P. What force acts on it?
A. 1.0 × 10⁻² N, north
B. 1.0 × 10⁻² N, south
C. 2.5 × 10⁹ N, south
D. 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ N, south

**8.** A positively charged rod is held near a neutral metal sphere on an insulating stand. The sphere is earthed for a moment, the earth wire is removed, then the rod is taken away. What is the sphere's final charge?
A. Zero
B. Positive, spread uniformly
C. Negative, spread uniformly
D. Positive on the near side, negative on the far side

**9.** A test charge of +2.0 nC placed at a point P feels a force of 6.0 × 10⁻⁵ N directed west. What is the electric field at P, and what does it become if the test charge is replaced by +6.0 nC?
A. 3.0 × 10⁴ N/C west; unchanged at 3.0 × 10⁴ N/C
B. 3.0 × 10⁴ N/C east; unchanged at 3.0 × 10⁴ N/C
C. 3.0 × 10⁴ N/C west; rises to 9.0 × 10⁴ N/C
D. 3.3 × 10⁻⁵ N/C west; unchanged

**10.** Point charges of +3.0 μC and −3.0 μC attract each other with a force of 8.1 N in vacuum. Take k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C². What is the separation between them?
A. 0.010 m
B. 0.10 m
C. 10 m
D. 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ m

**11.** At a point 5.0 cm from an isolated point charge Q the electric field is 7.2 × 10⁴ N/C, pointing toward Q. Take k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C². What is Q?
A. −4.0 × 10⁻⁷ C
B. +2.0 × 10⁻⁸ C
C. −2.0 × 10⁻⁸ C
D. −2.0 × 10⁻⁴ C

**12.** A negatively charged rod is held near a neutral metal sphere on an insulating stand. The sphere is never earthed. Which describes the sphere while the rod is near, and after the rod is taken away?
A. Near side negative, far side positive; net charge zero throughout
B. Near side positive, far side negative; the sphere is left positive after the rod goes
C. The sphere gains a net negative charge while the rod is near, and keeps it
D. Near side positive, far side negative; net charge zero throughout, and unpolarised after the rod goes

---

## Answers

**1. B** — *Counting electrons with q = ne.* q = ne, so n = |q|/e = 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁷ / 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 4.0 × 10² = 400 electrons. The charge is negative, so they are excess electrons.

2.5 × 10⁻³ divides the wrong way round (e/q); 1.0 × 10⁻³⁵ multiplies q by e instead of dividing; 4.0 × 10³ is a power-of-ten slip when subtracting the exponents (−17 − (−19) = 2, not 3).

**2. D** — *Charge transferred by friction from an electron count.* q = ne = (5.0 × 10¹¹)(1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹) = 8.0 × 10⁻⁸ C. The rod gained electrons, so it is −8.0 × 10⁻⁸ C; the fur lost the same electrons, so it is +8.0 × 10⁻⁸ C. Check: the total is still zero, as before rubbing.

The first option has the signs backwards (the receiver of electrons is negative); −3.1 × 10³⁰ C divides n by e instead of multiplying; 'fur 0' forgets that charge is conserved, so the fur must carry the equal and opposite charge.

**3. A** — *Charge sharing between identical conductors.* Charge is conserved and identical spheres share it equally: q′ = (q₁ + q₂)/2 = (−8.0 + 2.0)/2 = −6.0/2 = −3.0 nC on each.

−5.0 nC ignores the sign of the +2.0 nC and averages 8 and 2; −6.0 nC is the total, not halved; +3.0 nC drops the sign of the larger charge.

**4. C** — *Coulomb's law with two charges.* Convert 3.0 cm to 0.030 m and square it: r² = 9.0 × 10⁻⁴ m². F = kq₁q₂/r² = (9.0 × 10⁹)(2.0 × 10⁻⁶)(3.0 × 10⁻⁶)/(9.0 × 10⁻⁴) = 5.4 × 10⁻²/9.0 × 10⁻⁴ = 60 N. Both charges are positive, so the force is repulsive.

1.8 N forgets to square r (divides by 0.030); 6.0 × 10⁻³ N leaves r in centimetres (divides by 9); 6.0 × 10⁻⁵ N reads μC as 10⁻⁹ C and gets the direction wrong as well.

**5. A** — *Inverse-square dependence of the Coulomb force.* F ∝ 1/r². Replacing r by r/2 gives 1/(r/2)² = 4/r², so the force becomes 4F.

2F treats the law as 1/r; F/4 has the dependence upside down (a smaller gap must give a larger force); F/2 combines both errors.

**6. D** — *Field of a point charge.* E = kQ/r² with r = 0.020 m, r² = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ m²: E = (9.0 × 10⁹)(4.0 × 10⁻⁹)/(4.0 × 10⁻⁴) = 36/4.0 × 10⁻⁴ = 9.0 × 10⁴ N/C. The field of a positive charge points away from it.

9.0 N/C leaves r in centimetres (divides by 4); 1.8 × 10³ N/C forgets to square r; the 'toward Q' option has the magnitude right but the direction of a positive charge's field wrong.

**7. B** — *Force on a charge in a field, F = qE.* F = |q|E = (2.0 × 10⁻⁶)(5.0 × 10³) = 1.0 × 10⁻² N. The field direction is defined for a positive charge, so a negative charge feels the force the opposite way: south.

The 'north' option forgets the sign reversal; 2.5 × 10⁹ N divides E by q instead of multiplying; 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ N reads μC as 10⁻⁹ C.

**8. C** — *Sign of the charge left by induction.* The positive rod pulls the sphere's free electrons to the near side and leaves the far side positive. Earthing lets electrons flow up from the earth to cancel that positive far side, so the sphere now has extra electrons. Removing the earth wire first traps them; when the rod goes, they spread out. The sphere is negative, opposite to the rod.

'Zero' is what happens if the rod is removed before the earth wire; 'positive' is the sign given by conduction (touching), not induction; the separated near/far pattern is polarisation while the rod is present, not the final state.

**9. A** — *Field from the force on a test charge, E = F/q₀.* E = F/q₀ = 6.0 × 10⁻⁵ / 2.0 × 10⁻⁹ = 3.0 × 10⁴ N/C, and the test charge is positive, so E points the way the force does: west. Replacing it with +6.0 nC triples the force to 1.8 × 10⁻⁴ N, but E = F/q₀ is still 3.0 × 10⁴ N/C, because the field belongs to the source charges and not to whatever is placed in it.

East reverses the direction for a positive test charge; 9.0 × 10⁴ N/C makes the field follow the force; 3.3 × 10⁻⁵ N/C divides q₀ by F instead of F by q₀.

**10. B** — *Finding the separation from Coulomb's law.* Rearrange F = kq₁q₂/r² to r² = kq₁q₂/F = (9.0 × 10⁹)(3.0 × 10⁻⁶)(3.0 × 10⁻⁶)/8.1 = 8.1 × 10⁻²/8.1 = 1.0 × 10⁻² m², so r = 0.10 m, or 10 cm.

0.010 m is r² reported as r, the square root step forgotten; 10 m comes from inverting the fraction to r² = F/kq₁q₂; 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ m reads the charges as nanocoulombs.

**11. C** — *Finding a source charge from its field.* E = k|Q|/r², so |Q| = Er²/k = (7.2 × 10⁴)(0.050)²/(9.0 × 10⁹) = (7.2 × 10⁴)(2.5 × 10⁻³)/(9.0 × 10⁹) = 2.0 × 10⁻⁸ C. The field points toward Q, and field lines run into negative charges, so Q = −2.0 × 10⁻⁸ C, or −20 nC.

−4.0 × 10⁻⁷ C forgets to square r; −2.0 × 10⁻⁴ C leaves r in centimetres; +2.0 × 10⁻⁸ C has the right size but the wrong sign.

**12. D** — *Polarisation of a conductor without earthing.* The rod is negative, so it repels the sphere's free electrons to the far side and leaves the near side positive. Nothing enters or leaves the sphere, because there is no path to earth, so the net charge stays zero at every stage; once the rod is removed the electrons spread back and the sphere is neutral and unpolarised again.

The first option moves the electrons toward the rod instead of away; the second and third describe induction with earthing, which this sphere never had.
