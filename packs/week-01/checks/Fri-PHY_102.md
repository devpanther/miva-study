# Friday — PHY_102 nightly check

*12 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 12.*

**1.** Sphere X carries −16 nC. It is touched to an identical neutral sphere Y and separated. Y is then touched to an identical neutral sphere Z and separated. What charge does Z carry?
A. −8.0 nC
B. −5.3 nC
C. −4.0 nC
D. −2.0 nC

**2.** Charges are fixed on the x-axis: +2.0 μC at x = 0, +1.0 μC at x = 0.20 m and −1.0 μC at x = 0.30 m. Take k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C². What is the net force on the +1.0 μC charge?
A. 1.35 N in the +x direction
B. 0.45 N in the +x direction
C. 0.90 N in the +x direction
D. 1.35 N in the −x direction

**3.** Point charges of +4.0 μC and −2.0 μC are fixed 0.20 m apart. Take k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C². What is the resultant electric field at the midpoint between them?
A. 1.8 × 10⁶ N/C, toward the −2.0 μC charge
B. 5.4 × 10⁶ N/C, toward the +4.0 μC charge
C. 1.35 × 10⁶ N/C, toward the −2.0 μC charge
D. 5.4 × 10⁶ N/C, toward the −2.0 μC charge

**4.** In a hydrogen atom the electron is 5.3 × 10⁻¹¹ m from the proton. Take k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C². Take e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. What is the electric force between them?
A. 8.2 × 10⁻⁸ N, repulsive
B. 8.2 × 10⁻⁸ N, attractive
C. 4.3 × 10⁻¹⁸ N, attractive
D. 5.1 × 10¹¹ N, attractive

**5.** A +1.0 μC charge sits at the origin. A +3.0 μC charge is at x = 0.10 m and a −4.0 μC charge is at y = 0.10 m. Take k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C². What is the magnitude of the net force on the +1.0 μC charge?
A. 20.25 N
B. 4.5 N
C. 6.3 N
D. 0.90 N

**6.** Sphere A carries −6.0 nC and sphere B carries +2.0 nC. The spheres are not the same size. They are touched together and separated, and A is found to carry −1.0 nC. What charge does B now carry?
A. −2.0 nC
B. +3.0 nC
C. −4.0 nC
D. −3.0 nC

**7.** At a distance d from a point charge Q the electric field has magnitude E₀. What is the field at a distance 3d from a charge 3Q?
A. E₀/3
B. E₀
C. 3E₀
D. E₀/9

**8.** The field at a point is found by measuring the force on a +1.0 nC test charge and dividing by 1.0 nC. The test charge is replaced by +3.0 nC and the measurement is repeated. How do the force and the field compare with before?
A. Force unchanged, field one third
B. Force tripled, field unchanged
C. Force tripled, field tripled
D. Force unchanged, field tripled

**9.** An electron is placed 2.0 cm from a fixed point charge of +5.0 nC in vacuum. Take k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C² and e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. What is the field at the electron's position, and the force on the electron?
A. 1.1 × 10⁵ N/C; 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁴ N directed away from the charge
B. 2.3 × 10³ N/C; 3.6 × 10⁻¹⁶ N directed toward the charge
C. 1.1 × 10⁵ N/C; 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁴ N directed toward the charge
D. 11 N/C; 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁸ N directed toward the charge

**10.** A +1.0 μC charge is fixed at x = 0 and a +9.0 μC charge at x = 0.40 m. At which point between them is the resultant electric field zero?
A. x = 0.040 m
B. x = 0.20 m
C. x = 0.30 m
D. x = 0.10 m

**11.** Identical conducting spheres carrying +10 nC and −2.0 nC are touched together, separated, and placed with their centres 4.0 cm apart. Take k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C². What is the force between them?
A. 9.0 × 10⁻⁵ N, repulsive
B. 1.1 × 10⁻⁴ N, attractive
C. 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ N, repulsive
D. 3.6 × 10⁻⁶ N, repulsive

**12.** Two identical metal spheres, one carrying +6.0 nC and one neutral, are touched together and separated. A student says both now carry +6.0 nC, since the charge spreads onto the second sphere. What is wrong, and what are the true charges?
A. It breaks quantisation of charge; each sphere carries +3.0 nC
B. Nothing is wrong; each sphere carries +6.0 nC
C. It breaks conservation of charge; each sphere carries +3.0 nC
D. It breaks conservation of charge; the first keeps +6.0 nC and the second stays neutral

---

## Answers

**1. C** — *Charge through two successive contacts.* Each contact between identical spheres halves the charge in play. First contact: X and Y each get (−16 + 0)/2 = −8.0 nC. Second contact: Y and Z each get (−8.0 + 0)/2 = −4.0 nC. So Z has −4.0 nC (and X keeps −8.0 nC).

−8.0 nC stops after the first contact; −5.3 nC splits −16 nC three ways as if all three spheres touched at once; −2.0 nC halves one time too many.

**2. A** — *Net force from two collinear charges.* From the +2.0 μC charge (0.20 m away): F₁ = (9.0 × 10⁹)(2.0 × 10⁻⁶)(1.0 × 10⁻⁶)/(0.20)² = 0.018/0.040 = 0.45 N, repulsive, so in the +x direction. From the −1.0 μC charge (0.10 m away): F₂ = (9.0 × 10⁹)(1.0 × 10⁻⁶)(1.0 × 10⁻⁶)/(0.10)² = 0.0090/0.010 = 0.90 N, attractive, toward x = 0.30 m, so also +x. Both point the same way: net = 0.45 + 0.90 = 1.35 N in the +x direction.

0.45 N subtracts the two forces as if they opposed; 0.90 N ignores the +2.0 μC charge; the −x option has the direction of the attraction backwards.

**3. D** — *Superposition of fields at the midpoint.* Each charge is 0.10 m from the midpoint. E from +4.0 μC = (9.0 × 10⁹)(4.0 × 10⁻⁶)/(0.10)² = 3.6 × 10⁶ N/C, pointing away from it, that is toward the −2.0 μC charge. E from −2.0 μC = (9.0 × 10⁹)(2.0 × 10⁻⁶)/(0.10)² = 1.8 × 10⁶ N/C, pointing toward the −2.0 μC charge. Same direction, so they add: 5.4 × 10⁶ N/C toward the −2.0 μC charge.

1.8 × 10⁶ N/C subtracts the two fields as if they opposed; the 'toward +4.0 μC' option has the direction reversed; 1.35 × 10⁶ N/C uses the full 0.20 m separation instead of the 0.10 m to the midpoint.

**4. B** — *Coulomb force between electron and proton.* Both charges have magnitude e. F = ke²/r² = (9.0 × 10⁹)(1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹)²/(5.3 × 10⁻¹¹)² = (9.0 × 10⁹)(2.56 × 10⁻³⁸)/(2.81 × 10⁻²¹) = 2.30 × 10⁻²⁸/2.81 × 10⁻²¹ = 8.2 × 10⁻⁸ N. Opposite signs, so attractive.

The 'repulsive' option gets the sign of the interaction wrong; 4.3 × 10⁻¹⁸ N forgets to square r; 5.1 × 10¹¹ N uses e once instead of e².

**5. B** — *Adding perpendicular Coulomb forces.* From +3.0 μC: F = (9.0 × 10⁹)(3.0 × 10⁻⁶)(1.0 × 10⁻⁶)/(0.10)² = 2.7 N, repulsive, pushing the charge along −x. From −4.0 μC: F = (9.0 × 10⁹)(4.0 × 10⁻⁶)(1.0 × 10⁻⁶)/(0.10)² = 3.6 N, attractive, pulling along +y. The two are perpendicular, so the resultant is √(2.7² + 3.6²) = √(7.29 + 12.96) = √20.25 = 4.5 N.

20.25 N forgets the square root; 6.3 N adds the magnitudes as if they were parallel; 0.90 N subtracts them as if they were opposite.

**6. D** — *Conservation of charge in a transfer.* Charge is conserved: the total before is −6.0 + 2.0 = −4.0 nC, and the total after must also be −4.0 nC. B has whatever A does not: −4.0 − (−1.0) = −3.0 nC.

−2.0 nC assumes the spheres are identical and share equally, which the stem rules out; +3.0 nC has the sign of the remainder wrong; −4.0 nC gives B the whole total and forgets that A still carries −1.0 nC.

**7. A** — *Scaling E with both charge and distance.* E = kQ/r². Tripling Q multiplies E by 3; tripling r divides it by 3² = 9. Together: 3/9 = 1/3, so the field is E₀/3.

E₀ treats the distance dependence as 1/r, so the two factors of 3 cancel; 3E₀ changes the charge but forgets the distance; E₀/9 forgets the charge.

**8. B** — *Field as a property of the source, not the test charge.* F = qE. The field at the point is set by the source charges alone, so E is the same; the force on a three-times-larger test charge is three times larger. Dividing the tripled force by the tripled charge returns the same E.

'Field tripled' confuses E with F; 'force unchanged' forgets that F scales with q; 'field one third' divides the same force by a larger charge, but the force is not the same.

**9. C** — *Field of a point charge then force on an electron.* E = kQ/r² = (9.0 × 10⁹)(5.0 × 10⁻⁹)/(0.020)² = 45/4.0 × 10⁻⁴ = 1.125 × 10⁵ N/C, pointing away from the positive charge. Then F = eE = (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹)(1.125 × 10⁵) = 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁴ N, and the electron is negative, so the force is opposite to E: toward the +5.0 nC charge.

'Away' forgets the electron's sign; 2.3 × 10³ N/C leaves r unsquared; 11 N/C keeps r in centimetres.

**10. D** — *Locating the point where two fields cancel.* Between two positive charges the two fields oppose, so they cancel where the magnitudes match: k(1.0 × 10⁻⁶)/x² = k(9.0 × 10⁻⁶)/(0.40 − x)². Cancel k and take square roots: (0.40 − x)/x = √9 = 3, so 0.40 = 4x and x = 0.10 m, that is 0.10 m from the smaller charge.

x = 0.040 m uses the ratio 9 without taking its root; x = 0.30 m puts the null nearer the larger charge; x = 0.20 m is the midpoint, which would only work for equal charges.

**11. A** — *Charge sharing followed by Coulomb's law.* Identical spheres share the total charge equally, so each ends with q′ = (+10 − 2.0)/2 = +4.0 nC. Then F = kq′²/r² = (9.0 × 10⁹)(4.0 × 10⁻⁹)²/(0.040)² = 1.44 × 10⁻⁷/1.6 × 10⁻³ = 9.0 × 10⁻⁵ N, and both spheres are now positive, so it is repulsive.

1.1 × 10⁻⁴ N attractive uses the original charges and skips the contact; 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ N averages +10 and +2.0, dropping the minus sign; 3.6 × 10⁻⁶ N forgets to square r.

**12. C** — *Conservation of charge applied to a contact.* The student's answer makes the total +12 nC where it was +6.0 nC, so charge would have been created. Conservation of charge forbids that: the charge is shared, not copied, and identical spheres split it equally, giving (+6.0 + 0)/2 = +3.0 nC each with the total still +6.0 nC.

Quantisation is about charge coming in multiples of e and is not broken here; +6.0 nC each is the student's own error; leaving the second sphere neutral ignores that contact between conductors does share charge.
