## Short answer

**13.** Distinguish active listening from passive listening, and give the example the lesson uses for each.

**14.** Name the four key language skills in the order the lesson presents them, and give the course's definition of any two of them.

**15.** A newspaper column argues that assisted suicide should be legalised, opening with the writer's own position and then presenting arguments for it as well as ethical objections. Classify the writing style, and state which two elements of the lesson's persuasive-speech structure are visible.

## Answers

**1. c** — The slide defines listening as understanding, interpreting and responding to spoken language; hearing alone is not listening.
**2. a** — Sound is present but not comprehended; the slide's own example of passive listening is background music.
**3. d** — Effective speaking lists pronunciation, tone, audience engagement, voice control, body language and delivery; handwriting belongs to writing.
**4. b** — The reading-comprehension slide names main ideas, context and subtext.
**5. a** — This is the lesson's set example for reading: extracting key findings from a complex research paper.
**6. d** — The writing-styles slide names persuasive, descriptive, narrative and expository.
**7. b** — Job Seeker B is the negative model: irrelevant and unstructured, against Job Seeker A's focused answer.
**8. c** — The writing slide's exact wording is "convey thoughts, information, and emotions through written language".
**9. d** — The four takeaways are Undermining Legitimacy, Obstructing Access to Support, Misallocation of Resources, and Honesty and Understanding.
**10. a** — The listening slide's example is listening to a TED Talk and summarising the main ideas.
**11. b** — The case study's method emphasised emotive language for resonance plus precision in conveying marketing messages.
**12. c** — The closing "Language Skills" slide names critical thinking, engaging complex discourse and a global perspective.

**13.** Active listening involves full engagement and comprehension — example: listening during a lecture. Passive listening is merely hearing without deep comprehension — example: background music.
**14.** Listening, speaking, reading, writing. Listening = the ability to understand, interpret and respond to spoken language; speaking = the ability to articulate thoughts, ideas and information coherently and effectively; reading = the ability to comprehend, interpret and analyse written text; writing = the capacity to convey thoughts, information and emotions through written language. (Any two definitions.)
**15.** Persuasive writing — the writer takes a side and works to convince the reader. The visible structural elements are the introduction stating the writer's position, and a body that presents arguments in favour (personal autonomy, compassion, reducing suffering) alongside ethical considerations on the other side (sanctity of life, potential for abuse), so both sides are covered.
