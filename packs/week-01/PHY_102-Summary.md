# PHY_102 — Week 1 Summary

*General Physics II · Week 1 (7–13 Sep 2026) · Topic: Electric Charge*

## The 7 most examinable things this week

1. **The three properties of charge**: additivity, conservation, quantisation — be able to state and apply each.
2. **Quantisation**: q = ±ne, with elementary charge **e = 1.602×10⁻¹⁹ C** (electron −e, proton +e). Counting electrons is a guaranteed calculation.
3. **The three methods of charging** — friction, conduction, induction — and the **final sign** each produces.
4. **Induction with grounding**: sequence, direction of electron flow, and why the sphere ends up with the **opposite** sign to the rod.
5. **Conduction vs induction**: contact/same sign vs no contact/opposite sign.
6. **Coulomb's law**: F = k|q₁q₂|/r², **k = 8.99×10⁹ N·m²/C²** = 1/(4πε₀), ε₀ = 8.85×10⁻¹² C²/(N·m²).
7. **Electric field**: the region round a charge where it exerts force; E = F/q (N/C), weakens with distance.

## Core definitions (state these exactly)

- **Electric charge**: a fundamental property of matter that causes it to experience a force in an electric field. Two kinds: positive and negative. **Like charges repel, unlike charges attract.**
- **Additivity**: charges add *algebraically*. Q = q₁ + q₂ + … (a +5 μC and a −3 μC give +2 μC).
- **Conservation**: total charge in an isolated system is constant. Charging never *creates* charge — it only *transfers* electrons.
- **Quantisation**: charge occurs only in discrete units of e. A net charge of 0.5e is impossible.
- **Charging**: supplying electrons to, or removing electrons from, an object. **Only electrons move** in solids; a positive object is one with an electron *deficit*.
- **Static electricity**: build-up of charge on a surface that does not flow continuously as a current does.
- **Electric field**: region around a charged object where it exerts electric force on other charges; weaker the farther from the charge.

**Friction** — rubbing transfers electrons. The loser becomes positive, the gainer negative, by **equal magnitudes** (conservation).

**Conduction** — contact with a charged body; charge is *shared*, so both end with the **same sign**.

**Induction** — no contact. The rod's field redistributes the conductor's free electrons (near face gains the sign opposite the rod). Ground the conductor → electrons flow to/from earth → **remove the ground wire first, then the rod** → the conductor keeps a net charge **opposite in sign to the rod**, spreading uniformly. Extent of induction **increases as the rod is brought closer**.

## Worked example 1 — Coulomb's law

q₁ = +5.0 μC, q₂ = −2.0 μC, r = 0.30 m. Find the force.

1. Convert: q₁ = 5.0×10⁻⁶ C, q₂ = 2.0×10⁻⁶ C (use magnitudes).
2. Product: (5.0×10⁻⁶)(2.0×10⁻⁶) = 1.0×10⁻¹¹ C².
3. r² = (0.30)² = 0.090 m².
4. F = (8.99×10⁹)(1.0×10⁻¹¹)/0.090 = 0.0899/0.090 = **1.0 N**.
5. Sign: unlike charges → **attractive**, along the line joining them.

## Worked example 2 — quantisation

A sphere carries −4.8 μC. How many excess electrons?

1. q = 4.8×10⁻⁶ C.
2. n = q/e = (4.8×10⁻⁶)/(1.602×10⁻¹⁹).
3. n = **3.0×10¹³ electrons** (excess, because the charge is negative).

## Worked example 3 — superposition

+3.0 μC at x = 0 and −3.0 μC at x = 0.40 m; force on +2.0 μC at the midpoint x = 0.20 m.

1. Each distance r = 0.20 m, r² = 0.040 m².
2. From the +3.0 μC: F = (8.99×10⁹)(3.0×10⁻⁶)(2.0×10⁻⁶)/0.040 = 1.35 N, **repelled → +x**.
3. From the −3.0 μC: same magnitude 1.35 N, **attracted → +x**.
4. Same direction, so add: **2.7 N toward the negative charge.**

## Commonly confused

- **Conduction vs induction**: conduction needs contact and gives the *same* sign; induction needs no contact and gives the *opposite* sign.
- **Induction with vs without grounding**: without a ground the conductor is only *polarised* and returns to neutral when the rod leaves; with a ground it keeps a real net charge.
- **Charge vs charge carrier**: charge is the property; the electron is the carrier. Positive regions in a metal are electron *deficits* — protons never migrate. (The slides loosely say "positive charges move to one end"; read that as "electrons move away from that end".)
- **Electric field vs electric force**: the field E exists at a point whether or not a charge sits there; the force F = qE only exists when a charge is placed in it.
- **Attraction implies charge**: a charged rod also attracts a *neutral* object, by polarising it.
