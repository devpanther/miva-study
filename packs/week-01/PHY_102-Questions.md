# PHY_102 — Week 1 retention set

*General Physics II · Week 1 (7–13 Sep 2026) · sat seven days later, notes closed.*
*12 multiple choice, then 3 short answer. Take k = 8.99×10⁹ N·m²·C⁻² and e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C.*

**1.** Electric charge is best described as:
a) a form of energy stored on the surface of an object
b) a fundamental property of matter that makes it experience a force in an electric field
c) the flow of electrons through a conducting material
d) the force one charged object exerts on another

**2.** The smallest unit of electric charge, carried by a single electron, is approximately:
a) −1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C
b) −9.1×10⁻³¹ C
c) −8.99×10⁹ C
d) −6.0×10²³ C

**3.** "When several charges are present, the net charge of the system is the algebraic sum of the individual charges." This states the property of:
a) additivity
b) quantisation
c) conservation
d) polarisation

**4.** Which method charges an object without any contact between the charged object and the object being charged?
a) friction
b) conduction
c) all three involve contact
d) induction

**5.** A negatively charged metal sphere is touched to an identical neutral one. Afterwards:
a) both are neutral
b) the first is negative and the second positive
c) both are negative
d) the first is neutral and the second negative

**6.** Static electricity is:
a) a steady current of electrons flowing round a closed circuit
b) the attraction between a proton and an electron in an atom
c) charge accumulated on a surface, not flowing continuously
d) the field that surrounds every stationary mass

**7.** In the photocopier case study, the sequence that puts the image on the paper is:
a) the drum is charged, light discharges the exposed areas, toner sticks to the still-charged areas, then heat fuses it to the paper
b) light charges the drum, toner sticks to the uncharged areas, then the paper is cooled to set it
c) the paper is charged, the drum discharges it, then toner is sprayed on the discharged areas
d) toner is charged, the drum attracts it uniformly, and light then burns away the unwanted parts

**8.** An object carries a net charge of +3.2×10⁻¹⁵ C. This corresponds to:
a) an excess of 2.0×10⁴ electrons
b) a deficit of 2.0×10⁴ electrons
c) a deficit of 5.1×10⁻³⁴ electrons
d) an excess of 5.0×10⁻⁵ electrons

**9.** Two fixed point charges attract with a force F. The separation between them is then tripled. The new force is:
a) F/3
b) F/6
c) F/9
d) 3F

**10.** Identical conducting spheres carrying −6.0 nC and +2.0 nC are touched together and separated. Each then carries:
a) −4.0 nC
b) −8.0 nC
c) +4.0 nC
d) −2.0 nC

**11.** The electric field a short distance from an isolated negative point charge points:
a) radially away from the charge
b) in circles around the charge
c) nowhere — a negative charge produces no field
d) radially toward the charge

**12.** A neutral conductor is being charged by induction with a positive rod held nearby. The correct order of the final two steps is:
a) remove the rod, then break the earth connection
b) break the earth connection, then remove the rod
c) remove both at exactly the same instant
d) the order makes no difference to the final charge

**13. (short answer)** State the three properties of electric charge covered this week and give one sentence illustrating each.

**14. (short answer)** A neutral metal sphere on an insulating stand is to be charged by induction using a positively charged rod. Set out the steps in order, state the sign the sphere ends up with, and explain what goes wrong if the earth connection is broken *after* the rod is taken away instead of before.

**15. (short answer)** How many electrons must be removed from a neutral metal sphere to leave it with a charge of +1.6×10⁻⁶ C? If a second, identical sphere carrying the same charge is placed 10 cm away, what force acts between them? Show your arithmetic.

## Answers

1. **b** — charge is a property of matter, not energy, flow, or force; the force is a consequence of it.
2. **a** — e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C; 9.1×10⁻³¹ is the electron's *mass* in kg.
3. **a** — additivity is the algebraic (sign-respecting) sum; conservation is about the total not changing in time.
4. **d** — in induction the rod only comes near; the transfer of charge is to or from earth.
5. **c** — contact is conduction, so charge is shared and both end with the charger's sign.
6. **c** — the slides define static electricity as charge accumulating on a surface rather than flowing as a current.
7. **a** — the photoconductive drum is charged, light discharges it where the page is blank, and toner is attracted to the areas that keep their charge.
8. **b** — n = 3.2×10⁻¹⁵/1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 2.0×10⁴, and a *positive* charge means electrons are missing.
9. **c** — F ∝ 1/r², so tripling r divides the force by 3² = 9.
10. **d** — q′ = (−6.0 + 2.0)/2 = −2.0 nC each; the signs must be carried into the sum.
11. **d** — the field direction is that of the force on a positive test charge, which is attracted inward.
12. **b** — the earth path must be cut while the rod still holds the induced charge in place; otherwise the charge simply flows back and the sphere ends neutral.
13. Quantisation (q = ne, so 2.4×10⁻¹⁹ C is impossible); conservation (rubbing gives +4 nC and −4 nC, total still zero); additivity (+5 nC with −2 nC on one body is a net +3 nC).
14. Bring the positive rod near (electrons in the sphere are attracted to the near side, far side becomes positive) → earth the sphere (electrons flow *up* from earth onto the sphere) → break the earth connection with the rod still in place → remove the rod; the extra electrons spread out and the sphere is left **negative**, opposite to the rod. If the rod is removed first, nothing holds the extra electrons on the sphere and they drain back to earth, leaving it neutral.
15. n = 1.6×10⁻⁶ / 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = **1.0×10¹³ electrons removed**. F = (8.99×10⁹)(1.6×10⁻⁶)²/(0.10)² = (8.99×10⁹)(2.56×10⁻¹²)/(1.0×10⁻²) = 2.30×10⁻²/1.0×10⁻² = **2.3 N, repulsive**.
