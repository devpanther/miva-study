## Multiple choice

**1.** The elementary charge e is closest to:
A. 1.602×10⁻¹⁹ C  B. 1.602×10⁻¹⁶ C  C. 9.11×10⁻³¹ C  D. 8.99×10⁹ C

**2.** The three properties of electric charge emphasised this week are:
A. attraction, repulsion, neutrality
B. additivity, conservation, quantisation
C. conduction, induction, friction
D. magnitude, direction, polarity

**3.** Which method of charging *requires* direct contact between the charged body and the object being charged?
A. Induction  B. Polarisation  C. Conduction  D. Friction only

**4.** A **positively** charged rod is brought near an isolated neutral metal sphere. The sphere is earthed, the earth wire is removed, and then the rod is removed. The sphere is finally:
A. positively charged  B. negatively charged  C. neutral  D. positive on the near side, negative on the far side

**5.** The Coulomb constant k has the value:
A. 8.85×10⁻¹² N·m²/C²  B. 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/C²  C. 8.99×10⁹ N·m²/C²  D. 9.11×10⁻³¹ N·m²/C²

**6.** Two point charges of +1.0 μC each are 1.0 m apart in vacuum. The force between them is about:
A. 9.0×10⁻³ N, repulsive  B. 9.0×10⁻³ N, attractive  C. 9.0×10³ N, repulsive  D. 9.0 N, repulsive

**7.** Exactly 3.0×10¹³ electrons are **removed** from a neutral sphere. Its charge becomes about:
A. −4.8 μC  B. +4.8 μC  C. +3.0 μC  D. +4.8 C

**8.** "Static electricity" is best described as:
A. charge flowing steadily through a conductor
B. charge that has built up on a surface and does not flow continuously
C. the force between two moving charges
D. the field produced only by protons

**9.** An electric field is:
A. the force a charge feels when touched by another charge
B. the region around a charged object in which it exerts electric force on other charges
C. a property only of negatively charged objects
D. a region of constant strength at all distances from the charge

**10.** Two objects are rubbed together. The object that **gains** electrons:
A. becomes positively charged  B. becomes negatively charged
C. stays neutral because friction creates no charge  D. loses protons to the other object

**11.** Which of the following could **not** be the net charge on an object?
A. 1.602×10⁻¹⁹ C  B. 3.204×10⁻¹⁹ C  C. 8.0×10⁻²⁰ C  D. 4.806×10⁻¹⁹ C

**12.** A charged rod is used to induce charge on a sphere without touching it. The extent of the induction:
A. increases as the rod is moved closer to the sphere
B. increases as the rod is moved farther from the sphere
C. is the same at all distances
D. only occurs once the rod touches the sphere

## Short answer

**13.** State the law of conservation of charge, and use it to explain what happens to the total charge of a glass rod + silk cloth system when the two are rubbed together.

**14.** In two sentences, give the two differences between charging by conduction and charging by induction (mention contact and final sign).

**15.** An object carries a net charge of −1.0 nC. Calculate the number of excess electrons on it, showing your working.

## Answer key

**1. A** — value of the elementary charge (B, C, D are decoy magnitudes; C is the electron *mass* in kg).
**2. B** — the three properties of charge (additivity, conservation, quantisation).
**3. C** — conduction is the contact method; induction is explicitly contactless.
**4. B** — charging by induction always leaves the **opposite** sign to the rod (electrons flow *up* from earth to a positive rod).
**5. C** — the Coulomb constant, k = 1/(4πε₀); A is ε₀, B is the gravitational constant.
**6. A** — Coulomb's law with like charges: F = (8.99×10⁹)(1.0×10⁻¹²)/1.0 = 9.0×10⁻³ N, repulsive.
**7. B** — quantisation, q = ne: removing electrons leaves a positive charge, (3.0×10¹³)(1.602×10⁻¹⁹) ≈ 4.8×10⁻⁶ C.
**8. B** — definition of static electricity (accumulated, non-flowing charge).
**9. B** — definition of the electric field as a region of influence, weakening with distance.
**10. B** — charging by friction: the electron-gainer becomes negative.
**11. C** — quantisation: 8.0×10⁻²⁰ C is about 0.5e, not an integer multiple of e.
**12. A** — the field strength (and so the redistribution of free electrons) grows as separation falls.
**13.** *Conservation of charge* — the total charge of an isolated system is constant. Rubbing only *transfers* electrons: the glass loses n electrons (charge +ne) and the silk gains them (charge −ne), so the total remains **zero**.
**14.** *Conduction vs induction* — conduction requires physical contact and leaves the object with the **same** sign as the charging body; induction requires **no** contact and (with grounding) leaves it with the **opposite** sign.
**15.** *Quantisation applied* — n = q/e = (1.0×10⁻⁹)/(1.602×10⁻¹⁹) = **6.2×10⁹ excess electrons**.
