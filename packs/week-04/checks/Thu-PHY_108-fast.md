# Thursday — PHY_108 fast-hour check

*Verifying Kirchhoff's Current Law: the principle, sign convention, readings and KCL versus KVL.*
*Five questions, straight after the fast hour. Recall only. Score out of 5.*

**1.** Kirchhoff's Current Law follows from the conservation of what?
A. Energy
B. Momentum
C. Magnetic flux
D. Electric charge

**2.** Under this experiment's sign convention, I1 + I2 = I3 is written how?
A. I1 + I2 + I3 = 0
B. -I1 - I2 + I3 = 0
C. I1 - I2 - I3 = 0
D. -I1 + I2 - I3 = 0

**3.** How is the law verified from the recorded readings?
A. Comparing I1 + I2 with the measured I3
B. Averaging the three readings
C. Plotting I3 against the emf
D. Checking every reading is positive

**4.** The measured I2 is negative in every row. What does that mean?
A. The ammeter is wired backwards
B. R2 is too small
C. That branch's current leaves the junction
D. The supply emf is unstable

**5.** Kirchhoff's Second Law, KVL, concerns what?
A. Currents at a node
B. Voltages around a loop
C. Charge stored on a capacitor
D. Power dissipated in a resistor

## Answers

**1. D** — Charge is neither created nor destroyed nor stored at a node, so what enters must leave. Energy conservation is the idea behind the voltage law instead.

**2. B** — I1 and I2 enter node b so they are positive in equation 1 and negative when moved across; I3 leaves. Flipping the convention is legal but must never be mixed within one node equation.

**3. A** — For each emf setting you compute I1 + I2, keeping the sign of I2, and compare it with the third ammeter's reading. It is a row-by-row comparison, not an average.

**4. C** — A negative value simply means the current runs against the assumed positive sense, that is, out of the node. It is not an error, and it makes I1 + I2 smaller than I1.

**5. B** — KVL is the loop law for voltages; KCL is the node law for currents, and I1 + I2 = I3 is a KCL equation even where the deck's slide mislabels it.
