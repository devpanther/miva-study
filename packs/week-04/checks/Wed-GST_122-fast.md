# Wednesday — GST_122 fast-hour check

*Paragraph components, transition categories, and the punctuation marks and their jobs.*
*Five questions, straight after the fast hour. Recall only. Score out of 5.*

**1.** Which paragraph component states the main idea and usually opens the paragraph?
A. Coherence
B. Unity
C. The topic sentence
D. A supporting detail

**2.** Which quality means ideas are logically ordered and joined by cohesive links?
A. Unity
B. Coherence
C. Emphasis
D. One idea at a time

**3.** The transition word 'Furthermore' belongs to which category?
A. Contrast
B. Comparison
C. Emphasis
D. Addition

**4.** Which mark joins two related independent clauses without a coordinating conjunction?
A. Semicolon
B. Colon
C. Comma
D. Hyphen

**5.** What are square brackets used for?
A. Enclosing the writer's own aside
B. Marking added words inside a quotation
C. Indicating a number range
D. Joining a compound adjective

## Answers

**1. C** — The topic sentence previews the paragraph, so five paragraphs need five topic sentences. Everything after it is a supporting sentence; unity and coherence are qualities, not sentences.

**2. B** — Coherence is logical order plus links such as conjunctions and pronouns. Unity is having one central theme, so the morning-run example is unified but incoherent.

**3. D** — Furthermore adds to the point, alongside Additionally and Moreover. However and Nevertheless mark contrast, Similarly marks comparison, and Indeed marks emphasis.

**4. A** — A semicolon needs a complete statement on both sides. A colon introduces what the first half announced, a comma needs and, but or or between clauses, and a hyphen joins words.

**5. B** — Brackets mark your own insertion into someone else's quoted words, as in 'The city [London]'. Parentheses hold your own aside, an en dash spans a range, and a hyphen joins compounds.
