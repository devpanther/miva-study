# Wednesday — GST_122 fast-hour check

*Paragraph components, transition categories, and the punctuation marks and their jobs.*
*5 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 5.*

**1.** Every sentence in Tobi's paragraph is about exam stress, but the sentences jump from causes to remedies and back again with no linking words and no logical order. Which component does the paragraph satisfy, and which does it fail?
A. Unity; coherence
B. Coherence; unity
C. Topic sentence; unity
D. One idea at a time; topic sentence

**2.** 'The bridge was closed for repairs; as a result, traffic on the ring road doubled.' To which category of transition words does 'as a result' belong?
A. Addition
B. Sequence/Order
C. Cause and Effect
D. Contrast

**3.** 'The kit contains three items ___ a ruler, a compass and a protractor.' Which punctuation mark belongs in the gap?
A. Semicolon
B. Colon
C. Comma
D. Hyphen

**4.** A student writes 'a well known author' and is told a mark is missing between 'well' and 'known'. Which mark, and why?
A. En dash, to mark a range
B. Em dash, to set off information
C. Apostrophe, to show possession
D. Hyphen, to join a compound adjective before a noun

**5.** Which component of a good paragraph requires that all the advantages of a situation be covered in one paragraph and all the disadvantages in a separate one?
A. Unity
B. Coherence
C. One idea at a time
D. Topic sentence

---

## Answers

**1. A** — *Unity versus coherence in a paragraph.* Unity requires a single central theme with every sentence relating to it, which this paragraph has. Coherence requires ideas to be orderly and logically connected by cohesive links such as conjunctions and pronouns, which it lacks.

Reversing the pair confuses the two; the topic sentence and one idea at a time are the other two components, but the scenario says nothing about a missing main-idea sentence or a point carried across paragraphs.

**2. C** — *Sorting a transition word into its category.* 'As a result' sits in the Cause and Effect column with Therefore, Thus, Consequently and Accordingly: the second clause is the effect of the first.

Addition words (Furthermore, Moreover) add a point; Sequence words (First, Next, Finally) order steps; Contrast words (However, Nevertheless) oppose the previous idea.

**3. B** — *Colon versus semicolon.* A colon introduces a list, explanation or quotation after a complete statement; here the second half delivers the items the first half announced.

A semicolon joins two closely related independent clauses, and 'a ruler, a compass and a protractor' is not a clause; a comma there would run the list into the statement; a hyphen joins compound words.

**4. D** — *Hyphen versus dashes.* A hyphen connects the words of a compound adjective placed before a noun, as in 'well-known author' and 'state-of-the-art technology'.

An en dash indicates a range such as pages 10-15; an em dash sets off or emphasises information in place of parentheses or commas; an apostrophe marks possession or a contraction.

**5. C** — *One idea at a time.* One idea at a time means a paragraph focuses on one idea exclusively, any point introduced is fully discussed there, and a new paragraph never continues a point: advantages in one paragraph, disadvantages in another.

Unity is about every sentence relating to one theme within a paragraph; coherence is about logical order and cohesive links; the topic sentence is the sentence carrying the main idea.
