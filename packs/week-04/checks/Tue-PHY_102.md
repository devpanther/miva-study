**1.** A cell of emf 9.0 V and internal resistance 0.40 Ω delivers a current of 2.5 A to a load. What is the terminal voltage of the cell?
A. 10.0 V
B. 8.0 V
C. 9.0 V
D. 8.6 V

**2.** The terminal voltage V of a cell is plotted against the current I it supplies. The points lie on a straight line through (0 A, 6.0 V) and (2.0 A, 5.0 V). What is the internal resistance of the cell?
A. 0.50 Ω
B. 2.0 Ω
C. 6.0 Ω
D. 2.5 Ω

**3.** Charges of +3.0 nC and −3.0 nC are held 2.0 mm apart. What is the magnitude of the electric dipole moment?
A. 1.2 × 10⁻¹¹ C·m
B. 6.0 × 10⁻⁹ C·m
C. 6.0 × 10⁻¹² C·m
D. 1.5 × 10⁻⁶ C·m

**4.** A dipole has moment p = 4.0 × 10⁻¹² C·m. Taking k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C², find the potential at a point on the dipole axis 2.0 cm from the centre, on the +q side.
A. 1.8 V
B. 0 V
C. 4.5 × 10³ V
D. 90 V

**5.** A dipole of moment 5.0 × 10⁻¹² C·m sits in a uniform field of 2.0 × 10⁵ N/C, with p at 30° to E. What is the magnitude of the torque on the dipole?
A. 5.0 × 10⁻⁷ N·m
B. 8.7 × 10⁻⁷ N·m
C. 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ N·m
D. 5.0 × 10⁻⁶ N·m

**6.** A parallel-plate capacitor has plate area 4.0 × 10⁻² m² and plate separation 0.50 mm, filled with a dielectric of κ = 3.0. Taking ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m, find its capacitance.
A. 0.71 nF
B. 2.1 nF
C. 0.24 nF
D. 21 nF

**7.** An applied field of 8.0 × 10⁴ V/m is reduced to 2.0 × 10⁴ V/m inside a dielectric slab. What is the electric susceptibility χ_e of the material?
A. 4.0
B. 0.25
C. 3.0
D. 5.0

**8.** In a superposition calculation on a two-battery circuit, a 9 V ideal battery is being 'zeroed' so that the other battery can act alone. What replaces the 9 V battery in the diagram?
A. A break (open circuit)

C. A wire (short circuit)
D. The same battery reversed

**9. (show your working)** A cell of emf 6.0 V and internal resistance 0.30 Ω is connected to a 2.7 Ω resistor. Calculate the current, the terminal voltage, and the power wasted inside the cell. Show your working.

**10. (show your working)** A dipole has moment 6.0 × 10⁻¹² C·m. Taking k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C², calculate the field at a point 3.0 cm from the centre on the equatorial plane, give its direction relative to p, and state the axial field at the same distance. Show your working.

**11. (show your working)** A 50 pF air capacitor is charged to 200 V and then disconnected from the battery. A slab of dielectric constant κ = 4.0 is slid in to fill the gap. Calculate the charge on the plates, the new capacitance and the new voltage. Show your working.

**12. (show your working)** An electric dipole is placed in a uniform electric field at some angle to the field. Explain why the net force on the dipole is zero but the torque is generally not, and state the orientation at which the torque vanishes.

## Answers

**1. B** — *Terminal voltage of a real source.* On discharge the terminal voltage is the emf minus the drop across the internal resistance: V = ε − Ir = 9.0 − (2.5)(0.40) = 9.0 − 1.0 = 8.0 V.

10.0 V adds Ir instead of subtracting it (that is the charging case); 9.0 V ignores the internal resistance altogether; 8.6 V subtracts r without multiplying by the current.

**2. A** — *Internal resistance from a V against I graph.* V = ε − Ir is a straight line with intercept ε and gradient −r. Gradient = (5.0 − 6.0)/(2.0 − 0) = −0.50 V/A, so r = −(gradient) = 0.50 Ω. Units check: V/A is Ω.

2.0 Ω is the negative reciprocal of the gradient, which has units of siemens, not ohms; 6.0 Ω reads the intercept, which is the emf in volts; 2.5 Ω is V/I at the second point, the load resistance, not r.

**3. C** — *Dipole moment from charge and separation.* p = qd, where q is the magnitude of either charge: p = (3.0 × 10⁻⁹)(2.0 × 10⁻³) = 6.0 × 10⁻¹² C·m, directed from −q to +q.

1.2 × 10⁻¹¹ C·m uses 2q, adding the two charges as though the dipole were not neutral; 6.0 × 10⁻⁹ C·m leaves the separation in millimetres; 1.5 × 10⁻⁶ C·m divides q by d instead of multiplying.

**4. D** — *Potential on the axis of a dipole.* On the axis θ = 0, so V = kp cos θ/r² = kp/r² = (9.0 × 10⁹)(4.0 × 10⁻¹²)/(0.020)² = 0.036/(4.0 × 10⁻⁴) = 90 V.

1.8 V forgets to square r (0.036/0.020); 0 V is the value on the equatorial plane, θ = 90°, not on the axis; 4.5 × 10³ V divides by r³, which belongs to the field formula, not the potential.

**5. A** — *Torque on a dipole in a uniform field.* τ = pE sin θ = (5.0 × 10⁻¹²)(2.0 × 10⁵)(sin 30°) = (1.0 × 10⁻⁶)(0.50) = 5.0 × 10⁻⁷ N·m.

8.7 × 10⁻⁷ N·m uses cos 30° instead of sin 30° (cos belongs in the potential energy, not the torque); 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ N·m drops the angle factor entirely, which is the maximum torque at 90°; 5.0 × 10⁻⁶ N·m is a power-of-ten slip.

**6. B** — *Capacitance of a parallel-plate capacitor with a dielectric.* C = κε₀A/d = (3.0)(8.85 × 10⁻¹²)(4.0 × 10⁻²)/(0.50 × 10⁻³) = (3.0)(7.08 × 10⁻¹⁰) = 2.12 × 10⁻⁹ F ≈ 2.1 nF (rounded to two significant figures).

0.71 nF is the vacuum value ε₀A/d, forgetting the dielectric; 0.24 nF divides by κ instead of multiplying (a dielectric raises capacitance); 21 nF uses d = 0.050 mm, a power-of-ten slip in the separation.

**7. C** — *Dielectric constant and susceptibility from field reduction.* Inside a dielectric E = E₀/κ, so κ = E₀/E = 8.0 × 10⁴/2.0 × 10⁴ = 4.0. Then χ_e = κ − 1 = 3.0, dimensionless.

4.0 is κ itself, stopping one step early; 0.25 is E/E₀, the reciprocal of κ; 5.0 adds 1 to κ instead of subtracting it.

**8. C** — *Zeroing an ideal voltage source.* A zeroed ideal voltage source holds 0 V across itself whatever current flows, which is exactly what a wire does, so it is replaced by a short circuit.

An open circuit is what replaces a zeroed current source (it passes 0 A at any voltage), the commonest mix-up; a 9 Ω resistor confuses the emf with a resistance; reversing the battery leaves it active, and superposition needs it switched off.

**9.** *Current and terminal voltage of a cell driving a load.* Single loop: I = ε/(R + r) = 6.0/(2.7 + 0.30) = 6.0/3.0 = 2.0 A. Terminal voltage V = ε − Ir = 6.0 − (2.0)(0.30) = 5.4 V; check V = IR = 2.0 × 2.7 = 5.4 V. Power wasted inside the cell P = I²r = (2.0)²(0.30) = 1.2 W.

A correct answer states I = 2.0 A, V = 5.4 V and P = 1.2 W with units. Accept 5.4 V reached either as ε − Ir or as IR. I = 6.0/2.7 = 2.2 A (r forgotten) or V = 6.0 V (I = 0 assumed) are wrong.

**10.** *Equatorial and axial field of a dipole.* Equatorial: E = kp/r³ = (9.0 × 10⁹)(6.0 × 10⁻¹²)/(0.030)³ = 0.054/(2.7 × 10⁻⁵) = 2.0 × 10³ N/C, directed antiparallel to p (from +q toward −q). Axial at the same r: E = 2kp/r³ = 4.0 × 10³ N/C, parallel to p.

A correct answer gives 2.0 × 10³ N/C (accept 1.9 × 10³ to 2.1 × 10³), says antiparallel or opposite to p, and gives 4.0 × 10³ N/C for the axial value, exactly twice. Dividing by r² (giving 60 N/C) or stating the equatorial field is parallel to p is wrong.

**11.** *Dielectric inserted at constant charge.* Q = C₀V₀ = (50 × 10⁻¹²)(200) = 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ C. With the battery disconnected this charge cannot change. New capacitance C = κC₀ = 4.0 × 50 pF = 200 pF. New voltage V = Q/C = 1.0 × 10⁻⁸/2.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ = 50 V, which is V₀/κ.

A correct answer states Q = 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ C (10 nC), C = 200 pF and V = 50 V, and keeps Q fixed. Saying V stays at 200 V while Q rises to 4.0 × 10⁻⁸ C describes the battery-still-connected case and is wrong here.

**12.** *Force and torque on a dipole in a uniform field.* In a uniform field the two charges feel forces of equal magnitude qE in opposite directions (+q along E, −q against E), so the forces cancel and the net force is zero. But the two forces act along different lines, separated by the dipole's length, so they form a couple with moment τ = pE sin θ, which is non-zero unless sin θ = 0. The torque vanishes when p is parallel to E (θ = 0°, stable) or antiparallel (θ = 180°, unstable).

A correct answer mentions equal and opposite forces qE and −qE, the different lines of action giving a couple, τ = pE sin θ, and θ = 0° (accept 180° as well) for zero torque.
