# Monday — GST_112 fast-hour check

*The four constitutions, the early political parties, and the 1914 amalgamation debate.*
*5 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 5.*

**1.** A Governor consulted widely with Nigerians, renamed the Legislative Council the House of Representatives with 136 members, and made Nigerians ministers without ministries or portfolios. Which constitution is this?
A. Clifford Constitution, 1922
B. Richards Constitution, 1947
C. Macpherson Constitution, 1951
D. Lyttleton Constitution, 1954

**2.** Which of the following statements about the Clifford Constitution of 1922 is correct?
A. Lagos and the South were under legislative council law while the North was under proclamation
B. It introduced regionalism with a House of Assembly in each region
C. It was drafted after wide consultation with Nigerians
D. It gave Northern Nigeria strong representation in the council

**3.** A historian argues that trade, wars and shared origin legends already linked North and South long before 1914, so the amalgamation only put on paper what already existed. Which school of thought is this?
A. The amalgamation created a deeply divided nation
B. The amalgamation merely formalised pre-existing inter-group relations
C. The amalgamation was a jigsaw puzzle that could never be solved
D. The amalgamation was the work of Nigerian leaders acting from self-interest

**4.** Put these parties in the order they were formed: (A) National Council of Nigeria and Cameroon; (B) Nigerian National Democratic Party; (C) Action Group; (D) Nigeria Youth Movement.
A. B, A, D, C
B. D, B, A, C
C. B, D, C, A
D. B, D, A, C

**5.** The Lyttleton Constitution of 1954 introduced the federal system. What does a federal system mean here?
A. All law-making is done by one central legislative council for the whole country
B. Power is shared between the central and regional governments, each with its own law-making areas
C. The regions are governed by proclamation from the Governor
D. Each region has a House of Assembly with advisory functions only

---

## Answers

**1. C** — *Identifying a constitution from its provisions.* Wide consultation, the 136-member House of Representatives and ministers without portfolios are the marks of the Macpherson Constitution of 1951. Clifford (1922) was drafted with no local input and had a 45-member council; Richards (1947) also had 45 members and no consultation; Lyttleton (1954) was the work of a Colonial Secretary and introduced the federal system.

**2. A** — *Clifford Constitution of 1922.* Under Clifford, Lagos Colony and the Southern Provinces were subject to legislative-council-made laws, whereas the Northern Provinces were under proclamation. Regionalism came with Richards in 1947. Wide consultation was Macpherson's approach in 1951; Clifford's constitution had no local input. Northern Nigeria was not represented at all, which is one of the constitution's main criticisms.

**3. B** — *Two schools of thought on the 1914 amalgamation.* There are two schools of thought on the 1914 amalgamation: one that it merely formalised pre-existing inter-group relations, the other that it created a deeply divided nation. The historian's argument that links already existed is the first school. The jigsaw puzzle is only a metaphor for the diversity brought together, and self-interest is a criticism of the leaders of independent Nigeria, not a school of thought about the amalgamation.

**4. D** — *Chronology of early political parties.* NNDP 1923 (B), NYM 1935 (D), NCNC August 1944 (A), Action Group 1951 in Ibadan (C): the order is B, D, A, C. The first option puts the NCNC before the NYM, the second puts the NYM before Macaulay's NNDP, and the third puts the Action Group before the NCNC even though the AG was formed partly to forestall the NCNC's control of the Western Region.

**5. B** — *Definition of the federal system.* Under the federal system power was shared between the central and regional governments, and the regions and the centre each had jurisdiction in law-making over the areas allocated to them. A single central legislative council for all of Nigeria was the Richards arrangement. Government by proclamation was how the North was ruled under Clifford, and advisory functions only describes Lugard's Nigerian Council of 1914.
