# Friday — PHY_102 nightly check

*Problem-solving hour on the same Week 4 material of General Physics II.*

*It sets up and executes numerical work on real sources: finding current and terminal voltage from emf, internal resistance and load, with the power balance as a check.*

*Multi-source resistive networks three ways: by superposition, by nodal analysis with a chosen reference node, and by reduction to a Thevenin or Norton equivalent.*

*Then electric dipoles. Computing the dipole moment from charge and separation. The far-field potential k p cos theta over r squared. The axial and equatorial field magnitudes and directions. The torque and potential energy of a dipole in a uniform field.*

*Finally a parallel-plate capacitor problem with a dielectric inserted at fixed charge.*

*Emphasis throughout is on choosing the right relation, carrying units, and diagnosing the specific wrong routes that give plausible but incorrect numbers.*
*Sit cold, notes closed, about 15 minutes. Score out of 12.*

**1.** A cell of emf 12.0 V and internal resistance 0.50 ohm drives a 5.5 ohm load. What is the terminal voltage?
a) 10.9 V
b) 11.0 V
c) 12.0 V
d) 13.0 V

**2.** A 12 V source through a 4 ohm resistor and a 6 V source through a 2 ohm resistor both feed node A, which returns to ground through a 4 ohm resistor. Using superposition, what current does the 12 V source ALONE drive through the 4 ohm ground resistor?
a) 0.50 A
b) 1.50 A
c) 3.00 A
d) 0.75 A

**3.** For that same network, the 4 ohm ground resistor is treated as the load and removed. The Thevenin equivalent seen at node A and ground is
a) 8.0 V in series with 1.33 ohm
b) 6.0 V in series with 1.33 ohm
c) 8.0 V in series with 6.0 ohm
d) 9.0 V in series with 2.67 ohm

**4.** For the same network with the 4 ohm ground resistor in place, which KCL equation at node A is correct?
a) (V minus 12)/4 + (V minus 6)/2 = V/4
b) 12/4 + 6/2 = V/4
c) (12 minus V)/4 + (6 minus V)/2 = V/4
d) (12 minus V)/4 + (6 minus V)/2 + V/4 = 0

**5.** Two charges of 2.0 nC and minus 2.0 nC are 1.0 mm apart. What is the potential at a point 5.0 cm from the centre of the dipole, at 60 degrees to the dipole axis?
a) 0.18 V
b) 3.6 V
c) 6.2 V
d) 7.2 V

**6.** At the same distance r from a dipole, how does the field on the axis compare with the field on the equatorial plane?
a) Half as large, and parallel to p.
b) Equal in magnitude, but perpendicular to p.
c) Four times as large, because the axial field falls as one over r cubed while the equatorial field falls as one over r to the fourth.
d) Twice as large and parallel to p, while the equatorial field is antiparallel to p.

**7.** A dipole of moment 2.0e-12 C m sits at 30 degrees to a uniform field of 3.0e5 N/C. What is the torque on it?
a) Zero, because the net force on a dipole in a uniform field is zero.
b) 5.2e-7 N m
c) 3.0e-7 N m
d) 6.0e-7 N m

**8.** A 177 pF air-spaced parallel-plate capacitor is charged to 100 V and then disconnected from the battery. A slab of dielectric constant 5.0 is slid fully between the plates. What is the new potential difference?
a) 20 V
b) 50 V
c) 100 V
d) 500 V

**9. (explain why)** Explain why a student who finds the node voltage of a network with the load still connected, and then uses that value as V_Th, gets the wrong load current — and why the error vanishes as the load resistance grows.

**10. (explain why)** Explain why dividing the emf by the external resistance alone always overestimates the current from a real cell, and why the size of that overestimate depends on the ratio r to R rather than on r by itself.

**11. (explain why)** Explain why the formula V = k p cos theta over r squared must not be used at distances comparable with the charge separation, and what has to be done instead.

**12. (explain why)** Explain why the energy stored in an isolated charged capacitor falls when a dielectric slab is inserted, and where that energy goes.
