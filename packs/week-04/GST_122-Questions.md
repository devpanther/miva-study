## Multiple choice (12)

**1.** "The concept of body positivity, encouraging self-acceptance and diverse body appreciation, has become a subject of controversy in recent times. Advocates argue that it challenges societal norms. Critics claim it may unintentionally normalise unhealthy lifestyles. Debates surround concerns about potential exclusions. Ongoing discussions center on its impact on mental health." Which sentence is the topic sentence?
a) The first sentence  b) The second sentence  c) The third sentence  d) The last sentence

**2.** Which sentence breaks the coherence of this set: "In the morning, I woke up early to go for a run. The sun was shining brightly in the sky. My favourite breakfast is pancakes with maple syrup. After my run, I went to the park to read a book."?
a) Sentence 1  b) Sentence 2, because it does not logically connect to the run or breakfast that follow it  c) Sentence 3, because pancakes are unhealthy  d) Sentence 4

**3.** "The results were promising; ___, further testing is required before the drug can be approved." Which transition word correctly fills the blank?
a) Similarly  b) However  c) For example  d) Furthermore

**4.** "The manager approved the budget, the team celebrated." What is wrong with this sentence?
a) Nothing — it is a correctly punctuated compound sentence
b) It is a comma splice — a comma alone cannot join two independent clauses
c) It needs a colon instead of a comma
d) It needs an apostrophe after "manager"

**5.** "The key to success is simple; hard work, dedication, and persistence." Is the semicolon correct here, and why?
a) Yes — it joins two independent clauses
b) No — a colon is needed, because the second part explains/lists what "the key to success" is, not a second independent clause
c) Yes — semicolons always precede a list
d) No — a comma is needed instead

**6.** Which sentence correctly uses a colon?
a) "The team includes: writers, editors, and designers."
b) "The research project requires various elements: a literature review, data analysis, and interviews."
c) "She said: 'I will arrive at noon.'" (as the only punctuation needed)
d) "The manager was: late again."

**7.** "The student's submitted their essays a day late." What is the punctuation error?

**8.** "The company changed it's policy this year, and it's board approved the change." What is wrong?

**9.** "The well known author gave a lecture on state of the art technology." What punctuation is missing?

## Short answer (3)

**13.** Explain what a topic sentence does in a paragraph, where it can be positioned, and state the rule linking the number of paragraphs in an essay to the number of topic sentences.

**14.** Distinguish how a comma, a semicolon, and a colon each handle two independent clauses or an explanation — give one correctly punctuated example sentence for each mark.

**15.** Name at least four of the writing strategies covered this week (e.g., persuasion, narration...) and explain why the lesson compares writing strategies to programming languages.

## Answers

**1. a** — *the first sentence*: it states the paragraph's central claim (body positivity has become controversial); the following sentences are supporting sentences (advocates' view, critics' view, exclusion concerns, ongoing debate).
**2. b** — *sentence 2*: "The sun was shining brightly" makes sense alone but does not connect logically to waking up for a run or the breakfast that follows — this is what breaks coherence, not any factual claim about pancakes.
**3. b** — *however*: the sentence pivots from a positive result ("promising") to a contrasting caution ("further testing is required"), which is a contrast relationship, not addition, comparison, or illustration.
**4. b** — *comma splice*: two independent clauses ("The manager approved the budget" / "the team celebrated") are joined by a comma alone. Fix with a coordinating conjunction ("..., so the team celebrated") or a semicolon ("...budget; the team celebrated").
**5. b** — *colon needed*: "hard work, dedication, and persistence" is not an independent clause — it explains/lists what "the key to success" is. A semicolon requires two independent clauses on both sides; a colon is correct here.
**6. b** — *correct colon use*: it introduces a list of items ("a literature review, data analysis, and interviews") after a complete introductory clause. (a) wrongly inserts a colon after "includes" mid-clause; (c) and (d) misuse or omit the colon needed to introduce a quotation/point.
**7. b** — *"student's" -> "students"*: the sentence needs a plain plural (more than one student submitting), not a possessive at all — no apostrophe belongs in "students" here.
**8. b** — *"it's" -> "its"*: neither instance means "it is," so both should be the possessive pronoun "its," which never takes an apostrophe.
**9. b** — *missing hyphens*: "well-known" and "state-of-the-art" are compound adjectives sitting directly before the noun they modify ("author," "technology"), so both need hyphens.
**10. a** — *correct ellipsis*: the ellipsis sits inside the quotation marks, at the exact point where words from the original quotation were omitted, rather than outside the quote or replacing punctuation that changes the quoted meaning.
**11. b** — *process analysis*: an ordered, step-by-step explanation of how to do or make something is process analysis; chronological order narrates events over time (not necessarily instructional), and narration/comparison/description do different jobs.
**12. b** — *audience fit*: the case study's point is that deliberately choosing and practising writing strategies — matched to purpose and audience, then refined via feedback — improved tone/style fit and, in turn, engagement and campaign communication.
**13.** A topic sentence states a paragraph's main idea and previews what the paragraph will discuss, acting as a roadmap for the reader. It is most often the first sentence but can also appear in the middle or at the end of a paragraph. The rule: every paragraph has exactly one topic sentence, so an essay of five paragraphs needs five topic sentences — one central idea per paragraph.
**14.** A **comma** joins two independent clauses only together with a coordinating conjunction: "The results were significant, so the team celebrated." A **semicolon** joins two independent, closely related clauses with no conjunction: "The results were significant; the team celebrated." A **colon** introduces a list, explanation, or quotation after a complete clause, and what follows it need not be independent: "The key to success is simple: hard work and persistence."
**15.** Writing strategies covered this week include persuasion, narration, description, exposition, comparison, contrast, cause and effect, problem-solution, definition, chronological order, and process analysis (any four qualify). The lesson compares them to programming languages because each strategy has its own "syntax and rules" suited to a specific communication purpose — just as a programmer picks the right language for a task, a writer must pick the strategy that fits the purpose, audience, and desired outcome.
