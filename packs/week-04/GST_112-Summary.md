## Most likely to be examined

1. **Clifford Constitution, 1922** — first **written** constitution of Nigeria; introduced the **elective principle**; council of **45** members = **26 official (European) + 15 nominated + 4 elected Nigerians (3 Lagos, 1 Calabar)**. Causal chain: elective principle → emergence of political parties (NNDP, 1923) → establishment of newspapers.
2. **Nigerian Council, 1914** — set up by **Lord Lugard**, **36 members** including **6 unofficial**, **advisory functions only**; laid the foundation of constitutional development in Nigeria.
3. **Richards Constitution, 1947–1951** — **Sir Arthur Richards**; introduced **regionalism**; a regional House of Assembly in each of the three regions; **bicameral in the North, unicameral elsewhere**; of **45** council members **28 were Nigerians, 4 elected**; in **1949** two Nigerians were appointed to the Executive Council. Criticism: **no local consultation**; franchise still limited to **Lagos and Calabar**.
4. **Macpherson Constitution, 1951** — Macpherson became Governor in **1948**; **wide consultation** with Nigerians; the Legislative Council was renamed the **House of Representatives**, **136 members**, made supreme; the Central Executive Council was renamed the **Council of Ministers** (Governor as **Prime Minister** + **twelve ministers**). It **did not last beyond three years because of Enahoro's motion for independence**.
5. **Lyttleton Constitution, 1954** — introduced by **Sir Oliver Lyttleton**, a **Colonial Secretary, not a Governor**; introduced the **federal system**; created the office of **Premier** in the regions; **Lagos made Federal Capital Territory**; judiciary regionalised; revenue allocation based on **derivation**.
6. **Party founding dates and founders** — NNDP **1923** (Herbert Macaulay); NYM **1935** (Ernest Ikoli, Samuel Akisanya); NCNC **August 1944** (first President Herbert Macaulay, first General Secretary Nnamdi Azikiwe); Action Group **1951, Ibadan** (from Egbe Omo Oduduwa, 1945); NPC **1951** (from Jam'iyyar Mutanen Arewa; Ahmadu Bello, Sardauna of Sokoto, as President).
7. **Party → newspaper pairs** — NNDP → *Lagos Daily News*; NYM → *Daily Service*; NCNC → *West African Pilot*.
8. **Summary-PDF strand: the Amalgamation of 1914** and the **two schools of thought** on it — one that it merely **formalised pre-existing inter-group relations**, the other that it **created a deeply divided nation**. Named commentators: **Dudley, Olusanya, E. Osaghae, Claude Ake**, plus **Lord Lugard**.

## Strand 1 — Constitutional development

### Before Richards: the governors

| Years | Governor | What he did |
|---|---|---|
| 1914 | **Lord Lugard** | Set up the **Nigerian Council**: 36 members, 6 unofficial, advisory functions only; foundation of constitutional development |
| 1919– | **Sir Hugh Clifford** | Took over after Lugard's tenure ended; devised the **1922** constitution |
| 1926– | **Sir Graham Thompson** (source spelling; almost certainly Sir Graeme Thomson) | "No concessions to his credit" |
| 1930–1936 | **Sir Donald Cameron** | Abolished the **office of Lieutenant Governor**; abolished the **provincial court**; introduced the **doctrine of separation of powers** |
| 1935– | **Bernard Bourdillon** | Prepared the ground for a new constitution; appealed to **northern leaders** to join the legislative council |
| 1948– | **Sir John Macpherson** | Governor from 1948; produced the 1951 constitution |

(The source dates Cameron 1930–1936 and Bourdillon from 1935 — the overlap is the source's own.)

### The four constitutions

| Constitution | Year(s) | Author | Key provisions | Significance | Criticism / weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Clifford** | **1922** | Sir Hugh Clifford | First **written** constitution; political division into **three units — Lagos Colony, Southern Provinces, Northern Provinces**; Lagos and the South subject to legislative-council law, the **North under proclamation**; council of **45** (26 official Europeans, 15 nominated, 4 elected Nigerians: **3 Lagos, 1 Calabar**); **Cameroon became part of Nigeria** | Introduced the **elective principle** → emergence of **political parties** → encouraged **newspapers** | Governor had **veto power**; **no local input** in drafting; council predominantly white; **Northern Nigeria not represented** — laid the foundation for disintegration |
| **Richards** | **1947–1951** | Sir Arthur Richards (successor to Bourdillon) | Introduced **regionalism**; a **regional House of Assembly in each of the three regions**; **bicameral legislature in the North**, unicameral in the others; one **central legislative council** for the whole of Nigeria; **one Executive Council at the centre** | Of **45** members, **28 Nigerians**, of whom **4 elected**; **1949** — two Nigerians appointed to the Executive Council; tried to unite North and South under one legislative body | **Locals not consulted** in drafting; franchise remained **limited to Lagos and Calabar** |
| **Macpherson** | **1951** (Governor from **1948**) | Sir John Macpherson | **Wide consultations** with Nigerians, avoiding predecessors' mistakes; Legislative Council renamed the **House of Representatives**, **136 members**, made **supreme**; HoR members selected **through the regional legislature**; **Lagos Colony removed from the Western Region**, made an independent area; Executive Council of **Chief Commissioners (3 per region)**, the **Attorney General**, **Financial Secretary**, **Director of Medical Services**, **Director of Education**; **bicameral legislature for the Western and Northern Regions**; Nigerians made **ministers without ministries or portfolios**; Central Executive Council renamed the **Council of Ministers** — Governor as **Prime Minister** + **twelve ministers** | Merit: led to the formation of more political parties (**AG, NPC**) | Demerit: **did not last beyond three years**, ended by **Enahoro's motion for independence** |
| **Lyttleton** | **1954** | **Sir Oliver Lyttleton**, a **Colonial Secretary**, not a Governor | Introduced the **federal system** — power shared between central and regional governments; regional **Lieutenant Governors to be addressed as Governors**; centre and regions each had law-making jurisdiction over allocated areas; created the position of **Premier** as leader of government in the regions | **Judiciary regionalised**; **Lagos made Federal Capital Territory**; ministers controlled the departments in their ministries; **revenue allocation based on derivation** | Argued in the **House of Commons** that the three regions could not work effectively in a closely knit structure |

## Strand 3 — Summary-PDF: the Amalgamation of 1914

- **Event:** the **Amalgamation of 1914** united **Northern and Southern Nigeria** into a single nation, bringing together diverse ethnic groups, cultures, languages and traditions — represented metaphorically as a **challenging jigsaw puzzle**.
- **Two schools of thought** (examinable pair):
  1. The amalgamation **merely formalised pre-existing inter-group relations**.
  2. The amalgamation **created a deeply divided nation**.
- **Named figures:** **Dudley**, **Olusanya**, **E. Osaghae**, **Claude Ake** — differing perspectives on the event's consequences and on the role of Nigerian leaders. The case study examines texts, speeches and perspectives of **Lord Lugard**, **E. Osaghae** and **Claude Ake**.
- **Historical-context argument:** Nigeria's daunting challenges — **civil unrest, poverty, terrorism, underdevelopment** — are attributed to issues that existed **long before independence**; the **Nigerian civil war** and challenges to **national integration** are linked to the 1914 amalgamation.
- **Leadership and the British role:** leaders of independent Nigeria had opportunities to address the legacies of colonialism, but **self-interest often prevailed**; **British officials' lack of experience with multi-racial states** is noted.
- **Case study:** "The Amalgamation of 1914: Uniting Nigeria's Diversity" — objective: analyse the historical context and differing viewpoints; method: **historical research** on texts, speeches and perspectives.
- **Skills claimed:** critical thinking, historical research, perspective-taking, communication, civic engagement.
- **Key references given:** M. Ediagbonya, *Nigeria Constitutional Development in Historical Perspective, 1914–1960*, American Journal of Humanities and Social Sciences Research, vol. 4 no. 2 (2020); Walter Rodney, *West Africa and the Atlantic Slave-Trade* (Tanzania, 1967).

## Commonly confused

- **1946 vs 1947** — the NCNC delegation protested the "**1946** constitution"; the constitution itself is dated **1947–1951** and named after **Richards**.
- **Nigerian Council (1914) vs Legislative Council (1922)** — the Council was **advisory only, 36 members, 6 unofficial**; the 1922 Legislative Council made law and had **45 members with 4 elected Nigerians**.
- **45 members, twice** — Clifford 1922: **45** = 26 official Europeans / 15 nominated / **4 elected**. Richards: also **45**, but **28 Nigerians** of whom **4 elected**. Discriminator: *26 Europeans* = Clifford; *28 Nigerians* = Richards.
- **136 vs 45** — **136** is the **Macpherson House of Representatives**; **45** belongs to both Clifford and Richards.
- **Macpherson 1948 vs 1951** — **1948** he became Governor; **1951** he produced the constitution.
- **Lyttleton was a Colonial Secretary, not a Governor** — every other constitution-maker here was a Governor.
- **Regionalism vs federalism** — **Richards 1947** introduced **regionalism**; **Lyttleton 1954** introduced **federalism**.
- **Premier vs Prime Minister** — **Premier** = leader of government in the **regions** (Lyttleton 1954); **Prime Minister** = the title of the **Governor** in Macpherson's Council of Ministers (1951).
- **1935, twice** — the **NYM was formed in 1935** and **Bourdillon took over as Governor in 1935**.
- **1951, three times** — the **Macpherson Constitution**, the **Action Group** and the **NPC** all date from **1951**.
- **Macaulay's two parties** — he **founded the NNDP (1923)** and was **first President of the NCNC (1944)**; **Azikiwe** was NCNC's **first General Secretary** and became President only after Macaulay died.
- **Newspapers** — *Lagos Daily News* = **NNDP**; *Daily Service* = **NYM**; *West African Pilot* = **NCNC**.
- **Cultural body → party** — **Egbe Omo Oduduwa (1945)** → **Action Group (1951)**; **Jam'iyyar Mutanen Arewa** → **NPC (1951)**.
- **1944, twice** — the **NCNC was formed** in August 1944 and the **NYM was destroyed** by internal disputes in 1944.
- **What killed the Macpherson Constitution** — **Enahoro's motion for independence**, not the Lyttleton Constitution.
- **Ikoli/Akisanya vs Azikiwe/Awolowo** — Ikoli and Akisanya were the NYM's famous leaders; Azikiwe, Awolowo and Abayomi **joined later**, which is when the Lagos Youth Movement became the NYM.
