# GST_112 — Week 4 Summary

*Nigerian Peoples and Culture · Week 4 (28 Sep–4 Oct 2026) · Topic: Constitutional Development and the Formation of Political Parties in the Quest for Independence*

## The 8 most examinable things this week

1. **The four constitutions and their four men, in order:** **Clifford 1922** (Sir Hugh Clifford), **Richards 1946/1947–1951** (Sir Arthur Richards), **Macpherson 1951** (Sir John Macpherson), **Lyttleton 1954** (Sir Oliver Lyttleton). Learn the order, the year and the name together.
2. **Clifford introduced the elective principle** (which produced the political parties and the newspapers). **Richards introduced regionalism.** **Lyttleton introduced federalism.** These three "firsts" are the single most-asked thing in the topic.
3. **Clifford's numbers: 45-member legislative council — 26 official (European) members, 15 nominated, 4 elected Nigerians (3 Lagos, 1 Calabar).** Political division into **three units: Lagos Colony, Southern Provinces, Northern Provinces**. **Cameroon became part of Nigeria.**
4. **The Nigerian Council, 1914, set up by Lord Lugard** — purely **advisory**, **36 members including six unofficial members**; it **laid the foundation for constitutional development in Nigeria**. It is not a constitution.
5. **Macpherson's House of Representatives — 136 members**, the renamed Legislative Council, made **supreme**; the **Council of Ministers** with the **Governor as Prime Minister and twelve ministers**; **Lagos removed from the Western Region** and made an independent area. It **did not last beyond three years**, killed by **Enahoro's motion for independence**.
6. **Lyttleton 1954 package:** federal system, **judiciary regionalised**, **Lagos made Federal Capital Territory**, **revenue allocation based on derivation**, **Premier** created as leader of government in the regions, **Lieutenant Governors renamed Governors**.
7. **The five parties, founders and bases:** **NNDP 1923** — Herbert Macaulay, **Lagos**, *Lagos Daily News*. **NYM 1935** — Ernest Ikoli and Samuel Akisanya, *Daily Service*. **NCNC August 1944** — Macaulay first President, **Azikiwe first General Secretary**, *West African Pilot*, **Eastern**. **AG 1951, Ibadan** — Awolowo, from **Egbe Omo Oduduwa (1945)**, **Western**. **NPC 1951** — **Ahmadu Bello, Sardauna of Sokoto**, from **Jam'iyyar Mutanen Arewa**, **Northern**.
8. **The four criticisms of Nigerian political parties:** **ethnocentric syndrome; lack of political ideology; weakened the central government; sacrificed national interest for ethnic interest.**

## The constitutions — name, year, man, what it introduced, what it was criticised for

| Instrument | Year | Governor/author | Introduced | Criticised for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nigerian Council** | **1914** | **Lord Lugard** | Advisory body only; **36 members, six unofficial**; laid the foundation of constitutional development | Advisory functions only — no law-making power |
| **Clifford Constitution** | **1922** | **Sir Hugh Clifford** (took over from Lugard in **1919**) | Nigeria's **first written constitution**; the **elective principle**; three units — **Lagos Colony, Southern Provinces, Northern Provinces**; 45-member legislative council (**26 official European, 15 nominated, 4 elected Nigerians — 3 Lagos, 1 Calabar**); **Cameroon became part of Nigeria** | The **Governor had veto power**; **no local input** in the drafting; the council was **predominantly white**; **Northern Nigeria was not represented**, laying the foundation for disintegration |
| **Richards Constitution** | **1947–1951** (drafted/enacted **1946**) | **Sir Arthur Richards** (successor to **Bourdillon**) | **Regionalism**; a **regional House of Assembly in each of the three regions**; **bicameral legislature in the North, unicameral elsewhere**; a **central legislative council for the whole of Nigeria** (**45 members, 28 of them Nigerians, of whom 4 were elected**); in **1949 two Nigerians appointed to the Executive Council** | **Locals were not consulted** in the drafting; the **franchise remained limited to Lagos and Calabar** |
| **Macpherson Constitution** | **1951** (Macpherson Governor from **1948**) | **Sir John Macpherson** | **Wide consultation with Nigerians**; **House of Representatives** (renamed Legislative Council) of **136 members**, made **supreme**; members reached the HoR **through the regional legislatures**; **Lagos taken out of the Western Region** as an independent area; **bicameral legislature for the Western and Northern Regions**; Executive Council of **Chief Commissioners (one per region), the Attorney General, the Financial Secretary, the Director of Medical Services, the Director of Education**; **Central Executive Council renamed the Council of Ministers — Governor as Prime Minister plus twelve ministers**; **Nigerians made ministers but without ministries or portfolios** | **Ministers without portfolios**; **did not last beyond three years**, collapsing over **Enahoro's motion for independence**. *Merit:* it led to the formation of more political parties — **AG and NPC** |
| **Lyttleton Constitution** | **1954** | **Sir Oliver Lyttleton** — a **Colonial Secretary, not a Governor** like his predecessors | The **federal system**: power shared between **central and regional governments**, each with law-making jurisdiction over allocated areas; **Lieutenant Governors in the regions to be addressed as Governors**; the office of **Premier** as leader of government in the regions; **judiciary regionalised**; **Lagos made Federal Capital Territory**; **ministers to control the departments in their ministries**; **revenue allocation based on derivation** | Argued at the **House of Commons** that the **three regions could not work effectively in a closely knit structure** — hence the loosening into federation |

### The gap years between Clifford and Richards (1926–1943)

- **Sir Graham Thompson**, from **1926** — **no concessions to his credit**.
- **Sir Donald Cameron**, **1930–1936** — **abolished the office of the Lieutenant Governor**, **abolished the provincial court**, **introduced the doctrine of separation of powers**.
- **Sir Bernard Bourdillon**, from **1935** — **prepared the ground for a new constitution** and **appealed to the northern leaders to join the legislative council**.

## The political parties — name, year, founder, base, newspaper

| Party | Founded | Founder/leaders | Base | Newspaper |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **NNDP** — Nigerian National Democratic Party | **1923** | **Herbert Macaulay** | **Lagos** | ***Lagos Daily News*** |
| **NYM** — Nigeria Youth Movement | **1935** | **Ernest Ikoli** and **Samuel Akisanya**; later joined by **Azikiwe, Awolowo, Abayomi** | Began as the **Lagos Youth Movement**; first **national** outlook | ***Daily Service*** |
| **NCNC** — National Council of Nigeria and Cameroon | **August 1944** | First President **Herbert Macaulay**; first General Secretary **Nnamdi Azikiwe**, who became President after Macaulay died | **Eastern Region** | ***West African Pilot*** |
| **AG** — Action Group | **1951**, at **Ibadan**; from **Egbe Omo Oduduwa**, formed **1945** by **Awolowo, the Ooni and Ajasin** | **Obafemi Awolowo** | **Western Region** | — |
| **NPC** — Northern Peoples Congress | **1951**; **conservative**; grew out of the cultural organisation **Jam'iyyar Mutanen Arewa** | **Sir Alhaji Ahmadu Bello, the Sardauna of Sokoto** (President), **Tafawa Balewa**, **Yahaya Gusau**, **Aminu Kano** | **Northern Region** | — |

### Aims and achievements to quote

- **NNDP** — won **all three seats allocated to Lagos in 1923, 1928 and 1933**.
- **NYM aims**: fight for the improvement of **Yaba Higher College** by getting it **affiliated to a British university**; gain **independence**; work towards the **unity** of Nigeria. **Achievements**: the **first nationalist movement with a national outlook**; promoted national unity and consciousness; founded the ***Daily Service***; set the machinery in motion for political emancipation. In **1938 it won all the elective seats from the NNDP**; **internal party disputes destroyed it in 1944**.
- **NCNC aims**: **self-rule** for Nigeria; **spread political education**; **arouse political consciousness**; provide **a means of expression** for Nigerians. **Achievements**: helped the struggle for self-government; introduced and published the ***West African Pilot***; **raised funds to send a protest delegation to London against the 1946 constitution**; **formed the first regional government of the Eastern Region**.
- **AG aims**: mobilise **Western Nigerians to forestall the NCNC's control of the Western Region**; cooperate with other nationalist parties to win **independence**.
- **NPC objectives**: **unite the peoples of Northern Nigeria**; **retain northern regional autonomy**; **enhance the power of traditional Muslim rulers**.
- **How the parties advanced independence** (four points): **establishment of newspapers; sensitisation of Nigerians; protest; call for independence.**
- **Criticism of the parties** (four points): **ethnocentric syndrome; lack of political ideology; weakened the central government; sacrificed national interest for ethnic interest.**

## From the week's Summary PDF — the 1914 Amalgamation

- The **Amalgamation of 1914** united **Northern and Southern Nigeria** into one nation, likened to a **jigsaw puzzle** of ethnic groups, cultures and traditions.
- **Two schools of thought**: one holds the amalgamation **merely formalised pre-existing inter-group relations**; the other holds it **created a deeply divided nation**.
- **Named figures**: **Lord Lugard**, **Dudley**, **Olusanya**, **E. Osaghae**, **Claude Ake**.
- The **Nigerian civil war** and the failures of **national integration** are linked back to 1914; leaders of independent Nigeria had chances to address colonial legacies but **self-interest often prevailed**, and **British officials lacked experience with multi-racial states**.

## Commonly confused

- **Elective principle vs regionalism vs federalism** — **Clifford 1922 = elective principle** (the first vote); **Richards 1947 = regionalism** (the three regions get Houses of Assembly, but the country is still unitary); **Lyttleton 1954 = federalism** (power actually shared between centre and regions). This is the trio the exam swaps.
- **Richards vs Macpherson vs Lyttleton** — **Richards did NOT consult Nigerians**; **Macpherson DID** ("wide consultations, avoiding the mistakes of his predecessors"); **Lyttleton was not a Governor at all** but the **Colonial Secretary**. If a question says "consulted widely", it is Macpherson; "no consultation", Richards.
- **1946 vs 1947** — the Richards Constitution is dated **1947–1951** in the lecture, but the NCNC's London protest delegation is against **"the 1946 constitution"** — same instrument, drafted 1946, operative 1947.
- **Nigerian Council (1914) vs Clifford's legislative council (1922)** — the Nigerian Council was **advisory only, 36 members, Lugard**; Clifford's was a **law-making legislative council, 45 members**, with **elected** Nigerians. The **first written constitution is Clifford's, not the Nigerian Council**.
- **The two "45s"** — Clifford's council had **45 members with only 4 elected Nigerians**; Richards' central council also had **45 members, but 28 Nigerians of whom 4 elected**. Same total, different composition.
- **136 vs 45 vs 36** — **136 = Macpherson's House of Representatives**; **45 = Clifford's and Richards' councils**; **36 = Lugard's Nigerian Council**.
- **Who was where in Lagos** — Clifford gave Lagos **3 of the 4 elected seats** (Calabar 1); Macpherson **removed Lagos from the Western Region**; Lyttleton made Lagos the **Federal Capital Territory**. Three different Lagos facts, three different constitutions.
- **NCNC vs NPC vs AG founders and bases** — **NCNC: Macaulay then Azikiwe, Eastern, *West African Pilot***; **AG: Awolowo, Western, out of Egbe Omo Oduduwa, formed at Ibadan**; **NPC: Ahmadu Bello, Northern, out of Jam'iyyar Mutanen Arewa, conservative**. Note **AG and NPC were both formed in 1951** — the same year as the Macpherson Constitution, which is why the lecture credits Macpherson with producing them.
- **Macaulay's two parties** — he founded the **NNDP (1923)** and was also the **first President of the NCNC (1944)**. Azikiwe was the NCNC's **first General Secretary**, becoming President only **after Macaulay died**.
- **The newspapers** — ***Lagos Daily News* = NNDP**; ***Daily Service* = NYM**; ***West African Pilot* = NCNC**. Do not attach the *Pilot* to the NYM.
- **NYM's dates** — formed **1935**, beat the NNDP at the polls in **1938**, destroyed by internal disputes in **1944** — the same year the NCNC was formed. **Egbe Omo Oduduwa 1945 ≠ Action Group 1951**: the culture group comes first, the party six years later.
- **Enahoro** — his **motion for independence** is what broke the **Macpherson** Constitution (not Lyttleton's, which was the response to the breakdown).
