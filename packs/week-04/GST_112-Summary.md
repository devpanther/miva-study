# GST_112 — Week 4 Summary

*Nigerian Peoples and Culture · Week 4 (28 Sep – 4 Oct 2026) · Topic: Constitutional Development and the Formation of Political Parties*

## The 8 most examinable things this week

1. **The Nigerian Council (1914) and the governors before Richards.** Lord **Lugard** set up the Nigerian Council in **1914** — 36 members (including 6 unofficial), **advisory only**, but it laid the foundation for constitutional development. Chain of governors: **Hugh Clifford** (from 1919, wrote the first constitution) → **Graham Thompson** (1926, no concessions) → **Donald Cameron** (1930–1936, abolished the office of Lieutenant Governor, abolished the provincial court, introduced separation of powers) → **Bernard Bourdillon** (from 1935, prepared the ground for a new constitution, appealed to northern leaders to join the legislative council) → **Arthur Richards**.
2. **Clifford Constitution (1922).** Nigeria's **first written constitution**. Divided the country into **three units**: Lagos Colony, Southern Provinces, Northern Provinces (Lagos/South under legislative-council law, the North under proclamation). 45-member Legislative Council: **26 official (European), 15 nominated, 4 elected Nigerians (3 Lagos, 1 Calabar)**. Cameroon became part of Nigeria. Significance: introduced the **elective principle**, which triggered political parties and newspapers. Criticism: Governor held veto power, no local input in drafting, council predominantly white, the **North was unrepresented** — sowing the seed of disintegration.
3. **Richards Constitution (1947).** Introduced **regionalism**: three regions, with a **bicameral** legislature in the North only (others unicameral), plus one central Legislative Council for all Nigeria. Of the 45 Council members, **28 were Nigerian (4 elected)**; two Nigerians joined the Executive Council in **1949**. Criticism: locals were **not consulted** in drafting, and the franchise stayed limited to Lagos and Calabar.
4. **Macpherson Constitution (1951).** Macpherson became Governor in **1948**; consulted widely (unlike his predecessors). Created the central **House of Representatives (136 members, supreme)**, with members selected via the regional legislature. **Lagos became an independent area**, no longer part of the Western Region. Widened Executive Council (3 Chief Commissioners per region, Attorney General, Financial Secretary, Directors of Medical Services and Education); bicameral legislature in West and North; Nigerians made ministers but **without portfolios**; the Executive Council was renamed the **Council of Ministers** (Governor as Prime Minister + 12 ministers). Merit: spurred the formation of **more parties (AG, NPC)**. Demerit: **did not survive three years** — it fell to **Enahoro's motion for independence**.
5. **Lyttleton Constitution (1954).** Introduced by **Sir Oliver Lyttleton**, a **Colonial Secretary, not a governor** (unlike his predecessors). Argued the three regions could not work effectively as one closely-knit structure, so it introduced a true **federal system** (power shared between centre and regions). Lieutenant Governors were renamed **Governors**; created the office of **Premier** to lead each regional government. Significance: judiciary regionalised, **Lagos made the Federal Capital Territory**, ministers controlled their own departments, and **revenue allocation was based on derivation**.
6. **The five nationalist parties, by name.** **NNDP** (1923, Lagos, founder **Herbert Macaulay**, paper: **Lagos Daily News**) → **NYM** (1935, leaders **Ernest Ikoli and Samuel Akisanya**, grew from the Lagos Youth Movement once Azikiwe, Awolowo, Abayomi and others joined, paper: **Daily Service**) → **NCNC** (Aug **1944**, 1st President **Herbert Macaulay**, 1st General Secretary **Nnamdi Azikiwe** who became President on Macaulay's death, paper: **West African Pilot**) → **Action Group** (Ibadan, **1951**, grew out of **Egbe Omo Oduduwa** — founded 1945 by Awolowo, the Ooni, and Ajasin — aim: forestall NCNC control of the Western Region) → **NPC** (**1951**, conservative, grew out of the cultural body **Jamiyyar Mutanen Arewa**, founders **Ahmadu Bello (Sardauna of Sokoto, President), Tafawa Balewa, Yahaya Gusau, Aminu Kano**).
7. **Party performance and criticism.** NNDP won **all three Lagos seats in 1923, 1928 and 1933**; NYM won **all elective seats from the NNDP in 1938**, then was destroyed by **internal disputes in 1944**. Standing criticisms of Nigerian parties generally: **ethnocentric syndrome, lack of political ideology, weakened the central government, sacrificed national interest for ethnic interest**. Parties pushed independence through: **establishing newspapers, sensitising Nigerians, protest, and the call for independence**.
8. **Summary PDF — the 1914 Amalgamation.** The Summary PDF actually looks back at the **Amalgamation of 1914** (Northern + Southern Nigeria into one nation), framed as a "jigsaw puzzle." Two schools of thought: one that it merely **formalised pre-existing inter-group relations**, the other that it **created a deeply divided nation**. Key figures cited: **Dudley, Olusanya, E. Osaghae, Claude Ake**, and **Lord Lugard**. Today's civil unrest, poverty and terrorism are traced back to unresolved pre-independence issues.

## Commonly confused

- **Richards (1947) vs Macpherson (1951) vs Lyttleton (1954)** — three different constitutions, four years apart each: Richards brought regionalism, Macpherson brought the House of Representatives, Lyttleton brought the federal system and Premiers. Don't merge their provisions.
- **Clifford vs Lyttleton — who governed vs who legislated from London.** Clifford, Richards, Macpherson were all **Governors** who produced their constitutions in post. Lyttleton was a **Colonial Secretary**, not a governor — the source flags this explicitly.
- **Donald Cameron (1930–1936) vs Bernard Bourdillon (from 1935)** — Cameron abolished the Lieutenant Governor's office and the provincial court and introduced separation of powers; Bourdillon prepared the ground for the *next* constitution and courted northern leaders. Different governors, different contributions, overlapping years.
- **Herbert Macaulay vs Nnamdi Azikiwe in the NCNC** — Macaulay was NCNC's **first President** (and NNDP's founder); Azikiwe was NCNC's **first General Secretary**, becoming President only after Macaulay died. Don't swap their starting roles.
- **NYM vs NCNC** — both are "national outlook" parties but a generation apart: NYM (1935, Ikoli/Akisanya, beat the NNDP in 1938, collapsed 1944) is not the same organisation as NCNC (Aug 1944, Macaulay/Azikiwe, published the West African Pilot).
- **Egbe Omo Oduduwa (1945) vs Action Group (1951)** — Egbe Omo Oduduwa was the founding **cultural** organisation (Awolowo, the Ooni, Ajasin); the Action Group is the **political party** that formed out of it six years later, in Ibadan. The same pattern applies to **NPC vs Jamiyyar Mutanen Arewa** — the party (1951) grew out of the earlier cultural body.
- **The three newspapers, matched to their parties** — Lagos Daily News (NNDP), Daily Service (NYM), West African Pilot (NCNC). Neither AG nor NPC has a newspaper named in the source — don't invent one.
- **1938 vs 1944** — 1938 is when NYM beat NNDP in every elective seat; 1944 is the year NYM was destroyed by internal disputes, which is *also* the year NCNC was founded (August 1944). Two unrelated events sharing a year.
