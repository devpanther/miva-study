## Multiple choice

**1.** For a cell of emf ε and internal resistance r delivering a current I to an external load R, the terminal voltage is
a) V = ε + Ir
b) V = ε − Ir
c) V = ε − I(R + r)
d) V = ε/(R + r)

**2.** In a superposition analysis, an ideal current source that is being "zeroed" is replaced by
a) a short circuit
b) a resistor equal to its internal resistance of zero ohms
c) a 1 Ω resistor carrying the original current

**3.** For two charges +q and −q separated by d, the electric dipole moment vector points
a) from −q to +q, with magnitude qd
b) from +q to −q, with magnitude qd
c) from −q to +q, with magnitude 2qd
d) from +q to −q, with magnitude 2qd

**4.** At a point on the perpendicular bisector (equatorial plane) of a dipole, far from it,
a) the potential is kp/r² and is a maximum there
b) the potential is kp/r³ and the field is kp/r²
c) the potential is zero while the field is kp/r³ directed antiparallel to p
d) both the potential and the field are zero

**5.** A cell of emf 9.0 V and internal resistance 1.0 Ω is connected to an 8.0 Ω resistor. The terminal voltage is

b) 7.9 V

**6.** Superposition may legitimately be used to find
a) the power dissipated in each resistor of a two-battery circuit
b) the current in a circuit containing a diode

**7.** The Thévenin resistance of a linear network at a pair of terminals is found by

## Short answer

**13.** A cell of emf 6.0 V has a terminal voltage of 5.4 V when it supplies 1.2 A. Find its internal resistance, and state the terminal voltage when the current is 2.0 A. Explain in one sentence what the terminal voltage would read on open circuit.

**14.** A 12 V source with negligible internal resistance is in series with a 6.0 Ω resistor; a 3.0 Ω resistor runs from that junction to ground, and the output terminals are taken across the 3.0 Ω resistor. Find the Thévenin equivalent (V_Th and R_Th) at those terminals, then find the current that flows when a 6.0 Ω load is connected across them.

**15.** A dipole of moment 5.0×10⁻¹² C·m sits in a uniform field of 2.0×10⁵ N/C. Find (i) the torque when p is perpendicular to E, and (ii) the work needed to turn the dipole from θ = 0° to θ = 180°. State what the net force on the dipole is in each case and why.

## Answers

**1. b** — V = ε − Ir: the emf minus the drop across the internal resistance; (c) double-counts the external drop.
**2. d** — a source forced to deliver zero current at any voltage behaves as a break in the circuit; the short-circuit rule belongs to a zeroed *voltage* source.
**3. a** — p = qd, directed from the negative to the positive charge in the physics convention; q is the magnitude of one charge, not both.
**4. c** — cos 90° = 0 so V = 0 all over the equatorial plane, but the surviving field components add to kp/r³ pointing from +q toward −q.
**5. a** — I = 9.0/(8.0 + 1.0) = 1.0 A, so V = 9.0 − (1.0)(1.0) = 8.0 V; 7.9 V comes from wrongly using I = 9.0/8.0.
**6. c** — superposition needs linear elements and applies to currents and voltages; power is quadratic, and a diode is non-linear.
**7. b** — zeroing removes the sources' driving action but leaves every resistance in place; option (c) is the two rules swapped.
**8. d** — τ = p × E has magnitude pE sin θ, which peaks at θ = 90° and vanishes when p is aligned with E.
**9. a** — with the battery disconnected Q is fixed, so polarisation charge cuts E and V by κ while C = Q/V rises by κ.
**10. c** — charge × length gives C·m; dimensionally [I T][L] = [M⁰ L¹ T¹ I¹].
**11. b** — E = 2kp/r³ = 2(8.99×10⁹)(4.0×10⁻¹²)/(8.0×10⁻⁶) ≈ 9.0×10³ N/C; 4.5×10³ N/C would be the equatorial value.
**12. d** — χ_e = κ − 1 = 5.0.
**13.** r = (6.0 − 5.4)/1.2 = 0.50 Ω; at 2.0 A, V = 6.0 − (2.0)(0.50) = 5.0 V. On open circuit I = 0, so the meter reads the full emf, 6.0 V.
**14.** V_Th = 12 × 3.0/(6.0 + 3.0) = 4.0 V; R_Th = 6.0 ∥ 3.0 = 2.0 Ω (source shorted). With the 6.0 Ω load, I = 4.0/(2.0 + 6.0) = 0.50 A.
**15.** (i) τ = pE sin 90° = (5.0×10⁻¹²)(2.0×10⁵) = 1.0×10⁻⁶ N·m. (ii) W = ΔU = (+pE) − (−pE) = 2pE = 2.0×10⁻⁶ J. The net force is zero throughout, because the field is uniform: the forces qE and −qE are equal and opposite, and only their line of action differs, which produces torque but no translation.
