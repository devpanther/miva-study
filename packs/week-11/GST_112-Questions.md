## Section B — Short answer (3)

**B1.** State Herbert Macaulay's contribution to Nigerian nationalism, naming the policies he protested, the party he founded with its year, and what became of that party.

**B2.** Give the key dates and events in Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti's life as the deck presents them, including the organisation she helped create and the circumstances of her death.

**B3.** Compare Wole Soyinka and Gani Fawehinmi as figures who confronted undemocratic government, using the dates and specifics the deck gives for each.

## Answers

**A1 — B.** Herbert Macaulay founded the NNDP in 1923; Azikiwe co-founded the NCNC, not the NNDP.

**A2 — A.** 1864–1946. (1900–1978 is Ransome-Kuti, 1904–1996 Azikiwe, 1909–1987 Awolowo.)

**A3 — C.** The Nigerian Youth Movement; the merger produced the NCNC as a nationalist organisation advocating independence.

**A4 — B.** 1954 to 1960 as first premier of the western region. 1960–1963 and 1963–1966 are Azikiwe's.

**A5 — B.** The 1954 Lyleton Constitution, introduced while he led the Action Group.

**A6 — C.** Last governor-general 1960–1963; he was then first elected president 1963–1966 during the first republic.

**A7 — A.** Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti; Cheryl Johnson-Odim was the scholar who described her national standing.

**A8 — B.** The Federation of Nigerian Women's Societies (FNWS), set out to fight for women's rights against all odds.

**A9 — A.** Fela Anikulapo-Kuti (1938–1997); Burna Boy and Wizkid appear only in the closing list of contemporary musicians.

**A10 — D.** The left-leaning People's Redemption Party (PRP), which he joined after the civil war.

**A11 — B.** 1986; he dedicated the award to the imprisoned Nelson Mandela. 1967 is his imprisonment, 1994 his flight.

**A12 — A.** The 1996 Olympic Gold medal for Nigeria; the club honours (Premier League, UEFA Champions League, UEFA Cup, African Footballer) came separately in his career.

**B1.** Macaulay (1864–1946) is tagged the father of Nigerian nationalism and was among the first generation of Nigerian nationalists during the 1920s political agitation for self-rule. He protested British colonial policies including water rates, land issues and the management of railway finances. In 1923 he founded the Nigerian National Democratic Party (NNDP), Nigeria's first political party, whose members were the first to sit in the legislative council. It held sway in Nigeria's political theatre until the late 1930s, when it merged with the Nigerian Youth Movement to form the NCNC.

**B2.** Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti (1900–1978) stood against British colonial rule in the pre-independence era, using her influence to mobilise women — including market women, for picnics and festivals — and organise resistance, earning the moniker "Lioness of Lisabi". In 1953 she organised a conference in Abeokuta on women's welfare, which gave birth to the Federation of Nigerian Women's Societies (FNWS). On 18 February 1977 hundreds of soldiers raided the Lagos residence of her son Fela Kuti, and the 76-year-old was thrown from a second-story window. The injuries led to her death on 13 April 1978; Cheryl Johnson-Odim called her the only Nigerian woman of her time to rank as such a national figure.

**B3.** Soyinka (1934–Present) was imprisoned by the military government in 1967, as a 33-year-old graduate, for 22 months for speaking out against the civil war, and in 1994 fled Nigeria after the dictatorship charged him with treason for criticising the junta; he won the Nobel Prize for Literature in 1986 as the first African, dedicating it to the imprisoned Nelson Mandela, and has kept activating political discourse since the 1999 return to civilian rule. Fawehinmi (1938–2009) fought the same governments through law: practising in Lagos from 1965, he became "the people's lawyer" through pro bono work and campaigned on human rights for 40 years, suffering frequent detentions, beatings and the burning of his library. He defended journalists, notably seeking to re-open the unsolved case of Dele Giwa, killed by a parcel bomb in 1986, and demanded the removal of the federal attorney general from his sick bed before dying of cancer in 2009. Both confronted military rule, one chiefly through literature and public criticism, the other through litigation and legal defence of victims.
