## Section B — Short answer (3)

**B1.** State the deck's distinction between hearing and listening, and explain in one further sentence why the deck says effective listening matters for academic success.

**B2.** Name all six headings under "Importance of Listening" as given in the deck, and for any two of them give the specific benefit the deck attaches to it.

**B3.** The bullet-point summary lists six listening strategies; the case study lists six *practical* strategies. Name both sets and identify which items appear in one list but not the other.

## Answers

**A1 — B.** Sound reaches her but there is no conscious effort to interpret; hearing is passive and involuntary.

**A2 — B.** The deck's wording: listening is "an active and voluntary process requiring a conscious effort to interpret and understand."

**A3 — C.** Expressing concerns without interruption and identifying common ground are the deck's own words under Conflict Resolution.

**A4 — A.** Nonverbal communication is defined as attending to body language and facial expressions.

**A5 — C.** Avoiding prejudgment appears only in the case study's practical strategies; note-taking, summarizing and paraphrasing appear in both.

**A6 — B.** Reflective practice is "regular self-assessment to identify areas for improvement in listening abilities."

**A7 — D.** The deck credits good listeners with responding appropriately, asking relevant questions and giving thoughtful feedback — never with talking more.

**A8 — A.** "Leaders who listen well are perceived as approachable and are more likely to inspire trust and loyalty among their followers."

**A9 — C.** Cultural awareness is recognising and respecting communication differences across cultures.

**A10 — B.** These three are named verbatim in the first learning objective.

**A11 — B.** Empathetic listening is promoting understanding of the speaker's emotions and expressing empathy.

**A12 — C.** The six are Active Engagement, Critical Listening, Cultural Competence, Note-Taking Mastery, Empathetic Communication, Continuous Improvement.

**B1 — model answer.** Hearing is passive and involuntary: sound simply arrives. Listening is an active and voluntary process requiring a conscious effort to interpret and understand spoken messages. Effective listening is crucial for academic success because it aids in understanding course content, following instructions, and grasping complex or key concepts, which contributes to better academic performance. It is especially essential in subjects requiring comprehension of intricate details from lectures or discussions.

**B2 — model answer.** The six are Effective Communication, Building Relationships, Conflict Resolution, Learning and Growth, Leadership Effectiveness, and Avoiding Miscommunication. Building Relationships: when individuals feel heard and understood, trust and rapport are strengthened, and listening demonstrates empathy and respect for others' perspectives. Learning and Growth: attentive listening lets individuals absorb information, gain insights, and acquire new knowledge and skills, and is especially valuable in collaborative environments where diverse perspectives drive innovation and problem-solving.

**B3 — model answer.** The six listening strategies are active listening, nonverbal communication, empathetic listening, paraphrasing, summarizing, and note-taking. The six practical strategies are paraphrasing, summarizing, asking clarifying questions, minimizing distractions, avoiding prejudgment, and note-taking. Paraphrasing, summarizing and note-taking are common to both lists. Active listening, nonverbal communication and empathetic listening appear only in the strategies list (they are separate methodology headings in the case study), while asking clarifying questions, minimizing distractions and avoiding prejudgment appear only in the practical strategies.
