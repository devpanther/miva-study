# Tuesday — PHY_102 nightly check

*Faraday's law and Lenz's law as concepts.*
*Sit cold, notes closed, 15 minutes. 8 multiple choice, 4 written. Score out of 12.*

**1.** An average emf of 6.0 V is induced in a 300-turn coil while the flux through each turn changes by 4.0 mWb. How long did the change take?
A. 5.0 s
B. 0.20 s
C. 6.7 × 10⁻⁴ s
D. 7.2 s

**2.** A rectangular loop 20 cm by 15 cm lies in a uniform field of 0.40 T. The normal to the loop makes 60° with the field. What is the magnetic flux through the loop?
A. 1.2 × 10⁻² Wb
B. 1.0 × 10⁻² Wb
C. 60 Wb
D. 6.0 × 10⁻³ Wb

**3.** A flat loop lies in the page. A uniform field directed into the page is switched on, rising from zero. As seen by the reader, in which sense does the induced current flow, and which way does its own field point inside the loop?
A. Anticlockwise; induced field out of the page
B. Clockwise; induced field into the page
C. Clockwise; induced field out of the page
D. Anticlockwise; induced field into the page

**4.** A 200-turn coil of area 1.0 × 10⁻² m² is placed so that its plane is parallel to a uniform field. The field rises at a steady 50 T/s. What is the induced emf?
A. 50 V
B. 100 V
C. 0 V
D. 0.50 V

**5.** A rod of length 0.80 m moves at right angles to a uniform field of 0.50 T and to its own length. What speed is needed for an emf of 0.20 V between its ends?
A. 0.40 m/s
B. 2.0 m/s
C. 0.080 m/s
D. 0.50 m/s

**6.** An a.c. generator delivers an rms emf of 120 V. What is its peak emf?
A. 170 V
B. 85 V
C. 240 V
D. 120 V

**7.** A 50-turn coil of area 2.0 × 10⁻² m² has its normal at 60° to a uniform field that rises at a steady 3.0 T/s. What is the induced emf?
A. 3.0 V
B. 2.6 V
C. 1.5 V
D. 0.030 V

**8.** A bar magnet is held perfectly still with its north pole deep inside a closed coil. Which pair correctly describes the flux through the coil and the emf induced in it?
A. Zero flux, large emf
B. Large flux, zero emf
C. Large flux, large emf
D. Zero flux, zero emf

**9. (show your working)** A flexible loop of 5 turns lies with its normal parallel to a uniform field of 0.60 T. The loop is squeezed so that its area shrinks at a steady 0.020 m²/s. Calculate the induced emf. State the emf if instead the loop is held rigid while the field is kept steady at 1.2 T. Show your working.

**10. (show your working)** The flux through each turn of a 20-turn coil varies with time as Φ = 0.30t² Wb, with t in seconds. Calculate the induced emf at t = 2.0 s, and find the time at which the emf is 6.0 V. Show your working.

**11. (show your working)** An 80-turn coil of area 5.0 × 10⁻³ m² sits face-on to a uniform field of 0.25 T, so that its normal is along the field. It is turned through 90° in 0.10 s so that its plane now contains the field. Calculate the average emf induced. Show your working.

**12. (show your working)** A bar magnet is pushed north-pole-first into a coil connected to a centre-zero galvanometer, held still inside for a moment, then pulled out again. Describe what the galvanometer shows during each of the three stages and explain each reading using Faraday's law and Lenz's law.

## Answers

**1. B** — *Rearranging Faraday's law for the time.* ε = N ΔΦ/Δt, so Δt = N ΔΦ/ε = (300)(4.0 × 10⁻³)/6.0 = 1.2/6.0 = 0.20 s.

5.0 s is ε/(N ΔΦ), the ratio upside down; 6.7 × 10⁻⁴ s is ΔΦ/ε with N forgotten; 7.2 s multiplies N ΔΦ by the emf instead of dividing by it.

**2. D** — *Flux through a rectangular loop with an inclined normal.* A = (0.20)(0.15) = 0.030 m². The angle is already given from the normal, so Φ = BA cos θ = (0.40)(0.030) cos 60° = (0.012)(0.50) = 6.0 × 10⁻³ Wb.

1.2 × 10⁻² Wb is BA with the angle ignored; 1.0 × 10⁻² Wb uses cos 30°, treating 60° as the angle from the plane; 60 Wb leaves the sides in centimetres, giving an area 10⁴ times too big.

**3. A** — *Lenz's law with the right-hand grip rule.* Step 1: the external flux is into the page and increasing, so the induced field inside the loop must oppose the increase by pointing out of the page. Step 2: grip rule, thumb out of the page, fingers curl anticlockwise, so the current is anticlockwise.

'Clockwise; into the page' makes the induced field aid the growing flux, which is the answer for a decreasing field; the other two pair a sense with the field it does not produce, the grip rule applied backwards.

**4. C** — *Changing field with zero flux.* Plane parallel to B means the normal is at 90° to B, so Φ = BA cos 90° = 0 at every instant. Since Φ never changes, dΦ/dt = 0 and ε = 0, however fast B rises. A changing field is not the same as a changing flux.

100 V is NA dB/dt with cos θ = 1, treating the coil as face-on; 0.50 V is the same with N forgotten; 50 V quotes the rate of change of B as if it were a voltage.

**5. D** — *Rearranging the motional emf formula.* ε = BLv, so v = ε/(BL) = 0.20/((0.50)(0.80)) = 0.20/0.40 = 0.50 m/s.

0.40 m/s divides by B only and forgets L; 2.0 m/s is BL/ε, the ratio inverted; 0.080 m/s multiplies ε by BL instead of dividing.

**6. A** — *Peak emf from rms emf.* ε_rms = ε_peak/√2, so ε_peak = √2 × 120 = 1.414 × 120 = 170 V (169.7 V).

85 V divides by √2 instead of multiplying; 240 V doubles instead of multiplying by √2; 120 V treats rms and peak as the same number.

**7. C** — *Faraday's law for an inclined coil.* Only B changes, so ε = N A cos θ dB/dt = (50)(2.0 × 10⁻²)(cos 60°)(3.0) = (50)(2.0 × 10⁻²)(0.50)(3.0) = 1.5 V.

3.0 V ignores the angle; 2.6 V uses cos 30°, measuring the angle from the plane; 0.030 V forgets N.

**8. B** — *Flux versus rate of change of flux.* The magnet's field threads the coil, so the flux is large. But ε = −N dΦ/dt depends on the rate of change of flux, and a stationary magnet gives a constant flux, so the emf is zero. Faraday's conclusion: the existence of flux is not sufficient; the flux must be changing.

'Large flux, large emf' mistakes flux for its rate of change; 'zero flux' is wrong because a magnet inside a coil certainly threads it with field lines; the zero emf is right but the reason offered is wrong.

**9.** *Emf from a changing area.* With B and θ fixed, Φ = BA and dΦ/dt = B dA/dt. So ε = N B dA/dt = (5)(0.60)(0.020) = 0.060 V. If the loop is rigid and B is steady, nothing in Φ = BA cos θ changes, so ε = 0, however large the field.

Final answer: 0.060 V (accept 0.058 to 0.062), and 0 V for the rigid loop in a steady field. A correct answer uses the rate of change of area, multiplies by N and B, and says that a steady flux induces nothing. 0.012 V forgot N; 0.12 V for the second part treats the larger field as if it were a change.

**10.** *Instantaneous emf from a time-dependent flux.* Differentiate: dΦ/dt = 0.60t Wb/s, so |ε| = N dΦ/dt = 20 × 0.60t = 12t volts. At t = 2.0 s: ε = 12 × 2.0 = 24 V. For ε = 6.0 V: 12t = 6.0, so t = 0.50 s.

Final answer: 24 V at t = 2.0 s (accept 23.5 to 24.5) and t = 0.50 s. A correct answer differentiates before substituting and multiplies by N. 1.2 V at t = 2.0 s is Φ itself (0.30 × 4) with no differentiation; 1.2 V or 2.4 V forgets N; substituting t = 6 gives nonsense.

**11.** *Average emf from turning a coil through a right angle.* Initial flux per turn: Φ₁ = BA cos 0° = (0.25)(5.0 × 10⁻³) = 1.25 × 10⁻³ Wb. Final flux: Φ₂ = BA cos 90° = 0. So |ΔΦ| = 1.25 × 10⁻³ Wb and ε_av = N |ΔΦ|/Δt = (80)(1.25 × 10⁻³)/(0.10) = 0.10/0.10 = 1.0 V.

Final answer: 1.0 V (accept 0.98 to 1.02). A correct answer states the initial flux BA, the final flux zero, and divides N ΔΦ by 0.10 s; it may note that this is an average, since the flux does not fall uniformly. 0.0125 V forgot N; 2.0 V used a flux change of 2BA, which is a full flip through 180°, not a quarter turn.

**12.** *Faraday's magnet-and-coil experiment explained.* Stage 1, pushing in: the flux through the coil increases, so by Faraday's law an emf is induced and the needle deflects, say to the left. By Lenz's law the current makes the near face a north pole, opposing the approach. Stage 2, held still: the flux is large but constant, so dΦ/dt = 0, the emf is zero and the needle returns to the centre, even though the magnet is inside the coil. Stage 3, pulling out: the flux decreases, so an emf is induced again but the current reverses, the near face becomes a south pole to hold the magnet back, and the needle deflects the other way.

A correct answer gives deflection, zero, opposite deflection, ties each to the flux increasing, constant, decreasing, and names the pole on the near face or the reversal of current as Lenz's law.
