# Tuesday — PHY_102 nightly check

*Faraday's flux rule and Lenz's law as concepts: why only a changing flux induces an emf, what the minus sign actually asserts, how the induced field is fixed by the change rather than by the external field, and the energy argument underneath it.*
*Sit cold, notes closed, about 15 minutes. Score out of 12.*

**1.** A bar magnet is held at rest with its north pole deep inside a wire loop connected to a sensitive ammeter. The ammeter reads exactly zero. The best explanation is:
a) The flux through the loop is large but constant, and only its rate of change drives an emf.
b) The flux through the loop is zero, because the magnet's field lines all return outside the loop.
c) There is an induced emf, but the loop's resistance is too large for the current to register.
d) The north and south poles of the magnet contribute equal and opposite flux, which cancels.

**2.** In Φ_B = B*A*cos θ, the angle θ is measured between:
a) B and the plane of the loop.
b) B and the direction of the induced current.
c) B and the normal to the plane of the loop.
d) the normal to the loop and the induced magnetic field.

**3.** Which statement is the correct reading of the minus sign in ε = −N dΦ_B/dt?
a) The induced magnetic field inside the loop always points opposite to the external magnetic field.
b) The induced emf acts so as to oppose the change in flux that produced it.
c) The induced emf is always a negative quantity.
d) The induced current always flows opposite in sense to the current producing the external field.

**4.** A loop encloses a uniform field directed out of the page whose magnitude is steadily decreasing. The induced current is:
a) clockwise, since the induced field must be into the page — the induced field always opposes the external field.
b) zero, since a decreasing field cannot drive a current; only an increasing one can.
c) clockwise, from the right-hand grip rule applied with the thumb along the original external field.
d) counterclockwise, since the induced field is out of the page, replacing the flux that is being lost.

**5.** Suppose that in some hypothetical world the induced current instead produced a field that *reinforced* an increasing external flux. The immediate consequence would be:
a) The induced emf would simply be twice as large, and energy would still be conserved.
b) The loop would be pushed towards the magnet, violating Newton's third law.
c) The flux change would reinforce itself, and the loop would deliver electrical energy indefinitely with nothing doing work on it.
d) Nothing inconsistent: Faraday's law would still hold, showing that Lenz's law is a logically independent rule.

**6.** A magnet is pushed at a given speed towards a closed conducting loop, and then at the same speed towards an identical loop that has a small break cut in it. Comparing the force the hand must exert:
a) larger for the closed loop, because a current flows and the resulting magnetic force opposes the approach.
b) the same in both cases, because the induced emf is the same in both.
c) larger for the broken loop, because charge piles up at the break and repels the magnet.
d) zero in both cases, because magnetic forces do no work.

**7.** A loop is positioned so that no field lines pass through the area it encloses, although the region just outside the loop contains a strong and rapidly changing field. The induced emf in the loop is:
a) large, because the field near the wire is strong and changing rapidly.
b) large, but confined to the sections of wire nearest the field region.
c) non-zero and constant in time, because the field outside contributes a fixed offset.
d) zero, because the emf depends only on the flux through the enclosed area.

**8.** A circular loop is oriented so that a uniform magnetic field lies entirely *in the plane* of the loop. The magnitude of that field is then increased very rapidly. The induced emf is:
a) maximum, since dB/dt is large and the field lines lie as close to the wire as they can.
b) zero, since the flux through the loop is zero at every instant and so cannot change.
c) equal to A·dB/dt, since it is the magnitude of the field that changes, not the orientation.
d) zero, because the induced current would have nowhere to flow in this orientation.

**9. (explain why)** A loop is dragged at constant velocity across a region of *uniform* magnetic field: it enters, is briefly wholly inside, then leaves. Explain why a current flows while it enters and while it leaves, but not while it is wholly inside — even though the loop is moving the whole time and the field is present throughout.

**10. (explain why)** In a rotating generator coil the emf is zero at the very instant the flux through the coil is maximum. Explain why, and say what the coil's orientation is at that instant.

**11. (explain why)** Lenz's law is stated as "the induced effect opposes the *change* that caused it," not "opposes the external field." Using the case of a field out of the page that is decreasing, explain why those two statements are not the same, and which one is right.

**12. (explain why)** Explain, in terms of work and energy, why Lenz's law follows from conservation of energy rather than standing as an independent experimental rule of its own.
