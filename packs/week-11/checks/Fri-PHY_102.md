*12 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 12.*

**1.** A 120-turn circular coil of diameter 6.0 cm and total resistance 1.5 Ω has its plane perpendicular to a uniform field that rises at a steady 2.5 T/s. What current flows in the coil?
A. 0.85 A
B. 2.3 A
C. 0.57 A
D. 4.7 × 10⁻³ A

**2.** A 25-turn square coil of side 12 cm sits in a uniform field of 0.60 T with the plane of the coil at 40° to the field. What is the flux linkage NΦ?
A. 0.14 Wb
B. 0.17 Wb
C. 0.22 Wb
D. 5.6 × 10⁻³ Wb

**3.** A rod sliding on rails in a uniform field needs an applied force of 0.30 N to keep it moving at a steady 2.0 m/s against the induced current's retarding force. If it is instead kept moving at 6.0 m/s, what force is needed and what mechanical power is supplied?
A. 0.90 N and 1.8 W
B. 2.7 N and 16 W
C. 0.30 N and 1.8 W
D. 0.90 N and 5.4 W

**4.** A coil of 250 turns and area 0.012 m² rotates at 1200 revolutions per minute in a uniform field of 0.30 T, about an axis perpendicular to the field. What rms emf does it deliver?
A. 18 V
B. 80 V
C. 113 V
D. 1.1 × 10³ V

**5.** An 80-turn coil of area 3.0 × 10⁻³ m² and total resistance 4.0 Ω starts face-on to a uniform field of 0.50 T and is flipped right over (through 180°) in 0.10 s. How much charge circulates?
A. 0.060 C
B. 0.030 C
C. 0.60 C
D. 7.5 × 10⁻⁴ C

**6.** A generator coil has a peak emf of 60 V. What is the instantaneous emf at the moment when the normal to the coil makes 30° with the field?
A. 52 V
B. 60 V
C. 42 V
D. 30 V

**7.** A rod slides along horizontal rails so that the area enclosed by the circuit is decreasing. The uniform field is directed into the page. As seen by the reader, the induced current in the circuit is:
A. Anticlockwise; the induced field points out of the page
B. Anticlockwise; the induced field points into the page
C. Clockwise; the induced field points into the page
D. Zero; the field itself is not changing

**8.** A conducting rod moves at steady speed across a uniform field, but the rails it rests on are not connected to each other, so the circuit is open. Which of the following are non-zero?
A. Emf only
B. Emf and current
C. Current and retarding force
D. None of them

**9.** A 120-turn coil of area 6.0 × 10⁻³ m² and resistance 4.0 Ω has its normal along a uniform field that falls steadily from 0.80 T to 0.20 T in 0.30 s. Calculate the emf, the current and the charge that circulates.
A. 1.44 V, 0.36 A, 9.0 × 10⁻⁴ C
B. 1.44 V, 0.36 A, 0.108 C
C. 0.012 V, 0.0030 A, 9.0 × 10⁻⁴ C
D. 1.44 V, 5.76 A, 0.108 C

**10.** A rod of length 0.60 m and mass 0.20 kg moves at 5.0 m/s on frictionless horizontal rails in a vertical 0.40 T field; the circuit resistance is 0.30 Ω. Calculate the retarding force, and the rod's initial deceleration once the applied force is removed.
A. 0.29 N, 1.4 m/s²
B. 1.6 N, 8.0 m/s²
C. 0.96 N, 0.19 m/s²
D. 0.96 N, 4.8 m/s²

**11.** A generator coil of area 0.040 m² is to give a peak emf of 100 V at 50 Hz in a uniform field of 0.20 T. Calculate the number of turns needed and the rms emf it will deliver.
A. 250 turns, 71 V
B. 40 turns, 71 V
C. 40 turns, 141 V
D. 40 turns, 100 V

**12.** A generator produces a sinusoidal emf of peak value 100 V. A student says the average emf over one cycle is 70.7 V. What is wrong with that?
A. 70.7 V is the average, and the rms value is 100 V
B. The average over a cycle is 63.7 V, and 70.7 V is half the peak-to-peak value
C. 70.7 V is the rms value; the true average over a full cycle is zero
D. 70.7 V is the average of the positive half cycle, and the rms value is 50 V

---

## Answers

**1. C** — *Induced current from a rate of change of B.* Radius 3.0 cm = 0.030 m, so A = π(0.030)² = 2.83 × 10⁻³ m². Plane perpendicular to B means θ = 0. ε = NA dB/dt = (120)(2.83 × 10⁻³)(2.5) = 0.848 V, and I = ε/R = 0.848/1.5 = 0.57 A.

0.85 A is the emf quoted as a current, R never used; 2.3 A uses the 6.0 cm diameter as the radius, four times the area; 4.7 × 10⁻³ A forgets N.

**2. A** — *Flux linkage with the angle given to the plane.* The plane is at 40° to B, so the normal is at 90° − 40° = 50°. A = (0.12)² = 1.44 × 10⁻² m². Φ = BA cos 50° = (0.60)(1.44 × 10⁻²)(0.643) = 5.55 × 10⁻³ Wb per turn, and NΦ = 25 × 5.55 × 10⁻³ = 0.139 Wb, about 0.14 Wb.

0.17 Wb uses cos 40°, the angle from the plane; 0.22 Wb is NBA with the angle ignored; 5.6 × 10⁻³ Wb is the flux through one turn, N forgotten.

**3. D** — *Scaling of retarding force and power with speed.* F = B²L²v/R is proportional to v, so tripling the speed triples the force: F = 3 × 0.30 = 0.90 N. Power P = Fv = (0.90)(6.0) = 5.4 W, nine times the original 0.60 W, because P = B²L²v²/R goes as v².

'0.90 N and 1.8 W' scales the power with v instead of v²; '2.7 N and 16 W' scales the force with v² instead of v; '0.30 N and 1.8 W' assumes the force does not depend on speed at all.

**4. B** — *Rms emf of a generator from its rotation rate.* f = 1200/60 = 20 Hz, so ω = 2πf = 125.7 rad/s. ε_peak = NBAω = (250)(0.30)(0.012)(125.7) = 113 V. ε_rms = ε_peak/√2 = 113/1.414 = 80 V.

18 V uses f = 20 in place of ω, missing the 2π; 113 V is the peak, not the rms; 1.1 × 10³ V uses 1200 rev/min directly as ω.

**5. A** — *Charge circulated when a coil is flipped.* Flipping reverses the flux from +BA to −BA, so |ΔΦ| = 2BA = 2(0.50)(3.0 × 10⁻³) = 3.0 × 10⁻³ Wb. q = N|ΔΦ|/R = (80)(3.0 × 10⁻³)/4.0 = 0.24/4.0 = 0.060 C. The 0.10 s plays no part.

0.030 C uses ΔΦ = BA, forgetting that a flip doubles the change; 0.60 C is the average current (0.24/0.10/4.0) reported as a charge; 7.5 × 10⁻⁴ C forgets N.

**6. D** — *Instantaneous emf of a rotating coil.* Φ = BA cos ωt gives ε = ε_peak sin ωt, and ωt is the angle between the normal and B. So ε = 60 sin 30° = 60 × 0.50 = 30 V.

52 V uses cos 30°, which is the flux's factor, not the emf's; 60 V assumes the emf is at its peak, which happens at 90°, when the flux is zero; 42 V is the rms value, which is not an instantaneous emf at all.

**7. C** — *Current sense for a rod on rails.* The flux into the page is decreasing because the area is shrinking, so the induced field inside the loop must replace what is being lost: it points into the page. Grip rule, thumb into the page: the current is clockwise.

'Anticlockwise; out of the page' is the answer for an increasing area, opposing the field rather than the change; 'anticlockwise; into the page' has the grip rule backwards; 'zero' forgets that changing area changes flux just as well as changing B does.

**8. A** — *Emf without a closed circuit.* The motional emf BLv exists across the rod's ends whether or not the circuit is closed: charges are pushed to the ends until their electric field balances the magnetic push. With no closed path there is no current, and with no current there is no magnetic force BIL on the rod, so it slides freely.

'Emf and current' needs a closed circuit; 'current and retarding force' both need current; 'none' forgets that the emf is there regardless, and would light nothing only because nothing is connected.

**9. B** — *Faraday's law chained to current and circulated charge.* The flux change per turn is ΔB × A = (0.60)(6.0 × 10⁻³) = 3.6 × 10⁻³ Wb, so ε = N|ΔΦ|/Δt = (120)(3.6 × 10⁻³)/0.30 = 1.44 V and I = ε/R = 0.36 A. The charge is q = N|ΔΦ|/R = 0.432/4.0 = 0.108 C, matching I Δt = 0.108 C because the rate is uniform.

9.0 × 10⁻⁴ C leaves the 120 turns out of the charge alone; the third option leaves them out everywhere; 5.76 A multiplies the emf by R instead of dividing.

**10. D** — *Retarding force and deceleration of a released rod.* ε = BLv = (0.40)(0.60)(5.0) = 1.2 V, I = ε/R = 4.0 A and F = BIL = (0.40)(4.0)(0.60) = 0.96 N against the motion, equally B²L²v/R. With nothing else pushing, a = F/m = 0.96/0.20 = 4.8 m/s², and it shrinks as the rod slows.

0.29 N uses the emf 1.2 as if it were the current; 1.6 N drops one factor of L from B²L²v/R; 0.19 m/s² multiplies F by m instead of dividing by it.

**11. B** — *Designing a generator coil.* ω = 2πf = 2π(50) = 314 rad/s, and ε_peak = NBAω, so N = 100/[(0.20)(0.040)(314)] = 39.8, that is 40 turns. The rms value is ε_peak/√2 = 100/1.414 = 71 V.

250 turns uses f = 50 where ω = 314 belongs; 141 V multiplies by √2 instead of dividing; 100 V quotes the peak value as though it were the rms.

**12. C** — *Peak, rms and average emf.* 70.7 V is ε_peak/√2, the root-mean-square value: the steady d.c. voltage that would heat a resistor at the same rate, and what an a.c. meter reads. The emf ε = ε_peak sin ωt is positive for half of each cycle and negative for the other half, so its true mean over a whole cycle is zero, which is why that mean is never quoted; power depends on ε², which stays positive.

63.7 V (2/π of the peak) is the mean of one half cycle, not of a full cycle; half the peak-to-peak value is 100 V; and 70.7 V is not any kind of average.
