# Friday — PHY_102 nightly check

*Numerical Faraday and Lenz work: computing Φ = BA cos θ with the angle taken from the normal and areas converted to m², emf from ε = N A dB/dt and from ε = N ΔΦ/Δt, the factor of two when a coil is flipped rather than turned through 90°, induced charge q = NΔΦ/R and its independence of the time taken, motional emf ε = BLv with the retarding force B²L²v/R and a closed power audit, the rotating rod ε = ½BL²ω with the 2πf and tip-speed traps, geometries in which the emf is exactly zero, and the solenoid-and-ring case where the flux is that of the enclosed area and not of the coil.*
*Sit cold, notes closed, about 15 minutes. Score out of 12.*

*Take μ₀ = 4π×10⁻⁷ T·m/A.*

**1.** A flat square coil of **40 turns** and side **0.15 m** is placed in a uniform field of **0.60 T** with the **plane of the coil** making an angle of **30°** with the field. The flux through **one turn** is:
a) 1.35×10⁻² Wb
b) 6.75×10⁻³ Wb
c) 1.17×10⁻² Wb
d) 0.270 Wb

**Questions 2 and 3 refer to this coil.** A coil of **300 turns**, each of area **8.0 cm²**, lies with its **normal along** a uniform field which grows steadily from **0.10 T to 0.90 T in 0.50 s**. The total circuit resistance is **6.0 Ω**.

**2.** The magnitude of the induced emf while the field is changing is:
a) 3.8×10³ V
b) 0.43 V
c) 0.38 V
d) 1.3×10⁻³ V

**3.** The same field change is now made **ten times more slowly**, over 5.0 s. The charge that circulates in the circuit during the change is:
a) 32 mC — the same as before
b) 3.2 mC
c) 320 mC
d) 192 mC

**4.** A straight rod of length **0.50 m** is pivoted at one end and rotated at **120 revolutions per minute** in a uniform field of **0.20 T** parallel to the rotation axis. The emf between its ends is:
a) 0.63 V
b) 3.0 V
c) 0.31 V
d) 0.050 V

**5.** A square loop of side **0.20 m** and total resistance **0.50 Ω** is pulled at a steady **2.5 m/s** out of a region of uniform field **B = 0.30 T** directed into the page, moving in the plane of the page on a frictionless surface. The force that must be applied to keep it moving at that speed is:
a) 3.6×10⁻² N
b) 1.8×10⁻² N
c) 7.2×10⁻² N
d) zero, since the surface is frictionless

**6.** A coil of **50 turns**, each of area **4.0×10⁻³ m²**, sits in a uniform field of **0.25 T** with its normal along the field. It is **flipped through 180°** in **0.10 s**. The average induced emf is:
a) 0.50 V
b) 2.0×10⁻² V
c) 1.0 V
d) zero, since the magnitude of the flux is the same before and after

**7.** A coil of **200 turns** and area **30 cm²** is placed in a uniform field of **0.50 T** and spun at **40 revolutions per second** about an axis that lies **along B and in the plane of the coil**. The peak emf generated is:
a) 75 V
b) 12 V
c) 0.30 V
d) zero

**8.** A rod of mass **0.15 kg** and length **0.30 m** slides on frictionless rails at **4.0 m/s** in a field of **0.60 T**; the circuit resistance is **1.2 Ω**. The driving force is now removed and the rod is left to itself. The total heat eventually produced in the resistance is:
a) 1.2 J
b) 0.43 J
c) 2.4 J
d) indeterminate — the time the rod takes to stop must be known first

**9. (explain why)** A flat circular coil of **150 turns** and radius **6.0 cm** is placed in a uniform field with the **plane of the coil at 30° to the field**; the total circuit resistance is **18 Ω**. The field is raised steadily from **0.20 T to 0.80 T in 0.25 s**. Find (i) the flux through one turn at the start and at the end, (ii) the induced emf and the induced current, (iii) the sense of the induced current, stated relative to the direction of **B**, and (iv) the charge circulated. Then state what each of your answers becomes if the same rise takes **2.5 s**, explain the one that does not change, and say what the emf would have been had the coil been **face-on** to the field throughout.

**10. (explain why)** A square loop of side **0.25 m** and total resistance **0.80 Ω** travels at a constant **3.0 m/s** in the plane of the page, entirely crossing a **1.0 m wide** region of uniform field **B = 0.40 T directed into the page**. Find the emf, the current, the sense of the current and the force on the loop **while it is entering**, **while it is entirely inside**, and **while it is leaving**. Find the heat produced during entry and check it against the work done by the puller over that stage. Then give the total heat for the whole crossing and sketch in words the shape of the emf–time graph.

**11. (explain why)** A coil of **250 turns** and area **40 cm²** sits in a uniform field of **0.30 T**, and the total circuit resistance is **12 Ω**. **(a)** It is spun at **20 rev/s** about a diameter perpendicular to B: find ω, the peak emf, the r.m.s. emf and the output frequency, and say at which orientation the emf is **zero**. **(b)** Starting face-on, it is instead **flipped through 180° in 0.040 s**: find the average emf and the charge circulated, and explain why the answer is exactly **twice** what a 90° turn would give. **(c)** A rod of length **0.40 m** is rotated about one end at the same **20 rev/s** in the same field: find its emf and explain the factor by which it differs from BLv computed with the speed of the rod's far end.

**12. (explain why)** A long solenoid of radius **2.5 cm** has **2000 turns per metre**, and its current is increasing at **40 A/s**. A ring of **30 turns**, radius **8.0 cm** and resistance **1.5 Ω** is placed **coaxially around** it. Find (i) the rate of change of the field inside the solenoid, (ii) the rate of change of flux through the ring, (iii) the emf and the induced current in the ring, and (iv) the magnitude of the induced electric field at the ring. State the direction of the induced current relative to B. Then explain exactly what a student obtains, and by what factor they are wrong, if they use the **ring's** own area instead — and explain why nothing in this problem moves and yet an emf exists.

## Answers

**1. b** — *Φ = BA cos θ with θ measured from the **normal**, not from the plane.* A = (0.15)² = 2.25×10⁻² m². The **plane** is at 30° to B, so the **normal** is at **60°**: Φ = BA cos 60° = (0.60)(2.25×10⁻²)(0.500) = **6.75×10⁻³ Wb**. (c) puts the given 30° straight into the cosine, measuring from the plane — the central angle error of the topic, and one that inverts every extreme case downstream (it would make the flux greatest edge-on). (a) is BA, the angle ignored altogether: the maximum possible flux, obtained only face-on. (d) is 40 × 6.75×10⁻³ = 0.270 Wb, the **flux linkage** of all forty turns, where one turn was asked for; NΦ and Φ carry the same unit, so only reading the question protects you.

**2. c** — *ε = N A (dB/dt), with the **change** in B and the area in m².* A = 8.0 cm² = 8.0×10⁻⁴ m²; dB/dt = (0.90 − 0.10)/0.50 = 1.6 T/s; ε = (300)(8.0×10⁻⁴)(1.6) = (0.24)(1.6) = **0.38 V**. (b) is 0.43 V, from using the **final** field 0.90 T rather than the change 0.80 T — the value of B at the end of the process appears nowhere in Faraday's law. (a) is 3.8 kV, from leaving the area in cm²: a factor of 10⁴, and a voltage no such coil could produce. (d) is 1.3 mV, with N omitted — the emf of a single turn, when the turns are in series and their emfs add.

**3. a** — *q = N ΔΦ/R: the time cancels out.* ΔΦ per turn = A ΔB = (8.0×10⁻⁴)(0.80) = 6.4×10⁻⁴ Wb, so q = (300)(6.4×10⁻⁴)/6.0 = 0.192/6.0 = **3.2×10⁻² C = 32 mC**, in the 0.50 s version *and* in the 5.0 s version. Slowing the change divides the emf and the current by ten and multiplies the duration by ten; the product is untouched. (b) divides the charge by ten as though it followed the current — the whole point of the question. (c) multiplies by ten, treating the longer time as more charge delivered at the same current. (d) is 0.192 C = N ΔΦ, the flux linkage change quoted as a charge, with the division by R forgotten; the units alone condemn it, since webers are not coulombs.

**4. c** — *A rod rotating about one end: ε = ½BL²ω, with ω = 2πf in rad/s.* f = 120 rev/min = **2.0 rev/s**, so ω = 2π(2.0) = **12.57 rad/s**; ε = ½(0.20)(0.50)²(12.57) = ½(0.20)(0.25)(12.57) = **0.31 V**. (a) is 0.63 V, exactly double: the ½ has been dropped, which is the same as using the **tip** speed Lω = 6.28 m/s in ε = BLv when the correct speed is the **mean**, ½Lω. (b) is 3.0 V, from putting **120** straight in as ω — revolutions per minute treated as radians per second, an error of 2π × 60. (d) is 0.050 V, from using **f = 2.0** in place of ω = 2πf, an error of 2π ≈ 6.28.

**5. b** — *Only the side still inside the field carries current in the field.* ε = BLv = (0.30)(0.20)(2.5) = 0.15 V; I = ε/R = 0.30 A; F = BIL = (0.30)(0.30)(0.20) = **1.8×10⁻² N**, directed back into the field region, so an equal applied force is needed. Check: B²L²v/R = (0.09)(0.04)(2.5)/0.50 = 0.018 N ✔. (a) doubles it by counting a force on the trailing side as well — but that side is **outside** the field region, where B = 0, so it feels nothing. (c) uses the loop's **perimeter** 0.80 m as L; the L in BIL is the length of conductor lying in the field and carrying current **perpendicular** to it, here one side only. (The two sides parallel to the motion do carry force, but equal and opposite, and they cancel.) (d) is the trap the question is built on: "frictionless" removes the mechanical friction and has nothing to do with the magnetic retarding force, which is where the 4.5×10⁻² W of heat in the resistor comes from.

**6. c** — *Flipping reverses the sign of the flux, so |ΔΦ| = 2BA, not BA.* Initially Φ = +BA = (0.25)(4.0×10⁻³) = 1.0×10⁻³ Wb; after the flip the normal points **against** B, so Φ = −1.0×10⁻³ Wb. |ΔΦ| = **2.0×10⁻³ Wb**, and |ε| = N|ΔΦ|/Δt = (50)(2.0×10⁻³)/0.10 = **1.0 V**. (a) is 0.50 V, using ΔΦ = BA — the error of thinking the flux merely "goes away" rather than reversing; it is the correct answer for a **90°** turn, and here it is wrong by exactly two. (b) is 2.0×10⁻² V, with N omitted. (d) treats flux as a magnitude with no sign; Φ = **B·A** is a signed scalar, and it is precisely that sign change that makes a flipped coil generate twice as much.

**7. d** — *No component of B ever threads the coil, so Φ = 0 at every instant and ε = 0.* The rotation axis lies **along B and in the plane of the coil**, so the coil's **normal stays perpendicular to B** throughout the rotation; Φ = BA cos 90° = 0 always, dΦ/dt = 0, and there is **no emf at any speed**. (a) is 75 V, the value of NBAω = (200)(0.50)(3.0×10⁻³)(2π × 40) — the generator formula applied without checking the geometry, which is the whole discrimination in this question; NBAω is the peak emf only when the coil spins about an axis **perpendicular** to B. (b) is 12 V, that same misapplication compounded by using f = 40 in place of ω = 2πf. (c) is 0.30 V = NBA, the rotation rate omitted, which would have a stationary coil generating a voltage.

**8. a** — *All of the kinetic energy ends as heat: no other sink exists.* Once the driving force is removed the only horizontal force is the magnetic retarding force B²L²v/R, which decelerates the rod exponentially (v = v₀e^(−t/τ) with τ = mR/B²L² = (0.15)(1.2)/[(0.36)(0.09)] = 5.6 s). It never quite stops, but its kinetic energy is entirely converted: **heat = ½mv₀² = ½(0.15)(4.0)² = 1.2 J**. (b) is 0.43 J, the **initial power** in watts (ε = BLv = 0.72 V, I = 0.60 A, P = εI = 0.432 W) quoted as an energy in joules — dimensionally wrong, and in any case the power falls continuously as the rod slows. (c) is mv², the ½ dropped. (d) is the answer of someone who intends to compute ∫I²R dt and cannot: the point is that **energy conservation gives the total without integrating anything**, and it does so even though the motion formally never ends.

**9.** *Concept: Φ = BA cos θ with the angle from the normal, ε = N ΔΦ/Δt, the direction from Lenz's law, and q = NΔΦ/R independent of the rate.* **Set-up.** A = πr² = π(0.060)² = **1.131×10⁻² m²**. The **plane** is at 30° to B, so the **normal is at 60°** and cos θ = 0.500. **(i) Flux per turn.** Φ₁ = (0.20)(1.131×10⁻²)(0.500) = **1.131×10⁻³ Wb**; Φ₂ = (0.80)(1.131×10⁻²)(0.500) = **4.524×10⁻³ Wb**; ΔΦ = **3.393×10⁻³ Wb**. **(ii) Emf and current.** ε = N ΔΦ/Δt = (150)(3.393×10⁻³)/0.25 = 0.509/0.25 = **2.04 V**; I = ε/R = 2.04/18 = **0.113 A**. **(iii) Sense.** The flux is **increasing**, so the induced current's own field inside the coil must point **against B**. Look along B, so that B is going away from you: the induced field must come **towards** you, and by the right-hand loop rule (thumb towards you, fingers curl) the current runs **anticlockwise as you see it** — equivalently clockwise viewed from the far side. The mechanical check passes: whatever is strengthening the field is opposed. **(iv) Charge.** q = N ΔΦ/R = 0.509/18 = **2.83×10⁻² C (28 mC)**; cross-check I × t = (0.113)(0.25) = 0.0283 C ✔. **Over 2.5 s instead:** the emf becomes **0.204 V** and the current **1.13×10⁻² A**, each ten times smaller — but the charge is **still 28 mC**. **Why the charge is unchanged:** q = ∫I dt = ∫(N/R)(dΦ/dt) dt = **N ΔΦ/R**, in which the time has cancelled identically; a slower change means a proportionally smaller current flowing for a proportionally longer time. Only the total flux change and the resistance matter, which is what makes a ballistic galvanometer possible. **Face-on instead:** with the normal along B, cos θ = 1 and every flux doubles, so the emf would be **4.07 V**, the current 0.226 A and the charge 57 mC. The 30° in the question is worth a factor of two, and only because it was read from the plane and converted to 60° from the normal.

**10.** *Concept: the emf appears only while an edge crosses the boundary; direction reverses between entry and exit; and the energy books close.* **While entering.** Only the leading side sweeps flux: ε = BLv = (0.40)(0.25)(3.0) = **0.30 V**. I = ε/R = 0.30/0.80 = **0.375 A**. The into-page flux is **increasing**, so the induced field inside the loop must point **out of** the page ⇒ the current is **anticlockwise**. The leading side carries that current in the field, so it feels **F = BIL = (0.40)(0.375)(0.25) = 3.75×10⁻² N**, directed **backwards**, opposing the entry. (The two sides parallel to the motion carry equal and opposite forces that cancel; the trailing side is not yet in the field.) **While entirely inside.** All four sides are in the field, B, A and θ are all constant, so dΦ/dt = 0: **ε = 0, I = 0, F = 0** — and this is true even though the loop is moving as fast as ever. This stage lasts while the loop travels 1.0 − 0.25 = 0.75 m, i.e. **0.25 s**. **While leaving.** The into-page flux is now **decreasing**, so the induced field must point **into** the page to maintain it ⇒ the current is **clockwise**, the **reverse** of the entry sense. The magnitudes are unchanged: **ε = 0.30 V, I = 0.375 A, F = 3.75×10⁻² N**, again **backwards** — now pulling the loop back into the region it is leaving. **Heat during entry.** Entry lasts t = L/v = 0.25/3.0 = **8.33×10⁻² s**. Electrical power P = I²R = (0.375)²(0.80) = **0.1125 W** (check εI = (0.30)(0.375) = 0.1125 W ✔), so heat = (0.1125)(8.33×10⁻²) = **9.4×10⁻³ J**. **Check against the work done:** the puller must supply 3.75×10⁻² N over the 0.25 m of entry, i.e. W = (3.75×10⁻²)(0.25) = **9.4×10⁻³ J** ✔ — identical, as conservation of energy requires, since the loop's kinetic energy is constant throughout. **Total for the crossing.** Two identical stages of 9.4 mJ with a free-running gap between them: **total heat = 1.88×10⁻² J**, and total work done against the magnetic force = the same 18.8 mJ. **The emf–time graph.** Two rectangular pulses of equal height 0.30 V and equal width 83 ms, of **opposite sign**, separated by a 0.25 s stretch at exactly zero. The sign reversal is Lenz's law: the loop resists gaining flux on the way in and resists losing it on the way out, and in both cases the force opposes the motion.

**11.** *Concept: ε₀ = NBAω for a rotating coil, ΔΦ = 2BA for a flip, and ε = ½BL²ω for a rotating rod.* **Common quantities.** A = 40 cm² = **4.0×10⁻³ m²**; NBA = (250)(0.30)(4.0×10⁻³) = **0.300 Wb**. **(a) The generator.** ω = 2πf = 2π(20) = **125.7 rad/s** (using f = 20 in place of ω is an error of 2π ≈ 6.28). Peak emf ε₀ = NBAω = (0.300)(125.7) = **37.7 V**. R.m.s. emf = 37.7/√2 = **26.7 V** — what a meter reads; the peak is what the insulation must withstand. Output frequency = **20 Hz**, one full sine cycle per revolution. The emf is **zero when the coil is face-on** (plane perpendicular to B), because that is where the flux is at its **maximum** and therefore momentarily stationary — the emf follows the **slope** of the flux, not its size, so it is greatest a quarter-turn later when the coil is edge-on and the flux passes through zero. **(b) The flip.** Face-on, Φ = +BA per turn = (0.30)(4.0×10⁻³) = 1.2×10⁻³ Wb; after the flip the normal points against B, so Φ = **−1.2×10⁻³ Wb**. |ΔΦ| = **2BA = 2.4×10⁻³ Wb**, and ε = N|ΔΦ|/Δt = (250)(2.4×10⁻³)/0.040 = 0.600/0.040 = **15 V**. Charge: q = N|ΔΦ|/R = 0.600/12 = **5.0×10⁻² C (50 mC)**. **Why exactly twice a 90° turn:** turning through 90° takes the flux from +BA to **0**, a change of BA; flipping takes it from +BA to **−BA**, a change of **2BA**, because the flux does not merely vanish, it **reverses sign**. Φ = B·A is a signed scalar whose sign follows the chosen normal, and forgetting that is a clean factor-of-two error, not a rounding slip. **(c) The rotating rod.** ε = ½BL²ω = ½(0.30)(0.40)²(125.7) = ½(0.30)(0.16)(125.7) = **3.02 V**. Using BLv with the **tip** speed v = Lω = (0.40)(125.7) = 50.3 m/s gives (0.30)(0.40)(50.3) = **6.03 V**, exactly **twice** the correct value. The rod's speed is not uniform: it is **zero at the pivot** and Lω at the far end, so each element dr at radius r contributes B(ωr)dr and the integral ∫₀ᴸ Bωr dr = ½BL²ω. Equivalently the correct speed to use is the **mean**, v̄ = ½Lω = 25.1 m/s, which restores 3.02 V. Any answer that quietly uses the tip speed is asserting that the pivot end moves as fast as the tip.

**12.** *Concept: it is the flux through the **enclosed area** that induces an emf, not the field at the conductor — and the mechanism is the induced electric field.* **(i) Field rate inside the solenoid.** B = μ₀nI, so dB/dt = μ₀ n (dI/dt) = (4π×10⁻⁷)(2000)(40) = **0.1005 T/s**. **(ii) Flux rate through the ring.** Outside a long solenoid B ≈ 0, so **all** of the flux the ring encircles is that of the solenoid's own cross-section: A_sol = π(0.025)² = **1.964×10⁻³ m²**, giving dΦ/dt = (0.1005)(1.964×10⁻³) = **1.974×10⁻⁴ Wb/s** per turn. **(iii) Emf and current.** ε = N dΦ/dt = (30)(1.974×10⁻⁴) = **5.92×10⁻³ V (5.9 mV)**; I = ε/R = 5.92×10⁻³/1.5 = **3.9×10⁻³ A (3.9 mA)**. **(iv) Induced electric field at the ring.** For one turn, ∮E·dl = E(2πr) = 1.974×10⁻⁴ V, so E = 1.974×10⁻⁴/(2π × 0.080) = 1.974×10⁻⁴/0.503 = **3.9×10⁻⁴ V/m**. This field exists in the space at radius 8.0 cm **whether or not the ring is there**; the wire merely gives its charges something to be pushed along. **Direction.** If B inside the solenoid points **up** the axis and is increasing, the induced current must produce flux **down** the axis inside the ring, so it flows **clockwise seen from above** (looking along B, from the end B points towards). **The wrong route, and by how much.** A student who uses the **ring's** own area A_ring = π(0.080)² = 2.011×10⁻² m² gets dΦ/dt = (0.1005)(2.011×10⁻²) = 2.021×10⁻³ Wb/s and ε = (30)(2.021×10⁻³) = **6.06×10⁻² V (61 mV)** — too large by the ratio of the areas, (8.0/2.5)² = **10.24**. The error is to imagine the solenoid's field filling the whole disc bounded by the ring, when in fact it is confined to the 2.5 cm-radius core and is essentially zero over the remaining 97 % of that disc. Multiplying a field by an area it does not occupy is the commonest way to get a flux wrong. **Why there is an emf with nothing moving.** Faraday's law contains only **dΦ/dt**; motion is one way of producing it, not a requirement. Here nothing moves at all — the current in the solenoid rises, so B rises, so the flux through the ring's enclosed area rises, and an emf follows. Microscopically, the changing flux creates a **circulating electric field** in the surrounding space (∮E·dl = −dΦ/dt), and it is that field, not any magnetic force on moving charges, that drives the ring's current. This is exactly how a transformer works, and it is why "there must be relative motion between the coil and the field" is wrong.
