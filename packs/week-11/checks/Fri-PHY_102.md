# Friday — PHY_102 nightly check

*The same week's induction worked numerically: emf from a rate of change of B, flux with the angle trap, motional emf with its induced current and retarding force, generator peak and rms emf, circulated charge, and fixing the sense of the induced current.*
*Sit cold, notes closed, about 15 minutes. Score out of 12.*

**1.** A 50-turn coil of area 4.0×10⁻³ m² has its plane perpendicular to a uniform field that rises steadily from 0.20 T to 0.80 T in 0.30 s. The magnitude of the induced emf is:
a) 8.0×10⁻³ V
b) 0.12 V
c) 0.40 V
d) 0.53 V

**2.** A flat loop of area 0.020 m² sits in a uniform 0.50 T field with the *plane of the loop* inclined at 30° to the field. The flux through the loop is:
a) 5.0×10⁻³ Wb
b) 8.7×10⁻³ Wb
c) 1.0×10⁻² Wb
d) zero, because B is not perpendicular to the loop

**3.** A rod of length 0.50 m slides on rails at 4.0 m/s perpendicular to a 0.30 T field, the field being perpendicular to the plane of the circuit. The circuit's total resistance is 2.0 Ω. The induced current is:
a) 0.15 A
b) 0.30 A
c) 0.60 A
d) 1.2 A

**4.** The rod in question 3 is now pulled at 8.0 m/s instead of 4.0 m/s, everything else unchanged. The retarding magnetic force on the rod and the power dissipated in the resistance, respectively:
a) both double
b) the force doubles, the power quadruples
c) the force quadruples, the power doubles
d) both quadruple

**5.** A 200-turn coil of area 0.015 m² rotates at 60 revolutions per second in a uniform 0.40 T field, about an axis perpendicular to the field. The peak emf is about:
a) 72 V
b) 320 V
c) 452 V
d) 2840 V

**6.** For the generator of question 5, the rms emf is about:
a) 226 V
b) 320 V
c) 452 V
d) 640 V

**7.** The flux through each turn of a 100-turn coil of total resistance 20 Ω is changed by 0.040 Wb. The total charge that circulates round the coil is:
a) 0.20 C, but only if the change takes exactly 1.0 s; proportionally more if it is faster
b) 2.0 C, whatever the duration of the change
c) 0.20 C, whatever the duration of the change
d) indeterminate unless the time taken for the change is given

**8.** A rod slides to the right along horizontal rails, so that the circuit's enclosed area is increasing; the uniform field is directed into the page. Which is correct?
a) The induced current is clockwise, and the magnetic force on the rod points left, opposing the motion.
b) The induced current is counterclockwise, and the magnetic force on the rod points right, aiding the motion.
c) The induced current is clockwise, and the force on the rod is zero, since it carries current parallel to B.
d) The induced current is counterclockwise, and the magnetic force on the rod points left, opposing the motion.

**9. (explain why)** The flux through a coil goes from 0 to 0.30 Wb in 0.20 s, but not at a uniform rate. State what N|ΔΦ|/Δt gives you here, what it cannot tell you, and describe one situation in which the instantaneous emf would be many times larger than that figure.

**10. (explain why)** For the rod of question 3 (B = 0.30 T, L = 0.50 m, v = 4.0 m/s, R = 2.0 Ω), work out the retarding force and show numerically that the mechanical power supplied equals the electrical power dissipated. Explain why this equality would fail if the induced current ran the other way.

**11. (explain why)** A student doubles the number of turns on a coil in a given changing field, using the same wire, so that the coil's resistance doubles too. Explain what happens to the induced emf, to the induced current, and to the charge circulated by a given flux change, and why those three answers are not all the same.

**12. (explain why)** A generator's rotation frequency is doubled, with the same coil and the same field. State what happens to ε_peak, to ε_rms, and to the average emf over one complete cycle, and explain why one of those three answers is different in kind from the other two.
