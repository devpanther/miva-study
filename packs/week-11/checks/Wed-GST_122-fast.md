# Wednesday — GST_122 fast-hour check

*Hearing versus listening, the six areas of importance, and the strategy and competency lists.*
*5 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 5.*

**1.** On a crowded bus, Ifeoma can hear two passengers talking but pays them no attention. When one mentions her street, she begins consciously following what they say. What change has taken place?
A. From listening to hearing
B. From hearing to listening
C. From active listening to passive hearing
D. From listening to empathetic listening

**2.** In a collaborative seminar, Chike absorbs new information from other students' contributions and gains insights that help the group solve a problem. Under which of the six areas of the importance of listening does this fall?
A. Effective Communication
B. Building Relationships
C. Learning and Growth
D. Leadership Effectiveness

**3.** During a seminar, Bayo restates in his own words the point the speaker has just made, to check that he has understood it. Which listening strategy is he using?
A. Summarising
B. Note-taking
C. Paraphrasing
D. Asking clarifying questions

**4.** Which of the following is NOT one of the six practical strategies for listening named in the case study on effective listening?
A. Minimizing distractions
B. Avoiding prejudgment
C. Asking clarifying questions
D. Maintaining eye contact

**5.** Listening is defined as an active and voluntary process that requires what from the listener?
A. A conscious effort to interpret and understand spoken messages
B. The passive reception of sound through the ears
C. Repeating the speaker's words aloud
D. Writing down everything that is said

---

## Answers

**1. B** — *Hearing versus listening.* Hearing is passive and involuntary; listening is an active and voluntary process requiring a conscious effort to interpret and understand. The sound reached her all along, but only the deliberate effort turned hearing into listening.

The reverse order describes the opposite change; 'passive hearing' after 'active listening' is also backwards; nothing in the scenario involves understanding the speakers' emotions.

**2. C** — *The six areas of the importance of listening.* Learning and Growth covers listening as a key component of the learning process: absorbing information, gaining insights and acquiring new knowledge and skills, especially in collaborative environments where diverse perspectives contribute to innovation and problem-solving.

Effective Communication is about responding appropriately and giving feedback; Building Relationships is about trust and rapport; Leadership Effectiveness is about leaders making informed decisions and inspiring loyalty.

**3. C** — *Paraphrasing versus summarising.* Paraphrasing restates a point in your own words; it is one of the named strategies, along with summarising, asking clarifying questions and note-taking.

Summarising condenses the whole of what was said into its main points rather than restating one point; note-taking records it in writing; asking clarifying questions puts a question back to the speaker.

**4. D** — *The practical listening strategies list.* The six practical strategies are paraphrasing, summarizing, asking clarifying questions, minimizing distractions, avoiding prejudgment and note-taking.

Eye contact appears under the separate Active Listening heading (full attention, eye contact, nodding, verbal cues), which is why it is the tempting choice, but it is not one of the six practical strategies.

**5. A** — *Definition of listening.* Listening is an active and voluntary process requiring a conscious effort to interpret and understand spoken messages.

Passive reception of sound is hearing; repeating words aloud is not interpretation; writing everything down describes note-taking, one strategy, not the definition of listening.
