# GST_112 — Week 11 Summary

*Nigerian Peoples and Culture · Week 11 (16 – 22 Nov 2026) · Topic: The "Makers of Nigeria" — nine Nigerians in politics, the economy and the creative industry, and their contributions to national development*

## The 8 most examinable things this week

1. **The list is nine names, and the exam will want them attached to the right dates.** In the deck's own order: **Herbert Macaulay (1864–1946)**, **Obafemi Awolowo (1909–1987)**, **Nnamdi Azikiwe (1904–1996)**, **Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti (1900–1978)**, **Fela Anikulapo-Kuti (1938–1997)**, **Chinua Achebe (1930–2013)**, **Wole Soyinka (1934–present)**, **Gani Fawehinmi (1938–2009)**, **Nwankwo Kanu (1976–present)**. The Summary PDF names exactly the same nine.
2. **Macaulay is "the father of Nigerian nationalism"**, and in **1923** he founded the **Nigerian National Democratic Party (NNDP)**, **Nigeria's first political party**, whose members were **the first to sit in the legislative council**. It **held sway until the late 1930s**, when — on the deck's account — it **joined forces with the Nigerian Youth Movement to form the National Council of Nigeria and the Cameroons (NCNC)**. In the **1920s** he protested British policies on **water rates, land issues, and the management of railway finances**.
3. **Awolowo was the first premier of the Western Region, 1954 to 1960**, leader of the **Action Group (AG)**, "**Nigeria's foremost federalist**", who advocated **federalism as the only basis for equitable national integration** and was at the forefront of the campaign for a **federal constitution, introduced in the 1954 Lyttelton Constitution**. As **leader of the opposition** he challenged the **Balewa-led government**.
4. **Azikiwe held two offices with two date ranges — do not merge them.** **Last governor-general of Nigeria, 1960 to 1963**, and **first elected president, 1963 to 1966, during the first republic**. He worked to end British control **both as a journalist and as a politician**, **joined politics in 1944**, **co-founded the NCNC**, and **inspired the Zikist movement**.
5. **Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti is "the Lioness of Lisabi."** She **mobilised market women**, and in **1953 organised a conference in Abeokuta on women's welfare** that **gave birth to the Federation of Nigerian Women's Societies (FNWS)**. On **18 February 1977** soldiers raided the **Lagos residence of her son Fela**; the **76-year-old** was **thrown from a second-storey window** and died of those injuries on **13 April 1978**.
6. **Fela Anikulapo-Kuti is "Nigeria's most famous musician"** and is **widely credited as the creator of Afrobeat in the early 1970s**, which **rapidly became the most avidly followed African style of music**. He **used words as a form of protest and resistance**, and as he **rose to prominence through the 1970s** he **changed the relationship between music as an art form and Nigerian socio-political discourse**.
7. **The two literary figures, with their landmark dates.** **Achebe**: **Things Fall Apart (1958)**, his masterpiece and **the most widely read book in modern African literature**; he **supported the secessionist Biafra movement** and was **head of the Biafra Broadcasting Service**; after the civil war he joined the **left-leaning People's Redemption Party (PRP)** and in **1983** became its **deputy national vice president**; his critique of the polity is **The Trouble with Nigeria**. **Soyinka**: imprisoned by the military government in **1967**, aged **33**, for **22 months** for **speaking out against the Nigerian civil war**; **fled the country in 1994** when the dictatorship **charged him with treason**; won the **Nobel Prize for Literature in 1986**, the **first African to do so**, and **dedicated the award to the imprisoned Nelson Mandela**.
8. **The lawyer and the footballer.** **Gani Fawehinmi** began practising in **Lagos in 1965**, became known as "**the people's lawyer**" for his **pro bono** work, campaigned for **human rights for 40 years**, was **frequently detained and beaten** (**his books were seized and his library set on fire**), and pressed the **still unsolved case of Dele Giwa**, the magazine editor **killed by a parcel bomb in 1986**. **Nwankwo Kanu**'s contribution **won the 1996 Olympic Gold medal for Nigeria, beating Brazil and Argentina on the road to Gold**; he won the **African Footballer**, the **Premier League**, the **UEFA Champions League** and the **UEFA Cup**, and is a **UNICEF ambassador** whose **foundation has helped Nigerian children with heart complications**.

## Detail the table cannot carry

- **The learning objective, in the deck's words:** **identify Nigerians from all walks of life, like in politics, the economy, and the creative industry**; **examine their contributions to national development**; **assess their inspirations for contributions.** A question on "the aim of this lesson" is answered from these three lines.
- **The Zikist movement**, inspired by Azikiwe, is a **radical revolutionary and multi-ethnic youth body** which championed the **Nigerian struggle against imperialism** and the belief that **Nigerians and indeed Africans should manage their own affairs**.
- **Achebe's quotation, from the first page of *The Trouble with Nigeria*:** "**The Nigerian problem is the unwillingness or inability of its leaders to rise to the responsibility and to the challenge of personal example, which are the hallmarks of true leadership.**"
- **Cheryl Johnson-Odim on Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti:** "**the only Nigerian woman of her time to rank as such a national figure or have the international exposure and connections of Ransome-Kuti**."
- **Fawehinmi's last act as the deck reports it:** he sought to **re-open the Dele Giwa case** and **called on the federal attorney general to be removed for his indifference to corruption** — **all from his sick bed**.
- **The deck's own closing caveat:** the list **is not exhaustive**, and it names **Burna Boy, Wizkid, Davido and Rema** as contemporary figures **drawing positive attention to Nigeria through music** who **deserve a mention**.
- **The mid-lesson question** asks you to **mention two other prominent "Makers of Nigeria" across any field, and how their contributions have influenced the country's development and progress** — so be ready to argue for two names of your own, using the deck's own template of **field, contribution, national effect**.
- **From the Summary PDF only:** the week also covered the **Nigerian diaspora** — its contribution to national development **primarily through remittances**; historical roots in **pre-modern migrations, the slave trade, and contemporary economic migration**; remittances as **a key source of foreign exchange and a major contributor to Nigeria's GDP**; the **educational and professional achievements of Nigerians in the United States**; the need for **strategies to harness the diaspora's potential**; and **challenges related to criminal activities within a minority of the diaspora**. The case study is titled "**Nigerian Diaspora's Impact**", and the ponder question turns on the concept of "**Japa**".

## Commonly confused

- **Macaulay vs Azikiwe on the NCNC.** **Macaulay founded the NNDP (1923)**; the deck credits the **NNDP joining the Nigerian Youth Movement** with forming the **NCNC**, while the Azikiwe slide says **Azikiwe co-founded the NCNC**. Both men attach to the NCNC; only **Macaulay** attaches to the **NNDP**, and only **Azikiwe** to the **Zikist movement**.
- **NNDP vs NYM vs NCNC vs AG vs PRP.** Five party names in one deck. **NNDP** = Macaulay, 1923, first party. **NYM** = the body the NNDP merged with. **NCNC** = the nationalist organisation **designed to advocate for Nigeria's independence**. **AG** = Awolowo's party. **PRP** = **Achebe's** party, **left-leaning**, joined **after the civil war**.
- **Azikiwe's two offices and four dates.** **Governor-general 1960–1963** (and he was the **last**), **president 1963–1966** (and he was the **first elected**). Students routinely write "first president 1960" — the deck does not.
- **Awolowo's premiership vs the Lyttelton Constitution.** He was **premier of the Western Region from 1954 to 1960**; the **federal constitution** he campaigned for was introduced in the **1954 Lyttelton Constitution**. Both carry 1954; only one is an office.
- **Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti vs Fela Anikulapo-Kuti.** **Mother and son**, and the raid that killed her was on **his** Lagos residence. She is the **activist (1900–1978)**; he is the **musician (1938–1997)**. Her achievement is the **FNWS**; his is **Afrobeat**.
- **1953, 1977 and 1978 in the Ransome-Kuti story.** **1953** = the **Abeokuta conference** that produced the **FNWS**. **18 February 1977** = the **raid** and the fall from the **second-storey window**. **13 April 1978** = her **death** from those injuries. Three dates, three different events.
- **Achebe vs Soyinka in the civil war.** **Achebe supported Biafra** and **headed the Biafra Broadcasting Service**; **Soyinka spoke out against the war** and was **imprisoned for 22 months**. Opposite postures, same conflict.
- **Soyinka's three dates.** **1967** = imprisonment (aged **33**, **22 months**). **1986** = the **Nobel Prize for Literature**, **first African**, dedicated to **Mandela**. **1994** = **fled Nigeria**, **charged with treason**. **1999** = the **return to civilian rule** since which he has continued to activate political discourse.
- **1986 belongs to two different figures.** **Soyinka's Nobel Prize** and the **parcel bombing of Dele Giwa** that Fawehinmi pursued are both **1986**. Do not attach the bombing to Soyinka or the prize to Fawehinmi.
- **Gani Fawehinmi vs Fela Anikulapo-Kuti — both 1938.** Fawehinmi **1938–2009**, the **lawyer**; Fela **1938–1997**, the **musician**. Same birth year, different deaths (**cancer, 2009**) and different fields.
- **Kanu's medal is Olympic, not a World Cup.** **1996 Olympic Gold**, with **Brazil and Argentina beaten on the road to Gold** — not in a single final. His club honours (**Premier League, UEFA Champions League, UEFA Cup**) and the individual **African Footballer** award are separate from the **UNICEF ambassadorship** and the **heart foundation**.
- **The nine "Makers" vs the four contemporary musicians.** **Burna Boy, Wizkid, Davido and Rema** are named only in the **summary slide** as deserving a mention; they are **not** part of the examinable nine.
