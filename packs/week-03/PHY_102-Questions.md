## Short answer

**13.** Distinguish clearly between electromotive force and terminal potential difference. Write the equation relating them, explain what the term Ir represents physically, and state the condition under which the two are equal.

**14.** Starting from ε = work done per unit charge, derive the dimensional formula for electromotive force, showing each step.

**15.** A parallel-plate capacitor in vacuum has plate area 0.030 m² and plate separation 2.0 mm. The uniform field between the plates is 5.0×10⁴ V/m. Find (a) the energy density in the field, (b) the total energy stored, and (c) confirm your answer using ½CV². Show your working.

## Answers

**1. B** — *Definition of emf: energy per unit charge supplied by the source.* ε = W/Q, in joules per coulomb, i.e. volts. A takes the misleading name literally — emf is not a force and has none of the dimensions of one. C defines the *terminal p.d.*, which equals ε only at zero current. D describes power, in watts.

**2. C** — *The two working equations.* The internal resistance is in series with the load, so ε = I(R + r); of that, Ir is dropped inside the cell, leaving V = ε − Ir at the terminals. A and B invert the signs. D subtracts IR instead of Ir — but IR *is* the terminal p.d., so that would give V = 0.

**3. A** — *Volt = J/C, and [M¹L²T⁻³I⁻¹].* Work is [M¹L²T⁻²] and charge is [I¹T¹], so their quotient is [M¹L²T⁻³I⁻¹]. B answers the name rather than the quantity. C forgets that dividing by charge brings in an extra T⁻¹. D gives the dimensions of energy, before dividing by charge.

**4. C** — *I = ε/(R + r), then V = ε − Ir.* I = 2.0/(1.6 + 0.40) = 1.0 A; V = 2.0 − (1.0)(0.40) = 1.6 V (= IR ✓). A uses I = ε/R = 1.25 A, omitting r from the series total. B has the right current but ignores the lost volts. D quotes the lost volts (0.40 V) as the terminal p.d.

**5. B** — *V = −rI + ε is a straight line.* Gradient = −r (so the *internal* resistance, and negative), intercept = ε, the p.d. extrapolated to I = 0 — precisely the "no current flows" condition in the definition of emf. A drops the minus sign; C confuses the resistance being varied with the one being measured; D confuses an intercept with a term that is zero at that intercept.

**6. B** — *ε = Blv.* ε = 20 × 0.70 × 1.0 = 14 V. (The minus sign in ε = −Blv indicates direction only.) A divides instead of multiplying, C invents a spurious geometric factor, D simply quotes B.

**7. B** — *Faraday's law.* ε = −N dΦ_B/dt: it is the **rate of change** of flux that matters, not the flux itself (A), and the number of turns and the rate both count (C). D is wrong because a stationary coil in a changing field also has an induced emf — either the field or the area may change.

**8. B** — *Energy stored in a charged capacitor.* U = ½CV² = ½QV = Q²/2C. A omits the factor ½, which arises because the p.d. rises from 0 to V during charging so the average p.d. is V/2. C omits the ½ as well; D mangles the algebra.

**9. C** — *Energy density of an electric field.* u_E = ½ε₀E², in J/m³, and the total energy between parallel plates is u_E × (A·d). A confuses density with total energy — the commonest slip on this topic. B and D attach the wrong physical meaning and units entirely.

**10. A** — *C = ε₀A/d, with d converted to metres.* C = (8.85×10⁻¹²)(0.010)/(1.0×10⁻³) = 8.85×10⁻¹¹ F. B leaves d in millimetres; C misplaces the exponent; D inverts the fraction.

**11. C** — *Dielectric constant and dielectric strength.* C = Kε₀A/d = KC₀ with K ≥ 1 (K = 1 in vacuum, barely above 1 in air), so the capacitance rises; but the material also has a breakdown potential V_max, above which it conducts and the capacitor fails. A reverses the effect; B forgets that ε, not just A and d, is in the formula; D is right about K but forgets the breakdown limit — the half-answer that costs the mark.

**12. A** — *Parallel gives the largest equivalent capacitance, and U = ½CV².* In parallel the capacitances add, so C_eq exceeds the largest single capacitor, and at a fixed supply voltage the stored energy rises with C. B states the parallel rule but attaches it to "series"; C misdescribes series, where the supply voltage is *shared* between the capacitors; D ignores C in U = ½CV².

**13.** *EMF vs terminal potential difference.* The **emf** ε is the work done by the source on each coulomb of charge driven round the circuit — the total energy per coulomb the cell makes available, measured in volts (J/C). The **terminal potential difference** V is the p.d. across the cell's terminals once the circuit is switched on and current flows — the energy per coulomb actually delivered to the external circuit. They are related by **V = ε − Ir**, equivalently ε = V + Ir = I(R + r). The term **Ir** is the "lost volts": the energy per coulomb dissipated as heat inside the cell against its own **internal resistance** r. Hence V < ε whenever a current flows, and the two are numerically **equal only when no current flows** (open circuit, or measurement with an ideal voltmeter of infinite resistance) — which is why the intercept of a V–I graph at I = 0 reads the emf.

**14.** *Dimensional analysis of emf.* ε = work done × (charge)⁻¹.
Work done = force × displacement = (mass × acceleration) × displacement = [M][M⁰L¹T⁻²][L] = **[M¹L²T⁻²]**.
Charge = current × time = **[I¹T¹]**.
Therefore ε = [M¹L²T⁻²] × [I¹T¹]⁻¹ = [M¹L²T⁻²][I⁻¹T⁻¹] = **[M¹L²T⁻³I⁻¹]**.
(Sanity check: this is the dimension of the volt, and it is nothing like the newton's [M¹L¹T⁻²] — emf is not a force.)

**15.** *Energy density and total energy in a uniform field, cross-checked against ½CV².*
(a) u_E = ½ε₀E² = ½(8.85×10⁻¹²)(5.0×10⁴)² = ½(8.85×10⁻¹²)(2.5×10⁹) = **1.11×10⁻² J/m³**.
(b) Volume between the plates = A·d = (0.030)(2.0×10⁻³) = 6.0×10⁻⁵ m³; U = u_E × Ad = (1.11×10⁻²)(6.0×10⁻⁵) = **6.6×10⁻⁷ J**.
(c) Cross-check: V = Ed = (5.0×10⁴)(2.0×10⁻³) = 100 V; C = ε₀A/d = (8.85×10⁻¹²)(0.030)/(2.0×10⁻³) = 1.33×10⁻¹⁰ F; U = ½CV² = ½(1.33×10⁻¹⁰)(100)² = **6.6×10⁻⁷ J** ✓ — the same energy, as it must be, since substituting C = ε₀A/d and V = Ed into ½CV² gives ½ε₀E²(Ad) exactly. Note the units: (a) is a **density** in J/m³, (b) and (c) are **energies** in J.
