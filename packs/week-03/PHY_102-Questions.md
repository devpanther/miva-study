## Section B — Short answer (3)

**13.** Define electromotive force and state precisely how it differs from terminal potential difference. Write both ε = I(R + r) and ε = V + Ir, defining every symbol, and describe the laboratory procedure and graph used to determine the emf and the internal resistance of a cell.

**14.** Starting from ε = work done per unit charge, derive the dimensional formula of electromotive force, showing the dimensions of work and of charge separately. State the SI unit of emf and what one volt means.

**15.** A parallel-plate capacitor in vacuum has plate area 0.020 m² and plate separation 5.0 mm, and is charged to a potential difference of 1.0 V. Calculate (a) its capacitance, (b) the electric field between the plates, (c) the energy stored using ½CV², and (d) the energy density ½ε₀E². Show that (d) multiplied by the volume between the plates reproduces (c). Take ε₀ = 8.85×10⁻¹² F/m.

## Answers

**1. B** — *Definition of emf as energy per unit charge.* ε = W/Q, in joules per coulomb, i.e. volts. A is seduced by the word "force" — emf is not a force and is not measured in newtons. C is the *terminal potential difference*, which equals ε only at zero current. D is a garbled Ohm's law.

**2. C** — *Lost volts.* Ir is the potential difference dropped inside the cell across its own internal resistance r, so V = ε − Ir. A is V itself, the other term in the equation. B invents a stored quantity — a cell stores chemical energy, and Ir is not it. D is a *power* (I²r, in watts), not a voltage.

**3. D** — *emf equals terminal pd only on open circuit.* With I = 0 the term Ir vanishes and V = ε. A is exactly backwards: on short circuit R → 0, so V = IR → 0 and the whole emf is lost internally. B is the maximum-power-transfer condition, at which V = ε/2. C makes I large, so Ir is large and V is furthest below ε.

**4. A** — *Dimensions of emf.* ε = work/charge = [M L² T⁻²]/[I T] = [M¹ L² T⁻³ I⁻¹]. B divides work by current instead of charge (it forgets the T in [I T]). C is the dimension of resistance. D is force divided by charge, i.e. electric field strength.

**5. A** — *The V–I graph for a cell.* V = ε − Ir is y = c + mx with c = ε and m = −r. B confuses the internal resistance with the external one — R is the quantity being varied, not the gradient. C swaps intercept and gradient. D misreads the intercept: I = 0 is *minimum* current, not maximum.

**6. B** — *Motional emf ε = Bvl.* (0.20)(3.0)(0.50) = 0.30 V. A omits the length (Bv), C halves the result by importing the rotating-rod formula, D omits the speed (Bl).

**7. B** — *Statement of Faraday's law.* ε = −N dΦ_B/dt: it is the **rate of change** of flux that matters, and the minus sign records that the induced emf opposes the change. A confuses the flux with its rate of change — a steady flux, however large, induces nothing. C inverts the role of N, which multiplies. D is the definition of flux itself.

**8. B** — *Internal resistance in a real circuit.* I = ε/(R + r) = 1.5/3.0 = 0.50 A; V = IR = 1.25 V (equivalently ε − Ir = 1.5 − 0.25). A ignores the internal resistance entirely. C comes from using I = ε/R = 0.60 A, forgetting to add r before dividing. D quotes the lost volts Ir instead of the terminal voltage.

**9. B** — *U = ½CV².* ½(1.00×10⁻⁴)(50)² = ½(1.00×10⁻⁴)(2500) = 0.125 J. A drops the factor ½ (that is QV, the work done by the *battery*, half of which is dissipated in charging). C is the numerical value of Q = CV = 5.0×10⁻³ C mislabelled as an energy. D forgets to square V.

**10. C** — *Energy density of an electric field.* ½ε₀E² is energy per unit **volume**, in J/m³; the total is U = ½ε₀E²(Ad), where Ad is the volume between the plates. A is the error printed in the lecture slides — check the units and it collapses. B confuses energy per charge (volts) with energy per volume. D uses area instead of volume.

**11. A** — *C = ε₀A/d.* C is proportional to A and inversely proportional to d, so halving A halves C and doubling d halves it again: C → C/4. B applies only one of the two changes. C assumes the effects cancel, but they act in the same direction, not opposite ones. D inverts the dependence on d.

**12. A** — *Conductors versus insulators.* Conductors have free electrons that move from atom to atom, low resistivity, and zero internal field in electrostatic equilibrium; insulators have no free electrons and high resistivity, and also block heat and sound. B inverts the resistivity. C gives insulators free electrons, which is the defining thing they lack. D contradicts the zero-internal-field property of a conductor at equilibrium.

**13.** *Definition of emf, the circuit equation, and the V–I determination.* **Emf (ε)** is the work done per unit charge by the source in driving charge round the circuit — the energy in joules that the source gives to each coulomb passing through it. Its unit is the volt. **Terminal potential difference (V)** is the voltage measured across the terminals **when current is flowing**; it is the energy per coulomb actually delivered to the external circuit. They differ because some energy per coulomb is used up inside the source itself against its internal resistance: **ε = V + Ir**, so V < ε whenever I > 0, and V = ε only on open circuit. Equivalently **ε = I(R + r)**, where ε is the emf (V), I the circuit current (A), R the external or load resistance (Ω), r the internal resistance of the cell (Ω) and V = IR the terminal pd (V). **Procedure**: connect the cell to a variable resistor with an ammeter in series and a voltmeter across the terminals; set several values of R (as R increases the current falls), recording I and V at each; disconnect the cell between readings so that it does not drain and change its r. **Graph**: plot V on the y-axis against I on the x-axis. Since V = ε − Ir the points lie on a straight line whose **intercept on the V-axis is the emf ε** (the voltage at zero current) and whose **gradient is −r**, so the internal resistance is the magnitude of the gradient.

**14.** *Dimensional analysis of emf.* ε = work done × [charge]⁻¹. Work done = force × displacement = mass × acceleration × displacement = [M] × [M⁰ L¹ T⁻²] × [L] = **[M¹ L² T⁻²]**. Charge = current × time = **[I¹ T¹]**. Therefore ε = [M¹ L² T⁻²] × [I¹ T¹]⁻¹ = **[M¹ L² T⁻³ I⁻¹]**. The SI unit is the **volt (V)**; one volt is one joule per coulomb, i.e. the source gives one joule of energy to every coulomb of charge it drives through the circuit.

**15.** *Capacitance, field, stored energy and energy density in a parallel-plate capacitor.*
(a) C = ε₀A/d = (8.85×10⁻¹²)(0.020)/(5.0×10⁻³) = **3.54×10⁻¹¹ F** (35.4 pF).
(b) E = V/d = 1.0/(5.0×10⁻³) = **2.0×10² N/C** (200 V/m).
(c) U = ½CV² = ½(3.54×10⁻¹¹)(1.0)² = **1.77×10⁻¹¹ J**.
(d) u_E = ½ε₀E² = ½(8.85×10⁻¹²)(2.0×10²)² = ½(8.85×10⁻¹²)(4.0×10⁴) = **1.77×10⁻⁷ J/m³**.
Check: the volume between the plates is Ad = (0.020)(5.0×10⁻³) = 1.0×10⁻⁴ m³, so u_E × (Ad) = (1.77×10⁻⁷)(1.0×10⁻⁴) = **1.77×10⁻¹¹ J**, exactly the answer to (c). The two agree because U = ½CV² and u_E(Ad) are the same expression: substituting C = ε₀A/d and V = Ed into ½CV² gives ½ε₀E²(Ad). Note that (d) is in J/m³ and (c) in J — quoting 1.77×10⁻⁷ as "the energy stored" is wrong by the factor 10⁻⁴ m³.
