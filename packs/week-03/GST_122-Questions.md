## Multiple choice

**1.** "Although the generator failed, the students continued their exams, and the invigilator lit a lantern." By structure, this sentence is:
a) simple b) compound c) compound-complex d) complex

**2.** When a sentence is described by its **purpose**, it is being analysed by:
a) function b) structure c) syntax d) morphology

**3.** Which of the following is **not** listed as a part of speech falling under function words?
a) Articles b) Prepositions c) Pronouns d) Adverbs

**4.** "Please pass the salt." By function, this sentence is:
a) declarative b) imperative c) interrogative d) exclamatory

**5.** "She loves to read; he prefers to watch movies." By structure, this sentence is:
a) compound b) complex c) simple d) compound-complex

**6.** The "Rule of Proximity Agreement" requires the verb or noun to align with which subject?
a) the first subject in the sentence b) the plural subject c) the grammatical head of the sentence d) the closest subject

**7.** The discriminating test between a **complex** and a **compound-complex** sentence is:
a) whether a subordinating conjunction is present b) the number of independent clauses c) the total length of the sentence d) whether a semicolon is used

**8.** In "They have been sitting under the mango tree," which word is a **content** word?
a) They b) have c) sitting d) under

**9.** "If it rains, we will stay indoors." The lesson places this in a category **additional** to the four main functional types. That category is:
a) conditional b) exclamatory c) imperative d) declarative

**10.** "Neither the developers nor the designer ____ submitted the mock-up." Applying concord, the correct verb is:
a) have b) were c) are d) has

**11.** In spoken English, emphasis (stress) falls on:
a) function words b) content words c) articles only d) prepositions only

**12.** "Nneoma cooked dinner, while Lara slept." By structure, this sentence is:
a) simple b) compound c) complex d) compound-complex

## Short answer

**S1.** State the test that tells a **compound** sentence from a **complex** one, and give one original example of each, underlining or naming the joining word in both.

**S2.** Define **function words** as the lesson defines them, list the five parts of speech that fall under them, and state the one property that separates them from content words in **speech**.

**S3.** Give **both** labels — structure and function — for: "Close the window before the rain starts." Then state precisely how you tell an imperative sentence from a declarative one.

---

## Answers

**1. c** — Two independent clauses ("the students continued their exams" / "the invigilator lit a lantern") plus one dependent clause ("Although the generator failed") = compound-complex.
**2. a** — Function describes a sentence by its purpose; structure describes how it is made up of parts.
**3. d** — Adverbs are content (lexical) words; the function-word parts of speech are conjunctions, auxiliary/helping verbs, prepositions, articles and pronouns.
**4. b** — It makes a request/command with an understood subject "you", which is the imperative.
**5. a** — Two independent clauses joined by a **semicolon**; a semicolon makes it compound just as a coordinating conjunction would.
**6. d** — Proximity agreement makes the verb (or noun) agree with the **nearest** subject where several subject elements are present, not the first or the "main" one.
**7. b** — Complex has exactly one independent clause; compound-complex has two or more (both have at least one dependent clause, so the subordinating conjunction does not separate them).
**8. c** — "Sitting" is a main (lexical) verb; *they* is a pronoun, *have* an auxiliary, *under* a preposition — all function words.
**9. a** — Conditional ("if") sentences express conditions and their likely results, added beyond declarative, imperative, interrogative and exclamatory.
**10. d** — With "or"/"nor" the Rule of Proximity applies: the nearest subject is the singular *designer*, so the verb is singular — **has**.
**11. b** — Content words are accentuated; function words remain non-stressed — which is why the distinction refines pronunciation.
**12. c** — One independent clause ("Nneoma cooked dinner") plus one dependent clause introduced by the subordinating conjunction "while".

**S1.** Count the independent clauses and check the joining word. **Compound** = two or more clauses that could each stand alone, joined by a coordinating conjunction (and, but, or, so) or a semicolon — e.g. *The lecturer arrived late, but the class had already started* (joiner: *but*). **Complex** = one independent clause plus at least one dependent clause that cannot stand alone, usually introduced by a subordinating conjunction (although, because, while, since) — e.g. *Because the lecturer arrived late, the class started without him* (joiner: *because*).

**S2.** Function words (also called grammatical or structure words) have a primarily grammatical function and convey little or no meaning on their own; they connect or build relationships between content words and are crucial to a sentence's grammatical structure. The five parts of speech listed are **conjunctions, auxiliary/helping verbs, prepositions, articles and pronouns**. In speech, function words are **non-stressed**, whereas content words are stressed/accentuated.

**S3.** Structure: **complex** — one independent clause ("Close the window") plus a dependent clause ("before the rain starts"). Function: **imperative** — it gives a command/instruction. The test: an imperative's subject ("you") is understood rather than written, and the sentence tells someone to do something; a declarative states a fact, opinion or idea and carries an explicit stated subject (*You closed the window before the rain started.*).
