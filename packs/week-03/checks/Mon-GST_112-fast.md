# Monday — GST_112 fast-hour check

*Portuguese incursion, the trans-Atlantic slave trade, abolition, Lagos and company rule.*
*5 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 5.*

**1.** Put these four events in the order they happened: (A) Buxton's bill to abolish slavery; (B) the Royal Charter that created the Royal Niger Company; (C) Portuguese sailors begin exploring the West African coast; (D) the annexation of Lagos.
A. C, A, B, D
B. C, D, A, B
C. C, A, D, B
D. A, C, D, B

**2.** A body operated as both a trading company and an administrator, paid dividends solely from trade profits, and by 1887 controlled all the territories in the basin of the Niger. Which body was it?
A. The Royal Niger Company
B. The Central African Company
C. The Oil River Protectorate
D. The Company of African Merchants

**3.** Which of the following statements about the events leading to the annexation of Lagos is correct?
A. Lagos was annexed in 1851, immediately after the bombardment
B. Kosoko refused the 1851 treaty and, after the bombardment, fled to Epe
C. Akitoye refused the 1851 treaty and was driven out by the bombardment
D. Beecroft was appointed in 1861 to supervise the newly annexed Lagos

**4.** Which of the following is NOT one of the four named consequences of the Portuguese incursion into the Niger Area?
A. Opening up the area to the rest of the world
B. A series of wars to keep up the supply of slaves
C. The abandonment of African cultures
D. The decline of brass casting in Benin

**5.** What was the Spanish Asiento?
A. The right to trade in slaves in the New World
B. The Pope's division of the Old World between Portugal and Spain
C. The tax Nigerian chiefs charged on each slave sold
D. The sea crossing known as the Middle Passage

---

## Answers

**1. C** — *Sequence from Portuguese incursion to company rule.* Portuguese exploration began in the early 15th century (C); Buxton's bill was 1833 (A); Lagos was annexed in 1861 (D); the Royal Charter came in 1886 (B). So the order is C, A, D, B. The first option puts the charter before the annexation, the second puts the annexation before abolition, and the last puts abolition before the Portuguese had even reached the coast.

**2. A** — *Royal Niger Company as trader and administrator.* The Royal Niger Company (the chartered National African Company, 1886) traded and administered at once, paid dividends solely from trade profits, and by 1887 controlled all the territories in the Niger basin. The Central African Company was Laird's 1858 joint stock company; the Oil River Protectorate was a British protectorate of seven districts, not a company; the Company of African Merchants was one of the many rival firms of the 1860s.

**3. B** — *Annexation of Lagos.* The British signed a treaty with Kosoko in 1851 to end the slave trade; he refused, Lagos was bombarded, and on 29 December 1851 Akitoye regained the seat as Kosoko fled to Epe. The annexation itself came ten years later, in 1861, so the first option has the wrong year. The third option swaps the two rivals. Beecroft was appointed in 1849, before all of this, marking the beginning of Britain's direct influence.

**4. D** — *Consequences of the Portuguese incursion.* The four named consequences are: opening up the area to the rest of the world (leading eventually to colonialism), a series of wars to continue the supply of slaves, the abandonment of African cultures, and a major role in the trans-Atlantic slave trade. Brass casting in Benin actually grew as a result of Portuguese trade relations, so its decline is the outsider.

**5. A** — *Definition of the Asiento.* The Asiento was the Spanish system granting the right to trade in slaves in the New World; it later became transferable to other Europeans, including the Portuguese. The Pope's division gave Africa to Portugal and the Americas to Spain, which is a different arrangement. The per-slave tax was imposed by Nigerian chiefs at the ports, and the Middle Passage is the Atlantic crossing itself.
