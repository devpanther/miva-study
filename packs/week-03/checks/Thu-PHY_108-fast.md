# Thursday — PHY_108 fast-hour check

*The non-ohmic resistor practical: Ohm's law, Joule's law, the two plots and the procedure.*
*Five questions, straight after the fast hour. Recall only. Score out of 5.*

**1.** Which plot comes out as a straight line in this experiment?
A. I against V
B. V against I
C. P against I²
D. R against V

**2.** On the P against I² graph, what does the gradient equal?
A. The resistance R
B. The reciprocal of resistance, 1/R
C. The current I
D. The power P

**3.** Why is a tungsten filament non-ohmic?
A. Its resistance falls as the current rises
B. Heating raises its temperature and its resistance
C. It conducts in one direction only
D. Its voltage stays constant

**4.** Over which voltage range are readings taken?
A. 0.1 V to 5 V
B. 1 V to 50 V
C. 0.1 V to 50 V
D. 0 V to 100 V

**5.** Ohm's law requires the resistance to be independent of what?
A. The magnitude and polarity of the applied p.d.
B. The room temperature
C. The length of the conductor
D. The meter used to read it

## Answers

**1. C** — P against I² is the straight line that verifies Joule's law. I against V is the curve that shows the resistor is non-ohmic, which is the whole point of the experiment.

**2. A** — Comparing P = R·I² with y = mx + c makes the gradient R, with an intercept of zero. 1/R is the slope of an I against V graph, a different pair of axes.

**3. B** — Current dissipates heat by P = I²R, the temperature rises and so does R, so the current stops rising linearly. Falling resistance describes a thermistor, and one-way conduction describes a diode.

**4. C** — The procedure starts at about 0.1 V and covers the whole available range up to the 50 V limit of the supply, since the curvature only shows at the high-current end.

**5. A** — A device is ohmic if resistance between two points is independent of the magnitude and polarity of the potential difference, which is why a diode counts as non-ohmic even though each branch looks straight.
