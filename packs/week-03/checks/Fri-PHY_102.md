**1.** A cell of emf 9.0 V and internal resistance 1.5 ohm is connected to a 3.0 ohm resistor. What is the potential difference across the 3.0 ohm resistor?
a) 9.0 V
b) 6.0 V
c) 3.0 V
d) 4.5 V

**2.** Solving the same circuit, a student writes I = epsilon/R and ignores r entirely. What is the effect on the answers?
a) The current is unchanged but the terminal p.d. comes out too small
b) Both the current and the terminal p.d. come out too small
c) The current comes out too small, and the terminal p.d. then exceeds the emf
d) The current comes out too large, and the terminal p.d. is wrongly reported as equal to the emf

**3.** A student cannot remember the motional-emf formula and wants to settle it by units. Which of these combinations has the units of volts?
a) Bvl
b) Bv/l
c) Bl/v
d) Bv/l squared

**4.** A dielectric of constant K = 2.5 fills the gap of a capacitor in which the field is 400 V/m. What is the energy stored per unit volume?
a) 7.08 x 10^-7 J/m^3
b) 3.54 x 10^-6 J/m^3
c) 1.77 x 10^-6 J/m^3
d) 1.42 x 10^-6 J/m^3

**5.** A 3.0 microfarad capacitor carries a charge of 6.0 microcoulombs. The energy stored is
a) 1.8 x 10^-5 J
b) 9.0 x 10^-6 J
c) 1.2 x 10^-5 J
d) 6.0 x 10^-6 J

**6.** A 100-turn coil of area 0.020 m^2 lies perpendicular to a magnetic field that is increasing steadily at 0.50 T/s. The magnitude of the induced emf is
a) 1.0 V
b) 0.010 V
c) 10 V
d) 0.10 V

**7.** Asked for the energy stored in a 4.7 microfarad capacitor charged to 50 V, a student reports 1.2 x 10^-4 J instead of the correct 5.9 x 10^-3 J. What did the student do?
a) Used Q^2/2C with the charge taken as CV^2 instead of CV
b) Divided by 2 twice, once in the formula and once when converting microfarads
c) Used CV/2 instead of CV^2/2, i.e. failed to square the voltage
d) Used the field-energy formula one-half epsilon-nought E squared instead of the capacitor formula

**8.** A rod of length 0.80 m slides at 5.0 m/s on rails in a field of 0.25 T perpendicular to the plane; the circuit has a total resistance of 0.50 ohm. What mechanical power must be supplied to keep the rod moving at constant speed?
a) 1.0 W
b) 2.0 W
c) 0.50 W
d) 4.0 W

**9. (explain why)** Explain why, in a problem giving emf, internal resistance and external resistance, you must add r to R before dividing into the emf, and show with a numerical illustration how large the error is if you do not.

**10. (explain why)** Explain why computing a capacitor's stored energy as one-half epsilon-nought E squared times Ad must give the same number as one-half CV squared, and why this makes a good check on a numerical answer.

**11. (explain why)** Explain why an answer of '1.77 x 10^-7 J' for the quantity one-half epsilon-nought E squared should be rejected on sight, and what the correct statement of that result is.

**12. (explain why)** Explain why, for a rod being pulled at constant speed along rails, the mechanical power you supply comes out equal to the electrical power I squared R dissipated in the circuit, and why this equality is a legitimate check on a motional-emf answer.
