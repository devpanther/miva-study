*Week 3 of General Physics II treated conceptually.*
*12 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 12.*

**1.** A cell of emf 9.0 V and internal resistance 0.50 Ω delivers a current of 2.0 A to a circuit. What is the terminal potential difference?
A. 10 V
B. 9.0 V
C. 8.0 V
D. 1.0 V

**2.** A metal rod 0.40 m long moves at 5.0 m/s at right angles to a uniform magnetic field of 0.25 T, the field being perpendicular to the rod. What emf is induced across the rod?
A. 0.10 V
B. 0.50 V
C. 1.25 V
D. 2.0 V

**3.** The magnetic flux through each turn of a 100-turn coil changes by 6.0 × 10⁻³ Wb in 0.020 s. What is the magnitude of the induced emf?
A. 30 V
B. 3.0 V
C. 0.30 V
D. 0.12 V

**4.** A 4.0 μF capacitor is charged to 50 V. How much energy does it store?
A. 1.0 × 10⁻² J
B. 5.0 × 10³ J
C. 5.0 × 10⁻³ J
D. 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ J

**5.** In vacuum the electric field in a region is 2.0 × 10⁴ N/C. Take ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m. What is the energy stored per unit volume in that region?
A. 8.85 × 10⁻⁸ J/m³
B. 1.77 × 10⁻³ J/m³
C. 3.54 × 10⁻³ J/m³
D. 1.77 × 10⁻³ J

**6.** A parallel-plate capacitor in vacuum has plates of area 0.050 m² separated by 1.0 mm. Take ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m. What is its capacitance?
A. 4.4 × 10⁻¹³ F
B. 1.8 × 10⁻¹³ F
C. 4.4 × 10⁻⁷ F
D. 4.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ F

**7.** A capacitor measures 20 pF with vacuum between its plates and 70 pF when the gap is completely filled with an insulating oil. What is the dielectric constant of the oil?
A. 50
B. 0.29

D. 3.5

**8.** A dry cell is marked '1.5 V'. Which statement says what this emf means?
A. The cell gives 1.5 J of energy to each coulomb it drives round the circuit

**9.** A 15 μF capacitor carries a charge of 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ C. What are the potential difference across it and the energy it stores?

**12.** A metal plate and a slab of glass are each placed in a steady external electric field. Why is the field inside the metal zero at equilibrium while the field inside the glass is reduced but not zero?
A. The metal has no electrons free to make a field inside it, while glass has electrons that make one
B. The metal's free electrons are used up cancelling the field; glass holds fewer electrons, so less is cancelled

D. Free electrons in the metal redistribute until they cancel the field; glass has bound electrons that only polarise, cutting it by K

## Answers

**1. C** — *Terminal p.d. from V = ε − Ir.* The lost volts inside the cell are Ir = 2.0 × 0.50 = 1.0 V, so V = ε − Ir = 9.0 − 1.0 = 8.0 V.

10 V adds the lost volts instead of subtracting them; 9.0 V ignores the internal resistance, which is only right when no current flows; 1.0 V is the lost volts themselves, not the terminal p.d.

**2. B** — *Motional emf ε = Bvl.* ε = Bvl = 0.25 × 5.0 × 0.40 = 0.50 V.

0.10 V is Bl and forgets the speed; 1.25 V is Bv and forgets the length; 2.0 V is vl and forgets the field.

**3. A** — *Faraday's law with N turns.* |ε| = N ΔΦ/Δt = 100 × 6.0 × 10⁻³/0.020 = 100 × 0.30 = 30 V.

0.30 V is the emf per turn, forgetting N; 3.0 V is a power-of-ten slip in 6.0 × 10⁻³/0.020; 0.12 V multiplies by the time instead of dividing by it.

**4. C** — *Energy stored in a capacitor, U = ½CV².* U = ½CV² = ½ × 4.0 × 10⁻⁶ × (50)² = ½ × 4.0 × 10⁻⁶ × 2500 = 5.0 × 10⁻³ J.

1.0 × 10⁻² J leaves out the ½ (that is QV, the work at constant voltage); 5.0 × 10³ J drops the μ prefix; 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ J uses V instead of V².

**5. B** — *Energy density of an electric field.* u = ½ε₀E² = ½ × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × (2.0 × 10⁴)² = ½ × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 4.0 × 10⁸ = 1.77 × 10⁻³ J/m³.

8.85 × 10⁻⁸ uses E instead of E²; 3.54 × 10⁻³ leaves out the ½; '1.77 × 10⁻³ J' has the right number with the wrong unit, since ½ε₀E² is energy per cubic metre and must be multiplied by a volume to give joules.

**6. D** — *Parallel-plate capacitance C = ε₀A/d.* Convert d = 1.0 mm = 1.0 × 10⁻³ m. C = ε₀A/d = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 0.050/1.0 × 10⁻³ = 4.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ F (0.44 nF).

4.4 × 10⁻¹³ F leaves d as 1.0 (millimetres not converted); 1.8 × 10⁻¹³ F swaps A and d; 4.4 × 10⁻⁷ F treats mm as 10⁻⁶ m.

**7. D** — *Dielectric constant from the change in capacitance.* A dielectric multiplies the capacitance by K, so K = C/C₀ = 70/20 = 3.5. K has no unit and is always above 1 for a real material.

50 subtracts the capacitances instead of dividing; 0.29 divides the wrong way round (a K below 1 is impossible); 90 adds them.

**8. A** — *Meaning of emf as energy per unit charge.* Emf is work done per unit charge, ε = W/Q, so 1 V = 1 J/C: the cell converts 1.5 J of chemical energy for every coulomb it drives round. It is not a force despite its name.

'1.5 N' confuses the volt with the newton; 'always 1.5 V apart' describes the terminal p.d., which falls below ε by Ir as soon as current flows; watts measure power, not energy per charge.

**9. A** — *Potential difference and energy from the charge on a capacitor.* V = Q/C = 3.0 × 10⁻⁴/15 × 10⁻⁶ = 20 V. Then U = ½QV = ½(3.0 × 10⁻⁴)(20) = 3.0 × 10⁻³ J, the same as Q²/2C = 9.0 × 10⁻⁸/3.0 × 10⁻⁵.

6.0 × 10⁻³ J leaves out the factor ½; 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ J uses ½CV instead of ½CV²; the last option multiplies Q by C instead of dividing.

**10. B** — *Dimensional formula of emf.* Work = force × displacement = [M][L T⁻²][L] = [M L² T⁻²], and charge = current × time = [I T]. Dividing, emf = [M L² T⁻²]/[I T] = [M L² T⁻³ I⁻¹].

[M L² T⁻² I⁻¹] forgets the T that comes with the charge; [M L T⁻³ I⁻¹] uses force in place of work; [M L² T⁻¹ I] multiplies by the charge instead of dividing.

**11. C** — *Breakdown potential from dielectric strength.* Between parallel plates V = Ed, and the largest field the film tolerates is its dielectric strength, so V_max = (3.0 × 10⁶)(0.20 × 10⁻³) = 6.0 × 10² V = 600 V.

6.0 × 10⁵ V leaves the thickness in millimetres; 6.0 × 10⁴ V reads 0.20 mm as 0.020 m; 1.5 × 10¹⁰ divides by the thickness instead of multiplying.

**12. D** — *Field inside a conductor versus inside a dielectric.* In a conductor the electrons are free, so they keep moving until the surface charge they build up produces a field that exactly cancels the applied one; any leftover field would move more charge, so equilibrium means zero field inside. In glass the electrons are bound to their atoms and can only shift slightly, so the polarisation produces an opposing field that reduces the applied field by the factor K but can never cancel it.

The other options either deny the metal its free electrons, treat electrons as a supply that runs out, or describe optics rather than electrostatics.
