# Wednesday — GST_122 fast-hour check

*Sentence structure and function, function versus content words, and concord.*
*Five questions, straight after the fast hour. Recall only. Score out of 5.*

**1.** Two independent clauses plus at least one dependent clause makes which type?
A. Simple
B. Complex
C. Compound-complex
D. Compound

**2.** Which functional type gives commands, instructions or requests?
A. Declarative
B. Imperative
C. Interrogative
D. Exclamatory

**3.** Which word class listed below is a function word class?
A. Nouns
B. Verbs
C. Adjectives
D. Pronouns

**4.** Under the Rule of Proximity Agreement, the verb agrees with which subject?
A. The first subject
B. The closest subject
C. The plural subject
D. The longer subject

**5.** In spoken English, which words are stressed or emphasised?
A. Content words
B. Function words
C. Articles and prepositions
D. Auxiliary verbs

## Answers

**1. C** — Compound-complex needs two or more independent clauses and at least one dependent clause. Complex has only one independent clause, compound has no dependent clause, and simple has one clause in total.

**2. B** — An imperative commands, with its subject 'you' understood rather than written. Declarative states information, interrogative asks, and exclamatory expresses strong emotion.

**3. D** — Function words are conjunctions, auxiliary verbs, prepositions, articles and pronouns. Nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs are the content or lexical words that carry the meaning.

**4. B** — With several subject elements, especially joined by 'or' or 'nor', the verb aligns with the nearest subject. Plain subject-verb agreement matches the single subject's number instead.

**5. A** — Content words are accentuated while function words remain non-stressed, which is why the distinction aids comprehension and pronunciation. Articles, prepositions and auxiliaries are all function words.
