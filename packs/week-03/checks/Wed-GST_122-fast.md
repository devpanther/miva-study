# Wednesday — GST_122 fast-hour check

*Sentence structure and function, function versus content words, and concord.*
*5 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 5.*

**1.** By structure, classify this sentence: 'Amaka and Bisi sang at the wedding.'
A. Simple
B. Compound
C. Complex
D. Compound-complex

**2.** By function, classify this sentence: 'Hand in the attendance sheet before noon.'
A. Declarative
B. Imperative
C. Interrogative
D. Conditional

**3.** In the sentence 'They have been digging quietly under the mountain', which of these words is a function word?
A. digging
B. quietly
C. mountain
D. been

**4.** Choose the verb that satisfies concord: 'Either the lecturers or the registrar ___ the timetable each term.'
A. sign
B. have signed
C. signs
D. are signing

**5.** Which term names the set of rules governing the agreement between different elements in a sentence?
A. Syntax
B. Concord
C. Structure
D. Function

---

## Answers

**1. A** — *Classifying a sentence by structure.* A simple sentence consists of one independent clause. Two subjects sharing one verb is still a single clause, so the sentence is simple.

'Compound' is the trap: a compound sentence needs two or more independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction or a semicolon, not two nouns joined by 'and'. Complex needs a dependent clause; compound-complex needs both features.

**2. B** — *Classifying a sentence by function.* Imperative sentences give commands, instructions or requests and tell someone to do something; the subject 'you' is understood rather than written.

A declarative would state information with an explicit subject ('You handed in the sheet'); an interrogative asks a question and ends in '?'; a conditional expresses an 'if' condition and its likely result.

**3. D** — *Function words versus content words.* Function words are conjunctions, auxiliary or helping verbs, prepositions, articles and pronouns; 'been' is a helping verb, so it is a function word even though it looks like a verb.

'digging' is a main verb, 'quietly' an adverb and 'mountain' a noun: all content words, which carry the main meaning and are stressed in speech.

**4. C** — *Rule of Proximity Agreement with 'or'.* With 'or' in the subject, the Rule of Proximity Agreement makes the verb align with the closest subject. The nearest subject is 'the registrar', which is singular, so the verb is 'signs'.

'sign', 'have signed' and 'are signing' all agree with the plural 'lecturers', the first subject, which is the common mistake of matching the first element rather than the nearest one.

**5. B** — *Definition of concord.* Concord, or grammatical agreement, is the set of rules governing agreement between different elements in a sentence; its fundamental rule is subject-verb agreement.

Syntax is the branch of language that sentences belong to; structure describes how a sentence is built from parts; function describes a sentence's purpose.
