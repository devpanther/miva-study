## Answers

**1. B** — *Which rule is which conservation law.* The **junction** rule says charge cannot pile up at a point in the steady state — **conservation of charge**. The **loop** rule says a coulomb returned to its starting point is back at its starting potential — **conservation of energy**. A is the straight swap, the single most common one-mark loss on this "state and explain" question; C collapses the two into one law and loses the energy statement entirely; D confuses a conservation principle with a constitutive relation — Ohm's law is what lets you *write* the IR terms, not what makes their sum vanish. **[Week 5]**

**2. A** — *Counting independent equations.* With *b* branches and *n* junctions you get **n − 1 = 3** independent junction equations and **b − n + 1 = 6 − 4 + 1 = 3** independent loop equations, **b = 6** in all — exactly one per unknown current. B writes an equation at every junction; the *n*th is the sum of the other three and produces a redundant, easily-contradicted set. C takes one loop too many — a loop built only from branches already used is a combination of earlier loops and carries no new information. D abandons the junction rule and cannot close the system. **[Week 5]**

**3. B** — *The two sign rules are independent decisions.* The resistor term is fixed **only** by the arrow: walking **against** the assumed current you climb in potential, so the term is **+IR**. The emf term is fixed **only** by the terminals: entering at **+** and leaving at **−** is a fall, so **−ε**. A applies both rules backwards; C keeps the "a resistor always gives −IR" habit, which is true only when you walk *with* the arrow; D gets the resistor right but makes the battery's sign follow the current instead of the terminals. **[Week 5]**

**4. A** — *A negative current is not an error.* The magnitude is correct and only the drawn arrow was wrong; the branch carries 1.8 A the other way — here, into the **+** terminal of its cell, so that cell is being **charged**. B wastes exam time and gains nothing; C is the belief that a negative answer signals bad arithmetic; D is the dangerous one — dropping the sign corrupts I₃ = I₁ + I₂ and every power term after it. **[Week 5]**

**5. A** — *Opposing emfs subtract, all resistances add.* I = (12.0 − 6.0)/(0.50 + 4.0 + 0.50) = 6.0/5.0 = **1.2 A**. B adds the emfs, which is the series-*aiding* case; C divides by the external 4.0 Ω only, forgetting that both internal resistances sit inside the same loop; D uses the larger emf alone and ignores the opposing cell. **[Week 5]**

**6. B** — *Charging: V = ε + Ir.* Current is driven **into** the + terminal of the 6.0 V cell, so it absorbs energy and its terminal p.d. is **6.0 + (1.2)(0.50) = 6.6 V**. A applies the discharging formula ε − Ir; C forgets that V = ε only at zero current; D is the terminal p.d. of the **12.0 V** cell, 12.0 − (1.2)(0.50) = 11.4 V — the discharging one. **[Week 5]**

**7. A** — *V_max = √2 V_rms.* Quoted mains figures are always **rms**, so V_max = (1.414)(240) = **339 V**. B divides instead of multiplying, turning 240 V into 170 V — the √2 applied the wrong way round; C confuses peak with peak-to-peak (V_pp = 2V_max = 679 V here, and even that is not what B asks); D denies that mains values are rms at all. **[Week 6]**

**8. A** — *ELI the ICE man.* In **L**, v = L(di/dt), so the voltage **leads** the current by 90°; in **C**, i = C(dv/dt), so the **current** leads the voltage by 90°. B reverses both and inverts the whole character of any circuit built from them; C gives the capacitor the inductor's behaviour; D puts a phase shift in the resistor, where v and i are always **in phase**. **[Week 6]**

**9. A** — *X_C = 1/(2πfC).* ω = 2π(50) = 314.16 rad s⁻¹; ωC = (314.16)(40×10⁻⁶) = 1.2566×10⁻²; X_C = **79.6 Ω**. B drops the 2π and computes 1/(fC) = 500 Ω, a factor of 6.28 out; C writes X_C = ωC instead of 1/ωC, the standard slip; D leaves C in microfarads, a factor of 10⁶. **[Week 6]**

**10. A** — *Z = √(R² + (X_L − X_C)²), and the net reactance decides lead or lag.* X = 12 − 6.0 = **+6.0 Ω**, so Z = √(64 + 36) = **10 Ω**, and since X_L > X_C the circuit is **inductive** and the current **lags**. B has the right impedance but reads the lead/lag rule backwards. C adds the three oppositions arithmetically, which ignores that they peak at different instants. D adds the reactances (12 + 6 = 18) before squaring instead of subtracting them, giving √(64 + 324) = 19.7 Ω. **[Week 6]**

**11. A** — *KVL applied vectorially.* V_s = √(V_R² + (V_L − V_C)²) = √(60² + (40 − 120)²) = √(3600 + 6400) = √10000 = **100 V**. B adds the three readings arithmetically (220 V), which is nonsense because they peak at different instants. C adds the reactive drops instead of subtracting them: √(3600 + 160²) = 171 V. D is the misconception that a component drop can never exceed the supply — V_L and V_C are 180° apart and cancel before the resultant is taken, so either may legitimately be many times V_s. **[Week 6]**

**12. A** — *f₀ = 1/(2π√(LC)).* LC = (0.20)(20×10⁻⁶) = 4.0×10⁻⁶, √(LC) = 2.0×10⁻³ s, f₀ = 1/(2π × 2.0×10⁻³) = **79.6 Hz**. B is ω₀ = 1/√(LC) = 500 rad s⁻¹ reported as a frequency in hertz — the commonest error here. C quotes the **period**, T = 2π√(LC) = 1.3×10⁻² s, as though it were a frequency. D loses the square root, computing 1/(2πLC) = 4.0×10⁴ Hz. Note the answer does not involve R at all. **[Week 6]**

**13. A** — *P = V_rms I_rms cos φ.* P = (240)(5.0)(0.60) = **720 W**. B is the **apparent** power S = VI = 1200 VA, which is drawn but not consumed; C is the **reactive** power Q = VI sin φ = (240)(5.0)(0.80) = 960 var, borrowed and returned every cycle and measured in var, not watts; D applies cos φ twice. *Test*: if your answer did not use cos φ, you have computed S. **[Week 7]**

**14. A** — *P = I²R is always safe; P = V²/R usually is not.* Z = √(30² + 40²) = 50 Ω; I = 200/50 = 4.0 A; P = I²R = (16.0)(30) = **480 W**. B uses V²/R with the **supply** voltage, but only V_R = IR = 120 V sits across the resistor — 120²/30 = 480 W ✓, whereas 200²/30 = 1333 W is nearly three times too big. C is the apparent power VI = 800 VA. D is I²X_L = 640 var, the **reactive** power: an inductor dissipates nothing. **[Week 7]**

**15. A** — *S² = P² + Q².* S = √(480² + 640²) = √(230 400 + 409 600) = √640 000 = **800 VA**. B adds the two arithmetically; C subtracts them; D gets the number right but the unit wrong — watts measure what is consumed, volt-amperes what is drawn, and they coincide only at unity power factor. **[Week 7]**

**16. A** — *Negative instantaneous power = energy returning to the source.* p(t) = S[cos φ − cos(2ωt − φ)] dips to S(cos φ − 1) < 0 twice per cycle in **any** circuit with net reactance; the reactance is handing back energy it stored earlier in the cycle. B treats a signed instantaneous quantity as an impossibility; C invents destruction of energy — a reactance dissipates **zero** on average, it does not consume; D is backwards: at resonance φ = 0, p_min = 0, and p never goes negative. **[Week 7]**

**17. A** — *For a resistor, P_peak = 2P_av and the ripple is at 2f.* I_rms = 240/60 = 4.0 A, P_av = V_rms I_rms = **960 W**; p = V₀I₀ sin²ωt swings from 0 to V₀I₀ = (339.4)(5.657) = **1920 W**; and since v and i reverse together, the product completes two cycles per supply cycle — **100 Hz**. B forgets the ripple entirely; C reports the peak as the power dissipated, doubling the answer, and keeps the ripple at 50 Hz; D halves the average. **[Week 7]**

**18. A** — *Correction leaves P untouched, so I = P/V.* At unity power factor the whole apparent power is real: I = 3600/240 = **15.0 A** (before correction, S = 3600/0.75 = 4800 VA and I = 20.0 A). B is the uncorrected current — correction that changes nothing; C divides by the power factor instead of multiplying, sending the current *up*; D applies the factor twice. Note the general result: correcting to unity multiplies the line current by the old power factor. **[Week 7]**

**19. A** — *μ = NIA, a vector along the normal.* A = (0.060)(0.040) = 2.4×10⁻³ m²; μ = (40)(3.0)(2.4×10⁻³) = **0.288 ≈ 0.29 A·m²**, along the **normal** to the coil, sense by the right-hand rule (curl the fingers along the current, the thumb gives **μ**). B forgets the 40 turns; C leaves the area as 24 cm², a factor of 10⁴; D has the magnitude right but places the moment in the plane of the loop — **μ** is never in the plane, it is perpendicular to it. **[Week 8]**

**20. B** — *θ in τ = μB sin θ is measured from the normal.* If the **plane** contains **B**, the normal is perpendicular to **B**, so θ = 90°, sin θ = 1 and τ = **μB**, the maximum. A measures the angle from the plane instead of the normal — the error that swaps sin and cos in every following line. C is a fabricated factor. D is a genuine statement about the **force** (which is indeed zero in a uniform field) applied to the **torque**, which is a different quantity: the loop feels a couple, not a push. **[Week 8]**

**21. A** — *W = ΔU, and a full flip costs 2μB.* μB = (0.60)(0.25) = 0.15 J; W = U(180°) − U(0°) = (+0.15) − (−0.15) = **0.30 J**. B quotes μB, the energy difference from aligned to *perpendicular*, not to anti-aligned. C confuses the two **endpoints** having zero torque with there being no work in between — the torque is large throughout the turn. D is the maximum torque times the angle turned, (0.15)(π) = 0.47 J; you cannot multiply a **varying** torque by an angle, you must use ΔU. **[Week 8]**

**22. A** — *B = μ₀nI with n = N/L in turns per metre.* n = 800/0.40 = 2000 m⁻¹; B = (4π×10⁻⁷)(2000)(2.5) = **6.3×10⁻³ T**. B uses N = 800 in place of n, an answer short by the factor L. C leaves the length in centimetres, so n = 20 m⁻¹. D imports the intuition that a field must depend on the geometry's size — the solenoid field depends on the **core, n and I** only, and not on the radius at all. **[Week 8]**

**23. A** — *B = μ₀I/2πr, and the field is tangential.* B = (2×10⁻⁷)(15)/(0.060) = (3.0×10⁻⁶)/(0.060) = **5.0×10⁻⁵ T**, in **closed concentric circles** round the wire, sense by the right-hand grip rule. B carries over the radial picture from the 1/r field of a **line of charge** — the magnitudes fall off the same way but the directions are completely different. C leaves r in centimetres. D puts **B** along the current, which is the one direction it can never have. **[Week 8]**

**24. A** — *The minus sign is a statement about direction.* The electron's charge is negative, so its magnetic moment points **opposite** to its angular momentum **L**; the magnitude is g eL/2m, a positive number. B reads a vector statement as a negative magnitude. C confuses it with the sign of an energy. D conflates two unrelated minus signs — the one in U = −**μ**·**B** is what makes alignment the low-energy state and has nothing to do with the sign of the electron's charge. **[Week 8]**

**25. A** — *F = qvB sin θ, θ between v and B.* F = (3.0×10⁻⁶)(4.0×10⁵)(0.20)(sin 30° = 0.500) = (0.24)(0.500) = **0.12 N**, perpendicular to the plane of **v** and **B**. B omits sin θ, i.e. assumes v ⊥ B. C uses cos 30°, the sin/cos inversion that is the single commonest wrong answer in this topic — the force is **maximum** when v ⊥ B and **zero** when v ∥ B. D confuses "not perpendicular" with "parallel"; the force vanishes only at θ = 0° or 180°. **[Week 9]**

**26. A** — *The magnetic force does no work.* **F** ⊥ **v** always, so P = **F**·**v** = 0: the speed and the kinetic energy are constants of the motion and only the direction of **v** changes. B has the magnetic force doing work, which it never does. C confuses acceleration with speeding up — there is a real centripetal acceleration a = qvB/m, but it is perpendicular to **v**. D over-corrects: "no work" does not mean "no force"; the path is a circle, not a straight line. **[Week 9]**

**27. A** — *f_c = qB/(2πm), independent of speed and radius.* qB = (1.602×10⁻¹⁹)(0.40) = 6.41×10⁻²⁰; f = 6.41×10⁻²⁰/(2π × 1.67×10⁻²⁷) = 6.41×10⁻²⁰/(1.049×10⁻²⁶) = **6.1×10⁶ Hz**. B is ω_c = qB/m = 3.8×10⁷ rad s⁻¹ quoted as a frequency in hertz. C is the **period**, T = 1.6×10⁻⁷ s, quoted as a frequency. D is the belief that the lap time must depend on how fast the proton goes — doubling v doubles r in exact proportion, so the period is untouched. That independence is the whole principle of the cyclotron. **[Week 9]**

**28. A** — *v = E/B, and it contains neither q nor m.* qE = qvB gives v = (3.0×10⁵)/(0.50) = **6.0×10⁵ m/s**. Reversing the sign of the charge reverses **both** forces together, so the balance survives — the same speed passes for electrons and for positive ions, which is why it is a *speed* filter and not a mass filter. B invents a charge dependence; C multiplies E by B instead of dividing; D inverts the ratio. The mass dependence appears only in a second, magnet-only stage, where r = mv/qB turns the device into a mass spectrometer. **[Week 9]**

**31.** *Kirchhoff's rules, a negative current, terminal p.d.s and the power balance.* **[Week 5]**
(a) **Junction rule (KCL)**: at any junction ΣI = 0, i.e. the total current entering equals the total leaving. It expresses **conservation of charge** — a junction has no capacity to store charge, so whatever flows in per second flows out per second.
**Loop rule (KVL)**: round any closed loop ΣV = 0, i.e. Σε = ΣIR. It expresses **conservation of energy** — potential is single-valued, so a coulomb returned to its starting point is back at its starting potential.
**Counting**: b = **3** branches (three unknown currents), n = **2** junctions (a and b). So **n − 1 = 1** junction equation and **b − n + 1 = 2** loop equations, three in all — exactly one per unknown. Writing a second junction equation at b would merely repeat the first.
Series/parallel reduction fails outright in a **bridge** network: two arms on the same side are not in series (a junction taps between them, so the current is not common) and not in parallel (they share only one end, so the p.d. is not common). Nothing reduces, and Kirchhoff's rules are the only route.
(b) **Junction a**: **I₁ + I₂ = I₃**.
**Loop 1** (up branch 1 through ε₁ and R₁, down through R₃), starting at b: +18.0 − 2.0 I₁ − 4.0 I₃ = 0.
**Loop 2** (up branch 2 through ε₂ and R₂, down through R₃), starting at b: +6.0 − 2.0 I₂ − 4.0 I₃ = 0.
Substitute I₃ = I₁ + I₂:
18.0 = 6.0 I₁ + 4.0 I₂ …(i)
6.0 = 4.0 I₁ + 6.0 I₂ …(ii)
(i)×3 − (ii)×2: 54.0 − 12.0 = 18 I₁ − 8 I₁ → 42.0 = 10 I₁ → **I₁ = 4.2 A**.
From (ii): 6.0 = 16.8 + 6.0 I₂ → **I₂ = −1.8 A**, hence **I₃ = 4.2 − 1.8 = 2.4 A**.
Check in loop 1: 2.0(4.2) + 4.0(2.4) = 8.4 + 9.6 = 18.0 ✓. Loop 2: 2.0(−1.8) + 4.0(2.4) = −3.6 + 9.6 = 6.0 ✓.
(c) **I₂ = −1.8 A** means the magnitude 1.8 A is correct but the **arrow was drawn backwards**: branch 2 actually carries 1.8 A **down from a to b**, i.e. **into the + terminal of the 6.0 V cell**, which is therefore **being charged** by the 18.0 V cell. You must **not** re-solve with the arrow reversed, and you must **not** quietly drop the sign — −1.8 A goes with its sign into I₃ and into every later expression.
**Terminal p.d.s**: the 18.0 V cell is **discharging** (current leaves its + terminal), so V = ε − I₁r = 18.0 − (4.2)(2.0) = **9.6 V**. The 6.0 V cell is **charging**, so V = ε + |I₂|r = 6.0 + (1.8)(2.0) = **9.6 V** — note the **+** sign.
**V_ab by three routes**: (i) across R₃, V = I₃R₃ = (2.4)(4.0) = 9.6 V; (ii) down branch 1, 18.0 − (4.2)(2.0) = 9.6 V; (iii) down branch 2, 6.0 + (1.8)(2.0) = 9.6 V. All three agree — a network solved correctly gives **one** value of V_ab by every route.
(d) **Delivered** by the 18.0 V cell: ε₁I₁ = (18.0)(4.2) = **75.6 W**.
**Absorbed chemically** by the 6.0 V cell: ε₂|I₂| = (6.0)(1.8) = **10.8 W**.
**Dissipated**: I₁²R₁ = (17.64)(2.0) = 35.28 W; I₂²R₂ = (3.24)(2.0) = 6.48 W; I₃²R₃ = (5.76)(4.0) = 23.04 W; total = **64.8 W**.
Balance: 10.8 + 64.8 = **75.6 W ✓**. Every watt is accounted for. (Note the charging cell receives VI = (9.6)(1.8) = 17.28 W at its terminals, of which 10.8 W is stored chemically and 6.48 W is wasted as I²r inside it — εI, I²r and VI are three different numbers for a charging source.)

**32.** *A full series RLC solve, with the vector check and resonance.* **[Week 6]**
(a) The **rms** value of an alternating voltage is the **steady direct voltage that would dissipate the same average power in the same resistance** — it is defined by equal heating, not by any geometrical average of the waveform. For a sinusoid V_rms = V_max/√2, so **V_max = √2 V_rms = (1.414)(200) = 283 V**.
(b) **ω = 2πf = 2π(50) = 314.2 rad s⁻¹.**
**X_L = ωL = (314.2)(0.15) = 47.1 Ω.**
**X_C = 1/(ωC) = 1/[(314.2)(30×10⁻⁶)] = 1/(9.425×10⁻³) = 106.1 Ω.** (30 μF = 30×10⁻⁶ F.)
**Net reactance X = X_L − X_C = 47.1 − 106.1 = −59.0 Ω** — negative, so the circuit is **capacitive**.
**Z = √(R² + X²) = √(1600 + 3481) = √5081 = 71.3 Ω.**
**I = V/Z = 200/71.3 = 2.81 A** — the same current in all three components.
**V_R = IR = (2.81)(40) = 112 V; V_L = IX_L = (2.81)(47.1) = 132 V; V_C = IX_C = (2.81)(106.1) = 298 V.**
Arithmetic sum = 542 V, which is nonsense. **Vector sum**: √(112² + (132 − 298)²) = √(12 544 + 27 556) = √40 100 = **200 V ✓** — the supply, as KVL requires.
(c) **V_C = 298 V exceeds the 200 V supply and this is perfectly legal.** V_L and V_C are **180° apart**: the inductor's voltage peaks exactly when the capacitor's is at its trough, so at no instant do they both stand at their maxima. Kirchhoff's voltage law still holds, but it must be applied **vectorially** — the two large reactive drops cancel down to 166 V before being combined in quadrature with V_R.
(d) **tan φ = X/R = −59.0/40 = −1.475 → φ = −55.9°.** Negative, so the **current leads** the supply voltage by 55.9° — a **capacitive** circuit.
**Power factor = cos φ = R/Z = 40/71.3 = 0.561 leading.**
**P_av = V_rms I_rms cos φ = (200)(2.81)(0.561) = 315 W.** Cross-check I²R = (7.90)(40) = **316 W ✓** — all of it in the resistor; L and C dissipate exactly nothing.
**Resonance**: f₀ = 1/(2π√(LC)) = 1/(2π√(0.15 × 30×10⁻⁶)) = 1/(2π × 2.121×10⁻³) = **75.0 Hz**. There X_L = X_C = 70.7 Ω and cancel, so **Z = R = 40 Ω** (a minimum), **I = 200/40 = 5.00 A** (a maximum), **φ = 0**, **power factor = 1**, and P = V²/R = **1000 W**. Since the supply is at 50 Hz, below f₀, the circuit is capacitive — exactly what the sign of X said.

**33.** *The three powers, the power triangle, and power-factor correction.* **[Week 7]**
(a) **S = V_rms I_rms = (240)(30) = 7200 VA.**
**P = VI cos φ = (7200)(0.60) = 4320 W.**
cos φ = 0.60 ⇒ sin φ = 0.80, so **Q = VI sin φ = (7200)(0.80) = 5760 var**, and it is **lagging** (inductive), so Q is positive. φ = cos⁻¹0.60 = **53.1°**.
Triangle check: P² + Q² = 4320² + 5760² = 18 662 400 + 33 177 600 = 51 840 000 = 7200² = S² ✓. Note they do **not** add arithmetically: 4320 + 5760 = 10 080, not 7200.
(b) **Z = V/I = 240/30 = 8.00 Ω**; **R = Z cos φ = (8.00)(0.60) = 4.80 Ω**; **X = Z sin φ = (8.00)(0.80) = 6.40 Ω** (inductive).
Check: I²R = (900)(4.80) = **4320 W ✓**; I²X = (900)(6.40) = **5760 var ✓**; I²Z = (900)(8.00) = 7200 VA ✓.
**Why P = V²/R fails here.** The 240 V stands across the **whole** load, not across R. Using it would give 240²/4.80 = **12 000 W**, nearly three times the true figure. The resistor's own drop is only V_R = IR = (30)(4.80) = 144 V, and 144²/4.80 = 4320 W ✓. **P = I²R is always safe**; V²/R is legal only when the whole supply voltage sits across R — a purely resistive circuit, or one at resonance.
(c) For **unity** power factor the capacitor must supply the whole of the load's reactive power: **Q_C = 5760 var**.
**C = Q_C/(ωV²) = 5760/[(314.2)(240²)] = 5760/[(314.2)(57 600)] = 5760/1.810×10⁷ = 3.18×10⁻⁴ F = 318 μF**, connected **in parallel** with the load.
(Check: I_C = ωCV = (314.2)(3.18×10⁻⁴)(240) = 24.0 A, and Q_C = (240)(24.0) = 5760 var ✓.)
**New line current** = P/V = 4320/240 = **18.0 A**, down from 30.0 A.
**Cable loss**: it goes as I², so it falls by the factor (18.0/30.0)² = 0.36 — a **64 % reduction**. The supply transformer now needs 4.32 kVA of capacity instead of 7.20 kVA.
**Not one watt of the workshop's real consumption has changed**: P is still 4320 W, and the meter bill (kWh) is untouched. What has been removed is the reactive current the cable had to carry.
(d) With v = V₀ sin ωt and i = I₀ sin(ωt − φ), the identity sin A sin B = ½[cos(A−B) − cos(A+B)] gives
**p(t) = (V₀I₀/2)[cos φ − cos(2ωt − φ)] = S[cos φ − cos(2ωt − φ)] = 7200[0.60 − cos(628.3 t − 53.1°)] W.**
**Average** = S cos φ = (7200)(0.60) = **4320 W** — the constant term is the whole of the average power, because the ripple averages to zero.
**Minimum** = S(cos φ − 1) = (7200)(0.60 − 1) = **−2880 W** — negative, meaning energy flows **back to the source** for part of every cycle. (Maximum = S(cos φ + 1) = +11 520 W.)
**Ripple frequency = 2f = 100 Hz**, not 50 Hz: v and i reverse sign together, so their product completes two cycles per supply cycle.

**34.** *Moment, torque and energy of a coil; then the fields of a solenoid and a wire.* **[Week 8]**
(a) **A = (0.100 m)(0.060 m) = 6.00×10⁻³ m².** (60 cm² would give an answer 10⁴ times too big.)
**μ = NIA = (120)(1.5)(6.00×10⁻³) = 1.08 A·m²** (= 1.08 J/T — the same unit). It is a **vector**, directed along the **normal to the plane of the coil**, in the sense given by the **right-hand rule**: curl the fingers along the current and the thumb points along **μ**. The face out of which **μ** emerges is the coil's north pole.
(b) **μB = (1.08)(0.35) = 0.378 J** — the energy scale of the whole problem.
**Maximum torque τ_max = μB = 0.378 N·m**, at **θ = 90°**, i.e. with the **plane of the coil containing B** (the normal perpendicular to the field). This is the orientation students most often mislabel "θ = 0".
At **θ = 40° from the normal**: **τ = μB sin 40° = (0.378)(0.643) = 0.243 N·m**, acting to turn **μ** towards **B**; and **U = −μB cos 40° = −(0.378)(0.766) = −0.290 J** — negative, because the coil is closer to aligned than to anti-aligned. Note **sin in the torque, cos in the energy**; using cos 40° in the torque gives 0.290 N·m, the commonest wrong answer.
**Work to flip** from θ = 0° to θ = 180°: W = U(180°) − U(0°) = (+0.378) − (−0.378) = **0.756 J = 2μB**.
(c) The net **force is zero**. The forces on opposite sides of the loop are equal and opposite because **B is uniform**, so the coil experiences a pure couple: it **turns but does not translate**. A net force requires a **non-uniform** field — which is why a magnet is pulled towards a nail (it magnetises it and then sits in the nail's non-uniform field) while a compass needle merely swings.
(d) **Solenoid**: n = N/L = 600/0.30 = **2000 turns per metre** (the length in metres, and it is n that enters, not N).
**B = μ₀nI = (4π×10⁻⁷)(2000)(2.0) = (1.257×10⁻⁶)(4000) = 5.03×10⁻³ T**, uniform and axial inside, essentially zero outside, and **independent of the solenoid's radius**.
With the core: **B = μᵣμ₀nI = (500)(5.03×10⁻³) = 2.51 T.** The geometry is unchanged; the material multiplies the field by 500.
**Straight wire**: B = μ₀I/2πr = (2×10⁻⁷)(20)/(0.050) = (4.0×10⁻⁶)/(0.050) = **8.0×10⁻⁵ T**, in **closed concentric circles** in the plane perpendicular to the wire, sense by the right-hand grip rule (thumb along the current, fingers curl the way **B** points). It is **tangential**, never radial and never along the wire.
**Why every line is closed**: there are **no magnetic monopoles**. Cutting a bar magnet in half gives two complete magnets, each with a full N and a full S, all the way down to a single atom. Since a line can neither begin nor end on an isolated pole, it must close on itself — running N → S **outside** the magnet and S → N **inside** it. Equivalently, the flux out of any closed surface is zero, Φ_B = 0. This is the one structural difference from electrostatics, where a single isolated charge is perfectly possible.

**35.** *The Lorentz force, circular and helical motion, and electromagnetic waves.* **[Week 9]**
(a) **F = qE + q(v × B) = q(E + v × B)** — the total electromagnetic force on a point charge, with q **signed** and the two terms added as **vectors**. With **E** = 0 and v ⊥ B:
**F = qvB sin 90° = (1.602×10⁻¹⁹)(4.0×10⁶)(0.50) = 3.2×10⁻¹³ N**, perpendicular to both **v** and **B**.
Setting it equal to the centripetal force, qvB = mv²/r:
**r = mv/(qB) = (1.67×10⁻²⁷)(4.0×10⁶)/[(1.602×10⁻¹⁹)(0.50)] = (6.68×10⁻²¹)/(8.01×10⁻²⁰) = 0.0834 m = 8.34 cm.**
**T = 2πr/v = 2πm/(qB) = 2π(1.67×10⁻²⁷)/(8.01×10⁻²⁰) = 1.31×10⁻⁷ s**, so **f_c = 1/T = 7.6×10⁶ Hz = 7.6 MHz.**
**v has cancelled** out of T: speed the proton up and the radius grows in exact proportion (r = mv/qB), so the lap time is unchanged. That is the principle of the cyclotron.
(b) **KE = ½mv² = ½(1.67×10⁻²⁷)(4.0×10⁶)² = ½(1.67×10⁻²⁷)(1.6×10¹³) = 1.34×10⁻¹⁴ J ≈ 83 keV.**
After ten laps — or a million — it is **exactly the same, 1.34×10⁻¹⁴ J**. The magnetic force is **always perpendicular to v**, so the power it delivers is P = **F**·**v** = 0: it does **no work**, changes the **direction** of **v** and never its magnitude. A magnetic field bends; it cannot speed a particle up and cannot start one moving from rest.
An **electric** field would be entirely different: **F** = q**E** acts whether or not the charge moves, has a component along **v**, and therefore **does work** — it would change the speed and the kinetic energy.
(c) At 60° to **B**: **v_⊥ = v sin 60° = (4.0×10⁶)(0.866) = 3.46×10⁶ m/s**; **v_∥ = v cos 60° = 2.0×10⁶ m/s**.
The magnetic force acts only on **v_⊥**, so the radius uses v_⊥ alone:
**r = mv_⊥/(qB) = (1.67×10⁻²⁷)(3.46×10⁶)/(8.01×10⁻²⁰) = 0.0722 m = 7.2 cm.**
The **period is unchanged at 1.31×10⁻⁷ s** — it never contained v at all.
**Pitch** (advance per turn) = v_∥T = (2.0×10⁶)(1.31×10⁻⁷) = **0.262 m**.
The path is a **helix** of radius 7.2 cm and pitch 26 cm, winding **along** the field lines: v_∥ is untouched because **F** ⊥ **B**.
(d) **λ = c/f = (3.00×10⁸)/(9.0×10⁷) = 3.33 m.**
**Photon energy E = hf = (6.63×10⁻³⁴)(9.0×10⁷) = 5.97×10⁻²⁶ J** (= 3.7×10⁻⁷ eV).
For green light, f = c/λ = (3.00×10⁸)/(5.00×10⁻⁷) = 6.00×10¹⁴ Hz and **E = hf = 3.98×10⁻¹⁹ J ≈ 2.5 eV** — about **6.7×10⁶ times** the FM photon. That is why visible light can eject electrons from a metal and FM radio cannot: photon energy depends on **frequency**, not on how bright or how powerful the source is.
**Orientation**: the wave is **transverse** — **E** ⊥ **B**, and both are perpendicular to the direction of propagation, which is the direction of **E** × **B**. There is no longitudinal component.
**Spectrum in order of increasing frequency (and decreasing wavelength)**: **radio → microwave → infrared → visible → ultraviolet → X-rays → gamma rays.** All of them travel at c in vacuum, and c = fλ throughout.

**36.** *From F = BIL on each side to τ = μB sin θ on the loop — the Week 9 force building the Week 8 torque.* **[Weeks 8–9]**
(a) Take the initial orientation, in which **the plane of the coil contains B** and the rotation axis is parallel to the sides of length a.
**The two sides of length a = 0.120 m** run **perpendicular** to **B**, so each carries the full force, summed over all 25 turns:
**F_a = NBIa = (25)(0.20)(4.0)(0.120) = 2.40 N** each.
By RHR-1 with the conventional current in the role of **v**, these two forces are **equal in magnitude and opposite in direction** (the current runs opposite ways in the two sides), one out of the plane of the coil and one into it.
**The two sides of length b = 0.080 m** lie **along B** in this orientation, so θ = 0 and **F_b = NBIb sin 0° = 0**. (In a general orientation they carry equal and opposite forces directed **along the rotation axis**, which cancel and exert no torque.)
**Net force = 2.40 − 2.40 + 0 + 0 = 0** — the coil feels a **pure couple**.
(b) The two 2.40 N forces are separated by the full width **b = 0.080 m**, so
**τ = F_a × b = (2.40)(0.080) = 0.192 N·m.**
Independently, **μ = NIA = (25)(4.0)(0.120 × 0.080) = (25)(4.0)(9.6×10⁻³) = 0.96 A·m²**, and with the plane containing **B** the normal is at **θ = 90°** to **B**:
**τ = μB sin 90° = (0.96)(0.20)(1) = 0.192 N·m ✓** — identical. The two routes are the same statement: NBIa × b = NI(ab)B = μB.
(c) At **θ = 30° from the normal**:
**τ = μB sin 30° = (0.192)(0.500) = 0.096 N·m**, turning **μ** towards **B**.
**U = −μB cos 30° = −(0.192)(0.866) = −0.166 J.**
Work by an external agent from the initial orientation (θ = 90°, U = 0) to full alignment (θ = 0°, U = −μB = −0.192 J):
**W_ext = U(0°) − U(90°) = −0.192 − 0 = −0.192 J.**
The sign is **negative**: the agent does not supply energy, the **field does 0.192 J of work** on the coil. The agent must in fact **restrain** it, or the coil would arrive at θ = 0° with 0.192 J of kinetic energy and swing straight past. Turning the other way, from θ = 0° to θ = 180°, would cost the agent **+2μB = 0.384 J**.
(d) **Turns but does not translate**: in a **uniform** field the forces on opposite sides are equal and opposite, so ΣF = 0 while their moments about the axis add — a couple, which produces rotation about the centre of mass and no motion of it.
**Commutator**: as the coil swings past alignment (θ = 0) the torque τ = μB sin θ would reverse sign and pull it back, so the coil would merely oscillate. The commutator **reverses the current every half turn**, which reverses **μ** and so keeps the torque in one sense — the coil rotates continuously. Reversing **both** I and **B** would leave the force unchanged, which is also why an a.c. motor works.
**Non-uniform field**: the forces on the two sides would no longer be equal, ΣF ≠ 0, and the coil would experience a **net force** as well as a torque — it would be **pulled towards the stronger-field region**. That, and not the torque, is what "magnetic attraction" means.

| **Week 8** | Magnetic Fields and Magnetic Dipoles | 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 34, 36 | **μ = NIA** — do not drop N, and convert cm² to m² (1 cm² = 10⁻⁴ m²); it is a **vector along the normal**, by the right-hand rule. **θ in τ = μB sin θ is measured from the normal**, so "plane contains B" ⇒ θ = 90° ⇒ **maximum** torque. **sin in the torque, cos in the energy**: **U = −μB cos θ**, minimum (**stable**) at 0°, maximum (**unstable**) at 180°, and a full flip costs **2μB**. Use **ΔU, never τ × angle**. Uniform field ⇒ **torque only, zero net force**; a net force needs a **non-uniform** field. Fields: **B = μ₀I/2πr** (tangential circles, **not** radial), **B = μ₀nI** with **n = N/L per metre** and no radius dependence, **B = μ₀NI/2R** at a coil's centre. And the structural point: **no monopoles**, so every field line closes — N → S outside, S → N inside. |
| **Week 9** | The Lorentz Force, and Electromagnetic Waves | 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30, 35, 36 | **F = q(E + v × B)** is a **vector sum** — adding the two magnitudes is a special case, not a rule. **F = qvB sin θ with θ between v and B**: maximum at v ⊥ B, **zero** when v ∥ B or v = 0 (sin, never cos). **The magnetic force does no work** — F ⊥ v, so speed and kinetic energy are constant; only qE does work. **r = mv/qB depends on speed, T = 2πm/qB does not**, and f_c = qB/2πm; a component of v along **B** survives untouched and makes the path a **helix** of pitch v_∥T. **Velocity selector v = E/B**, containing neither q nor m. **F = BIL sin θ with θ between the current and B**, force ⊥ to the plane of the wire and **B**. In SI there is **no 1/c**. For EM waves: transverse, **E ⊥ B ⊥ propagation**, **c = fλ**, **E = hf** (frequency, not brightness), and the spectrum radio → microwave → infrared → visible → ultraviolet → X-ray → gamma. |
