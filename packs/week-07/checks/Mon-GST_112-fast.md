# Monday — GST_112 fast-hour check

*Where Nigerian human rights sit in law, and the five common abuses with their figures and statute.*
*5 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 5.*

**1.** Security services detain a journalist for reporting on political corruption, and the NBC is used to clamp down on radio and television stations. Which of the five common human rights abuses is this?
A. Extra-judicial killing
B. Freedom of expression
C. Domestic violence
D. Child marriage

**2.** A State fixes 18 as the minimum age of marriage for boys and 15 for girls, and enforces both ages strictly. Under international human rights law, this State is:
A. In violation, because the minimum age must be the same for boys and girls
B. Compliant, because a minimum age has been established and is enforced
C. Compliant, as long as no public official takes part in any child marriage
D. In violation only if it also fails to act with due diligence to prevent child marriage

**3.** Which of the following pairs a human rights figure or statute with its correct detail?
A. 43% of girls in Nigeria are married before the age of 15
B. The Violence Against Persons (Prohibition) Act was signed into law in 2009
C. The National Human Rights Commission received 1,701,519 cases in 2021, up from 1,287,280 in 2020
D. About 367,000 complaints were received by the National Human Rights Commission in 2020

**4.** Which of the following statements about the legal framework for human rights in Nigeria is correct?
A. The 1979 Constitution is where fundamental human rights are currently contained
B. The 2009 Rules are what make the rights enforceable in Nigerian courts
C. Article 1 of the UN Declaration of Human Rights forms part of Chapter IV of the Constitution
D. Human rights are contained in Chapter IV of the 1999 Constitution, Sections 33 to 46

**5.** What is a human rights abuse?
A. Anything that harms someone's human rights, including harm to people, communities and the environment
B. Any breach of an international treaty that a country has signed
C. An unlawful act committed only by the police or the military
D. A restriction on the media imposed by the National Broadcasting Commission

---

## Answers

**1. B** — *Identifying a human rights abuse from a scenario.* Detaining journalists for sensitive reporting and using regulatory oversight through the NBC to restrict broadcasters are the marks of the freedom of expression abuse, meaning the restriction of speech and press. Extra-judicial killing involves security forces using lethal or excessive force, as highlighted by #EndSARS. Domestic violence and child marriage are abuses within households and families, not against the press.

**2. A** — *Child marriage and the State's obligations.* A State violates international human rights law if it fails to establish a minimum age of marriage, enforce it, provide for and enforce the same minimum age for boys and girls, act with due diligence to prevent child marriage, or if it involves public officials in it. Setting different ages for boys and girls breaches the third condition on its own. Establishing and enforcing an age does not cure the inequality, keeping officials out of it addresses a separate condition, and the due diligence condition is an additional ground rather than a requirement for the violation.

**3. C** — *Figures and statute attached to the common abuses.* The NHRC received 1,701,519 cases of rights violations in 2021, higher than the 1,287,280 complaints of 2020, so the number rose. The 43% figure is girls married before their 18th birthday; 16% is the figure for before 15. The VAPP Act was signed into law in 2015; 2009 is the year of the Rules with shortcomings and inhibitions. The 367,000 figure is women and gender-related issues in 2021, not an NHRC complaint count.

**4. D** — *Where human rights sit in Nigerian law.* Human rights are contained in Chapter IV of the 1999 Constitution, Sections 33 to 46. The 1979 Constitution introduced the Fundamental Objectives and Directive Principles of State Policy and Fundamental Human Rights, but the rights now sit in the 1999 Constitution. It is the African Charter on Human and People's Rights that guarantees enforceability in Nigerian courts; the 2009 Rules are noted for their shortcomings. Article 1 of the UN Declaration supplies the definition of human rights, not a section of the Nigerian Constitution.

**5. A** — *Definition of a human rights abuse.* A human rights abuse is anything that harms someone's human rights, and the harm can be to people, communities or the environment. It is not defined by treaties: human rights are not validated by the number of international treaties a country has signed. Security forces are implicated in extra-judicial killings but abuses can also come from non-state actors, and NBC restrictions are only one example of the freedom of expression abuse.
