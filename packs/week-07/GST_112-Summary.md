# GST_112 — Week 7 Summary

*Nigerian Peoples and Culture · Week 7 (19–25 Oct 2026) · Topic: Human rights, the legal framework that guarantees them in Nigeria, and the common human rights abuses*

## The 8 most examinable things this week

1. **Definition.** Human rights are **rights inherent to all human beings, regardless of race, sex, nationality, ethnicity, language, religion, or any other status.** A **human rights abuse is anything that harms someone's human rights** — and the lesson is explicit that abuses include **harm to people, communities, and the environment**.
2. **Article 1 of the UN Declaration of Human Rights**, quoted verbatim: *"All human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights. They are **endowed with reason and conscience** and should act towards one another in the spirit of brotherhood."*
3. **Where the rights sit in Nigerian law: Chapter IV of the 1999 Constitution, Sections 33 to 46.** The **African Charter on Human and People's Rights** also guarantees that these rights are **enforceable in Nigerian courts of law**.
4. **The five rights the lesson lists**: **freedom of religion, freedom of speech, right to education, right to freedom of movement, right to life.** (Generally, human rights include the **right to life and liberty, freedom from slavery and torture, freedom of opinion and expression, the right to work and education**.)
5. **The constitutional history**: the **1979 Constitution** introduced **Fundamental Objectives and Directive Principles of State Policy** *and* **Fundamental Human Rights** — but there remain **shortcomings and inhibitions in the 2009 Rules**.
6. **The NHRC figures.** The **National Human Rights Commission** received **1,701,519 cases of rights violations in 2021**, higher than the **1,287,280 complaints received in 2020**.
7. **The five common abuses named for Nigeria, in the lesson's own order: extra-judicial killing, rape, freedom of expression, child marriage, domestic violence.**
8. **The numbers attached to them**: **#EndSARS, October 2020** (police brutality); **about 367,000 women and gender-related issues in Nigeria in 2021**; **43% of girls married before their 18th birthday and 16% before the age of 15** (Aliogo, 2023); the **Violence Against Persons (Prohibition) Act, signed into law in 2015**.

## The five abuses, with the specific detail attached to each

| Abuse | Who / what is implicated | The fact, figure or statute to quote |
|---|---|---|
| **Extra-judicial killing** | **Nigerian security forces, particularly the police and military** — extrajudicial killings, **torture**, and **excessive use of force** | The **#EndSARS protests of October 2020** brought **international attention to police brutality**. The categories given: **law enforcement officials, non-state actors, death sentences, and deaths in custody** due to use of force or **failure to protect the right to life** |
| **Rape** | Occurs **worldwide, including in Nigeria**; government and **civil organisations** are working on it | Challenges: **social stigma, underreporting of cases, inadequate legal frameworks, lack of access to justice, limited support services for survivors.** **About 367,000 women and gender-related issues in Nigeria in 2021** |
| **Freedom of expression** | The **government**, acting through **regulatory oversight**; **security services** detained and harassed journalists | The **National Broadcasting Commission (NBC)** monitors and regulates broadcast media, and government **controlled much of the electronic media** through it, **clamping down on television and radio stations**. Critics faced **threats, intimidation, arrest, detention and sometimes violence**; sensitive topics named are **political corruption and security** |
| **Child marriage** | A **discriminatory practice affecting mostly girls**, **prohibited under international law** | **43% of girls in Nigeria are married before 18; 16% before 15** (Aliogo, 2023) |
| **Domestic violence** | Occurs in Nigeria and other countries | The **Violence Against Persons (Prohibition) Act, 2015** criminalises various forms of abuse and provides mechanisms for **reporting, prosecution, and support services for survivors**. Remaining challenges: **underreporting due to social stigma, inadequate enforcement of laws, limited awareness and education, need for more accessible support services** |

## Child marriage — the five-part test to memorise

A State **violates international human rights law** if it fails to:

1. **establish a minimum age of marriage**;
2. **enforce it**;
3. **provide for and enforce the same minimum age of marriage for boys and girls**;
4. **act with due diligence to prevent** child marriage;
5. or if it **involves public officials in it**.

Point 3 is the one exam questions turn on: a State that sets an age but sets a **different** age for boys and girls is still in violation.

## Democracy and the rule of law (the week's framing, from the Summary PDF)

- **Democracy** — "government of the people, by the people, and for the people" — is treated as **the most legitimate form of governance in modern times**, emphasising **free and egalitarian societies built around strong institutions rather than individuals or groups**, and empowering the people **through elections**.
- **The rule of law** means a nation is **governed by laws rather than by arbitrary decisions of government officials**. Its **key principles: supremacy of law, equality before the law, and the protection of individual rights through the courts.**
- **Nigeria is classified as a hybrid state on the Democracy Index** — democracy is "a work in progress" because it does not meet the criteria of a **full democracy**.

## The conclusion (a likely essay prompt)

A nation's **attitudes towards human rights are indicators of the values its political and other leaders have espoused regarding human life**. Human rights are **not validated by the number of international treaties a country has signed** — the **international documents are only standards** for the additional safeguards individuals demand from the state. Consequently **every government agency has to enforce the norms established by the Constitution**, and **penalties should be imposed regardless of socioeconomic class**.

## Commonly confused

- **Chapter IV vs Chapter II.** **Fundamental Human Rights = Chapter IV, Sections 33–46** of the **1999** Constitution (justiciable). **Fundamental Objectives and Directive Principles of State Policy** are a separate thing, introduced with them by the **1979** Constitution.
- **The 1999 Constitution vs the 1979 Constitution vs the 2009 Rules.** **1999** = where the rights are now contained. **1979** = the constitution that *introduced* the Fundamental Objectives and Directive Principles and the Fundamental Human Rights. **2009 Rules** = the enforcement rules the lesson says still have **shortcomings and inhibitions**.
- **The UN Declaration vs the African Charter.** The **UDHR (Article 1)** supplies the **definition and the quotation**. The **African Charter on Human and People's Rights** is the instrument the lesson credits with making rights **enforceable in Nigerian courts**.
- **1,701,519 vs 1,287,280 vs 367,000.** **1,701,519 = NHRC cases in 2021**; **1,287,280 = NHRC complaints in 2020** (so the figure **rose**); **367,000 = women and gender-related issues in 2021**. Do not swap the direction of the NHRC change.
- **43% vs 16%.** **43% before the 18th birthday**, **16% before age 15** — the larger figure goes with the higher age.
- **NHRC vs NBC.** **National Human Rights Commission** receives complaints of violations; **National Broadcasting Commission** monitors and regulates broadcast media and is the vehicle of the **press-freedom** abuse.
- **"Freedom of expression" as a right vs as an abuse.** The lesson lists **"freedom of expression"** among the **abuses** — meaning its **restriction**. If asked to list the five abuses, give it as the lesson gives it, but understand it as **restriction of freedom of speech and press**.
- **VAPP Act 2015 vs the 2009 Rules.** **2015 = Violence Against Persons (Prohibition) Act**, on **domestic violence and other abuse**. **2009 = the Rules** on rights enforcement. Different years, different subjects.
