## Section B — Short answer (3)

**13.** Name the three benefits of active listening that the deck gives as separate headings, and state in one sentence each what the deck says about them.

**14.** List the six techniques the deck gives for becoming a more active listener, and explain the reason it attaches to any two of them.

## Answers

**Section A**

1. **c** — "The Cornell method involves dividing your paper into sections for notes, cues, and a summary" — the diagram labels them Cue Column, Note-Taking Area, Summaries.

2. **a** — Verbatim transcription causes information overload; capture main ideas, key concepts and supporting evidence instead.

3. **b** — Both examples appear on the "Use Abbreviations and Symbols" slide — a system to write faster without sacrificing clarity.

4. **d** — Highlighting or underlining uses different colours or underlines as a visual cue to ease review.

5. **a** — Enhanced Understanding, Conflict Resolution and Problem Solving each get their own slide under Importance of Active Listening.

6. **b** — These are the deck's verbatim examples of verbal and non-verbal affirmations, which encourage the speaker to keep sharing.

7. **c** — The deck stresses these are personal notes, not to be turned in; formal writing may not tolerate them.

8. **d** — "Interruptions can hinder the flow of communication and make the speaker feel unheard" — hence Avoid Interrupting.

9. **a** — That is the deck's definition of mind mapping; charting and outlining are not in this deck at all.

10. **b** — Paraphrasing or summarising periodically shows comprehension and "provides an opportunity for clarification."

11. **c** — Deductive reasoning draws specific conclusions from general premises; inductive works the other way, from specific observations to broader generalizations.

12. **d** — The list is pen/pencil, book/jotter/sticky notes, highlighters, paper clips, etc. — no device is mentioned.

**Section B**

**13.** Enhanced Understanding: active listening helps the listener better understand the speaker's message, grasping not only the words spoken but the underlying emotions, intentions and context, which is essential for meaningful and productive communication. Conflict Resolution: it is a key skill in resolving disputes because it lets individuals acknowledge each other's perspectives, a crucial step in finding common ground. Problem Solving: by fully comprehending the details of a problem or issue, the listener is better equipped to provide informed solutions or suggestions.

**14.** Maintain eye contact; avoid interrupting; minimise distractions; ask open-ended questions; use verbal and non-verbal affirmations; paraphrase and summarise. Maintain eye contact: it conveys attention and interest and is a non-verbal cue signalling active engagement in the conversation. Avoid interrupting: letting the speaker finish matters because interruptions hinder the flow of communication and make the speaker feel unheard. (Alternatively — paraphrase and summarise: it demonstrates you are not only listening but trying to comprehend the message accurately, and it provides an opportunity for clarification.)

**15.** The Cornell method involves dividing your paper into sections for notes, cues and a summary — a cue column, a note-taking area and a summaries strip — and it encourages you to summarise the main ideas shortly after the lecture. Mind mapping means creating visual representations of the content, connecting main ideas to subtopics and supporting details in a mind map format. The deck concludes that the combination of active listening and note-taking skills leads to more efficient information processing, better understanding and improved retention, benefiting both personal and professional contexts, and that the combined skills aid in collecting, organising and analysing information, which can lead to better informed decision-making.
