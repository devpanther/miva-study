# GST_112 — Week 12

*Nigerian Peoples and Culture · Week 12 (23 – 29 Nov 2026)*

## Most likely to be examined

1. **Crime vs deviance** — social vices split into **two**: **crime violates social laws**; **deviant behaviour violates social norms and rules**. The deck's own mid-lesson question tests exactly this.
2. **Four theories of deviance**: **Structural Functionalism**, **Social Strain Typology**, **Conflict Theory**, **Labelling Theory**. **Three theories of crime**: **Rational Choice Theory**, **Strain Theory**, **Social Learning Theory**. Learn which name goes with which one-line claim.
3. **Origin of campus cultism**: **early 1950s**, **Prof. Wole Soyinka** and friends founded the **Pirates Confraternity** as a **social group** at the **University of Ibadan** — founding beliefs **patriotic and philanthropic**, *not* intended as a secret cult.
4. **Forty-five (45) secret cults** operate in Nigerian tertiary institutions today, each with an **elaborate hierarchy, insignia and distinct attire**.
5. **Penalties for cultism**: allowing cult meetings in your residence/structure is a **felony — 3-year sentence**; **membership of a cult group, or soliciting members for an unlawful society — 21 years**.
6. **Kidnapping figures**: **838** reported cases in **2018**, risen to **4,600** in **2022** — cases had **quadrupled**.
7. **Armed robbery figures (prison records)**: **11,858** in **2013**, **reduced to 10,249** in **2014**.
8. **Causes of kidnapping** named by the deck: **unemployment** (the major cause — frustrated graduates job-hunting **three or more years**) and the **political factor** (politicians hiring kidnappers against opponents).

## The deck's own lists — learn them as groups

**Social vices (Asmau)**: prostitution, indecent dressing, robbery, cultism, pocket-picking, drug addiction, exam fraud, hooliganism, thuggery, gambling, smoking, premarital sexual activity, rape. *Definition:* actions that stray from accepted moral norms.

**Theories of deviance**
| Theory | Deck's claim |
|---|---|
| **Structural Functionalism** | Deviance plays a **constructive role** — it brings different parts of the population together to agree what is acceptable and what is not |
| **Social Strain Typology** | Deviance based on **two criteria**: (1) a person's motivations/adherence to cultural goals, (2) a person's belief in how to attain her goals |
| **Conflict Theory** | Deviance is a consequence of **material inequality** |
| **Labelling Theory** | Deviance is often a consequence of having a **deviant-like label applied** to a person |

**Theories of crime**
| Theory | Deck's claim |
|---|---|
| **Rational Choice Theory** | Criminals **weigh cost/risk against benefits** when deciding whether to commit crime |
| **Strain Theory** | **Social structures within society** may pressure citizens to commit crime |
| **Social Learning Theory** | Criminal behaviour is acquired by **observing and imitating others** |

**Pirates Confraternity founders**: Wole Soyinka, **Ralph Opara, Ikhehare Aig-Imokhuede, Sylvanus Egbuchie, Nathaniel Oyelola, Pius Oleghe, Olumuyiwa Awe**. **Three goals**: resist foreign norms **nonviolently but intellectually**; **resurrect the period of chivalry**; find a lasting solution to **tribalism and ethnicity**. Over time such organizations grew to deadly proportions.

**Activities of cultists**: meeting in odd places (**cemeteries, valleys, abandoned buildings**); dangerous weapons (**knives, daggers, guns, axe, arrows**) against erring members or non-members; **hand warfare, burglary, killing, thuggery, assault, torture**; **hard drugs, drinking of human blood**; **acid bathe**; **extravagant spending on non-profit ventures**.

**Reasons people join cults**: inherent instincts to **inflict pain, punishment and death**; **hostile and uncaring parenting**; **failing or expelled students** seeking to set the college on rampage; **rivalry/competition seeking girls**; **protection from rival cult boys**; **protection from victimization by lecturers**; **peer influence**; **militarization of the Nigerian polity**; **erosion of educational standard**; **societal decadence**.

**Kidnapping** = unlawful **abduction and detention** of individuals, usually for **financial gain** or motives such as **ransom or revenge**; inflicts trauma, creates fear, discourages investment and economic development. **Effects (10)**: Humanitarian Crisis · Loss of Lives · Economic Impact · Security Concerns · Negative Image · Ransom Culture · Deterioration of Social Trust · Escalation of Violence · Impact on Education · Human Trafficking. It undermines personal security, disrupts businesses and tourism, and pressures law enforcement.

**Armed robbery** = use of **weapons to steal** from individuals, homes, businesses or vehicles; endangers lives and property, often involves violence and brutality; creates fear, **reduces trust in law enforcement**, hampers economic growth.
**Causes (6)**: Socio-economic Factor · Corruption · Weak Law Enforcement · Drug Abuse · **Proliferation of Firearms** · Societal Factor.
**Effects (5)**: Loss of Lives and Injuries · Psychological Impact · Economic Consequences · Disruption of Daily Life · Erosion of Trust.

**Conclusion / national picture**: the **six geo-political zones** face different versions of kidnapping — **Boko Haram insurgency and herdsmen attacks in the North East and North West**, **militants in the Niger Delta (South-South) and South East**, and criminal opportunists kidnapping for ransom. **Corruption is linked to kidnapping**, with politicians using **youths as political thugs**. Cult groups and armed banditry are linked because access to **small arms and light weapons** motivates interaction between cult groups and armed robbers raising funds to sustain their activities.

**Summary PDF adds**: the vices listed include **bribery, corruption, cultism, examination malpractice, kidnapping, armed robbery**; government has set up agencies to combat them, e.g. the **Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC)** to fight corruption; a comprehensive approach needs **government–civil society collaboration**. ## Commonly confused

- **Crime vs deviance** — crime = **laws**, deviance = **norms and rules**. Deviance is the broader term; crime carries legal consequences (fines, imprisonment). "Interchangeable with no difference" is the deck's own wrong option.
- **The two 21/3 penalties** — **3 years** for *hosting* a cult meeting in a building you own; **21 years** for *being* a member or soliciting members. Don't swap the smaller number onto membership.
- **Which theory list** — Structural Functionalism, Social Strain Typology, Conflict and Labelling are **deviance**; Rational Choice, Strain and Social Learning are **crime**. Note **Strain Theory** (crime) and **Social Strain Typology** (deviance) are two different entries.
- **Kidnapping numbers rise, armed robbery numbers fall** — 838 (2018) → 4,600 (2022) is the *increase*; 11,858 (2013) → 10,249 (2014) is the *reduction*. Mixing the years is the easy trap.
- **Soyinka's role** — he founded the Pirates Confraternity as a **social group** with patriotic and philanthropic beliefs; the deck does **not** say he founded a secret cult.
- **Causes vs effects of armed robbery** — *Corruption* and *Proliferation of Firearms* are **causes**; *Erosion of Trust* and *Psychological Impact* are **effects**.
- **Kidnapping has only two causes named** (unemployment, political factor) but **ten effects**; armed robbery has **six causes** and **five effects**.
