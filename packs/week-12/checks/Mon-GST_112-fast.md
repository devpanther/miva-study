# Monday — GST_112 fast-hour check

*Social vices: crime versus deviance, the theory lists, campus cultism and the kidnapping figures.*
*5 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 5.*

**1.** A boy is repeatedly called a thief by his teachers and neighbours although he has stolen nothing; over time he begins to steal. Which theory of deviance best explains this?
A. Conflict Theory
B. Structural Functionalism
C. Labelling Theory
D. Social Strain Typology

**2.** A man decides to rob a shop only after noting that it has no guard and judging that the likely gain outweighs the risk of arrest. Which theory of crime does this illustrate?
A. Rational Choice Theory
B. Strain Theory
C. Social Learning Theory
D. Labelling Theory

**3.** An action that violates social laws, as opposed to one that merely violates social norms and rules, is called:
A. A social vice
B. A crime
C. Deviance
D. A felony

**4.** Which of the following was NOT one of the original goals of the Pirates Confraternity founded at the University of Ibadan in the early 1950s?
A. To resist foreign norms nonviolently but intellectually
B. To resurrect the period of chivalry
C. To find a lasting solution to tribalism and ethnicity
D. To protect members from boys of rival groups

**5.** Which of the following statements about the kidnapping figures in Nigeria is correct?
A. Reported kidnap cases rose from 838 in 2018 to 4,600 in 2022, roughly quadrupling
B. Reported kidnap cases fell from 11,858 in 2013 to 10,249 in 2014
C. Reported kidnap cases rose from 838 in 2018 to 1,676 in 2022, doubling
D. There were 4,600 kidnap cases in 2018, falling to 838 by 2022

---

## Answers

**1. C** — *Theories of deviance.* Labelling Theory holds that deviant behaviour is often a consequence of having a deviant-like label applied to a person, which is exactly what happens to the boy. Conflict Theory would explain his deviance by material inequality; Structural Functionalism says deviance plays a constructive role by helping society agree what is acceptable; Social Strain Typology classifies deviance by a person's adherence to cultural goals and belief in how to attain them.

**2. A** — *Theories of crime.* Rational Choice Theory argues that criminals weigh cost or risk against benefits when deciding whether to commit a crime, which is what the man does. Strain Theory would point to social structures pressuring him into crime; Social Learning Theory would require him to have observed and imitated others; Labelling Theory is a theory of deviance about labels applied to a person, not a weighing of risks.

**3. B** — *Crime versus deviance.* Social vices are classified into two: committing a crime violates social laws, while deviant behaviour violates social norms and rules. Social vice is the wider category covering both. Deviance is the norm-breaking half, not the law-breaking half. Felony is the label given to a particular offence (allowing cult meetings in your residence), not the general term for law-breaking.

**4. D** — *Origins of campus cultism.* The Pirates Confraternity, founded by Wole Soyinka and his friends as a social group with patriotic and philanthropic beliefs, had three major goals: to resist foreign norms nonviolently but intellectually, to resurrect the period of chivalry, and to find a lasting solution to tribalism and ethnicity. Protection from boys of rival cult groups is one of the reasons people join cults today, after such organisations grew to deadly proportions; it was not a founding goal.

**5. A** — *Kidnapping figures.* In 2018 there were 838 reported kidnap cases; by 2022 the figure had risen to 4,600, an indication that cases had quadrupled. The 11,858 to 10,249 fall from 2013 to 2014 is the prison record for armed robbery, not kidnapping. The 1,676 figure would be a doubling, which understates the rise, and the last option reverses the direction of the change.
