# Thursday — PHY_108 fast-hour check

*The Young modulus beam experiment: definitions, the depression formula, apparatus and the gradient.*
*Five questions, straight after the fast hour. Recall only. Score out of 5.*

**1.** Plotting depression Y against mass m, the gradient s gives E how?
A. E = 4 b d cubed s / (g l cubed)
B. E = g l cubed / (4 b d cubed s)
C. E = 48 s / (g l cubed)
D. E = g l cubed s / (4 b d cubed)

**2.** What is the unit of strain?
A. Pascals
B. Newtons per metre
C. Metres per kilogram
D. It has no unit

**3.** For a rectangular cross-section, the moment of inertia I equals what?
A. b d cubed / 12
B. d b cubed / 12
C. b d / 12
D. b cubed d cubed / 48

**4.** Which piece of apparatus reads the central depression?
A. The set of masses
B. The mounts
C. The dial gauge
D. The beam

**5.** What is the first mass loaded at the beam's centre?
A. 10 g
B. 50 g
C. 100 g
D. 500 g

## Answers

**1. B** — Rearranging Y = m g l cubed / (4 E b d cubed) puts E and s both in the denominator, so a stiffer material gives a shallower line. The other options invert or misplace the slope.

**2. D** — Strain is a relative change in dimension, so it is dimensionless. That is why E, being stress divided by strain, carries the same unit as stress: N/m squared, or pascals.

**3. A** — The cube goes on d, the dimension in the direction of bending, with b the breadth. Substituting this into Y = m g l cubed / (48 E I) turns the 48 into a 4.

**4. C** — The four listed items are the dial gauge, beams of different materials, a set of masses and two mounts; only the dial gauge reads Y. The mounts are the supports and the masses supply the load.

**5. B** — The procedure starts at 50 g, then 100 g, then 200, 300, 400 and 500 g, giving six points for the best-fit line. 500 g is the last load, not the first.
