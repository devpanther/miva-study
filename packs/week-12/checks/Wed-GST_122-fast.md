# Wednesday — GST_122 fast-hour check

*Active listening's three benefits and six techniques, the note-taking tips, and deductive versus inductive reasoning.*
*5 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 5.*

**1.** In a meeting, Yemi keeps cutting in with her reply before the speaker has finished his point, and he is left feeling unheard. Which technique for becoming a more active listener has she failed to apply?
A. Avoid Interrupting
B. Minimise Distractions
C. Maintain Eye Contact
D. Paraphrase and Summarise

**2.** Ada tries to write down every word of a lecture, falls behind, and ends up with pages of notes she cannot use for revision. Which note-taking tip has she ignored?
A. Use headings and subheadings to structure notes
B. Do not write verbatim; capture main ideas, key concepts and supporting evidence
C. Bring highlighters and sticky notes
D. Divide the page into cues, notes and a summary

**3.** Two roommates are in a dispute. A friend listens to each in turn until both acknowledge the other's perspective and they find common ground. Which named benefit of active listening is this?
A. Enhanced Understanding
B. Conflict Resolution
C. Problem Solving
D. Improved Retention

**4.** Bola hands in a formal essay containing 'w/' for 'with' and an arrow for 'leads to'. Which caution about abbreviations and symbols has she ignored?
A. They must follow a standard list shared by all students
B. They should replace full sentences wherever possible
C. They belong in personal notes and may not be tolerated in formal writing
D. They may only be used in the cue column of Cornell notes

**5.** Which form of reasoning starts with specific observations in order to make broader generalisations?
A. Deductive reasoning
B. Strawman argument
C. Hasty generalisation
D. Inductive reasoning

---

## Answers

**1. A** — *Techniques for active listening applied to a scenario.* Avoid Interrupting means letting the speaker finish their thoughts before responding, because interruptions hinder the flow of communication and make the speaker feel unheard.

Minimise Distractions concerns the environment, not cutting in; Maintain Eye Contact is a non-verbal cue of attention; Paraphrase and Summarise is restating the message to check understanding.

**2. B** — *Note-taking tips applied to a fault.* The tip is to listen attentively and not write what you hear verbatim, but to jot down main ideas, key concepts and supporting evidence in clear, simple sentences, which prevents information overload.

Headings and subheadings help you find information later; writing materials are a separate preparation tip; dividing the page is the Cornell method, which does not by itself stop transcription.

**3. B** — *The three benefits of active listening.* Conflict Resolution is the benefit under which active listening lets individuals acknowledge each other's perspectives, a crucial step in finding common ground and resolving disputes.

Enhanced Understanding is grasping the words plus the emotions, intentions and context; Problem Solving is giving informed solutions after comprehending a problem's details; improved retention is a payoff of note-taking, not one of the three headings.

**4. C** — *Abbreviations in notes versus formal writing.* A system of abbreviations and symbols is for personal notes that are not turned in or submitted; abbreviations and symbols may not be tolerated in formal writings.

The system is personal, not standardised; it is meant to speed up note-taking without sacrificing clarity, not to replace sentences in formal work; nothing restricts symbols to the cue column.

**5. D** — *Inductive versus deductive reasoning in presentations.* Inductive reasoning starts with specific observations to make broader generalisations; deductive reasoning draws specific conclusions from general premises.

Strawman arguments and hasty generalisations are named logical fallacies to be avoided in a presentation, not forms of reasoning to be applied.
