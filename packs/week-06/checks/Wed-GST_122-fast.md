# Wednesday — GST_122 fast-hour check

*The ordered steps and conventions for writing a summary, a letter and a report.*
*5 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 5.*

**1.** Bisi's summary of a long article is in her own words and follows the article's order, but it keeps every minor example and illustration the author used. Which step of summary writing has she skipped?
A. Maintain structure
B. Eliminate unnecessary details
C. Identify the thesis or main idea
D. Check for coherence

**2.** Put these letter-writing steps in the correct order: (1) choose the right format; (2) identify purpose and audience; (3) begin with a greeting; (4) introduction stating the reason for writing.
A. 2, 1, 3, 4
B. 1, 2, 3, 4
C. 2, 3, 1, 4
D. 1, 2, 4, 3

**3.** A sentence in a report on poverty reads: 'The state should increase investment in education and healthcare infrastructure and introduce social safety nets.' In which section of the report does it belong?
A. Findings
B. Conclusions
C. Recommendations
D. Methodology

**4.** Which of the following is NOT one of the sections in the structured format of a report?
A. Methodology
B. Salutation
C. Findings
D. Recommendations

**5.** Which term is defined as a condensed and concise version of a larger text or document, highlighting the main ideas, key points and essential information?
A. Report
B. Letter
C. Outline
D. Summary

---

## Answers

**1. B** — *Steps of summary writing applied to a fault.* 'Eliminate unnecessary details' requires omitting minor details and examples unless they are crucial to the main idea. Keeping every example breaks exactly this step.

'Maintain structure' is satisfied because she kept the original order; 'condense in your own words' is satisfied; nothing in the scenario says the main idea was missed or that the summary fails to flow.

**2. A** — *Ordering the steps of letter writing.* The steps run: identify purpose and audience, choose the right format, begin with a greeting, introduction, body, politeness and tone, closing, closing salutation, review and edit. The format is chosen on the basis of the purpose and audience, so it cannot come first.

'1, 2, 3, 4' puts format before purpose; '2, 3, 1, 4' greets before choosing a format; '1, 2, 4, 3' also states the purpose before the salutation.

**3. C** — *Conclusion versus recommendation in a report.* Recommendations suggest actionable steps based on the conclusions; a sentence that says what should be done is a recommendation.

A conclusion would interpret what the findings mean without prescribing action; findings present the data and evidence; methodology describes how the data were gathered.

**4. B** — *Sections of a report.* A report follows a structured format of introduction, methodology, findings, conclusions and recommendations.

A salutation is the greeting that opens a letter, not a section of a report; the other three options are all genuine report sections.

**5. D** — *Definition of a summary.* A summary is a condensed and concise version of a larger text that highlights the main ideas, key points and essential information, giving a brief overview without unnecessary details.

A report is a formal document presenting detailed information, analysis, findings and conclusions; a letter is a written message addressed to a specific person or group; an outline is a planning tool written before drafting.
