# Monday — GST_112 fast-hour check

*The indigenous trade system of 1500 to 1800 AD and Nigeria's economic story from 1960 to the loss of the American market.*
*5 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 5.*

**1.** Put these four episodes of Nigeria's economy in the order they happened: (A) austerity measures under the Buhari regime; (B) an oil boom during which the government saved nothing; (C) the Structural Adjustment Programme adopted under IMF pressure; (D) India replaces the US as the major buyer of Nigeria's crude.
A. B, A, C, D
B. B, C, A, D
C. A, B, C, D
D. B, A, D, C

**2.** In a pre-colonial Nigerian town a boy is attached to a master weaver, works under him for years and is then set up as a weaver himself. Which feature of the indigenous trade system does this illustrate?
A. Economic specialisation by region
B. Cultural diffusion
C. The apprenticeship system
D. Colonial intrusion

**3.** In 2014 the largest buyer of Nigeria's crude oil announced that it would buy less. Which country was it, and why?
A. India, because of its own shale oil production
B. The United States, because of its own shale oil production
C. The United States, because of oil pipeline vandalisation in Nigeria
D. The United Kingdom, because of a shift to renewable energy

**4.** Which of the following statements about the Nigerian economy around independence is correct?
A. Industry accounted for over fifty per cent of GDP before independence
B. GDP growth fell from 4.89% in 1960 to 1.89% in 1965
C. Nigeria was tipped to be one of the 20 largest economies by 1980, behind Malaysia
D. In 1960 agriculture absorbed over 75% of the workforce

**5.** Which of the following best describes Nigeria's indigenous trade system between 1500 and 1800 AD?
A. A crude-oil-dependent economy run through regional Development Corporations
B. Communities shaped by geography exchanged the goods they produced for the goods they needed
C. A Western capitalist economy based on the export of cash crops
D. A state-run economy directed by a national development plan

---

## Answers

**1. A** — *Sequence of Nigeria's economic story since 1960.* The 1970s oil boom came first with no savings (B); the oil crunch of the mid-1980s brought austerity under Buhari (A); the IMF then pressed Nigeria into SAP (C); the loss of the American market to shale oil and India's rise as the main buyer came after 2014 (D). The second option puts SAP before austerity, the third puts austerity before the boom that made it necessary, and the fourth puts the loss of the US market before SAP.

**2. C** — *The apprenticeship system.* The apprenticeship system is how skills and knowledge were passed down in the indigenous trade system: a learner worked under a master and was then established in the craft. Economic specialisation is about regions producing what their geography favoured, and cultural diffusion is the spread of ideas along trade routes; both are consequences of trade rather than the way skills were transmitted. Colonial intrusion is what later disrupted the system.

**3. B** — *Loss of the American market for Nigerian crude.* The United States was the largest buyer of Nigeria's crude, and in 2014, owing to shale oil production at home, it announced reduced purchases. India then replaced the US as the major buyer, so India is the successor, not the country that cut back. Pipeline vandalisation is one of the listed causes of Nigeria's economic woes but not the reason the US gave, and renewable energy is mentioned only as a future threat to demand for crude.

**4. D** — *The economy at independence.* In 1960 agriculture absorbed over 75% of the workforce, with industry and other sectors taking the rest. It was agriculture, not industry, that accounted for over fifty per cent of GDP before independence. GDP growth rose, not fell, from 1.89% in 1960 to 4.89% in 1965. Nigeria was tipped to be among the 20 largest economies by 1980 ahead of Malaysia, not behind it.

**5. B** — *Definition of the indigenous trade system.* The indigenous economies were shaped by geographical factors, with regions specialising in agriculture, trade or craftsmanship, and communities traded the goods they produced for those they needed, forming a web of economic connections along the Niger River. The Western capitalist model is what colonial intrusion turned that system into. Oil dependence and Development Corporations belong to the post-1960 story, and a state-run planned economy was the early First Republic approach.
