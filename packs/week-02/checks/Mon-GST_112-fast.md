# Monday — GST_112 fast-hour check

*The four indicators of pre-colonial intergroup relations, with their named origin, conflict, trade and religion examples.*
*5 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 5.*

**1.** The Illah people speak Igbo, yet their masquerades speak Igala, a result of territorial wars between the Igala and the Nsukka Igbo. Which indicator of pre-colonial intergroup relations does this illustrate?
A. Traditions of origin
B. Religion
C. Trade and commerce
D. Conflict

**2.** Two Nigerian peoples both trace their origin to Baghdad, one descending from Saif and the other from Bayajidda. Which two peoples are they?
A. Kanuri and Hausa
B. Yoruba and Borgu
C. Yoruba and Benin
D. Igala and Nsukka Igbo

**3.** Which of the following statements about pre-colonial trade is correct?
A. The Jukun exchanged salt for firewood from the Tiv
B. The Yoruba and Nupe joined the trans-Saharan trade through the Efik
C. Ibibio land was a trade corridor between the Hausa and the Tuaregs
D. The Nupe bought shea butter and brasswork from the Yoruba

**4.** A society and its masquerade originated among the Efik but are today popular among some Igbo subgroups. Which of the following is it?
A. Ibini Ukpabi
B. Ekpe and Ekpo
C. Bori cult
D. Igunu

**5.** Inter-group relations is defined as the interaction between two or more distinct groups of which kind?
A. Political and military groups
B. Linguistic and cultural groups
C. Religious and trading groups
D. Ethnic and geo-political zones

---

## Answers

**1. D** — *Four indicators of intergroup relations.* The Illah case is given under conflict: the Igala and Nsukka Igbo fought wars for territorial reasons, and those wars forged political ties and increased interaction. Religion is the tempting wrong answer because masquerades are mentioned, but the cause of the mixing was war. Traditions of origin would need a shared origin legend, and trade and commerce would need an exchange of goods.

**2. A** — *Traditions of origin as an indicator.* The Kanuri descended from Saif and the Hausa from Bayajidda, and both trace their origins to Baghdad. Yoruba and Borgu are the kingdoms founded by Kisra in a different legend; the Yoruba and Benin are linked through Oranmiyan and Eweka; the Igala and Nsukka Igbo are a conflict example, not an origin one.

**3. A** — *Trade and commerce as an indicator.* The Jukun exchanged salt for firewood from the Tiv. The Yoruba and Nupe entered the trans-Saharan trade through Kanem Bornu, not the Efik. Ibibio land linked the hinterland Igbo with the coastal Efik and Ijaw, not the Hausa and Tuaregs. The last option is reversed: it was the Nupe who supplied shea butter, bangles, brasswork, potash and straw hats to the Yoruba.

**4. B** — *Religion as an indicator.* Ekpe and Ekpo originated among the Efik and are now popular among some Igbo subgroups. Ibini Ukpabi also ended up with the Igbo but is of Ibibio origin, which is the usual mix-up. The Bori cult is common throughout Hausa land, and Igunu is a Yoruba deity traced to the Nupe Ndakogbaya.

**5. B** — *Definition of inter-group relations.* Inter-group relations is the interaction between two or more distinct linguistic and cultural groups, matching the two major factors for identifying a group: culture and language. The other options pick up themes from the indicators (conflict, religion, trade) or from the geo-political zones, but they are not the definition.
