## Answers

**1. b** — force ∝ |q₁q₂| and ∝ 1/r²; the inverse *square* is the whole point of the law.
**2. d** — E = F/q₀, so newtons per coulomb (J/C is the volt, i.e. potential).
**3. a** — 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²; 8.85 × 10⁻¹² is ε₀, not k.
**4. c** — fields are vectors, so contributions are added head-to-tail, not by magnitude.
**5. d** — F ∝ 1/r², and 1/3² = 1/9.
**6. b** — lines leave positive charge and terminate on negative charge, or run to infinity if the charge is isolated.
**7. c** — potential is energy per unit charge, and energy has no direction.
**8. a** — the magnetic term is q**v** × **B**, which is zero if v = 0 or if **v** ∥ **B** (sin θ = 0).
**9. b** — magnitude |q|E = eE, and because the charge is negative the force is opposite to **E**.
**10. d** — V = kq/(d/2) + k(−q)/(d/2) = 0; the two signed scalars cancel exactly.
**11. a** — both contributions point the same way (away from +q, toward −q), so they add rather than cancel.
**12. c** — F ∝ q₁q₂/r²: the numerator gains 2 × 2 = 4 and the denominator loses (1/2)² = 1/4, giving 4 × 4 = 16.

**13.** Coulomb's law gives the force for one *pair* of point charges: F = k|q₁q₂|/r², along the line joining them, repulsive for like signs and attractive for unlike. The superposition principle says the presence of other charges does not alter any pairwise force, so the net force on one charge is the vector sum of the three pairwise Coulomb forces acting on it. In practice: Coulomb's law supplies each magnitude, a sketch supplies each direction, and superposition licenses adding them (by components if the charges are not collinear).

**14.** From the +2.00 nC at r = 0.200 m: E₁ = (8.99 × 10⁹)(2.00 × 10⁻⁹)/(0.200)² = 17.98/0.0400 = 449.5 N/C, directed away from it, i.e. +x. From the −5.00 nC at r = 0.800 − 0.200 = 0.600 m: E₂ = (8.99 × 10⁹)(5.00 × 10⁻⁹)/(0.600)² = 44.95/0.360 = 124.9 N/C, directed toward it, i.e. also +x. Both point the same way, so E = 449.5 + 124.9 = **574 N/C in the +x direction**. Force on an electron: F = |q|E = (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)(574.4) = 9.19 × 10⁻¹⁷ N, and since the electron is negative the force is opposite to **E**: **9.19 × 10⁻¹⁷ N in the −x direction**.

**15.** Electric field is force per unit positive test charge, **E** = **F**/q₀; unit N/C (= V/m); a **vector**, added by components. Electric potential is electric potential energy per unit charge, V = kQ/r for a point charge; unit volt (J/C); a **scalar**, added as signed numbers. Example: at the midpoint between two equal *positive* charges the field is zero (the two arrows are equal and opposite) while the potential is not (two positive numbers add). Equivalently, at the midpoint between equal *opposite* charges the potential is zero while the field is not.
