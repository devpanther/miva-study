## Short answer

**13.** State Coulomb's law in words, then write its **vector** form, defining every symbol you use and explaining how the form tells you whether the force is attractive or repulsive.

**14.** State the superposition principle. Two charges q₁ and q₂ exert a force F on each other; a third charge q₃ is then placed on the line directly between them. Explain what happens to the force between q₁ and q₂, and why.

**15.** A +9.0 μC charge is at x = 0 and a +1.0 μC charge is at x = 0.40 m. Find the point on the x-axis between them where the net electric field is zero. Show your working.

## Answers

**1. C** — *Statement of Coulomb's law.* Product of charges, inverse **square** of distance. A drops the square, B inverts both relationships, D uses a sum instead of a product.

**2. A** — *The two constants and their units.* k = 1/(4πε₀). B swaps the two values, C swaps the units, D quotes the gravitational constant and the elementary charge.

**3. B** — *Coulomb's law with like charges.* F = (8.99×10⁹)(9.0×10⁻¹²)/(0.010) = 8.1 N, repulsive because both are positive. C comes from forgetting to square 0.10 m.

**4. D** — *Inverse-square scaling.* F ∝ 1/r², so r → r/2 multiplies F by 4. B is the answer you get if you treat the law as 1/r.

**5. C** — *Superposition principle.* The key words are **vector sum**, and each pairwise force is computed as though the other charges were absent.

**6. A** — *Newton's third law applied to Coulomb forces.* The expression k|q₁q₂|/r² is symmetric in the two charges. Their *accelerations* differ enormously if the masses differ, but the forces do not.

**7. B** — *Superposition with symmetry.* Equal charges at equal distances give equal magnitudes in exactly opposite directions, so the vector sum is zero. C is the answer for a charge sitting midway between a positive and a negative charge, not between two like charges.

**8. D** — *Definition of electric field.* Force per unit positive test charge, units N/C. The field is a property of the source and the point, not of the test charge.

**9. C** — *Field of a point charge.* E = k|q|/r² = (8.99×10⁹)(5.0×10⁻⁹)/0.090 ≈ 5.0×10² N/C, directed **outward** because the source is positive. A forgets to square r.

**10. A** — *F = qE with a negative charge.* Magnitude = (3.0×10⁻⁶)(2.0×10⁴) = 6.0×10⁻² N; direction is **opposite** to **E**, so west. C mishandles the powers of ten.

**11. D** — *Null point for unlike charges.* Between unlike charges the two fields point the same way and cannot cancel, so the null point must lie outside the pair — and on the side of the **smaller** charge, so that the extra distance compensates for the larger charge. A and B assume the like-charge geometry.

**12. B** — *Unit conversion before squaring.* r = 0.020 m, r² = 4.0×10⁻⁴ m²; F = (8.99×10⁹)(6.0×10⁻¹⁸)/(4.0×10⁻⁴) ≈ 1.3×10⁻⁴ N. A is what you get by leaving r in centimetres.

**13.** *Coulomb's law, magnitude and vector form.* In words: the electrostatic force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of the magnitudes of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them, acting along the line joining them. Vector form: **F**₁₂ = (k q₁q₂ / r₁₂²) **r̂**₁₂, where **F**₁₂ is the force on q₂ due to q₁, q₁ and q₂ are the charges **with their signs**, r₁₂ is their separation, **r̂**₁₂ is the unit vector pointing from q₁ toward q₂, and k = 8.99×10⁹ N·m²/C² = 1/(4πε₀). If q₁q₂ is **positive** the force lies along **r̂**₁₂ (q₂ is pushed away — repulsion); if the product is **negative** the force lies along −**r̂**₁₂ (q₂ is pulled back — attraction). By Newton's third law **F**₂₁ = −**F**₁₂.

**14.** *Superposition principle and the absence of shielding.* The principle: when several charges act on a charge simultaneously, the total force on it is the **vector sum** of the forces each of the others would exert individually. Placing q₃ between q₁ and q₂ leaves the force between q₁ and q₂ **completely unchanged** at F: each pairwise force still obeys Coulomb's law with that pair's own charges and separation. What changes is the *net* force on q₁ and on q₂, because a new term (the force from q₃) is now added to the sum. Charges do not screen or block one another.

**15.** *Null point for two like charges.* Both are positive, so the fields cancel only at a point **between** them. Let the point be a distance x from the 9.0 μC charge, so 0.40 − x from the 1.0 μC charge. Setting the magnitudes equal, k(9.0×10⁻⁶)/x² = k(1.0×10⁻⁶)/(0.40 − x)². Cancelling k and 10⁻⁶ and taking square roots: 3/x = 1/(0.40 − x) → 1.2 − 3x = x → 4x = 1.2 → **x = 0.30 m from the 9.0 μC charge** (0.10 m from the 1.0 μC charge). It sits nearer the smaller charge, as expected.
