## Answers

**1. C.** Her wording: "directly proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them." A replaces the product with a sum. B drops the square — that would be a 1/r law, which is the *potential*, not the force. D inverts the distance dependence entirely, so the force would grow with separation.

**2. B.** The deck writes k = 1/4πε₀ = 9.0 × 10⁹ N m²/C², and every answer on it is computed with that value. A is ε₀ itself (and its units are C²/N m², not these). C is the elementary charge e. D has the exponent sign flipped, which would make electrostatic forces vanishingly small.

**3. D.** The force goes as 1/r², so doubling r divides by 2² = 4: 16/4 = 4 × 10⁻¹¹ N. A doubles the force instead. B divides by 2 — treating the law as 1/r. C forgets that the distance changed at all.

**4. A.** The electron is negative, so it is attracted to both protons; the protons are on opposite sides, so the two pulls oppose and the vector sum is the difference, 16 − 4 = 12 × 10⁻¹¹ N, pointing toward the stronger (nearer) one. B adds two opposing arrows. C keeps the right magnitude but sends it toward the weaker pull. D would only be true if the electron were midway.

**5. B.** She took +x as positive and wrote (rightward F₃₁) − (leftward F₂₁); a negative result means the net force points along −x, with magnitude 4.2 × 10⁻³ N. A adds the two magnitudes instead of subtracting. C keeps the magnitude but ignores the minus. D treats the sign as part of the magnitude — forces have no negative magnitudes, only directions.

**6. C.** Q₁ is negative and to Q₂'s left, so it pushes Q₂ to +x: 9.0 × 10⁹(18 × 10⁻⁶)(12 × 10⁻⁶)/10² = 0.0194 N. Q₃ is positive and to Q₂'s right, so it pulls Q₂ to +x: 9.0 × 10⁹(24 × 10⁻⁶)(12 × 10⁻⁶)/6² = 0.0720 N. Same direction ⇒ add: 0.0914 N. A and B subtract, as if the arrows opposed — the mistake of copying part b's method without redrawing the diagram. D has the right size but the wrong way; both forces drive Q₂ to the right.

**7. D.** E = F/q₀ is newtons divided by coulombs. A confuses the field with the force it exerts. B is the volt, the unit of potential — a different quantity, not in this deck. C inverts the definition.

**8. A.** E = F/q₀ = 3.00 × 10⁻³/2.40 × 10⁻⁶ = 1.25 × 10³ N/C. B has the right number but the unit of force, not field. C inverts the fraction (q₀/F). D drops both metric prefixes, so 3/2.4 = 1.25 — the error is a factor of a thousand.

**9. C.** |E| = k|Q|/r² = 9.0 × 10⁹ × 6.8 × 10⁻⁶/7.4² = 61200/54.76 ≈ 1.1 × 10³ N/C, and around a negative charge the field points radially inward, toward the charge. A gives the outward direction, which belongs to +Q. B forgets to square 7.4 (61200/7.4 ≈ 8300). D is the slide's own omission: the question asks for direction and the slide never supplies it.

**10. B.** E₁ = 9.0 × 10⁹ × 2 × 10⁻⁹/(0.200)² = 450 N/C, pointing away from the positive charge, i.e. +x; E₂ = 9.0 × 10⁹ × 5 × 10⁻⁹/(0.600)² = 125 N/C, pointing toward the negative charge, also +x. Same direction ⇒ 575 N/C along +x. A subtracts because one charge is negative — but signs never subtract fields, arrows do. C uses r = 0.800 m for the second charge instead of 0.800 − 0.200 = 0.600 m, giving 70 N/C. D reverses the direction.

**11. D.** F = qE, so |F| = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 575 = 9.2 × 10⁻¹⁷ N, and because the electron's charge is negative the force is antiparallel to E, hence −x. A keeps the field's direction, forgetting the sign of the charge. B divides by q instead of multiplying. C quotes the field's value with a force unit.

**12. A.** Slide 14: the magnitude is "still given as" k|Q|/r², and the direction is radially inward. B is the +Q case (slide 13). C invents a negative magnitude — the sign of Q sets the direction, never the size. D confuses "attracts a positive test charge" with "produces no field".

**13.** The deck states it as: *"when two charges exert forces simultaneously on a third charge, the total force acting on that charge is the vector sum of the forces that the two charges would exert individually"*, and adds that Coulomb's law alone describes only two point charges, which is why the principle is needed. "Vector sum" is the phrase that decides everything: along a line, two forces add if their arrows point the same way and subtract if they oppose. In Example 1 the electron sits **between** the two protons, so the two attractions pull it in opposite directions and the sum is 16 − 4. In part f of Example 2, Q₁ is to the left of Q₂ and repels it rightward while Q₃ is to the right of Q₂ and attracts it rightward, so both arrows point +x and the sum is 0.0194 + 0.0720. The arithmetic sign comes from the free-body diagram, not from the signs of the charges.

**14.** With q₁ = q₂ = +e (protons) and q₃ = −e (electron), r₁ = 1.2 nm and r₂ = 3.6 − 1.2 = 2.4 nm:
|F₁₃| = k|q₁q₃|/r₁² = 9.0 × 10⁹ (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹)(1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹)/(1.2 × 10⁻⁹)² = 2.304 × 10⁻²⁸/1.44 × 10⁻¹⁸ = **16 × 10⁻¹¹ N**
|F₂₃| = k|q₂q₃|/r₂² = 2.304 × 10⁻²⁸/(2.4 × 10⁻⁹)² = 2.304 × 10⁻²⁸/5.76 × 10⁻¹⁸ = **4 × 10⁻¹¹ N** (a quarter of the first, since r₂ = 2r₁)
ΣF = 16 × 10⁻¹¹ − 4 × 10⁻¹¹ = **12 × 10⁻¹¹ N = 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ N, directed toward the nearer proton.**
They must be subtracted because the electron lies **between** the protons: it is attracted to each, so the two forces point along the line in **opposite** directions, and the vector sum of opposing arrows is their difference. (The deck computes both magnitudes and then stops — it never states this total, so supply it yourself.)

**15.** The heading says "POSITIVE CHARGE" while the charge named is **−Q**; it should read "ELECTRIC FIELD AROUND A **NEGATIVE** CHARGE, −Q". Only the heading is wrong: the body correctly says the magnitude "is still given as" |E| = |F|/q = k|Q|/r² — identical to the +Q case — and the diagram correctly shows arrows pointing radially **inward**, toward the charge. For Q = −4.0 μC at r = 3.0 m: |E| = 9.0 × 10⁹ × 4.0 × 10⁻⁶/(3.0)² = 3.6 × 10⁴/9.0 = **4.0 × 10³ N/C, directed radially inward (toward the charge).** Note that the magnitude is computed from |Q|; the minus sign of the charge is expressed as the inward direction, never as a negative field strength.
