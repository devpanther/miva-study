# GST_112 — Week 5 Summary

*Nigerian Peoples and Culture · Week 5 (5 – 11 Oct 2026) · Topic: Military in Nigeria Politics; Nigeria from Independence to the Nigeria Civil War*

## The 7 most examinable things this week

1. **Independence and First Republic instability.** Nigeria became independent on **October 1, 1960**. The First Republic's instability is listed by name: the **Election of 1959**, fear of dominance, the **quota system**, disproportionate project allocation, the **Western Region crisis**, the creation of the **Mid-Western Region**, the **census crisis of 1962–63**, the **1964 election crisis (UPGA vs NNA)**, and the **Western Region election of 1965**. Context: Africa's first military coup was in **Egypt, 1952**; Nigeria went on to see **six coups and two attempted coups**.
2. **First Coup — January 15, 1966.** Led by the "five majors": **Nzeogwu (Kaduna), Ifeajuna, Okafor, Anuforo, Ademoyega**. Killed: PM **Abubakar Tafawa Balewa**, the **Sardauna of Sokoto**, Finance Minister **Festus Okotie-Eboh**, Western Premier **Ladoke Akintola**, Brigadiers **Zakariya Maimalari** and **S. A. Ademulegun**, and NDA Commandant **R. A. Shodeinde**. **Nwafor Orizu** (acting Senate President) transferred power to **Maj-Gen. Johnson Aguiyi-Ironsi**, the highest-ranking officer. Widely labelled an "Igbo coup."
3. **Ironsi's rise and gaffes.** Ironsi appointed **Chukwuemeka Odumegwu Ojukwu** military governor of the Eastern Region, released the coup plotters, surrounded himself with Igbo advisers, and promulgated **Decree No. 34** (unification). This fed northern fears of Igbo domination.
4. **The Countercoup — July 29, 1966.** Northern NCOs and officers killed Ironsi in **Ibadan**. **Lt-Col Yakubu Gowon** became Head of State and repealed Decree 34. Ojukwu and Gowon clashed over seniority. This triggered **the Pogrom**: **80,000–100,000 easterners massacred** in the north, followed by a mass return of Igbos to the East.
5. **Road to war.** Failed negotiations (including the **Aburi Accord**) preceded Ojukwu's declaration of the East's independence as **Biafra on May 30, 1967**. The civil war lasted **two and a half years**. Gowon immediately declared a state of emergency and created **12 states** out of the regions (3 carved from the East, only 1 predominantly Igbo) — plus a coastal **blockade** and **currency change** to isolate Biafra economically.
6. **International intervention (named actors).** **OAU** refused to treat it as a civil war (treated it as a war between two independent nations); **US and UK stayed neutral**; **USSR backed the FMG** with aircraft and advisers. **Tanzania, Gabon, Ivory Coast, and Zambia** formally recognised Biafra in **1968**. **France and Portugal** gave Biafra supplies/logistics; **Israel** and **China** expressed support (China with no material aid); the **Catholic Church and International Red Cross** gave humanitarian aid.
7. **End of war and the coup chain that followed.** Biafra collapsed by **January 1970**; **Philip Effiong** surrendered to Gowon in Lagos on **January 12, 1970**; Ojukwu fled to Ivory Coast. Up to **3 million dead**, **3 million displaced**. Gowon declared **"no winner, no vanquished."** The **3 Rs** (reconstruction, reconciliation, rehabilitation) began but ultimately failed, leaving Igbo marginalisation. This is followed by a further chain of coups running to **1999** — see the table below.

## Commonly confused

- **The five majors (Jan 15, 1966) vs the countercoup (Jul 29, 1966)** — the first coup was led by junior officers (Nzeogwu, Ifeajuna, Okafor, Anuforo, Ademoyega) and mainly killed northerners/westerners; the countercoup was carried out by northern NCOs and officers and killed Ironsi. Six months apart, opposite regional actors.
- **Two Heads of State killed in office** — Ironsi (countercoup, Jul 29, 1966, Ibadan) and Murtala Muhammed (assassinated by Dimka, 1976). Don't swap the killer or the year.
- **Dimka's coup (1976) vs Orkar's coup (1990)** — both **failed**. Dimka assassinated Murtala Muhammed and was arrested; Orkar attacked Dodan Barracks, seized the FRCN, and declared southern secession before being crushed in 10 hours. Fourteen years apart, different targets.
- **July 29 appears twice** — the countercoup against Ironsi (1966) and the coup against Gowon led by Col. Joseph Nanven Garba (1975) share the same calendar date, nine years apart.
- **Buhari's coup (1983) vs Babangida's coup (1985)** — Buhari (GOC 3rd Armoured Division, Jos) ousted Shagari in 1983; Babangida (Chief of Army Staff) ousted Buhari in 1985. Two years apart, easy to reverse.
- **Decree No. 34 vs "No winner, no vanquished"** — Decree 34 was Ironsi's unification decree, repealed by Gowon in 1966; "no winner, no vanquished" was Gowon's 1970 post-war reconciliation statement. Different men, different moments, four years apart.
- **The census crisis (1962–63) vs the election crisis (1964) vs the Western Region election crisis (1965)** — three separate, consecutive First Republic crises; the source lists them in that order.
- **12 states (1967, Gowon) is the only state-creation figure the source gives** — do not confuse it with later state-creation exercises; only 3 of the 12 were carved from the Eastern Region, and only 1 of those 3 was predominantly Igbo.
- **Aguiyi-Ironsi vs Chukwuemeka Ojukwu** — Ironsi was the national Head of State (1966); Ojukwu was the officer Ironsi appointed military governor of the *Eastern Region*, who later declared Biafra. Don't merge the two roles.
