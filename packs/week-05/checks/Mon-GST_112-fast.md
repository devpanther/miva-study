# Monday — GST_112 fast-hour check

*The coup sequence from January 1966, the countercoup, the 1975 coup, the end of the civil war and foreign backing.*
*5 questions, straight after the hour. Score out of 5.*

**1.** Put these events in the order they happened: (A) Ojukwu declares the East's independence; (B) the countercoup in which Ironsi is captured and killed in Ibadan; (C) the five majors' coup against Balewa's government; (D) Effiong surrenders to Gowon in Lagos.
A. C, B, A, D
B. B, C, A, D
C. C, A, B, D
D. C, B, D, A

**2.** A Head of State promised to end military rule on 1 October 1976, but in 1974 announced a postponement, and on 29 July 1975 he was removed in a bloodless coup. Who was removed, and who became Head of State?
A. Ironsi removed; Gowon appointed
B. Gowon removed; Murtala Muhammed appointed
C. Gowon removed; Joseph Nanven Garba appointed
D. Murtala Muhammed removed; Obasanjo appointed

**3.** Which of the following was NOT one of the reasons for the July 1966 countercoup?
A. Fear of Igbo domination in the north
B. Ironsi's compromising management of the first-coup plotters
C. Ironsi's policies such as the Unification Decree
D. The postponement of the transition to civilian rule

**4.** Which of the following statements about foreign involvement in the Nigerian civil war is correct?
A. The US and UK supplied aircraft to the federal side
B. France and Portugal formally recognised Biafra in 1968
C. The USSR supported the Federal Military Government with aircraft and advisers
D. The OAU treated the war as an internal civil war

**5.** After the civil war Gowon launched the programme known as the 3 Rs. What were the 3 Rs?
A. Reconstruction, reconciliation and rehabilitation
B. Reform, restitution and reintegration
C. Recognition, reparation and reconciliation
D. Reconstruction, reunification and resettlement

---

## Answers

**1. A** — *Chronology from the first coup to the end of the civil war.* The five majors struck on 15 January 1966 (C); the northern countercoup followed on 29 July 1966 (B); Ojukwu declared the East's independence on 30 May 1967 (A); Effiong surrendered on 12 January 1970 (D). So the order is C, B, A, D. The second option puts the countercoup before the coup it was answering; the third has secession before the pogrom that caused it; the fourth has the surrender before the war had begun.

**2. B** — *The 1975 coup.* Gowon made the 1976 promise, postponed it in 1974, and was overthrown on 29 July 1975 by officers led by Colonel Joseph Nanven Garba, who appointed Brigadier-General Murtala Muhammed Head of State with Obasanjo as deputy. Garba led the coup but was not made Head of State. Ironsi being replaced by Gowon was the 1966 countercoup, and Murtala being replaced by Obasanjo followed Dimka's failed coup of 1976.

**3. D** — *Reasons for the 1966 countercoup.* The four reasons for the countercoup were the fear of Igbo domination in the north, Ironsi's compromising management of the first-coup plotters, Ironsi's policies such as the Unification Decree, and the need to exact revenge for the first coup. A postponed transition to civilian rule was Gowon's mistake in 1974 and the trigger for the 1975 coup, not for 1966.

**4. C** — *Foreign backing in the civil war.* The USSR supported the Federal Military Government and supplied aircraft and advisers. The US and UK chose to be neutral. France and Portugal gave Biafra supplies and logistics, but the states that formally recognised Biafra in 1968 were Tanzania, Gabon, Ivory Coast and Zambia. The OAU refused to treat the conflict as a civil war and treated it as a war between two independent nations.

**5. A** — *Definition of the 3 Rs.* The 3 Rs were reconstruction, reconciliation and rehabilitation, launched swiftly after Gowon's declaration of no winner, no vanquished. The other options mix in words that sound right but were not part of the programme; the 3 Rs are also said to have failed, which is why the Igbos have continued to suffer marginalisation.
