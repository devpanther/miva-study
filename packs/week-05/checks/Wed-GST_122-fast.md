# Wednesday — GST_122 fast-hour check

*The three stages of the writing process, and naming pre-writing techniques and post-writing activities.*
*Five questions, straight after the fast hour. Recall only. Score out of 5.*

**1.** What are the three stages of the writing process, in order?
A. Pre-writing, during-writing, post-writing
B. Drafting, pre-writing, publishing
C. Planning, proofreading, revising
D. Pre-writing, post-writing, during-writing

**2.** Which post-writing activity is limited to typos, misspellings and punctuation?
A. Revision
B. Editing for grammar and style
C. Peer review
D. Proofreading

**3.** A poet associates words and images around a central theme. Which technique is that?
A. Listing
B. Outlining
C. Clustering
D. Storyboarding

**4.** Which post-writing step marks the end of the writing process?
A. Finalising conclusions
B. Publishing or submitting
C. Formatting and presentation
D. Checking citations and references

**5.** Which pre-writing technique produces a roadmap of key points and logical flow?
A. Free-writing
B. Brainstorming
C. Research
D. Outlining

## Answers

**1. A** — The lecture names pre-writing, then during-writing or drafting, then post-writing. Option 4 has the last two reversed; the others mix single activities in as if they were stages.

**2. D** — Proofreading catches surface slips and comes last. Revision changes content and paragraph order, editing works at sentence and word level, and peer review is feedback from another writer.

**3. C** — Clustering radiates creative associations from one central theme, and the poet is the deck's own example. Listing is a linear enumeration, outlining fixes sequence and hierarchy, and storyboarding plans scenes visually.

**4. B** — Publishing or submitting hands the work to its intended audience and ends the process. The other three are earlier post-writing activities, not the closing step.

**5. D** — Outlining sets out key points, supporting evidence and a logical flow, giving structure and direction. Free-writing is continuous unstructured prose, brainstorming yields an unfiltered pool of ideas, and research gathers facts and evidence.
