# GST_122 — Week 5 Retention Set

*Communication in English II · Writing Activities: Pre-writing & Post-writing. Sit this seven days after the lecture, closed-book.*

## Multiple choice

**1.** A poet writes a central theme in the middle of a page and surrounds it with associated words and images to explore creatively. Which pre-writing technique is this?
a) Listing
b) Clustering
c) Storyboarding
d) Drafting questions

**2.** Which sequence gives the writing process as the lecture presents it?
a) Post-writing → pre-writing → during-writing
b) During-writing → pre-writing → post-writing
c) Pre-writing → post-writing → during-writing
d) Pre-writing → during-writing → post-writing

**3.** Pre-writing is defined as the phase that precedes actual composition and involves strategic activities aimed at:
a) generating and organizing ideas, establishing a purpose, and constructing a preliminary framework
b) eliminating typographical errors and punctuation mistakes
c) verifying citations and adhering to academic conventions
d) reorganizing paragraphs to improve the flow of a completed argument

**4.** A writer finishes an essay, then rewrites the introduction so it better captures the reader's attention and reorganizes several paragraphs to improve the flow of the argument. This is:
a) Proofreading
b) Editing for grammar and style
c) Revision
d) Formatting and presentation

**5.** Which is the discriminating test between editing and proofreading?
a) Editing happens before the draft exists; proofreading happens after
b) Editing changes sentences and words (grammar, structure, style consistency); proofreading only corrects surface slips such as typos, misspellings and punctuation
c) Editing is done by a peer; proofreading is done by the writer
d) Editing concerns layout and margins; proofreading concerns argument quality

**6.** A novelist writes continuously and without structure to discover a character's backstory and motivations. The technique is:
a) Freewriting
b) Outlining
c) Peer review
d) Research

**7.** The learning objectives name three divergent-thinking methods for advanced pre-writing. They are:
a) Listing, proofreading and drafting questions
b) Outlining, storyboarding and formatting
c) Research, revision and editing
d) Mind mapping, free-writing and concept clustering

**8.** A business professional preparing a report considers that the readers are executives and stakeholders and that the aim is to inform and persuade, and adjusts tone, style and content accordingly. This pre-writing activity is:
a) Brainstorming
b) Clustering
c) Identifying audience and purpose
d) Finalising conclusions

**9.** An academic researcher finishing a scientific paper reviews every reference for accuracy and proper formatting. This activity belongs to post-writing and is chiefly for:
a) maintaining academic integrity
b) generating a pool of ideas
c) visual appeal of the document
d) discovering unexpected insights

**10.** Which post-writing step marks the end of the writing process and the transition to sharing the work with its intended audience?
a) Polishing transitions
b) Peer review
c) Proofreading
d) Publishing or submitting

**11.** A student exchanges a draft research paper with a classmate and receives comments on the clarity of ideas and the strength of the arguments. This is:
a) Seeking feedback from instructors or editors
b) Peer review
c) Revision
d) Brainstorming

**12.** Which distinction separates outlining from mind mapping?
a) Outlining happens after the draft; mind mapping before it
b) Outlining is visual and branching; mind mapping is linear
c) Outlining fixes sequence and hierarchy as a roadmap for writing; mind mapping explores relationships between ideas without committing to order
d) Outlining is a post-writing activity; mind mapping is a during-writing activity

## Short answer

**13.** Name the three stages of the writing process in order, and give the one-line definition of the first and the last.

**14.** Distinguish revising, editing and proofreading. For each, state the level of the text it operates on and give one concrete activity from the lecture.

**15.** For each activity below, name the stage (pre-writing or post-writing) and the specific technique: (i) a filmmaker plans the sequence of scenes visually; (ii) a journalist writes interview questions before the interview; (iii) a writer smooths the links between paragraphs of a persuasive essay; (iv) a student sets font size, margins and spacing on a term paper.

## Answers

**1.** b — Clustering associates words or images around a central theme for creative exploration.
**2.** d — The lecture frames writing tasks as pre-writing, during-writing and post-writing stages in that order.
**3.** a — This is the deck's definition of pre-writing almost verbatim.
**4.** c — Reworking the introduction and reorganizing paragraphs is content/structure work, i.e. revision.
**5.** b — Editing is the language level; proofreading is the surface-error level and comes last.
**6.** a — Freewriting is unstructured writing that lets the writer discover unexpected details.
**7.** d — The objectives list mind mapping, free-writing and concept clustering as divergent thinking methods.
**8.** c — Weighing target audience and purpose is what shapes tone, style and content before writing.
**9.** a — The deck states this attention to citation detail is crucial for academic integrity.
**10.** d — Publishing or submitting ends the process and hands the work to its audience.
**11.** b — Peer review is the exchange of drafts with a fellow writer, not an instructor or editor.
**12.** c — Both are pre-writing; the outline commits to order and hierarchy, the mind map only to connections.
**13.** Pre-writing → during-writing (drafting) → post-writing. Pre-writing: the phase preceding actual composition, generating and organizing ideas, establishing purpose and building a preliminary framework. Post-writing: activities after the initial draft — revising, editing and refining for clarity, coherence and effectiveness, ending in a polished final version.
**14.** Revising = content and structure (e.g. rewriting the introduction, reorganizing paragraphs for flow). Editing = sentences and words (e.g. removing grammatical errors, improving sentence structure, keeping style consistent). Proofreading = surface only (e.g. catching typographical errors, misspellings, punctuation mistakes). Order: revise, then edit, then proofread.
**15.** (i) Pre-writing — storyboarding; (ii) Pre-writing — drafting questions; (iii) Post-writing — polishing transitions; (iv) Post-writing — formatting and presentation.
