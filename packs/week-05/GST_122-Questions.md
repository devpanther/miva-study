## Section B — Short answer (3)

**13.** Define pre-writing and post-writing as the lesson defines them, and give the analogy the lesson uses for each.

**14.** Distinguish revising, editing and proofreading, stating what each examines and the order in which they should be done.

**15.** Name any four pre-writing techniques from the lesson and, for each, state in one line what it produces and when it is the right choice.

## Answers

**1. b** — *the writing process*: pre-writing, during-writing (drafting) and post-writing, which the lesson insists must form one seamless, interconnected flow.
**2. b** — *pre-writing*: the phase before composition, aimed at generating and organising ideas, establishing a purpose and constructing a preliminary framework.
**3. a** — *divergent thinking*: mind mapping, free-writing and concept clustering are the three named brainstorming techniques.
**4. c** — *freewriting*: continuous unstructured writing with no correcting or planning, used to discover unexpected details and insights.
**5. b** — *clustering*: associating words and images around a central theme, suited to creative exploration.
**6. c** — *outlining*: key points plus supporting evidence in logical order — the roadmap drafted from, not prose.
**7. b** — *revision*: the global post-writing stage that works on content, structure, paragraph order and coherence of the argument.
**8. c** — *proofreading*: the final, narrowest sweep for typographical errors, misspellings and punctuation.
**9. b** — *identifying audience and purpose*: a pre-writing decision that governs tone, style and content.
**10. b** — *editing*: sentence-level work on grammar, structure, word choice, redundancy and consistency of style; it changes nothing structural.
**11. b** — *the outline as roadmap*: it organises thoughts, structures the piece and gives clear direction.
**12. c** — *publishing or submitting*: the transition from writing to sharing the work with its intended audience.
**13.** *Pre-writing vs post-writing*: pre-writing is the phase preceding actual composition, a set of strategic activities (researching, brainstorming, outlining) that generate and organise ideas, establish purpose and build a preliminary framework — the lesson likens it to an architect planning a structure. Post-writing is the phase after the initial draft is complete, in which the writer revises, edits and refines for clarity, coherence and overall effectiveness to produce a polished final version — likened to polishing a gem for clarity and shine.
**14.** *Revising vs editing vs proofreading*: revising is global and comes first — content, structure, paragraph order, argument, transitions and conclusion, with whole paragraphs added, cut or reordered. Editing comes second and works at sentence level — grammar, sentence structure, word choice, redundancy and consistency of style. Proofreading comes last and is a surface check only — typographical errors, misspellings, punctuation, and matters of formatting and citation. Big to small: revise, then edit, then proofread, because polishing sentences in a passage you may delete is wasted effort.
**15.** *Pre-writing techniques* (any four): **brainstorming** produces a raw pool of possible ideas, right when you have a topic but no angle; **freewriting** produces continuous unedited prose that surfaces unexpected insights, right when you are blocked or exploring; **mind mapping** produces a branching visual map of how sub-themes relate, right for argumentative or two-sided topics; **listing** produces an ordered set of parallel items, right for compare-and-contrast work; **research** produces the evidence base and sources, right for any evidence-led topic; **drafting questions** produces the who/what/where/when/why/how the piece must answer, right when coverage must be complete; **outlining** produces the roadmap of key points and supporting evidence, right as the last step before drafting.
