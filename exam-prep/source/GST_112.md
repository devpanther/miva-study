    In Summary


In this week’s lesson, you explored the diverse ethnic groups and their contemporary geographical
divisions in Nigeria. Nigeria, with a history stretching back to 39,000 BC, is a melting pot of
cultures that have developed over millennia.
The North West Zone is characterised by the predominance of the Hausa and Fulani people,
who trace their heritage to conquest and assimilation, sharing a common language and Islamic
faith. The North East Zone showcases the Kanuri and Jukun communities, known for their
unique socio-political systems. Meanwhile, the North Central Zone stands out as the most
ethnically diverse region, housing groups like Borgu, Nupe, Igala, and Tiv.
The Yoruba people take centre stage in the South West Zone, with distinct subgroups and a
history deeply rooted in lineage and leadership. In the Southeast Zone, the Igbo community
prevails, once thriving under a democratic age-grade system.
Lastly, the South-south zone exhibits ethnic diversity, featuring groups such as the Edo, Itsekiri,
Ijaw, and Efik, all with democratic systems and influential kingdoms. These zones encapsulate
Nigeria’s rich history, cultures, and interactions, forming a diverse tapestry of traditions and
beliefs, evident in shared origin stories, conflicts, trade, and religious practises.



    Bullet point summary


•    Nigeria is the most populous country in Africa, with a history dating back to 39,000 BC.
•    Nigeria’s six geo-political zones include North West, North East, North Central, South West,
     Southeast, and South-South, each with distinct ethnic groups.
•    Notable ethnic groups in these zones include the Hausa and Fulani in the North West,
     Kanuri and Jukun in the North East, and various ethnicities in the North Central.
•    The South West is primarily occupied by the Yoruba, the Southeast by the Igbo, and the
     South-South has diverse ethnicities, showcasing Nigeria’s rich cultural diversity and history.

Europe, Africa, and the Americas.


The Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade was a massive and inhumane system that had a devastating impact on
Africa. It is estimated that over 15 million Africans were sold as slaves and transported to the Americas.
The slave trade also had a profound impact on European and American societies. It helped to shape the
economies of these societies and contributed to the rise of racism and white supremacy.
