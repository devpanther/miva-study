This week, I learned about Ohmic resistance, a fundamental concept in physics. Ohmic resistance is a property

by Ohm’s Law. I also learned about the different types of resistors, including Ohmic and non-Ohmic resistors.

resistors include varistors, photoresistors, and thermistors.


    Bullet point summary

     across it, provided the temperature remains constant.

     current and voltage values.
•    The connection between Ohm’s Law and Ohmic resistance lies in the fact that Ohm’s Law provides an
     equation that explains the behaviour of materials or components exhibiting ohmic resistance.
◊      Ohmic resistors have the following characteristics:
◊      Their resistance remains constant regardless of the voltage applied or the current flowing through them.

    In Summary

This week, I learned about Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law (KVL), a fundamental law of circuit analysis that states
that the sum of voltages around a closed loop in a circuit is equal to zero. KVL is rooted in the conservation
of energy, which states that energy cannot be created or destroyed.
In addition to learning about the theoretical aspects of KVL, I also learned how to experimentally verify this
law. This can be done using a simple circuit consisting of a battery, a resistor, and a voltmeter. The voltmeter
is connected across the resistor, and the circuit is completed by connecting the positive terminal of the
battery to the resistor and the negative terminal of the battery to the voltmeter. By measuring the voltage
across the resistor and the voltage of the battery, we can verify that the sum of the voltages around the
loop is equal to zero. This confirms KVL and demonstrates that it is a fundamental law of circuit analysis.
KVL has a wide range of applications in circuit analysis and design. It can be used to analyze the voltage
distribution in complex circuits with multiple components and sources, design efficient and reliable electronic
circuits, and troubleshoot and repair faulty circuits.


    Bullet point summary

•    Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law (KVL) states that the sum of voltages around any closed loop in a circuit is

    In Summary

This week, I learned about Young’s Modulus, a fundamental property of materials that measures their stiffness. It helps us understand how materials respond to stretching or compression.
Young’s Modulus can be calculated using the formula Y = stress/strain, where stress is the force applied to a material divided by its cross-sectional area, and strain is the change in
length compared to the original length.


Young’s Modulus is important in many engineering applications. It helps engineers design structures that can withstand the forces they will encounter. For example, Young’s Modulus is
used to design suspension bridges, airplane wings, mattress springs, and automobile suspension systems.

For example, engineers at Boeing used Young’s Modulus to design the wings of the Boeing 787 Dreamliner. The wings of the 787 Dreamliner are made of composite materials, which

aircraft.
