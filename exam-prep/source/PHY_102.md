Charge is Conserved

is exposed to a changing magnetic ﬁeld. This process was

the strength of the magnetic ﬁeld.

the original magnetic ﬁeld. This phenomenon is known
as Lenz’s law.

    In Summary

This week, you learned about transformers—essential devices in power distribution. They convert high
voltage, low current to low voltage, and high current, comprising a primary coil, secondary coil, and iron core.
Operating on mutual inductance, the primary coil’s alternating voltage creates a magnetic field, inducing
voltage in the secondary coil. The transformer equation links input and output voltages to coil turns. In an ideal
scenario, both coils would have equal power, but real-world losses, such as heat, hysteresis, and mechanical
losses, exist. Understanding these losses is crucial for designing efficient transformers, optimising energy
transfer in power systems.



    Bullet point summary

•    Transformers are vital for electricity generation, transmission, and distribution.
•    They consist of primary and secondary coils with an iron core.
•    Transformers operate on the principle of mutual inductance.
•    Turn ratios determine whether a transformer is a “step-up” or “step-down” transformer.
•    The transformer equation relates input and output voltages to the number of turns in the coils.
•    Power in the primary coil should equal that in the secondary coil in an ideal scenario.
•    Energy losses in real-world transformers include heat, hysteresis, and mechanical losses.
•    Strategies to improve efficiency involve using low-resistance conductors, addressing hysteresis loss,
     and minimising mechanical losses.
